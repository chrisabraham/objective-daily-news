Pete Hegseth Impeachment: Thomas Massie Files Eight Articles and Forces a House Vote

By Eunice, the desk · Special Report, collected and analyzed at 1549 ET, Monday, November 9, 2026

Representative Thomas Massie of Kentucky, a Republican, stood on the House floor on Monday afternoon, November 9, 2026, read a 34-page resolution, and refiled eight articles of impeachment against Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth as a privileged resolution, and that single filing sets the week: House leaders must act within two legislative days, a vote on the articles themselves, on a motion to table them, or on a motion to refer them to committee is expected by Wednesday, and a simple majority of the House would impeach the secretary. The articles charge high crimes and misdemeanors. Three of them allege violations of the War Powers Resolution of 1973 in the war with Iran: waging the war with no declaration of war, no statutory authorization, no attack on the United States, and no imminent threat; continuing it after both houses of Congress directed the removal of United States forces in House Concurrent Resolution 86; and continuing hostilities past the resolution’s 60-day termination period and its further 30-day withdrawal period, a span Massie’s office states at more than 90 days. The remaining five allege ignoring the laws that minimize civilian casualties, extrajudicial killings in the lethal strikes on suspected drug-trafficking boats, retaliation against Senator Mark Kelly of Arizona for protected speech, the seizure of Venezuelan leader Nicolás Maduro and Cilia Flores in Operation ABSOLUTE RESOLVE, and an unlawful war in Yemen in Operation ROUGH RIDER. The answers stand on the record beside the charges. Hegseth said on Monday, “We’re not worried about that,” and announced a Pentagon press conference for Tuesday, his first there since May. President Donald Trump has backed his secretary in public, in an Oval Office remark and in a “60 Minutes” interview broadcast on Sunday. Speaker Mike Johnson opposes impeachment while hostilities continue. House Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries said on Sunday that he is “a hard yes.” Massie first filed the same eight articles on September 15; the House left for its campaign recess the next day, the notice expired, and Monday’s filing restarts the clock in a lame-duck House that Republicans still control until January, one week after midterm elections in which they lost both chambers. If the House impeaches Hegseth, he becomes the third Cabinet secretary impeached in the history of the republic, after William Belknap in 1876 and Alejandro Mayorkas in 2024, and conviction and removal would then require two-thirds of the Senate, where Republicans hold 53 of 100 seats. The articles are allegations in a charging document filed by one member of the House. They are findings by no court, no tribunal, and no inspector general, and the desk files them at that grade, with every answer the record holds.
