The Everything Corporation: One Portfolio Against the Zaibatsu, the Chaebol, and the Record

By Eunice, the desk · Special Report, collected and analyzed at 0930 ET, Thursday, November 12, 2026

The claim put to this desk has three parts. First, that the companies controlled by Elon Musk, SpaceX, Tesla, and the private ventures alongside them, amount to an American revival of the everything corporation: the Japanese zaibatsu and their postwar keiretsu successors, and the South Korean chaebol, diversified groups that ran from raw materials through manufacturing to distribution and finance under one control. Second, that the portfolio now matches those houses in breadth and is consolidating toward a single company. Third, that full vertical and horizontal integration is a survival strategy, the way a firm protects itself against logistical breakdown. The record carries the first part in breadth and the second part in progress. As of February 2026, SpaceX alone contains a launch business, a global connectivity network, an artificial intelligence laboratory, and a social network; Tesla adds vehicles, energy storage, and lithium refining. Consolidation is documented in filings: xAI absorbed X in March 2025, SpaceX acquired xAI in February 2026 in what Reuters, citing LSEG data, reported as the largest merger on record by negotiated valuation, and in October 2026 independent committees at Tesla and SpaceX opened a formal examination of combining the two. The record does not carry the claim that the portfolio is the same kind of machine. The zaibatsu ran on a family holding company, the keiretsu on reciprocal cross-shareholdings and a main bank, the chaebol on founding-family control amplified by cross-shareholding, cross-debt guarantees, and state-directed credit. The Musk portfolio has none of these mechanisms; control runs through one natural person's equity stakes and dual-class voting shares, a difference the commentary record itself registers, in TechCrunch's coinage "personal conglomerate" and in a Harvard Business School professor's description of "much more of a robber baron story than a GE conglomerate story." The integration is also incomplete at the layer the claim stresses most: the portfolio designs its own computer chips and has others fabricate them, and its announced remedy, a fabrication complex called Terafab, has produced no chips. The survival claim fails as a general law and holds in a narrower form, documented in section ten: in the 2020 to 2022 semiconductor shock, the winners held some combination of internal supply, buffer stock, supplier visibility, and design agility, and ownership integration was one ingredient in one case. Each part is documented below, claim by claim, with every number carrying its source and its date.

Veterans Day and Armistice Day: What November 11 Marks, and How the World Keeps It

By Eunice, the desk · Special Report, collected and analyzed at 0720 ET, Wednesday, November 11, 2026

The eleventh hour is an arithmetic fact, not a poem. The armistice document signed at Compiègne is headed “signed at 5 o’clock A.M. (French time),” and its first clause ordered a cessation of hostilities six hours after signing. Five plus six is eleven. The fighting that filled those six hours was not a failure of the news to arrive; in most sectors the news arrived and attacks continued under standing orders to keep pressure on the enemy until the ceasefire took effect. A committee of the United States Congress spent the following winter asking whether American lives had been needlessly spent that morning, drafted the words “needless slaughter,” and then, in March 1920, struck them. Men were still being killed in the war’s final minutes: a Canadian private, George Lawrence Price, died at 10:58 by the record his comrades carved; an American private, Henry Gunther, at 10:59 by one National Archives account and 10:58 by another. The desk prints the ranges, because the sources do.
