The Kraków Suspect Dies in Hospital, the Mediator Comes to Washington, and Asia Buys Back Friday

The night file, while Washington slept: the one man who could have answered the Kraków questions will not answer them, and Poland’s second day of mourning opened with the investigation turned from a questioning to a reconstruction. The region’s other fire acquired an operator’s statement: the Khor Mor fire is doused, the damage is being assessed, and no restart is promised. The Iran mediation acquired a room and an hour: the Oval Office at noon, closed, with Qatar’s prime minister inside it and Tehran’s foreign minister on the record waiting for an answer. Asia’s markets, handed the same weekend, bought back part of Friday. The Senate count did not move for a third day. Monday holds the meetings; Tuesday still holds the talks and the deadlines.

The Night in Brief

The night closed one file by death, priced another by the barrel and the index, and moved no count, no coalition and no tanker claim from claim to confirmation. In Kraków, Małopolska police announced early Monday that the suspect, a 24-year-old man wounded in the shootout with police on Saturday, died in hospital, in the Guardian account and the second police account filed below. The dead of the case therefore stand at ten on the accounts that count him with his victims: six in the Main Market Square, three at his home in Ostrowiec Świętokrzyski, and the man himself. Prosecutors pursue the terrorism investigation on the sequence, the weapons and the emblems on his clothing; no motive has been established, and the man who held it is dead. In Iraqi Kurdistan, the operator Dana Gas said Monday it was assessing damage at Khor Mor after dousing the fire, with no injuries to staff, in the operator’s statement filed below; production stands halted and 2,800 megawatts stand off the grid. In Washington, the day’s schedule is the news: Stephen Miller takes the chief of staff’s office, and at noon the president meets Qatar’s prime minister behind closed doors on the Iran file, the vice president meeting him separately, in the schedule account filed below. In Paris the G7 foreign ministers meet Monday and Tuesday. In Tokyo the Nikkei closed its morning up 3.44 percent. The desk files the night in that order.

Kraków at 0400: The Suspect Is Dead, and the Questions Stay With the Living

Poland’s second day of national mourning began with an announcement from the police who shot the man. “We would like to inform you that the perpetrator of the tragic events died in hospital early this morning,” Małopolska police wrote on X, in the accounts filed below. The man, 24, named in Polish media as Michał O., had been in critical condition since Saturday, when officers shot him within minutes of the first shots in the Main Market Square. He had not been formally arrested and had not been questioned; prosecutors had said both waited on his medical condition. That waiting is now permanent. The investigation the national prosecutor’s office opened as a terrorist offence covers two acts, the killing of six people in the square with the attempted killing of many others including police officers, and the killing of the three at the home; with the suspect dead, its work is the work the overnight accounts list: the full sequence, the source of the weapons, the meaning of the emblems, and whether anyone else took part in planning.

The morning’s new evidence is clothing and guns, and both arrive attributed and unfinished. Interior ministry spokeswoman Karolina Galecka told TOK FM radio on Monday that the man’s clothes carried many different emblems, partly examined and interpreted, the patches associated with various motifs that specialists are still analysing; she would not say more while the investigation runs. The minister’s Saturday reading stands where it stood: fascination with far-right ideology and very radical, ultra-nationalistic views, in the interior minister’s words, and a reading is what it remains. On the weapons, one Monday account counts 11 firearms recovered in all, four from the man or his car in Kraków and seven from the family home, in the victims and firearms account filed below; Polish media report he held a firearms permit and may have obtained guns from the home, and police have not confirmed that account. The wounded stand at 17 injured in the prosecutors’ count carried in the morning accounts, against 15 and 16 in the weekend’s files, with the two officers’ conditions divided in the accounts between regained consciousness in the evening file and one officer in a coma in the Monday account; both are printed in the Cocked Hat. The six of the square stand as the midnight edition named them by age and nationality, and the three at home stand at father 53, mother 52 and sister 21 in the Sunday confirmation the morning accounts repeat. Kraków’s own mourning runs through November 11; the national mourning ends Monday night.

Al-Ayyat at Midnight: Seven Dead, 125 Injured in the Late Count, and No Cause

The Health Ministry’s staircase ran four dead and 88 injured, then seven dead and 112 injured, and late Sunday the chief prosecutor’s office put the injured at at least 125, with more than 70 of them discharged after treatment, in the Associated Press account filed below. Genocide and Conquest: The Definition, the Intent Test, and Where the Courts Drew the Line

“Genocide” is a word with a birth certificate: coined in 1944, defined by treaty in 1948 as five acts committed with intent to destroy a national, ethnical, racial, or religious group, in whole or in part, as such, and applied by courts only where that intent is proven. “Conquest” is the older word: it names the transfer of peoples and territory by force, and it carries no legal test at all. The desk files the coinage, the Convention, the older record the word was carried back to, and the judgments.

Deportation in America: Visa Overstays, Removal Orders, and How the Law Actually Works

Deportation in the United States is an administrative process, not a criminal sentence: being present without status is a civil violation, while entering improperly and reentering after removal are federal crimes, and the system runs on that seam. Its caseload is majority non-criminal in the recent record—two-thirds of fiscal 2024 removals involved no criminal conviction or pending charge—its court backlog stands above three million cases, more than 1.4 million people carry final removal orders unenforced, and the statute that governs it asks nothing about whether the person removed is good. The desk files the law, the ladder, the counts, and the exposure, in that order.

Election Integrity in America: Voting, Mail-In Ballots, and the Theft Claims of 2016, 2020, 2024, and 2026

American elections are run by thousands of local offices under fifty state systems, and the documented record of fraud in them is real, old, and small against the count: 1,619 proven cases in the most-cited database across forty-three years and roughly 2.26 billion federal ballots. Four consecutive national elections have now drawn theft claims, from four different directions, and none of the four has produced evidence that changed a certified result.
