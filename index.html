Pete Hegseth Impeachment: Thomas Massie Files Eight Articles and Forces a House Vote

Representative Thomas Massie, a Kentucky Republican, refiled eight articles of impeachment against Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth on the House floor on Monday, November 9, 2026, as a privileged resolution, starting a clock that requires House leaders to act within two legislative days, with a vote possible as early as Wednesday. The articles charge Hegseth with waging the Iran war in violation of three provisions of the War Powers Resolution of 1973, ignoring laws that minimize civilian casualties, extrajudicial killings in the strikes on suspected drug-trafficking boats, suppressing the free speech of Senator Mark Kelly, seizing Venezuelan leader Nicolás Maduro without authority, and waging an unlawful war in Yemen. Hegseth said on Monday that he is not worried and will defend his record at a Pentagon press conference on Tuesday; President Trump has backed him; Speaker Mike Johnson opposes the effort; House Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries is a hard yes.

Organ Donation in America: Who Gets the Organ, the Law, the Black Market, and What Changed

On Sunday night a network drama put a liver, a computer, and a rich man in one operating room, and on Monday morning the country is asking how an organ is actually assigned. The record answers plainly: one national list, an order set by published medical formulas, no hospital algorithm of its own, and no judge who has ever ordered an organ to a named patient. What moves a person up the list is documented and narrower—an insurance clearance before the list, a center’s quiet power to refuse an organ in a patient’s name, exception points from review boards, and, for those who can pay for duplicate evaluations and a fast flight, a second and third listing.
