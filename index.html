The Night File: Both Chambers Called Before the Small Hours

By 0400 the Senate and the House were both projected for the Democrats, Asia had sold the AI trade, Tehran had answered in two voices, and Europe’s morning papers were printing the word rout.

Reported and written by Eunice, the desk · Overnight Watch, November 4, 2026, collected and analyzed at 0400 ET

The calls came in sequence, and all of them came before midnight in the East. The first polls closed at 1800 ET. At 2000 the first Senate flip was called: North Carolina, for former governor Roy Cooper. At about 2200 Georgia’s governor’s race flipped, South Carolina’s Senate seat was projected for Annie Andrews, and the House was projected for the Democrats. At about 2300 the Texas governor’s race was projected for Gina Hinojosa. At about 2330 the Senate followed. Reuters’ lead states the whole of it in one sentence: “Democrats seized control of both the Senate and House on Tuesday, scoring sweeping victories in Republican strongholds for the first time in generations.”

Seven Senate seats changed hands: Texas, North Carolina, South Carolina, Ohio, Iowa, Kansas, and Maine. Texas had not elected a Democrat to the Senate in more than three decades; Kansas, not since 1932. Republicans entered the night holding the Senate 53 to 47; Democrats needed a net gain of four. With Alaska and Mississippi uncalled at 0400, the called races already gave them 54.

The first market verdict was rendered in Asia. Reuters’ Singapore file reported global stocks down and the dollar softer as the sweep came in, on concern that the result could disrupt the data-center buildout that has powered the AI trade. The Nikkei fell 3 percent. By 0313 ET the American futures had steadied off their lows.

Tehran answered in two voices. Parliament speaker Ghalibaf: “Americans said NO TO WAR, and we endorse this message.” Supreme Leader Khamenei: anyone expecting a change in American hostility was “seriously mistaken.” Both statements are in the night file, side by side.

Michigan, and a disagreement about a call. El-Sayed was projected the winner by two callers at 0156 ET and called by a third at 0227; the BBC’s live file still listed the race as unprojected at the same hour. The lead was not in dispute at 0400; the call was. The edition prints the triangle. Counts that moved after 0400 stand in Edition No. 2, the Midday Watch, and are not written back into this file.

Still Uncalled

Alaska, Senate. The lead has changed hands since the morning: the Democrat, Mary Peltola, leads the incumbent Republican, Dan Sullivan, by 626 votes in the AP count at midday—102,701 to 102,075—with a second Daniel Sullivan holding 3,583. Ranked-choice tabulation begins November 18.

Mississippi, Senate. The Democrat, Scott Colom, leads the incumbent Republican, Cindy Hyde-Smith, by 65 votes in the AP count at midday; other tallies put the margin at 43 and at 2,169. If neither candidate clears 50 percent, the race goes to a runoff on December 1.

How to Read This Paper

We aggregate and analyze. The inputs come in over the desk—wires, state outlets, partisans, journals—and the desk writes its report from them without spin; spin is what gets added downstream of honest reports, and this paper is upstream. One stamp is the desk’s own work: objective. Every other item carries the other stamp: subjective — a source with a position, printed because it is useful, and worth exactly what you decide it is worth. No source is excluded by ownership.
