The Three at Home Are Named in Kraków, a Mandate Is Given in Jerusalem, and Two Tankers Burn Without Names

The night file, while Washington slept: Poland entered its mourning at midnight with the Kraków case completed in its outline — the three found dead at the suspect’s home identified as his parents and his sister, the suspect himself named by the prosecutors’ spokesman, and no motive established. Jerusalem supplied the night’s one new mandate: the president chose the opposition leader over the sitting prime minister to try first at a government. In the Gulf of Oman an Emirati crew pulled 22 men off a burning ship that no authority has named, in the same waters where two tankers are reported burning without names of their own. The count in Mississippi and Alaska did not move. Sunday opens as a day of mourning in Poland and the main day of Diwali in India, with the markets closed and the week’s questions — Hormuz, Abu Dhabi, the count — all waiting on Tuesday.

The Night in Brief

The night named three dead, handed out one mandate, and rescued 22 men from a ship it did not name. In Poland, the mourning declared for Sunday and Monday began at midnight, and Sunday morning brought the identification the Midnight Watch left as a strong indication: the three people found dead at the Kraków suspect’s home in Ostrowiec Świętokrzyski are his parents and his 21-year-old sister, the police confirmed. The prosecutors’ spokesman confirmed the detained man is Michał O., 24. No charge has been announced and no motive has been established. In Jerusalem, President Isaac Herzog informed Gadi Eisenkot on Saturday evening that he will be tasked with forming Israel’s next government, after factions representing 67 lawmakers recommended him against 46 for Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu; the formal handover is set for Sunday afternoon, and Mr. Eisenkot will have 28 days. In the waters east of the Strait of Hormuz, the United Arab Emirates National Guard rescued all 22 crew of a cargo ship on fire in the Gulf of Oman, every man stable, the ship unnamed and the fire’s cause unstated — while Iran’s Revolutionary Guards issued the full text of their tanker statement: two tankers, one in the strait’s eastern part and one within it, burning after striking mines on unauthorized routes with their navigation and positioning systems off, their names and flags still given by no one. In Asunción, the pretrial detention ordered Saturday stood through its first night. In Kyiv, the president stated the purpose he assigns to Friday night’s barrage: pressure, ahead of Tuesday’s talks in Abu Dhabi. A magnitude 5.7 earthquake struck central Japan at 1:04 a.m. local time and injured more than twenty people, the count still rising through the morning. The Senate count did not move. The desk files the night in that order.

Kraków at 0400: The Three at Home Are Named, and the Country Is in Mourning

The indication the midnight file carried is now an identification. On Sunday morning, Polish police confirmed that the three people found dead at the suspect’s home in Ostrowiec Świętokrzyski had been identified as his parents and his 21-year-old sister, in the Press Association account filed below. The three were discovered by a drone sent into the flat first, because officers feared explosives might have been planted inside, in the German wire’s morning file; bomb disposal experts later found no explosives in the suspect’s car, though ammunition had been found in it, in the running account filed below. No cause or time of the three deaths at the home has been stated by any authority. The case’s dead therefore stand at nine: six in the Main Market Square — four women and two men, three of them Polish, one a British citizen, two not yet identified, in the police commander’s Saturday-night statement — and three in a block of flats two hours’ drive away. Prosecutors’ spokesman Tomasz Waszczuk has confirmed that the man detained is 24-year-old Michał O., according to Polish media carried in the running account; prosecutors are investigating with the Internal Security Agency, examining ideological and personal motives, and have not established where he obtained his weapons. Polish media, citing unofficial sources, describe a lathe operator who held a sporting firearms permit, had two guns registered in his name, and trained at a shooting club in Starachowice. No charges have been announced; whether and when he can be questioned depends on his condition in hospital, where he remains after several operations, in stable but very critical condition in the last full account.

Sunday in Poland is the first day of the mourning, and the morning’s file is a file of care and ceremony. The two days of national mourning began at midnight under the president’s signed order. Twelve of the injured were in critical or life-threatening condition, Governor Krzysztof Klęczar said, in the German wire’s file. Two of Kraków’s horse-drawn carriage drivers, who work the rank on the square, are among the most seriously injured and are fighting for their lives, Radio Kraków reported Sunday morning; the rank is closed, and hospitals have appealed for blood donors, including for the officer shot three times while stopping the gunman. Five carriage drivers are among the injured in total, in Polish media accounts. A Mass for the victims is set for 4 p.m. local time Sunday at St. Mary’s Basilica, celebrated by Cardinal Grzegorz Ryś; the football association has asked for a minute’s silence before this weekend’s matches; condolences arrived overnight from Czech President Petr Pavel and Prime Minister Andrej Babiš, added to Saturday’s file. Evidence gathering at the scene was expected to continue until at least Sunday, Justice Minister Waldemar Żurek said Saturday evening. Separately, police detained a 23-year-old man armed with a knife on Kapelanka Street overnight, after officers fired shots; police have not said whether that incident is connected to Saturday’s attack, and the desk files it as a separate report, unconnected on the record. The interior minister’s reading stands where the day left it — the suspect acted alone and held very radical views, with indications of fascination with far-right ideology still to be investigated — and it remains a reading. No motive has been established by any investigator, and no charge has been announced.

Three More Dead in Kraków, a Judge Jails Peña, and Tankers Burn in Hormuz

The Midnight in Brief: What Saturday Settled, What It Left Open

Saturday settled a detention, a quarter’s accounts, and nothing else it was asked to settle. In Asunción, Judge Humberto Otazú ordered former President Santiago Peña held in pretrial detention at a military facility and former central bank chief Carlos Carvallo held in a detention center, a ruling the Evening Watch left pending at 1800; Mr. Peña’s attorney said he is pleading not guilty. In Kraków, the day’s count of the dead grew by three without a shot in the square: police searching the suspect’s home in Ostrowiec Świętokrzyski found two women and a man dead, and the prime minister told a late briefing there are strong indications the three are members of the suspect’s immediate family. The six dead in the Main Market Square stand as filed at evening—four women and two men, one of them a British citizen—with sixteen wounded in the police commander’s count, and national mourning begins today, Sunday, November 8, running through Monday, with the city’s mourning through November 11. In the Strait of Hormuz, Saturday evening brought explosions heard on Qeshm Island and at Jask, Sirik, and Bandar Abbas, a report from Iran’s Revolutionary Guards Navy that two oil tankers hit mines on an unauthorized route and caught fire, and an American statement that Central Command late Saturday disabled a St. Kitts-flagged ship that ignored warnings and tried to leave the region after visiting an Iranian port. No American statement by midnight claimed the explosions, and no Iranian statement by midnight explained them as a strike. The larger Iran decision promised Thursday was not announced. The count did not move: Mississippi and Alaska stand uncalled for a fourth day, Colorado’s 4th stands uncalled with it, and the talks in Abu Dhabi open Tuesday, November 10, with both delegations now named. Sunday is Diwali’s main day in India, and Poland’s mourning. The desk files the day in that order.

Kraków at Midnight: Three More Bodies, at the Suspect’s Home

The evening’s new fact in Kraków was found 120 miles from the square. CNN’s updated file, filed below, reports that police discovered the bodies of three people—two women and a man—during a search of the suspect’s home in Ostrowiec Świętokrzyski, about 120 miles north of Kraków, and found many weapons and ammunition in his vehicle. Prime Minister Donald Tusk told the late Saturday crisis meeting in Kraków that there were “strong indications” the three are members of the suspect’s immediate family. That indication is filed as an indication; no identification of the three has been published, and no cause or time of their deaths has been stated by any authority in the files taken in by midnight. The square attack itself stands as the evening filed it: reports began at about 3 p.m. local time, the suspect drove into the Main Market Square firing at random, six people were killed, and police shot and arrested him. The wounded figures and the suspect’s age do not agree across the late files, and the desk prints the disagreement in the cocked hat below.

What Saturday night added on the record is care for the wounded and a country entering mourning. The same CNN file reports the suspect underwent multiple surgeries and is in stable but very critical condition; that the two wounded police officers both underwent surgery and are in stable condition, one shot once and the other three times; that the justice minister said one officer has a non-functioning lung and urgently requires AB+ blood; and that the injured include five foreign nationals, from Austria, Germany, Portugal, Russia, and Britain, in an Interior Ministry spokesperson’s account. The British account records the police commander’s statement that three Polish persons, one citizen of Great Britain, and two persons not yet identified make up the six dead, and the foreign secretary’s statement mourning the British national and the other victims. The interior minister’s reading stands where the evening left it: everything points to the shooter having been influenced by far-right ideology, a matter to be investigated. No charge had been announced by midnight, and no motive has been established. Poland’s president will declare national mourning for November 8 and 9; Kraków’s mayor has declared mourning in the city until November 11. Sunday therefore opens in Poland as a day of mourning over nine dead in one case—six in a public square, three in a private house—with the connection between the two sets of deaths stated by the prime minister as a strong indication and by no investigator yet as a finding.

  • Book Marks — five reviews you need to read this week, November 5, 2026
    Book Marks selects five reviews of the week: Dwight Garner in The New York Times on Annie Proulx's Stories: New & Selected, Clare Clark in The Guardian on Louise Kennedy's Stations, John Semly in The Nation on William Gass's The Tunnel, Daniel Mason in The New York Times on Tom Drury's West of Loveland, and Nick Holdstock in The Guardian on William T. Vollmann's A Table for Fortune. The Stations excerpt carries Clark's account in full: a relationship traced over 25 years in dated entries broken by long gaps, a London of squats and gas heaters, an Irish diaspora scarred by poverty, violence, addiction, shame and loss, and a novel that is at its heart a love story and an elegy to a generation of lost Irish boys, its title holding both the transience of travel and the Stations of the Cross.
  • Chicago Review of Books — a haunting first love, loss and life in exile: Stations
    Eileen Zampa reviews Stations on its American publication: since its European publication in September the novel has received critical acclaim in Ireland and England, and fans of Trespasses will not be disappointed. Róisín and Redmond are two outsiders navigating family trauma, addiction and exile in Ireland and London from the 1980s to the early 2000s; Kennedy creates a tension between a modern reader's sensibilities and Róisín's character evolution, refuses to wrap her life in a neat bow, and leaves her future unresolved in the final pages, which some readers may find frustrating. The review calls the novel sweeping in thematic scope, with deft prose and unforgettable characters, in the tradition of Irish literature from Edna O'Brien to Claire Keegan.
  • Ron Charles, The Tuesday Review — the demands of love: Stations
    Ron Charles reviews Stations as a story of devotion, addiction and the limits of friendship: Róisín McCaffrey and Red Camelin meet as teenagers, and neither recovers from the moment of happiness. Kennedy constructs the novel as a series of dated moments over a quarter century, with silent gaps of days and one of 18 years; her prose mixes narration and internal reflection, and her dialogue, without quotation marks, captures the erratic structure of real conversation. Charles records that Kennedy worked as a chef for decades before publishing, that Trespasses, published in 2022 when she was 55, was shortlisted for the Women's Prize for Fiction, and that with Stations she has confirmed her status as a writer whose stories compete with the reader's own life. The book is listed at 288 pages and $28 from Riverhead Books.
  • Watermark Books — Stations, the publisher's record and the praise filed with it
    The bookseller's page for Stations lists Riverhead Books, publication November 3, 2026, 288 pages, ISBN 9780593714607, and the November 2026 Indie Next List.   • Publishers Weekly — Louise Kennedy has found her home

The novel is shot through with the political and social issues of Thatcher's England, including the Troubles and the AIDS crisis, and is focused primarily on the characters: Róisín McCaffrey and Red Camelin, on the dole in a decrepit London squat.
