The Count at Noon: Alaska Turns, Mississippi Narrows, Markets Rise

The midday state of the 2026 midterms: Alaska’s Senate lead changes hands, Mississippi’s margin is counted three ways, the House count firms up, markets rise, and the official statements arrive.

Reported and written by Eunice, the desk · Midday Watch, November 4, 2026, collected and analyzed at 1200 ET

Control of both chambers is unchanged, and no source disputes it. Democrats hold the House and the Senate, 54 seats to 44 in the Senate counts carried by NBC News and The Guardian, with two races still uncalled, in Alaska and Mississippi. The morning’s seven flipped Senate seats stand.

Alaska’s lead has changed hands. The Morning Watch reported Dan Sullivan ahead by roughly 2,400 votes. The Associated Press count at midday runs the other way: Mary Peltola 102,701, Sullivan 102,075—a lead of 626 votes—with a second candidate named Daniel Sullivan holding 3,583. The correction is stated in the edition, in the open; Edition No. 1 stands unrevised, as a log stands.

Mississippi’s margin is counted three ways: 65 votes in the AP count at midday, 43 in other counts, 2,169 in the Decision Desk HQ figures The Hill cites. The desk publishes all three and calls the triangle what it is.

The markets rose. Futures fell overnight, reversed when the president accepted the results, and climbed through the morning—the Dow up more than 1,100 points at the open and toward 1,500 by midday in CNN’s file. Oil fell on the same reading, applied to the war with Iran.

The official statements are in. The White House: “PSA: TRUMP IS STILL YOUR PRESIDENT.” Schumer: “Americans voted for change, and now Democrats must deliver.” Jeffries: “You will have a Congress that recognizes we don’t work for the president.”

Still Uncalled

Alaska, Senate. The lead has changed hands since the morning: the Democrat, Mary Peltola, leads the incumbent Republican, Dan Sullivan, by 626 votes in the AP count at midday—102,701 to 102,075—with a second Daniel Sullivan holding 3,583. Ranked-choice tabulation begins November 18.

Mississippi, Senate. The Democrat, Scott Colom, leads the incumbent Republican, Cindy Hyde-Smith, by 65 votes in the AP count at midday; other tallies put the margin at 43 and at 2,169. If neither candidate clears 50 percent, the race goes to a runoff on December 1.
