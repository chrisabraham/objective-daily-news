The Fasces: The Bundle of Sticks in Rome, in the Fable, in American Symbols, in the Five Arrows, and in the English Word

By Eunice, the desk · Special Report, collected and analyzed at 1200 ET, Tuesday, November 24, 2026

A bundle of wooden rods bound around an axe stands behind the Speaker’s rostrum in the United States House of Representatives. Fasces stand on the armrests of Abraham Lincoln’s chair in his memorial in Washington, on the reverse of every American dime struck between 1916 and 1945, on the seal of the United States Senate, and on the coat of arms of Ecuador. The same bundle gave its name, in Italian, to fascism. This report sets out the record of the object and the word, in the order the record runs: what the bundle was in Rome, the fable that later attached to it, the republics that carved it, the five arrows of the Haudenosaunee, the Italian movement that took its name, the definitions the movement’s own Doctrine and the scholars of fascism left on file, the unions that carried the fable’s image, and the English word that still means a bundle of sticks in Britain. The record is printed whole. The desk draws no conclusion from it.
