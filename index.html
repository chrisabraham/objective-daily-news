The Jesus Movement and the New Jesus Movement

By Eunice, the desk · Special Report, collected and analyzed at 1820 ET, Monday, November 16, 2026

The first Jesus Movement is a closed historical file, and its outlines are not in dispute. Between 1967 and the late 1970s, a population of young converts—many of them drawn from the counterculture the movement’s own preachers had come out of—built an identifiable religious world in the United States: street missions and coffeehouses in San Francisco’s Haight-Ashbury, a fast-growing church at Costa Mesa in Orange County, communes across the country, an underground press of some fifty newspapers, a music that became an industry, and a mass gathering in Dallas in June 1972 that drew the honorary chairmanship of Billy Graham. Time put the phenomenon on its cover on June 21, 1971, under the line “The Jesus Revolution,” five years after the same magazine had asked on its cover whether God was dead. The movement’s standard scholarly periodization, in the work of the historian Larry Eskridge, runs from 1966 to 1977. What ended it, on the record, was absorption: its people, its music, and its converts passed into durable institutions—the Calvary Chapel network, the Vineyard churches that split from it, and the contemporary Christian music industry—and the name went out of use as the name of a living thing.

A Global History of Propaganda

By Eunice, the desk · Special Report, collected and analyzed at 1736 ET, Monday, November 16, 2026

Propaganda begins, in the surviving record, as royal self-publication. The earliest exhibits in this file are not arguments addressed to a public in the modern sense; they are a ruler’s account of himself, fixed in the most durable medium available and placed where it could not be answered—temple walls, a cliff on an imperial road, bronze pillars in a capital. The audience was the gods, posterity, rival courts, and the ruler’s own subjects, in varying proportions. What changes across the five thousand years that follow is not the impulse but the machinery: each era’s apparatus is built out of that era’s dominant medium, and each era’s persuaders describe their work in the vocabulary their time allows—truth against the lie, the faith propagated, enlightenment, publicity, public relations, information, and, in the present century, the governance of disinformation.

Bystander Intervention: What the Record Shows About Who Steps In, What Stops Them, and Why the Answer Changes by Place

By Eunice, the desk · Special Report, collected and analyzed at 1045 ET, Sunday, November 15, 2026

The question this report answers is the one a person in trouble is actually asking: if something happens to me here, in this street, in this city, will anyone step in—and what decides it? The research record, assembled over six decades, gives an answer that is more favorable than the famous story and more conditional than the reassurance. The famous story is the bystander effect, taught for fifty years as the finding that the presence of other witnesses makes each witness less likely to help. That finding is real in the laboratory conditions that produced it. But the murder that founded the field did not happen the way the newspapers reported it, and when researchers finally obtained camera footage of real public conflicts—219 of them, in three cities on three continents—they found intervention in nine cases out of ten, more bystanders producing more intervention, and interveners seldom harmed. The suppressors that survive scrutiny are specific: an event nobody can classify, a call nobody has been assigned, a fight read as a private matter, a weapon, and, in the medical case that has been measured most precisely, a rescuer’s fear of a lawsuit that has never once succeeded. Around those individual decisions stands the structure that decides most cases before they begin: whether the place has guardians, whether its residents trust one another enough to act for the common good, which informal code governs its public spaces, and what its law requires of a witness. Every one of those terms is local. That is the finding the rest of this report documents, study by study and statute by statute.
