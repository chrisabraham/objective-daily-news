The House Took Fifty Years to Grow Hands: X10 and the Relay Era, the Sensor Stack, the Luxury Tier, and the Humanoid Machines of 2026

By Eunice, the desk · Special Report, collected and analyzed at 2315 ET, Friday, November 20, 2026

The arc divides into three eras. In the first, X10, developed by Pico Electronics in 1975 and sold from 1978, sent commands over the house wiring as bursts at the zero crossings of the alternating current—one-way, unacknowledged, unauthenticated. A controller could order a lamp on; nothing in the protocol could confirm that the lamp was on, and a neighbor on the same distribution transformer could receive the signal. In the second era, the house acquired senses in sequence: the learning thermostat in 2011, the voice assistant in 2014, the doorbell camera, the LiDAR vacuum that mapped the home in 2016, and the Matter standard in 2022 to bind them. Through 2020 the flagship stack was eyes, ears, and a floor-bound puck—perception complete, manipulation absent. The era’s architecture risk has a date: April 15, 2022, when Insteon’s servers shut down without notice and hub-dependent homes went dark. The third era is the present one, and its machines are countable. 1X’s NEO, at $20,000 or $499 a month, is the only humanoid a consumer can order; its Hayward factory entered full-scale production on April 30, 2026, and the verified count of units delivered to a paying customer as of November 13, 2026 is zero, with early output routed to internal testing and a company statement pointing to first shipments in December. The story’s beats divide into three classes under the modern record. Three beats are rerouted: the dismissal, the removal from the office, and the arrest each still happen, but each now generates process—a personally communicated order, a desk appearance ticket or an arraignment inside about a day, a competency examination if the defendant cannot or will not participate.
