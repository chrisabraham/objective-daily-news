Explosions Again on Qeshm, a President Removed in Asunción, and the Count Still Waits

The night file, while Washington slept: the war reported itself again from Qeshm Island — two projectiles in the Suza area, a drone claimed down, no American confirmation of the specific impacts and no damage assessment from Tehran — and the mediation reported itself intact from Doha, where Qatar said reports of its failure were false. South America supplied the night’s one completed act: Paraguay’s Senate removed its president and swore in his successor. The count in Mississippi and Alaska did not move. Saturday opens with the markets closed and one scheduled item, Berkshire Hathaway’s third-quarter report at 0800 ET.

The Night in Brief

Nothing the week left open was closed in the night, and one presidency was. The open file at midnight — the larger Iran decision, the two Senate races, the leadership questions — stands open at 0400 in the same figures. What the night added is a second round of reports from Qeshm Island, a Qatari statement that the mediation between Washington and Tehran has not failed, and the first full accounts of Friday’s completed act in Asunción, where Paraguay’s Senate removed President Santiago Peña over the collapse of Ueno Bank, placed him under house arrest, and swore in Vice President Pedro Alliana to finish the term to August 2028. Beijing’s confirmation that talks are under way on a three-way meeting of Presidents Xi, Trump, and Putin at Shenzhen on November 18 and 19 stands beside them. The president, in Florida, added a sentence of his own: Russia and Ukraine, he said, want to settle, for the first time. The desk files the night in that order.

Qeshm Again: Two Projectiles Reported, a Drone Claimed, No Confirmation of the Impacts

Iranian media reported a second night of explosions on Qeshm Island, and no government has confirmed what the explosions were. Reuters’ Cairo file, filed below, reports that Tasnim said early Saturday that initial reports indicated two missiles had struck Qeshm Island, with no official information on possible damage, and that IRNA had earlier reported local witnesses saying two projectiles struck the Suza area after the sound of an aircraft was heard. A second account adds that state television reported two explosions in the Souza area late Friday with no casualties, that Fars reported explosions in Qeshm and in Bandar Khamir, and that none of the accounts has been independently confirmed and there was no immediate American confirmation of a new strike. A third file records the American position in one sentence: the United States military has not commented on the report. The reports arrive twelve hours after the president confirmed Friday evening’s limited strike on the same island, and the fullest account states plainly that the precise relationship between the reported impacts and the confirmed operation has not been independently established. That sentence stands at 0400.

Tehran’s second claim of the night is a drone, and it is the second in two days. The Iranian Army announced that its integrated air defense network destroyed an American MQ-9 Reaper drone over Qeshm, in the army’s account as carried in Tehran’s file, timed 0052 GMT on Saturday. A second file pairs it with Friday’s claim, in which the Revolutionary Guards said their air defenses destroyed an American MQ-1 drone over the same island. The account of the president’s remarks carries the only American sentence in the drone file: Central Command said the situation was “relatively calm” and declined further comment. The United States has not confirmed either claim, and no wreckage or imagery allowing independent verification appears in any file the desk took in.

A Limited Strike at Qeshm, a Wider Lead in Mississippi, and a Lawsuit Dropped

The last entry of the day, filed before the log turns: Friday settled the labor market at 165,000 jobs, closed the worst week for stocks since June on a split day, and ended the Justice Department’s California ballot lawsuit by the department’s own filing. It left open the decisions it had open at dawn—the president struck an air-defense site on Qeshm Island in the evening and called it minor, while the larger Iran decision he promised stayed unannounced—and the count, where Mississippi’s Democratic lead widened to 2,574 votes, Alaska’s held at 637, and no race was called for a third day.

The Midnight in Brief: What Friday Settled, What It Left Open

Friday settled the labor market, the week in the markets, and one lawsuit. It left open the war, the count, and both parties’ leadership. The day’s largest settled fact arrived at 0830 and never moved: employers added 165,000 jobs in October against a consensus near 60,000, unemployment fell to 4.1 percent, and the two prior months were revised up by a combined 84,000. The week’s markets closed on that fact and against it at once—the Nasdaq up 1.09 percent and the Dow down 0.46 percent on Friday, the week down 2.7 percent for the S&P 500, its worst since June. In the evening the war produced the day’s last fact: the president confirmed, after landing in West Palm Beach, that American forces had struck an air-defense installation on Iran’s Qeshm Island, in the Strait of Hormuz, and called the strike minor. The larger decision he promised on Thursday—whether to resume the war in full—was not announced. In the count, Mississippi’s Democratic lead widened all day and stands at 2,574 votes in the wire’s Friday-evening figures; Alaska’s did not move from 637. No Senate race was called, for the third day. In the courts, the Justice Department dismissed its own lawsuit over 20,000 California ballots. The House called races on Friday in both directions and stands at 254 seats called for the Democrats and 162 for the Republicans in the wire’s Friday-afternoon tally, with 19 still open. Tuesday, November 10, now carries more of the open file than any day since the election: consumer prices at 0830, Mississippi’s ballot deadline, Alaska’s next count, both congressional parties’ leadership business, and the opening of the Abu Dhabi talks. The desk files the day in that order.

The Strike at Qeshm: A Limited Strike Confirmed, the Large Decision Still Unannounced

The evening’s new fact is a strike, and its scale is stated by the man who ordered it. President Trump told reporters after landing in West Palm Beach on Friday evening that American forces had knocked out an Iranian anti-aircraft installation on Qeshm Island: “They built a little bit of an anti-aircraft device and we knocked it out in about 15 minutes, maybe five,” in the Associated Press account filed below. The same account records that Iranian media reported two explosions in the Suza area of the island, that these were the first American strikes since Tuesday’s elections, and that Central Command had released no account of its own when the wire filed. A second account of the confirmation carries the Iranian side of the same hour: Tasnim reported early Saturday that two missiles had struck Qeshm, and IRNA cited witnesses who heard an aircraft before two projectiles struck the Suza area; no official Iranian damage assessment and no confirmed casualty figures had been released, and the account states plainly that the precise relationship between the reported missile impacts and the American operation has not been independently established. A third file, carrying the same Tasnim report, adds what two American officials told the president’s advisers’ circle: that some advisers say the window for a major operation is closing and urge action before the new Congress takes office in January. The strike was the second on the island in five days; the first, on Monday, November 2, followed a missile fired from Qeshm toward an American aircraft, in the accounts filed earlier this week.

How to Read This Paper

We aggregate and analyze. The inputs come in over the desk—wires, state outlets, partisans, journals—and the desk writes its report from them without spin; spin is what gets added downstream of honest reports, and this paper is upstream. One stamp is the desk’s own work: objective. Every other item carries the other stamp: subjective — a source with a position, printed because it is useful, and worth exactly what you decide it is worth. No source is excluded by ownership.
