Podcasting: What the Record Shows About Who Invented It, Who Named It, and Who Made It Famous

By Eunice, the desk · Special Report, collected and analyzed at 1700 ET, Friday, November 13, 2026

Podcasting's mechanism was working before it had a name, its name existed before most of its famous hosts had recorded anything, and its mass market opened on June 28, 2005, when Apple put more than 3,000 shows into iTunes and took a million subscriptions in two days, four years before Adam Carolla, Marc Maron, and Joe Rogan launched the shows the popular story starts with. The mechanism is one element in a syndication format: the RSS enclosure, proposed by Tristan Louis in October 2000, implemented by Dave Winer in the RSS 0.92 specification of December 25, 2000, and demonstrated by Winer on January 11, 2001, in an essay, “Payloads for RSS,” that credits Adam Curry's persistence as what got it built. The first sustained original content arrived in September 2003, Christopher Lydon's interview feed, built by Winer; Kevin Marks demonstrated delivery from feed to iPod in October 2003; Ben Hammersley put the word podcasting in print in February 2004 and Dannie Gregoire gave the practice its working name that September; Curry's Daily Source Code, from August 13, 2004, was the developer community's test feed and the medium's first celebrity-fronted show. Curry himself was not the mascot of the story but one of its principals: an MTV host who registered mtv.com in 1993 and fought his network over it in federal court, then co-founded a web company absorbed into a public company in a 1999 merger the SEC records at a reported $230 million valuation, though what he personally took from it is documented nowhere, and his own later figure for it is his own. The comedians' contribution is real and different in kind: Carolla proved a fired radio host could rebuild an audience independently, Maron built the confessional interview into the medium's prestige form, Rogan built its largest audience and its licensing price, and none of the three invented, or claimed to invent, the machinery. The record also corrects the record-keepers: the first download record belongs to Ricky Gervais's newspaper show in 2006, Carolla's 2011 Guinness title is a certification his own certified total contradicts against Gervais's published numbers, and the Library of Congress introduces Curry as the Podfather in a sentence that misdates his show by a year. The argument the inventors started, over whether the medium stays open, is still running: it is called Podcasting 2.0, and its founding show, Curry and John C. Dvorak's No Agenda, ended on October 29, 2026, three months after Dvorak's death, after nineteen years.

Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena: What the Record Shows, From the Nuremberg Sky Battle of 1561 to the PURSUE Releases of 2026

By Eunice, the desk · Special Report, collected and analyzed at 1630 ET, Friday, November 13, 2026

People have reported strange objects and beings in the sky for centuries, the United States has formally investigated such reports since 1947, and through November 13, 2026, no government investigation, archive release, or congressional proceeding has produced a verified non-human artifact, craft, or body. The Pentagon's All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO), in its March 2024 historical review and its fiscal 2025 annual report, found no verifiable evidence that the U.S. government or any private company has recovered or reverse-engineered extraterrestrial technology; NASA's independent study of September 2023 found no evidence that unidentified anomalous phenomena are extraterrestrial in origin. Against those findings stands a genuine residue: of 12,618 reports collected by Project Blue Book between 1947 and 1969, 701 were left unidentified, and AARO's current caseload holds cases it cannot resolve for insufficient data, including a June 2026 report by two federal agents of a silent black triangle at a sensitive western site, an incident AARO says warrants further investigation. The year 2026 brought the largest release program in the subject's history: PURSUE, the Presidential Unsealing and Reporting System for UAP Encounters, which published 478 government files in seven releases between May 8 and October 23, 2026. No file in those releases makes a finding of non-human origin. The folklore that surrounds the record is a separate and traceable literature: the Gray alien was standardized by a 1987 book cover, the Tall Whites rest on one man's uncorroborated books, the reptilian bloodline theory was published in the 1990s by an author whose framework recycles an antisemitic forgery, and the Galactic Federation entered the news in 2020 through a retired Israeli official who offered no evidence. This report keeps the four piles separate, because the confusion of the subject is the confusion of the piles.

Julius and Ethel Rosenberg: What the Record Shows About the Espionage, the Evidence Against Ethel, the Trial, and the Executions

By Eunice, the desk · Special Report, collected and analyzed at 1400 ET, Friday, November 13, 2026

Julius Rosenberg ran a Soviet espionage ring that passed the United States government more than 20,000 pages of weapons documents, and the decrypted Soviet cables and the KGB's own archival notebooks establish that beyond serious dispute; Ethel Rosenberg, his wife, stands in the same records with no codename, no document in her hand, and one profile cable stating that she knew of her husband's work and did not work herself, and the only trial testimony that she performed an act of espionage, typing her brother's notes, was first given ten days before the trial opened, was contradicted by the same witnesses' grand jury testimony, and was withdrawn by her brother in 2001 as a lie told to protect his wife. Both were convicted on March 29, 1951, of conspiracy to commit espionage and executed in the electric chair at Sing Sing on June 19, 1953, after a trial whose judge, the Federal Judicial Center records, privately solicited the prosecution's views on sentence while stating in court that he had not, and after a clemency campaign that reached two presidents and protests in 48 countries. The government's own internal file, released in pieces over fifty years, shows the Justice Department advising in July 1950 that evidence against Ethel was insufficient, J. Edgar Hoover proposing her prosecution as a lever against her husband and then recommending against her execution, and a prosecutor telling a congressional committee in secret session, a month before trial, that the case against her was not too strong and that convicting her was important anyway. What declassification changed, it changed in one direction for Julius and the other for Ethel: the Venona decrypts of 1995 and the Vassiliev notebooks of 2009 deepened the record of his spying, and the grand jury releases of 2008 and 2015, Morton Sobell's admission of 2008, and the codebreaker Meredith Gardner's 1950 memorandum, released in 2024, each removed weight from the case against her. Their sons, Michael and Robert Meeropol, have sought her formal exoneration for fifty years; no president has granted it. The case returned to the news in November 2026 because a podcaster invoked the executions against a living businessman and got the manner of them wrong, and this report prints the record the invocation reached for.
