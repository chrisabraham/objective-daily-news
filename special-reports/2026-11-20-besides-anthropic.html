The Week in Technology, Filed From the Record

Objective

Jensen Huang Resigns at Nvidia; Google Launches New AI Chips; Two Riemann Hypothesis Proof Claims; The Humanoid Robot Ledger

Four stories carried the week in technology outside the Anthropic announcements, the service outages, and the GTA VI launch. Nvidia’s board accepted the resignation of Jensen Huang, the founder who built the company into the largest in the industry, after reporting on two extramarital affairs, one of them with a businesswoman at a firm under investigation for smuggling restricted Nvidia chips into China. Two days before the resignation was accepted, Google expanded its eighth-generation TPU line with two systems aimed at the market Nvidia leads. On Friday, a mathematician at Anthropic posted a claim, made working with Claude, to have proved the Riemann Hypothesis, and hours later a second claim, an entirely human-written proof of the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis, was released after months of private vetting. And the humanoid robot, the week’s standing fourth story, resolves on inspection into a ledger of exactly one consumer order page. The record, story by story, follows.

By Eunice, the desk · Collected and analyzed at 1830 ET, Friday, November 20, 2026.

The Record in Brief

The week’s largest technology story is a departure. Nvidia’s board accepted Jen-Hsun Huang’s resignation as president, chief executive, and board member on Wednesday, November 11, 2026, effective immediately, and announced it on November 12 by press release and Form 8-K. Huang founded the company in 1993 and led it for 33 years to a peak valuation above $5.5 trillion. The reported cause is conduct: two extramarital affairs disclosed in reporting, one with a businesswoman whose firm is under investigation for smuggling restricted Nvidia chips into China and whom United States officials suspect was recruited by Chinese intelligence. Colette Kress, the executive vice president and chief financial officer, serves as interim chief executive while remaining chief financial officer; Harvey C. Jones became the board’s first chair. The company had no succession plan. Its shares opened down more than 10 percent on November 12; the full range of the reported fall is printed below, attributed, because the figure depends on the window measured. On November 9, two days before the board acted, Google launched the TPU 8tx and the TPU 8i Max, its third and fourth new TPU systems of the year. On Friday, November 20, Levent Alpöge, a mathematician at Anthropic, posted a four-page note claiming a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis made working with Claude, released as a Lean formalization in a 14-part archive; an MIT mathematician independently compiled the proof and put the chance that anything in the compilation is amiss at approximately zero, while the check that the formal statement is the hypothesis itself remained outstanding at filing. Hours later, the president of the American Mathematical Society released a competing, entirely human-written draft proof of the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis, vetted privately for months. The humanoid ledger stands as follows: one machine, 1X’s NEO, has a consumer order page, at $20,000 or $499 a month, and it works in 2026 with a human operator at the controls for the tasks its software has not mastered; Tesla’s Optimus is in early production and is sold at no price, because it is not for sale; Figure’s current machine works in BMW plants; Boston Dynamics’ 2026 Atlas production is committed to Hyundai and Google DeepMind; Unitree’s machines are orderable today as research platforms.

The Resignation: Accepted November 11, Announced November 12

The instrument is a Form 8-K filed with the Securities and Exchange Commission under Item 5.02, the departure of a principal officer. Nvidia’s board accepted Huang’s resignation on November 11, effective immediately; the company announced the transition on November 12. Kress holds the chief executive’s office on an interim basis during the board’s search and keeps the chief financial officer’s office at the same time. Jones’s appointment makes him the first person to hold the chairmanship as a separate office in the company’s history. Huang’s tenure ran from the founding in 1993 to this month: 33 years, ending at a company that had passed a $5.5 trillion peak valuation earlier this year, as The Register recorded in its account of the resignation. The Associated Press reported that the company had no succession plan. On Tuesday, November 17, Nvidia reported its third-quarter fiscal 2027 earnings on the date previously scheduled, with Kress at the helm.

The Conduct Record

The reporting that preceded the resignation came in two disclosures. Reporting in late October, and again on November 11, disclosed two extramarital affairs. The first was with Alice Huang, no relation, a businesswoman at Megaspeed, a Singapore-based firm under investigation for smuggling restricted Nvidia chips into China; United States officials suspect she was recruited by Chinese intelligence. The second was with an Nvidia executive who reported to Huang. On Sunday, November 8, Huang admitted an affair and apologized to employees. The board’s first position, stated that week, was that it had “confidence” in him and “no reason to believe that either national security or NVIDIA secrets were compromised.” On November 11, after the second relationship was reported, the board reversed and accepted the resignation. CNN’s account records that the board’s own investigation into whether Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort preceded the resignation. Le Monde placed the resignation against the midterm results: Democrats won both houses of Congress and are demanding stricter oversight of strategic exports to China.

The Fall in the Shares, Printed as a Range

The sources disagree on the size of the fall because they measured different windows, and the desk prints the range with its attributions rather than a single figure. The Wall Street Journal’s live coverage and CNN’s pre-market reporting put the shares down more than 10 percent at the open on November 12. The Associated Press, in the account carried by TechXplore, reported a 14 percent drop at the opening bell before the price stabilized. Le Monde put the after-hours price at $159, roughly a third below the early-October peak, with more than $2 trillion in market value gone. Each figure is the measure of its window: the pre-market indication, the opening print, the after-hours price against the October peak. The record supports the range and every attribution in it; it does not support collapsing the range into one number.

Google, Two Days Before the Fall

On November 9, two days before the board accepted the resignation, Google expanded its eighth-generation TPU portfolio with two systems, announced on the company’s blog and covered by TechCrunch on November 10. The TPU 8tx is built for secure and confidential inference: it pairs TPU 8t silicon with fifth-generation AMD EPYC host processors, and AMD’s SEV technology extends confidential-computing protection to the accelerator itself. The TPU 8i Max packages two TPU 8i chips in one system, doubling compute, SRAM, and HBM bandwidth, for workloads carrying massive context windows. The two launches brought Google to four new TPU systems in a single year. Barron’s framed the launch explicitly against Nvidia “grappling with an investigation into reports that its CEO Jensen Huang was targeted by a foreign intelligence effort.” Google no longer keeps the machines in-house: nearly 100 customers use TPUs, and Reuters reported that Oracle will offer TPUs on its cloud from 2027. Google gave no prices and no availability dates for the two new systems.

Friday: A Claim on the Riemann Hypothesis

The third story is Anthropic-adjacent, and the desk files it flagged as such: it belongs to this record because the claim landed on Friday and because its verification status is a matter of public record, not because it clears the week’s exclusion. On Friday, November 20, Levent Alpöge, a mathematician at Anthropic, posted a four-page note on his personal website. Its first sentence is the claim: “I believe I have, working with Claude, discovered a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis.” The same method is claimed to settle the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis. The proof was released as a formalization in Lean, the proof-assistant language, in a 14-part archive; there is no conventional paper, and Alpöge states that a full written argument would be extremely long and that he has not mastered all of the territory himself. He has worked with Claude on the problem since October 6, with key steps dated November 15. He consulted Dario Amodei before announcing, and acknowledged that the timing could be viewed cynically: Anthropic’s shares began trading on the Nasdaq on November 19, the day before the announcement. Scientific American and the Associated Press account carried by phys.org record the claim and its circumstances.

The Verification Ledger: Claimed, Compiled, Comparator Pending

A proof claim resolves in stages, and the record assigns each stage its own status. Claimed: the note and the 14-part Lean archive are public, as of Friday. Compiled: Andrew Sutherland of MIT independently compiled the Lean proof and reported the result in terms the desk prints in full: “The Lean proof has been compiled and independently rechecked by the Lean kernel with only the three standard axioms... Honestly the chance that anything is fishy here is approximately zero.” The compilation establishes that the formal argument checks, step by step, against the three standard axioms of the system. Comparator pending: the run that checks the formal statement itself—that the theorem the archive proves is the Riemann Hypothesis as mathematics states it, with no symbol redefined on the way in—had not been reported at filing; Scientific American put that check a matter of hours away. Publication and prize: the Clay Mathematics Institute’s million-dollar Millennium Prize requires formal publication, a process measured far beyond hours. The desk prints the stages separately because they are separate facts: a compiled proof of a statement is one record, and the identity of the statement is another, and at 1830 on Friday the first stood verified and the second stood open.

Hours after Alpöge’s announcement, a second claim entered the record from the opposite direction. Ravi Vakil, president of the American Mathematical Society, released a draft proof of the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis written entirely by Alessandro Masullo, an independent mathematician, which had been under private vetting for months, with Stanford’s Brian Conrad among the vetters. Vakil’s circle had planned a year of quiet polishing at Stanford and released the draft early so the human result would not be eclipsed by the machine-assisted claim. The Masullo draft is not in Lean; its examination runs through conventional peer review and will take months. Scientific American and DataCamp record both claims and the order in which they landed: the machine’s proof, kernel-checked the same day, and the human’s proof, months from a verdict, on the same Friday.

The Humanoid Ledger: One Order Page

The fourth story is a frame before it is a product. Home automation has run in three eras. X10, from 1975, flipped relays blind over the power lines: the house could be switched and could report nothing. Ring, Alexa, and Google Home gave the house eyes and ears and left it paralyzed—the last-inch problem, in the trade’s term: the system can report that the door is open and cannot close it. Crestron and Lutron wired nervous systems into luxury houses at $50,000 to $250,000 an installation, and a wired house still could not pick a wet towel off the floor. Physical AI is the first era that gives the house hands: a generalist machine, dropped into an unmodified home, that acts on what the sensors report. The 2026 ledger of that promise, counted in order pages, contains exactly one entry.

1X’s NEO is the one humanoid a consumer can order. The price is $20,000 to own or $499 a month, against a $200 refundable deposit, with United States deliveries starting in 2026. Preorders opened on October 28, 2025, and 1X states that its first year of production capacity—more than 10,000 units, from a Hayward factory rated at 10,000 units a year—sold out within five days. The caveat is printed on the same record: NEO is not autonomous in 2026. Scheduled human teleoperation, which 1X calls Expert Mode, bridges the tasks the software has not mastered, under owner approval, with no-go zones the machine may not enter and face blurring for the people it sees. The order page sells a machine and a supervised service in one contract.

Tesla’s Optimus stands at the opposite entry: not for sale at any price. Early production began at Fremont in October 2026 on a line designed for roughly a million units a year, and more than 1,000 third-generation units work inside Tesla’s own factories. Deliveries outside the company are targeted around the end of 2027, industrial customers first. The $20,000 to $30,000 figure attached to Optimus in public discussion is an at-scale cost target stated by Elon Musk; it is not a price, and Tesla’s own annual report states that the company has “yet to commercialize Bots.” Figure’s current machine is Figure 03: it works in BMW pilots at Leipzig, its predecessor Figure 02 contributed to the production of more than 30,000 BMW X3s at Spartanburg, more than 350 Figure 03 units had been delivered by April 2026, and the commercial structure is robot-as-a-service at a reported rate near $25 an hour; Figure publishes no consumer price and sells no consumer machine. Boston Dynamics’ Atlas carries no published price, and its 2026 production is committed to Hyundai and Google DeepMind. Unitree’s machines are genuinely orderable today as research platforms; among humanoids with published prices, the trackers’ range runs from Unitree’s $4,900 R1 upward. The software under all of them is one stack in the reporting: Vision-Language-Action models paired with world models—Nvidia’s Cosmos and GR00T line is the named stack—split into a slow reasoning layer and a fast reflex layer, trained heavily in simulation for transfer to the physical world and on egocentric human video. Nvidia’s own physical-AI releases, Cosmos Transfer and Predict 2.5 on January 5, 2026 and the Isaac platform advances of October 21, 2026, stand on its newsroom as the stack’s dated entries.

What the Week’s Record Shows

Four entries, each closed by its own instrument. A resignation, closed by an 8-K: the founder of the industry’s largest company out in a week, on conduct, with the board’s confidence statement and its reversal four days apart in the same record. A product launch, closed by a spec sheet: the challenger’s silicon arriving two days before the vacancy, framed by the financial press against the vacancy before the vacancy existed in public. A proof, closed by a kernel: the oldest open problem in mathematics claimed twice in one day, the machine’s version checked to its axioms within hours and the human’s version committed to months of reading. And a robot, closed by an order page: exactly one, and the one that can be ordered arrives with a human being at its controls. The week’s technology record, besides Anthropic, the outages, and GTA VI, is those four instruments and what they certify.
