Objective Daily News is kept in editions, special reports, sections, and feeds. This page is the index of the whole: every standing part of the paper, what it is for, and the road to it.

The record

Part of the paperWhat it is
The Front Page The briefing sheet. The newest publication leads—headline, byline, and the piece’s own lede—with the day’s earlier editions and the newest special reports below it, arranged by the desk. Its rail carries the standing boxes, among them How to Read This Paper and From the Shelf—The Briefs, the lead edition’s filed clippings in compact form.
The Watches The full log of editions, filed by watch, each entry carrying its headline, its date, and its collection hour. Nothing expires.
The Editions Every edition the paper has filed, in order—the archive of the log itself, watch editions and weekend features together.
The Special Reports Every report filed outside the watches: unscheduled, badged articles that stand on their own when a single story is large enough. A special report carries the objective stamp like an edition’s report, is not numbered in print, and never takes an edition number or a watch name.

The four watches

The paper keeps four watches a day, the way a ship keeps them. Each watch collects the world’s reporting, the desk writes its analysis at the watch’s hour, and the edition is stamped with that hour—the collection and analysis time, not the posting time. All hours are Eastern.

WatchCollectsRemit
The Overnight Watch 0400 The night file: what the wires and the world’s morning papers carry while Washington sleeps.
The Midday Watch 1200 The news at midday: corrections to the morning’s counts, the markets’ reading, the official statements.
The Evening Watch 1800 The evening file: the day’s developments whole, and the first considered responses from every side.
The Midnight Watch 0000 The last entry of the day: what the day settled, what it left open, filed before the log turns.

A fifth watch, the Morning Watch (0600), was retired by the publisher’s ruling on the paper’s first day: two hours after the Overnight collects, there is nothing new to file. The Overnight is the morning paper. The weekend carries its own features inside the editions—Saturday culture and lifestyle, and the Sunday Review of Reviews.

Corrections

This paper is an archive and a log. Editions and reports are not revised after filing, and nothing published here expires or is silently withdrawn. What the desk later learns it got wrong is appended to the piece itself as a dated errata section, in the open, so the text as filed stands beside the correction of record. The policy is stated in full in The Fine Print.

The fine print, in brief. Objective, as this paper uses the word, means the desk is not driven by an agenda, is not an advocate, is not a politician, is not modeling behavior, and is not training up citizens: it is reporting the news as if it were objective. Subjective means the item comes from a source with its own position, as all sources have; it is printed because it is useful information, weighed by the reader. Objectivity is relative; everything is relative, even relativity is relative; absolutism is wrong. No source in these pages is more reputable than a press release issued directly from Langley.
