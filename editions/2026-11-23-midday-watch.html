The Midday in Brief: A Restoration on Wall Street, an Answer From Moscow, and a Missing Count Cut in Dymer

The midday file is a boardroom reversal that moved the whole market, an answer where the night file carried silence, and a search count corrected downward by nine. Nvidia’s board voted unanimously to reinstate Jensen Huang as president, chief executive and board director, effective immediately, in the company’s announcement filed below, eleven days after it accepted his resignation and a week after it said on its earnings call that it did not intend to bring him back. By the midday readings the Nasdaq Composite was up 5.9 percent, the S&P 500 up 4.1 percent and the Dow up 2.7 percent, nearly 1,300 points, with Nvidia itself up about a third, in the market account filed below. In Moscow, the Kremlin spokesman answered the Benghazi claim the Overnight Watch had carried unanswered: Russia has “taken note” of President Zelensky’s statement, and the defence ministry is handling it, in the updated claim account filed below. In Dymer, the toll stands at 11 and the fate of two people is still unknown, in the wire account filed below—against the eleven people rescuers were still searching for in the Sunday file. The desk takes the files in that order.

What midday settled is plain: that Mr. Huang is back, by unanimous vote, with the board stating it received input from senior levels of the United States government and is confident his return poses no national security risk; that the cash market held and enlarged the futures bounce the night file had left open; that Moscow has spoken, in two words and a referral; and that Dymer’s unknown are two, not eleven. What it left open is the rest: the board’s continuing investigation, the close at 1600, the defence ministry’s account of Benghazi if it issues one, Dymer’s final count, the North Atlantic Council’s results in Brussels, and the answers on the November 13 ground stop that fall due today in Washington.
