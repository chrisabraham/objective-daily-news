The Midnight in Brief: What Tuesday Settled, What It Left Open

Tuesday settled its close, its last Senate race, and the second full day of the Huang restoration—and in the hours after the bell it unsettled the first of those: the futures are down at the stamp, the week’s largest new stock closed under its offering price, and the President told a newspaper it is probably not too late for his government to take a stake in Nvidia. The futures stand lower in both late readings in the file—Nasdaq-100 futures down 0.8 percent in the overnight account filed below and down 1.17 percent at 2151 Eastern in the late account filed below—with Nvidia down 2 percent in late trade. After the bell Dell Technologies filed a record quarter, and Anthropic closed at $81.91, below its $83 offering price for the first time. In Washington, investigators into the November 13 ground stop have a culprit in a laboratory and a report in preparation, and the formal findings stand unreleased. In Ukraine, late strikes killed two in the Odesa region and injured two in Kyiv. In Riyadh, a drone struck the Public Investment Fund tower a second time in a week and wounded 11.

What Tuesday settled is the close; Dell’s quarter and its raised year; Anthropic’s first close under its offering price; Dymer’s twelve, with two more of the dead now named; and the strait’s Tuesday at three crossings in the visible counter. What it left open is Wednesday’s session and the inflation print before it; whether a government stake in Nvidia is an idea or a proposal; the ground stop’s unwritten report; the response Riyadh promised; Tehran’s unnamed tankers; and the defence ministry’s word on the Merkury, unissued into a fourth day.

The Ground Stop at Midnight: A Culprit Recreated in a Laboratory, and the Findings Still Unreleased

The ground stop’s file moved on Tuesday for the first time since the industry’s $30 billion ask on Monday, and it moved by leak rather than by release. Investigators have recreated the November 13 failure in a laboratory and believe they have found the culprit, according to a source familiar with the investigation in the investigation account above: an overlooked router setting, affecting how the new router at the remote Arkansas site interfaced with the decades-old routers being decommissioned, with software updates believed necessary to prevent a repeat. The investigators are preparing a report, and the source put them at 90 percent sure the disruption was not caused by nefarious actors. The formal findings—the release the administrator expected by last Friday and the Senate Commerce Democrats asked after for Monday—stand unreleased at the stamp, and the desk files the laboratory account as an account, not as the findings. Administrator Bedford and Secretary Duffy are set to appear at hearings next week.

The argument beneath the finding is also in the account, in both voices. The technicians’ union holds that the failed maintenance was coordinated under a system reduced to four liaisons nationwide and that a layer of agency review was eliminated to speed the work. The agency’s statement holds that it performs continuous risk assessments and oversees the contractor, which owns the infrastructure, through performance monitoring rather than field supervision. The account closes with a precedent: on November 19, 2009, a misconfigured router in Los Angeles blocked about 75 percent of the same network. Settled: a mechanism, in a laboratory, on a source’s word. Open: the report, the patches, and the hearings.

Riyadh at Midnight: The Tallest Tower Struck a Second Time in a Week, Eleven Wounded, and a Response Promised

Riyadh’s file is a repetition, and the repetition is the news. On Tuesday a drone struck the tallest tower in the Saudi capital—the 385-metre headquarters of the Public Investment Fund in the King Abdullah Financial District—for the second time in less than a week, wounding at least 11 people, Saudi Civil Defense reported, in the Riyadh account above. The injured were two Saudis, two Indians, three Nepalese and four Egyptians, in the casualty account filed below. The first strike, on November 19, damaged the same tower and injured no one. Attribution in the file is Saudi: the authorities attribute the strike to Yemen’s Houthi fighters, and in the casualty account the Houthis had issued no claim by 1600 GMT on Tuesday. Settled: the strike, the count of the wounded, and the promise of a response. Open: the response itself, and any word from the party named as the striker.
