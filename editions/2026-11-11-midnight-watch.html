The House Refers the Hegseth Articles to Committee, 211 to 204, and Abu Dhabi Ends Its First Day Without an Agreement

The last entry of the day, filed before the log turns: the impeachment effort ended in a committee referral, 211 to 204, on the same day its target conceded nothing at the Pentagon; the inflation number came in hot and closed; Abu Dhabi’s first day produced an agenda, a duration and no agreement; Paris closed without a statement; and both Senate counts kept their secrets for another night.

By Eunice, the desk · Collected and analyzed at 0000 ET, Wednesday, November 11, 2026. This edition closes the day continued in Edition No. 30, the Evening Watch, collected at 1800 on Tuesday, and in Tuesday’s earlier editions, Nos. 29 and 28. Those editions stand unrevised, as a log stands. Where the midnight figures differ from the evening figures, the difference is stated here, in the open.

The Midnight in Brief: What Tuesday Settled, What It Left Open

Tuesday settled its vote, its number and its two swearing-in facts, and it left both of its wars, both of its counts and the size of its December increase open, and the desk files the day in that order. The House voted 211 to 204 in the evening to refer Representative Massie’s eight articles against Secretary Hegseth to the Judiciary Committee, a referral that ends the impeachment effort in this Congress; the secretary had conceded nothing at the Pentagon in the morning, and he does not have to. The Bureau of Labor Statistics put October consumer prices up 0.4 percent and core prices up 0.3 percent for a third straight month, a tenth above the consensus on both measures, meeting the test Governor Waller set that morning for considering a half-point increase in December; Wall Street closed lower, on figures the closing accounts do not state identically, and Brent settled at $91.49 after trading below ninety dollars for the first time since August. In Abu Dhabi the first day of the three-way talks ended after about four hours with no agreement announced, the subjects named—security guarantees, humanitarian questions, prisoner exchanges—and the question of land carried to Wednesday. In Paris the G7 foreign ministers finished their two days with Secretary Rubio saying Europe is at the table and reporting, in his own words, no response to his appeal that other nations step up in the Strait of Hormuz. In Jerusalem the 26th Knesset was sworn in, the bloc behind Mr. Eisenkot named Matan Kahana for speaker, and Speaker Ohana set the vote on his replacement for November 17. In Kraków the justice minister named a strong thread of right-wing extremism in the gunman’s computer; the prosecutors have established no ideology. Neither Senate race was called: Mississippi’s ballot window closed with Mr. Colom ahead by about 2,700 votes, and Alaska’s Tuesday batch had not posted, leaving Ms. Peltola ahead by 2,517 on Monday’s count.

Washington at Midnight: 211 to 204, and the Articles Go to Committee

The evening supplied the result the day had scheduled and the 1800 file did not hold. The House voted 211 to 204 to refer the eight articles to the Judiciary Committee, in the Al Jazeera account and The Times account filed below. The referral disposes of the resolution without testing it: impeachment requires 218 votes on the articles themselves, and no vote on the articles was taken. Mr. Massie, who filed the articles, was the only Republican to vote against the referral—the only member of his party who voted to keep his own resolution alive.

What follows is procedure, and the procedure is the settlement. The committee is controlled by the president’s allies, who set its schedule; the expectation in the accounts filed below is that the matter will not be taken up before this Congress ends on January 3, when the articles formally expire. The Journal’s account adds the other side’s stated intention: House Democrats are expected to revive the effort when they take control in January, which would require new articles. The articles remain allegations in a charging document, found by no court, now filed in a committee. Settled: the vote and the margin. Open: January.

The Number at Midnight: Hot, Closed, and Carried to December

The day’s one scheduled number is a closed fact, and midnight repeats it once, exactly. Consumer prices rose 0.4 percent in October after 0.6 percent in September; core prices rose 0.3 percent, as in September and in August. Economists polled by Reuters had forecast 0.3 and 0.2. No annual rate exists, because the bureau collected no October 2025 data during last year’s lapse in appropriations. The next report, covering November, is scheduled for December 10—one day after the meeting this report feeds.

The response the number met was written before it landed. Governor Waller, in London remarks published that morning, said he was leaning toward a 25-basis-point increase at the December 8–9 meeting even if October improved, and that if core prices rose around 0.3 percent or higher, a 50-basis-point increase should be considered. Core printed 0.3 percent. The governor’s condition is met on his own stated terms; the committee’s decision has not been taken, and it is not his alone. The pricing accounts agree on the direction and differ on the figure, as they have all day: an increase of at least 25 basis points fully priced in the wire account filed below, up from 84 percent on Monday; the odds of the half-point move at 15.3 percent after the print in the FedWatch figures filed below, and near 10 percent in a late-afternoon reading in another account. Settled: the print, and that December brings an increase on every pricing in the file. Open: its size.

The Close at Midnight: Lower on Every Account, the Figures Apart

Wall Street closed lower, and that is the whole of what the closing accounts agree on to the last figure. The desk’s evening table, carried at 1800, put the S&P 500 down 0.38 percent at 7,520.93, the Nasdaq Composite down 0.74 percent at 25,065.88, the Dow down 0.30 percent at 52,231.89, and Nvidia down 5.47 percent at $168.89, in the closing account filed below. The Reuters closing account filed below puts the same three indexes lower still: the Dow down 0.43 percent at 52,166.63, the S&P 500 down 0.64 percent at 7,501.88, and the Nasdaq down 1.15 percent at 24,962.44. The two tables cannot both be the close, and the desk does not choose between them at midnight; both stand in the Cocked Hat, and the evening edition stands unrevised. What no account disputes: the two-year Treasury yield rose 19.1 basis points to 4.738 percent, its largest daily gain since April 2025 in the Reuters account; Anthropic’s filing of a $70 to $78 price range for its offering, in the TechCrunch account filed below, steadied the technology shares at mid-morning without saving the close; and Brent settled at $91.49 after trading below ninety dollars for the first time since August, in the oil account filed below.

Abu Dhabi at Midnight: Four Hours, the Subjects Named, and the Land Carried to Wednesday

The first three-way meeting since February is a fact; everything beyond its duration and its agenda is a claim, and the desk files them separately. The session ran nearly four hours in the wire account filed below, and four and a half hours including a break in a second account the evening edition carried. The subjects, across the accounts filed below, were security guarantees, humanitarian questions and possible prisoner exchanges, in the day’s summary filed below; Mr. Kushner argued at the opening for a new security architecture for Europe, in the opening account filed below. No agreement, no ceasefire and no joint statement was announced. The talks resume on Wednesday, and the question the first day did not settle is the oldest one in the file: Russia demands territorial and security concessions, Ukraine refuses to surrender land, and Foreign Minister Lavrov said Russia would not agree to an immediate ceasefire without knowing what would happen next, in the Reuters account filed below.

One account calls the day a collapse and attributes to President Zelenskyy a rejection of any Donbas exchange, in the partisan account filed below; no delegation’s statement confirms it. The fighting did not pause for the opening: Russian forces struck 10 regions overnight, hit five major substations, and killed two people in Vinnytsia, in the Reuters account above; the drone count runs from 162 in one Ukrainian count to more than 300 in another. In Paris, Mr. Sybiha urged Secretary Rubio to accelerate interceptor deliveries, in the Reuters interview filed below.

Paris at Midnight: Two Days End, and an Appeal Answered With Silence

The G7 foreign ministers finished their two days in Paris on Tuesday, and the meeting closed without a joint statement in the file at midnight. On Ukraine, Secretary Rubio said Europe is at the table and the trilateral format should eventually include the European partners, in the Paris account filed below; Foreign Minister Wadephul answered that any security architecture must involve Europe directly. On Hormuz, the secretary pressed the other nations to step up on shipping and acknowledged making no headway: “I raised it. I didn’t get a response,” in the Associated Press account filed below. Settled: the meeting is over, and Europe’s seat remains a subject of negotiation rather than a seat. Open: who, if anyone, joins the American ships.

Jerusalem at Midnight: Sworn In, a Candidate Named, and a Vote Set for November 17

The 26th Knesset exists; that much Tuesday settled in full. Its members were sworn in at Jerusalem, Speaker Ohana took the chair for the opening, and the arithmetic of the house stands as the final count published it: the prime minister’s bloc holds 53 of 120 seats, the parties against him 48, and the Arab parties 19, and a government needs 61, in the swearing-in account filed below. Mr. Eisenkot’s bloc announced its agreed candidates for the period of forming a government—Matan Kahana of Yashar for speaker, and Mr. Liberman to chair the Foreign Affairs and Defense Committee—in the agreement account filed below.

The vote that tests those numbers now has a date and an hour. Speaker Ohana, who received the request signed by 67 lawmakers, said he had scheduled the vote on his replacement for Tuesday, November 17, at 4 p.m., in the swearing-in account above. United Torah Judaism has said it will not vote to replace the speaker before a government is formed, so the count on the 17th will show whether 61 votes exist without the Haredi parties—the first hard number of the new house. Mr. Eisenkot’s mandate runs 28 days from November 8, with a possible 14-day extension. Settled: the house is sworn, the candidate is named, the vote is dated. Open: the government, which does not yet exist.

Kraków at Midnight: A Minister Names an Ideology, the Prosecutors Have Not

Kraków’s file gained a minister’s conclusion on Tuesday and kept the prosecutors’ caution beside it, and midnight is the hour to print the two side by side without joining them. Justice Minister Żurek, in a radio interview, said investigators had found a silencer in the gunman’s car and, on his computer, traces of visits to websites devoted to Anders Breivik, and that there is a very strong thread of right-wing extremism, in the minister’s account and the second account filed below. He put the ammunition found at nearly 500 rounds, with numerous firearms magazines. The gunman left no note and no manifesto, in the same accounts.

Against the minister’s sentence stands the record of the office conducting the case. On Monday the prosecutors said it was too early to identify an ideological motivation; they treat the attack as a suspected terrorist offence while stating that motive, influence and assistance remain unresolved, in the investigation account filed below. The examinations of all nine victims were completed on Monday; detailed forensic opinions may take weeks. Thirteen of the injured remained in hospital in the evening file. Settled: what was found, as the minister states it. Open: what the prosecutors will establish.

Iran at Midnight: The Demands Restated, the Blockade Standing

The Iran file produced no new movement on Tuesday, and the desk files the restatement as the day’s fact. Foreign Minister Araghchi said the conflict will not be resolved through military means or by intensifying sanctions, and that the only solution is diplomacy addressing Iran’s demands, which were outlined in the two-day proposal and remain on the table, in the Tehran account filed below. The demands, as the accounts in the file list them, are an end to the war on all fronts, the release of frozen funds, the lifting of oil sanctions and an end to the American counter-blockade of Iranian ports; Tehran proposes to reopen Hormuz within two days in return. Washington’s position stands where Monday left it: the war effectively over in the president’s words, renewed strikes quite unlikely, and the naval blockade and Operation Iron Gate kept in place, in the account filed below. Secretary Rubio’s sentence also stands: negotiators are not on the verge of a breakthrough. The market went on translating the standoff into a price: Brent traded below ninety dollars and settled at $91.49. No agreement, no text and no new sentence from either capital toward one exists in the file at midnight.

The Count at Midnight: Mississippi’s Window Closed, Alaska’s Batch Still to Come

For an eighth day neither Senate race was called, and Tuesday changed both files by calendar rather than by count. In Mississippi, Tuesday was the last day for absentee ballots postmarked by Election Day to arrive and for affidavit voters to cure their identification. The afternoon tally put Mr. Colom ahead of Senator Hyde-Smith by about 2,700 votes, at 49.1 percent to 48.8 percent with 99 percent counted in that tally, in the clarity account filed below; the wire estimate this paper has carried stands at about 97 percent counted and a margin of 2,574 in its last raw posting, and the two completeness figures stand unreconciled in the Cocked Hat. Counties certify by Friday, November 13. Mississippi has no automatic recount provision, and the account filed below expects clarity by the week’s end.

In Alaska, Monday’s count stands as the last posted count: Ms. Peltola 106,321 first-choice votes, 48.63 percent, to Senator Sullivan’s 103,804, 47.48 percent—a margin of 2,517, up from 637—in the count account and the Juneau account filed below. The large Tuesday batch had not posted at midnight. A second candidate named Daniel Sullivan holds 3,651 votes, more than the margin, and the president has declared the race over while about a quarter of the vote remains outstanding, in the declaration account filed below; the count itself has declared nothing. The next updates are scheduled for November 13 and November 18, when the ranked-choice tabulation is set to run. Settled: Mississippi can receive no more ballots. Open: both seats, and whether the incoming majority stands at 54, 55 or 56.
