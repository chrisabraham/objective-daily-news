The Midnight in Brief: What Friday Settled, What It Left Open

Friday settled its week on three continents, its oil settlement, and its storm’s direction—and left Sunday’s producers’ meeting, Monday’s full session, a Saturday parade, and three standing silences to the next day of the log. Wall Street’s finals stand in the closing account filed below: the Dow up 5.46 percent for the week, the S&P 500 up 5.6 percent, the Nasdaq up 6.68 percent. The storm’s dark stands at 360,000 on Friday evening, in the cleanup account filed below, down from more than 900,000 at Thursday’s peak. China’s closes stand at a record for its semiconductor index. Brent settled at $95.44. The strait counted eight crossings for Friday. Ukraine’s day count stands at six dead, with Kyiv’s own strikes filed in return.

What Friday left open is Sunday’s meeting, and whether its reported agreement in principle becomes a decision; Monday’s full sessions in New York and Shanghai, the first full test of both rallies; the last restorations, promised by midnight Saturday for most and into Sunday for a few; Ras Tanura, undescribed by its owner into a fourth day; every hull Tehran has counted this week, still unnamed; and the Russian defence ministry’s account of its corvette, unissued into a seventh day.

Wall Street at Midnight: The Week Stands at 5.46, 5.6 and 6.68 Percent, Counted Two Ways on the Day

The week is the settled figure, and the desk prints it first. For the week, the Dow Jones Industrial Average climbed 5.46 percent, its biggest weekly gain since October 2022; the S&P 500 gained 5.6 percent, its biggest since April 2025; and the Nasdaq Composite rose 6.68 percent, its largest weekly advance since April of this year—in the closing account above. A second account, in the markets account filed below, rounds the same week to 5.5, 5.6 and 6.7 percent and calls the Dow’s the best week since 2022 without the month. The buying on Friday ran where the month’s selling had run, into the hard-hit artificial-intelligence shares, in a session that closed at 1300 Eastern.

Friday’s own close stands in two forms, and the desk prints both rather than choosing. In the closing account above, the Dow rose 591.99 points, or 1.18 percent, to 50,774.94; the S&P 500 gained 114.71 points, or 1.60 percent, to 7,297.49; and the Nasdaq gained 543.70 points, or 2.31 percent, to 24,129.50. In the wire’s finals as printed by the Evening Watch, the Dow rose 597.69 points to 50,780.64 and the S&P 500 rose 115.56 points to 7,298.34, the Nasdaq agreeing in both at 24,129.50—a difference of 5.70 points on the Dow, standing in the Cocked Hat. The closing account’s caution stands beside the week: both the S&P 500 and the Nasdaq remain on track for their worst November since 2008. Settled: the week. Open: Monday’s full session, and November’s final count.

The Storm at Midnight: 360,000 Dark on Friday Evening, the Utilities’ Hours Named, and a Parade Rebuilt for Saturday

The restoration halved the count and then halved it again. The peak stood at more than 900,000 customers without power on Thursday; the Evening Watch printed about 480,000 in the afternoon; and by Friday evening the count had fallen to 360,000, Pennsylvania the most and Missouri, where snow caused problems, next, in the cleanup account above. The utilities’ own figures give the work its local shape. PECO, in the briefing account filed below, lost nearly 225,000 customers, restored about 85 percent of them by 1000 Friday, and stood at roughly 34,000 still out across southeastern Pennsylvania, Chester and Delaware counties having taken nearly 80 percent of the outages at the height. Its stated hours are plain: another major wave by Friday night, the vast majority back by midnight Saturday, and a limited number in heavily damaged areas possibly into Sunday. PPL, in its running updates filed below, counted more than 181,000 out in total and roughly 50,000 still out at 1745 Friday, the effort stated as multi-day.

The flood file stands open beside the power file: rain easing, rivers and tides still watched, water rescues recorded from New York to Virginia in the cleanup account, and no new fatality count filed in the accounts at the stamp. The holiday’s own appointment is kept, two days late. The 100th Macy’s parade steps off at 0830 Saturday over its 2.5-mile route, in the parade account filed below, with 95 percent of its planned elements, all of its giant character balloons, two late additions to the bill, and at least two bands unable to make the change, among them the parade’s own Great American Marching Band. Settled: the peak, the direction, and the parade’s form. Open: the last tens of thousands, on the utilities’ own hours.

China at Midnight: A Record for the Chip Index, the Two Large Indexes Swapped Between Accounts, and Tokyo and Seoul Closed Higher

China’s Friday is settled at the close, and its figures stand in two forms that agree on the day and differ on the assignment. In the detailed account, in the index account filed below, the Shanghai Composite rose 3.73 percent to 3,606.49 and the CSI 300 rose 4.17 percent to 4,063.06, each its biggest daily gain since October 2024; the STAR 50 rose 15.07 percent; and the CSI Semiconductor Industry index rose 17.59 percent to 6,685.06, the largest one-day rise since its base date in December 2016. In the wire’s closing form, in the rally account filed below, Shanghai ended 3.7 percent up and the CSI 300 4.2 percent higher—the same two numbers, rounded the other way between the two indexes—with the STAR 50 up 15 percent and a semiconductor sub-index up 19 percent, a record on that measure. The desk takes the detailed account’s assignment as the settled form and prints the swap in the Cocked Hat.

The cause stands in the cabinet’s own readout, posted at midday in Beijing: the capital market is vital to overall economic development; listed companies are to pay more dividends and step up repurchases; state-backed funds are to play a greater part in steadying the market; and Central Huijin’s quasi-stabilisation-fund function is to provide sufficient liquidity. Shanghai stood 0.28 percent up at the lunch break and 3.73 percent up at the close, on turnover half again Thursday’s. Hong Kong followed, the Hang Seng up 2.1 percent to 22,694 and its tech index up 6.5 percent. Tokyo and Seoul closed their own rallies: the Nikkei up 2.11 percent at 58,711 and the Topix up 1.73 percent at 3,815; the Kospi up 4.34 percent at 4,915, Samsung Electronics up 4.55 percent and SK Hynix up 5.41 percent, in the Hong Kong account. No account in the file gives a figure for Friday’s state purchases themselves. Settled: the closes, and the signal. Open: the size of the buying, and Monday in Shanghai.

Europe and Oil at Midnight: 643.85 and a Week Up 2.91 Percent; Brent at $95.44, and Sunday’s Meeting Reported Before It Is Held

Europe’s close stands where the Evening Watch printed it, confirmed in the data account filed below: the STOXX Europe 600 up 6.87 points, or 1.08 percent, on Friday to 643.85, and up 18.20 points, or 2.91 percent, for the week, its highest close since November 18. The wire’s closing account adds the file’s next European date in its headline—markets await the Spanish election—and the December central-bank meetings stand ahead, in Frankfurt on December 16 and 17 and in Tokyo, where the Bank of Japan’s meeting ends December 18 with Tokyo core inflation at 2.9 percent, the fastest since July 2025.

Oil gave back part of its week and kept the rest. Brent for January delivery fell 1.2 percent to settle at $95.44 a barrel on Friday, in the settlement account filed below, finishing the week up roughly 3 percent in one account and about 4 percent in another, its first weekly gain of November. Sunday’s business is reported before it is transacted: three sources say the producers have an agreement in principle to keep first-quarter 2027 output targets unchanged, while a second report has the alliance considering keeping current quotas through January only, with members at odds over sustainable capacity. The capacity review is the meeting’s second item: preliminary findings put Iraq at 4.9 million barrels a day, in the producers account filed below, with Iraq pressing for a higher ceiling. The group’s quotas expire December 31. Ras Tanura stands where it has stood since Wednesday: claimed hit, smoke in the pictures, and no word from its owner into a fourth day. Settled: the settlement, and Europe’s week. Open: Sunday, in full.

The Strait at Midnight: Eight Crossings Close Friday, 40 in Seven Days, and Another Hull Claimed North of Ghavam Island

Friday closed in the visible counter at eight crossings, four inbound and four outbound, one of them a tanker, the last the Hartati, an oil products tanker inbound at 2146 UTC, in the counter’s brief filed below. The seven-day count stands at 40 crossings—21 inbound, 19 outbound, 13 of them tankers—at 5.7 a day, about 7 percent of the pre-crisis rate. The status stands severely restricted, as it has stood since October 19. Friday’s eight is the week’s fullest day in the counter, against two on Thursday and six on Wednesday, and the desk prints the rise as a count, not a trend: one day is one day.

Against the eight counted crossings stands the Guards’ Friday claim, in the Guards account filed below: a tanker hit by an explosion after striking a mine on an unauthorised route, north of Ghavam Island, with a second tanker forced to turn back, in the claim account filed below. The vessel is unnamed, its flag, cargo and crew unstated, and no British maritime advisory, no owner’s statement and no Central Command response had confirmed the explosion at the account’s publication. The claim account lists the week whole: two tankers claimed on November 23, three on November 24, a carrier on November 25, two off Khasab on November 26, and Friday’s one—eight hulls claimed in five days, none publicly named or confirmed by the British office or any owner in the file. Settled: Friday’s eight, and the claim as a claim. Open: the hulls, all of them.

Ukraine at Midnight: Six Dead in the Day Count, and Kyiv’s Strikes Filed Against Tula, Novoshakhtinsk and a Buk-M3

The night’s file stands as the day printed it: 155 drones launched from 1800 Thursday, 110 shot down or suppressed by 0800, strikes recorded at 27 locations and debris at four, three injured in Kharkiv and six in Izium, in the drone account filed below. The day’s wider count, as the Evening Watch carried it from the regional authorities, stands at six dead and 25 injured across the country, with power cut in six regions. Russia’s defence ministry’s own account of its night’s work—industrial facilities, logistics centres and naval vessels, across five regions and the capital—stands as the evening printed it.

Kyiv’s return file is the day’s addition, and it is specific. The General Staff confirmed a strike on the Efremov Synthetic Rubber Plant in Tula Oblast, a producer of material for solid rocket fuel, carried out with a resistance movement inside Russia; a strike on the Novoshakhtinsk refinery in Rostov Oblast that destroyed 20 storage tanks and damaged 32 more and a section of pipeline; the destruction of a Buk-M3 air-defence system, command posts and a fuel depot in occupied territory in an operation named Hell-3; and, on the human file, the return of nine Ukrainian children from occupied territories and Russia through the mediation of the United States first lady, announced by President Zelensky on Friday—all in the war account filed below. The same account carries Kyiv’s stated difficulty: restrictions on a United States-provided satellite system are hampering its strike operations in occupied territory. Moscow’s account of the Tula and Novoshakhtinsk strikes had not been filed at the stamp. Settled: both capitals’ claims, as claims, in the same day’s file. Open: the verification of each, and the larger strike Kyiv says is still being prepared.

Libya at Midnight: The Pledge Stands, the Inquiry Stands, and the Ministry Is Silent Into a Seventh Day

Benghazi’s file did not move on Friday in the accounts at the stamp, and the desk prints the standing state plainly. The eastern army’s two dead officers stand named in its own words—Al-Taher Abdullah al-Sadiq and Lieutenant Ali Mohammed al-Said—its pledge of retaliation against Kyiv at a time and place of its choosing stands unexecuted in the file, Italy’s word for the operation stands as a mistake, and the eastern government’s case stands before the United Nations Security Council, with an international investigation asked for and not yet reported. Kyiv’s confirmation stands as given, against the Libyan officials’ account of four drones intercepted and two striking, of seven Kyiv says it deployed.

Against all of it, the one word the file has waited for since Sunday stands unissued for another day. The Kremlin’s spokesman said on Monday that any information would come from the Defence Ministry; the ministry has confirmed nothing, denied nothing, and published no account of the corvette Merkury or her crew. At the midnight stamp that silence enters its seventh calendar day since the strike. Settled: nothing new, and the desk says so. Open: the pledge, the inquiry, and the ministry’s word.
