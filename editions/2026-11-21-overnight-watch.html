Paris and Madrid at 0400: A Law’s Text, Still Unpromulgated, and a Bullring Filling at Noon

France’s file gained its text overnight and no new act. Firearms use remains permitted in the five situations the article names, every use must remain absolutely necessary and strictly proportionate, the presumption does not cover municipal police, and any contrary evidence may rebut it at any time, in the same account. The Interior Minister’s position stands in both tellings: protection for officers in dangerous conditions, in the Paris account filed below, and, in the text account, that the measure does not authorise unrestricted shooting and prosecutors can still open investigations. Against it stand the United Nations rapporteur’s letter, as the file has carried it, and an opinion of November 5 from the Organisation for Security and Co-operation in Europe’s democracy office recommending that the measure be reconsidered, in the text account. Bars Across—France Approves Rebuttable Presumption for Police Firearms Use

France has approved a bill creating a rebuttable presumption that National Police officers and gendarmes used firearms lawfully. The Senate adopted it on November 20 by 205 votes to 110, with 27 abstentions and six senators not voting; the National Assembly approved the text in July. It has not yet been promulgated or entered into force. The measure creates no new Penal Code defence; it amends Article L.435-1 of the Internal Security Code. Firearms use remains permitted in five situations, and every use must remain absolutely necessary and strictly proportionate. The presumption covers national police and gendarmes, not municipal police, and any contrary evidence may rebut it at any time. The Interior Minister said the measure does not authorise unrestricted shooting and prosecutors can still open investigations. An opinion issued on November 5 by the Organisation for Security and Co-operation in Europe’s Office for Democratic Institutions and Human Rights recommended reconsidering the measure. The Socialist group says it will refer the bill to the Constitutional Council before promulgation; promulgation should follow within 15 days unless the Council is seized.

The Traveler—How the Nov. 13 FAA Ground Stop Shapes 2026 Holiday Travel

A rare nationwide ground stop on November 13 has intensified scrutiny of the air traffic control network just as tens of millions of travellers head into the Thanksgiving and Christmas rush, with operations largely back to normal but political fallout and lingering disruption risks shaping how airlines and passengers plan the holidays. Federal statements attribute the disruption to problems within ageing network infrastructure interacting with ongoing modernisation work, not to a cyberattack. Early findings point to overloaded legacy switches and router hardware at several sites generating unusually high volumes of network traffic.
