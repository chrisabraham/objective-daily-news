The Evening in Brief: A Tow Begun, a Rescue Halted, and a Summit Ended Without a Text

The day whole is a burning ship handed back across a maritime border, a rescue in Ukraine stopped by the threat that made it necessary, and a climate summit that ran longer than any before it and produced no agreement. The Sparta II, struck by two drones on Saturday west of Gogland island, went out of Finnish waters under tow on Sunday afternoon behind the Russian rescue vessel Murman, after seven vessels of three countries fought her fire through the night; her containers were still warm on the thermal cameras, no flames were visible from outside, and one of her 16 crew was slightly injured, in the tow account filed below and in the departure account filed below. In Dymer, north of Kyiv, a Russian drone destroyed part of a five-storey apartment building on Sunday morning; four people were killed in the region, about ten may still be trapped, 14 were out of contact in the President’s count, and the rescuers themselves had to stop and take shelter in the afternoon, in the Kyiv region account filed below. In Antalya, COP31 ended on Sunday with no overarching agreement: the European Union and other countries refused a final text that did not mention fossil fuels, and the conference president conceded there was no consensus, in the collapse account filed below. Bahrain’s file closed the day with its two injured standing, its condemnations joined by the Arab League, the Gulf Cooperation Council and the Arab Parliament, and the claimed Patriot radars still without an American word addressed to them. The desk takes the files in that order.

What the evening settled is the Sparta II’s tow and her departure from the Finnish responsibility area; the Dymer toll at four dead in the region, with its trapped and missing still counted two ways; the Antalya outcome, which is a non-outcome, stated in the principals’ own words; the regional condemnation of the Bahrain attack, joined in full; and the strait’s Sunday at two crossings in the visible counter, with Saturday corrected a third time, now to 14. What it left open is the rest: the radars, the rubble’s full count, the Kharg convoy’s sailing, a factory’s missing in Banten, and Monday’s open, on a week that begins with a council in Brussels.

The Baltic at Evening: Seven Vessels, One Tow, and a Handover at the Border

The Baltic file is the day’s one file that closes, and it closes with cooperation across the line the war drew. A Russian rescue vessel on Sunday began towing the Sparta II out of Finnish waters after two days of firefighting, Finland’s coast guard said, in the tow account above. “Thermal cameras show that some containers are warm, but the vessel is stable enough for towing to begin,” the coast guard said, and responsibility for the rescue transfers to Russia once the ship leaves Finnish waters, in the same account. The evening update carries the tow to its end inside the Finnish area: the Murman began towing on Sunday afternoon, the ship listing slightly and stable enough to move, no flames visible from outside and no fuel or hazardous-substance leaks detected; the Finnish patrol vessel Uisko, Estonia’s Kindral Kurvits and tugs operated by Alfons Håkans supported the tow inside the responsibility area, the maritime and aviation restrictions were lifted, and the ship left both the economic zone and the environmental-damage area, ending the Border Guard’s mission, in the departure account.

The firefighting’s company is stated plainly in the file, and the desk prints it as it stands: of the seven vessels helping to fight the fire, two were Russian, two were Estonian and the rest were Finnish, in the tow account—the same three flags that stand on opposite sides of the war’s other files, working one burning ship in one sea. The crew’s account gains its last detail: the 16 were evacuated onto a nearby Russian ship, and one crew member was slightly injured, in both accounts—the first injury in the Sparta II’s file, where the week’s earlier accounts carried none. The ship herself stands as the file has carried her: 121 metres, Russian-flagged, owned by Oboronlogistika, bound for Kaliningrad, damaged in international waters west of Gogland island, in Finland’s Defence Forces’ placing, corroborating an account by Russian officials, in the tow account. Finland’s attribution stands where Finland placed it—the strike was likely carried out by Ukraine—and Kyiv has claimed nothing in any account in the file, as at every earlier stamp. Settled: the tow, the handover, the crew at 16 with one slight injury, and the fire reduced to warm containers. Open: the cargo, the tow’s destination and arrival, and responsibility in Kyiv’s word.

Ukraine at Evening: Dymer’s Rubble, a Rescue Stopped at 1535, and a Call for Patriots Renewed

Sunday’s new Ukrainian file opened in the morning in the Vyshhorod district and was still open at the stamp. A Russian drone struck a five-storey apartment building in Dymer, a small town north of Kyiv, destroying part of the building and collapsing flats across its floors; the bodies of three people were found in the building, about ten people may still be trapped under the rubble, and at least four people were killed in the region’s drone attacks on Sunday in all, in the Kyiv region account above. Nine people were evacuated and the fire was contained, in the President’s words in the same account: “Part of a five-story apartment building has collapsed following a Russian drone attack. I thank our first responders for acting quickly.” Four people, including a teenager, were injured, in the same account; the oblast administration’s running count names two dead recovered from the building itself and four casualties, with a teenager and a 22-year-old woman injured in the district, in the rubble account filed below. The wire count stands at at least three killed and four wounded in the building, with nearly a dozen feared buried and 11 believed trapped in the Emergency Service’s earlier figure, in the wire account filed below. The President’s figure for the missing is the largest in the file: contact lost with 14 people after the morning attack, some of whom could have been under the rubble, in the rubble account.

The afternoon added the file’s hardest fact. At 3:35 p.m. local time the rescuers had to stop their search for survivors because of an incoming aerial threat; shortly after, three explosions rang through Dymer, footage showed an unidentified aerial object on fire falling from the sky, and civilians and rescuers took shelter in nearby buildings, in the Kyiv region account. The President renewed his call, in the day’s file, for the United States to approve deliveries of Patriot interceptor missiles financed by allies. The standing files did not move, and the desk records them as standings: Thursday night’s toll at at least nine killed, the three Dnipro crossings in their two states—all closed in the BBC’s account, the Podilsko-Voskresenskyi restored in Kyiv’s—with the Darnytskyi assessment still unpublished, and Kryvyi Rih’s toll at two dead and 60 wounded. The North Atlantic Council meets in Brussels on Monday, with the Secretary General’s Warsaw guarantee of Saturday in the file before it and Dymer now in the same file. Settled: four dead in the region, nine evacuated, the fire contained, and the search interrupted. Open: the trapped, the missing, and the count when the rubble is cleared.

The West Bank at Evening: A Dozen Cars Burned, an Investigation Opened, and a Word Spoken in Jerusalem

The Middle East’s second file of the day opened before dawn in the villages around Ramallah and ran through Israel’s politics by evening. Settlers attacked Palestinian villages in the occupied West Bank early on Sunday, starting fires and scrawling graffiti; burned-out vehicles and walls spray-painted with slogans such as “Revenge” stood in villages near Ramallah, no one immediately claimed responsibility, and no injuries were reported, in the Ramallah account filed below. At least a dozen cars were torched and several homes vandalized across the night, in the villages account filed below, which names Kufr Malik, Deir as-Sudan and Deir Abu Masha’al; the military’s own list of the areas its soldiers and police were dispatched to adds At-Tuwani, in the Ramallah account. Israeli police said they had opened an investigation, and the military condemned the violence, in the same account.

The responses are the evening’s substance, and the desk prints them in the order they stand. Gadi Eisenkot, the opposition leader asked to form a government, called the attacks “Jewish terrorism”: “extremist groups that set fire to vehicles and homes are undermining regional stability, putting lives at risk and doing grave damage to Israel’s national security and foreign relations,” in the Ramallah account, and, in the villages account, “acts of Jewish terrorism, and we must call them by their name,” with a call for administrative detention and targeted operations against the assailants. He laid the violence to the policies of Itamar Ben-Gvir and Bezalel Smotrich, who he said had brought the area to the brink of anarchy, in both accounts. Ben-Gvir replied on X that the condemnation amounted to selling out to Arab parties, in the Ramallah account. Yousef Jabareen, chair of the Arab Joint List, said settler terrorism in the West Bank does not take place in a vacuum, in the villages account. The standing figure in the file is the United Nations’ count: 410 settler attacks related to the olive harvest since the beginning of September, in the same account. Mr. Eisenkot has four more weeks to attempt to form a government, in the villages account; the parliament was sworn in on November 10 with no new government, the caretaker prime minister remains in office, in the Ramallah account. Settled: the attacks, the burned cars, the investigation, and the word spoken. Open: the investigation’s findings, and the government’s formation.

Antalya at Evening: The Longest Summit Ends With No Pact, and the Two Words Are the Reason

The climate file closed on Sunday in Antalya, Turkey, and it closed without a text. International talks broke down in overtime over how to address fossil fuels; delegates from nearly 200 countries failed to reach an overall agreement covering electrification targets, adaptation finance, and how to respond to the planet’s approaching breach of a key warming threshold, in the wire account filed below. The talks ran two days longer than expected and became the longest climate conference in the history of the summits, in the same account; the collapse is the first major breakdown since Copenhagen in 2009, in the collapse account. At the final meeting called to approve the Antalya Pact, consensus was not there among the more than 190 countries present; the United States, which has withdrawn from the process, was not among them, in the collapse account. The European Union, Panama, the Marshall Islands and Norway were among those that rejected the deal, in the same account.

The reason is stated in the principals’ own words, and the desk prints them together. “We will not endorse a cover decision that does not even mention fossil fuels, which is the key driver of climate change,” Darragh O’Brien, Ireland’s climate minister speaking for the Union, said, in the finale account filed below; and, in the rift account filed below: “It is unconscionable that we would have a climate act that makes no reference to fossil fuel, no reference to transitioning away from fossil fuel either.” Mr. O’Brien left the room with Commissioner Wopke Hoekstra, in the wire account. The conference president, Murat Kurum, conceded the failure in the room: “We have done our utmost,” he said, but “it is clear that there are parties in this hall who oppose this text. We recognize that there is no consensus on this document,” in the collapse account. The drafting history stands in the same account: the first draft of the cover decision called for “the immediate transition away from fossil fuels in energy systems”; every subsequent draft stripped the words out, against a push by more than 100 countries to keep them in. What did get signed stands beside the failure: more than 90 countries signed a pledge to ramp up the electrification of their economies, in the collapse account, and only $142 million in additional funding was pledged at the summit, in the wire account. Chris Bowen, the Australian minister who ran the negotiations, said the transition away from fossil fuels is happening regardless of what the agreements say, in the wire account. The analysis in the evening file adds the context the delegates themselves gave: the failure follows weak outcomes at recent conferences, the absence of a major power backing a positive outcome was named by the Caribbean group’s lead as a big issue, and the determination of the large oil producers, led by Saudi Arabia, to block the fossil-fuel language arguably reflected the scale of the transition already achieved, in the aftermath account filed below; the energy shock from the Iran war made developing countries more reluctant to limit investment in new supplies, in the rift account. Settled: no pact, no consensus, and the reason in two words. Open: where the process goes next, which no account in the file answers.

The World at Evening: A Capture Confirmed in Mexico City, a Factory’s Missing in Banten, and Two Standings in France

Mexico’s file reached its confirmed form over the weekend and stands complete at the stamp. Mexican special forces captured Fausto Isidro Meza Flores, known as “El Chapo Isidro,” in Guasave, Sinaloa, on Friday night in an operation conducted with United States intelligence, and he was flown to Mexico City, in the capture account filed below. Four soldiers and seven gunmen were killed, in the same account; the Defense Ministry’s count of the operation’s dead is 11 in all, in the operation account filed below, which also records that the gunfire sent fans fleeing from a baseball game. Seven other alleged members of the organization were arrested, including its second-in-command, Germán “N,” alias “Serafín,” and its alleged financial operator, José “N,” in the operation account. The man himself is stated plainly: 44 years old, on the FBI’s ten most-wanted list, under a United States indictment since 2012 with a $5 million reward, wanted in Mexico on organized-crime and drug-trafficking charges, his group controlling smuggling routes for fentanyl and other narcotics along the border, in the capture account. Security Secretary Omar García Harfuch called the capture an important step toward the pacification of Sinaloa, in the same account. The accused stand accused; no account in the file reports a plea or a court date.

Indonesia’s file is a count that has not finished. A fire swept the PT Fonusa Agung Mulia candy factory in the Serang district of Banten province at 5 p.m. local time on Saturday; at least three workers are dead, in the Jakarta account filed below. About 300 workers were inside the compound and 65 were believed to be in the affected area; as of Sunday afternoon 24 had returned home and four were receiving medical treatment, while 37 remained unaccounted for, the company’s chief executive, Steven Wijaya, said at a joint news conference, in the same account. The second count in the file runs higher: 54 initially reported missing, 15 rescued including four injured, leaving 36 unaccounted for in the rescue agency’s arithmetic, while police and the owner placed the number of missing closer to 50, in the search account filed below, which also records seven body bags recovered and an identification process that could take two to three weeks, with the victims not identifiable by sight. Survivors told Indonesian media the flames originated from a cooling unit on the third floor; police said the cause remains under investigation, in the same account. The factory produces Milkita candies, exported to more than 40 countries, in the Jakarta account. Settled: the fire, its hour, and three dead. Open: the missing, counted at 36, 37 and near 50 in the file’s three arithmetics.

We recognize that there is no consensus on this document.” The first draft of the cover decision called for the immediate transition away from fossil fuels in energy systems; all mentions of fossil fuels were stripped out of every subsequent draft, despite a push by more than 100 countries to keep them in. More than 90 countries signed a pledge to ramp up the electrification of their economies.
