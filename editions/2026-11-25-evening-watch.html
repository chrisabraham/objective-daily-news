The Evening in Brief: A Rout Taken Back, a Survey Narrowed, and a Tariff File Answered

In brief: the last full session before Thanksgiving opened as a rout and closed as a split decision—the Dow up, the technology indexes down by fractions of their morning falls; the Federal Reserve’s Beige Book counted growth in eight districts where October counted nine, with costs rising faster than the prices firms can charge; the morning’s tariff filing acquired its full rate table and its first answers, from Beijing and from Brussels; and on the Gulf files the day ended as it began, with smoke confirmed by satellite and a refinery’s state still undescribed by its owner. The Dow Jones Industrial Average closed at 50,182.95, up 164.25 points, in the closing account filed below, after a morning low more than 1,100 points under Tuesday’s close. The Beige Book stands in the wire account filed below. The tariff answers stand in the tariff account and the Commission account, both filed below. The desk takes the files in that order.

What the evening settled is the close, in full; the Beige Book’s findings, with their November 16 collection date attached; and the tariff proposals’ two-tier rates. What it left open is the refinery at Ras Tanura, in any owner’s words; the two tankers Tehran counts at Hormuz and has not named; the ministry’s word on the Merkury, unissued into a fifth day; and the form of the retaliation President Putin called inevitable.

The Beige Book: Eight Districts Growing, Costs Ahead of Prices, and Wages Up a Notch

The afternoon’s document arrived at 1400, on the schedule the Midday Watch printed. The Federal Reserve’s Beige Book, in the survey account filed below, counts eight of the twelve districts reporting growth in the slight to moderate range and four reporting no change, with none declining. October’s book counted nine growing; the narrowing is one district, and the desk prints it as the book’s headline fact. Employment rose slightly. Wages and benefits rose moderately—an uptick from October’s modest increases, in the book’s own words. Prices rose moderately overall, and the book’s operative sentence is about the gap: input cost growth generally outpaced increases in selling prices, as competitive pressures and customer price sensitivity limited firms’ ability to pass costs through. Consumer spending was little changed, in the comparison account filed below.

The book carries its date limit in its own lines: the information was collected on or before November 16. It therefore describes the economy of the weeks after the midterms, before the week’s market break and before the tariff determinations it cannot mention. It is the last regional survey before the Federal Open Market Committee meets on December 8-9, with the policy rate at 3.75 to 4.00 percent. The book itself takes no position, and says so.

The Tariff File: A Two-Tier Rate Table, and the First Answers From Beijing and Brussels

The afternoon’s documents settle the disagreement the Midday Watch printed between its two rate accounts: both were printing one tier of a two-tier table. The Federal Register notices, as summarized in the legal account filed below, propose an initial rate for each economy and a higher rate if ongoing consultations and commitments are found unsatisfactory. China stands at up to 20 percent, rising to up to 40 percent on all goods in the unsatisfactory case—which the wire’s account ties to the trade truce expiring on January 10: the 20 percent complies with the truce, and 40 percent follows if it unravels. Vietnam stands at 20 percent with an agreement and 35 percent without; India at 17 percent with an agreement and 25 percent without; Singapore at 10 percent with an accord and up to 20 percent without; the European Union at 15 percent, rising to up to 20 percent; Bangladesh and Cambodia at up to 18 percent, rising to 20 and 25. The rates are proposals: they take effect, if they take effect, after the comment period and the January hearings—the hearing commences January 25, 2027, in the midday file.

The first considered responses arrived the same afternoon, and they divide by capital. Beijing’s was given before the formal release and stands unchanged after it: Foreign Ministry spokesperson Guo Jiakun called the overcapacity charge a false issue and said China opposes using it as an excuse for political manipulation; the wire’s account notes that Beijing stopped short of threatening retaliation. Brussels answered through a Commission spokesperson, in the TIME account filed below: the Commission will study the findings, and it strongly rejects the premise that Europe is a source of structural overcapacity—Europe, the spokesperson said, is among those most exposed to overcapacity, and the Commission regrets the lack of joint action. The operative sentence is the ceiling: under the August 2025 Joint Statement, the 15 percent all-inclusive ceiling applies to European goods, and the Commission expects any measure from this investigation to respect it in full. No answer from Tokyo, Seoul, Taipei, New Delhi or Hanoi is in the evening file.

Ras Tanura and the Red Sea: A Port Confirmed Hit at Jizan, Smoke at the Refinery, and No Word From the Owner

The Saudi file widened on Wednesday along the coast. The assessment account filed below, in the Institute’s update, records that Saudi Arabia struck Hudaydah Port on November 24, that the Houthis warned of attacks on all Saudi Red Sea ports in answer, and that they then claimed attacks on the Jeddah and Jizan ports on November 25. One of those claims carries a Saudi confirmation: Joint Forces Command Spokesperson Major General Turki al Maliki confirmed that the Houthis struck Jizan Port with an unspecified projectile and caused minor damage. On the Gulf coast, the same update dates the Ras Tanura attack to November 24 and the spokesman’s claim to November 25, and reads the satellite imagery—the large plumes of smoke the wire described at midday, over about 600 square kilometres—as confirming that drones struck the refinery. A second account, in the output account filed below, carries a Bloomberg source’s form of the same fact: the site was hit and the fire contained. The coalition’s own statement stands as it stood: four drones intercepted over Ras Tanura, fragments fallen, no casualties in the initial assessment, and no statement that the refinery was hit. Aramco and the Saudi government communications office have still not answered the wire’s requests for comment, and no statement from the owner describes the plant’s state. Settled: Jizan, in Riyadh’s own confirmation, and the smoke, in two instruments. Open: the refinery, in the owner’s words, for a second day.

Reuters, carried by KRRO — Iran says ships hit mines in Hormuz, warns vessels to coordinate passage

Two oil tankers struck naval mines, exploded and caught fire in the Strait of Hormuz on Wednesday, Iran’s Revolutionary Guards Navy said in a statement carried by state media. The statement said the vessels had been attempting to transit an unauthorised route through the waterway. Army spokesperson Mohammad Akraminia warned that vessels passing through without coordinating with Iranian forces had faced greater risks and struck mines in recent weeks. Tehran has told Washington it will not restore normal shipping through the Strait of Hormuz until its demands are met, including the lifting of a United States naval blockade of Iranian ports and an end to the war on all fronts. Under a proposal conveyed through Qatari mediators, Tehran offered to reopen the waterway within 48 hours of Washington meeting its conditions.

Euronews, carried by Ekkow — Russian strikes kill seven in Ukraine as Kyiv hits three refineries

Russia launched scores of drones at energy and civilian targets across Ukraine overnight, killing at least seven people in six regions, authorities said. The attack involved 112 drones, of which 88 were shot down or neutralised, Ukraine’s air force said. One person was killed in Bila Tserkva in Kyiv region; one person was killed and seven injured in Kharkiv region after a strike hit a minibus on a highway; two people were killed in Odesa region on Tuesday evening, a 41-year-old and a 17-year-old; and one person was killed and three wounded in Zhytomyr region. Russian forces struck a Naftogaz gas production facility in the Poltava region, which caught fire and suspended operations, and the energy company DTEK reported a large-scale strike on one of its thermal power plants, causing serious damage and forcing it to stop generating electricity. Ukrainian drones simultaneously struck three Russian oil refineries: the Kuibyshev and Syzran plants in Samara region, both Rosneft’s, with fires at both, and the Novoshakhtinsk plant in Rostov region, which suspended operations after a combined strike of more than 50 drones and missiles lasting six hours.

WSNext — Drone Strikes Knock Out Over Half of Russian Oil Refining Capacity

Intensified Ukrainian drone strikes have disabled over 55 percent of Russia’s oil refining capacity, according to military and industry assessments carried in the account, and analysts estimate combined crude processing volumes have fallen by 45 percent since late October. In the November 25 assault, Ukrainian forces launched drone strikes on three major Russian oil refineries—Kuibyshevsky and Syzran in Samara Oblast and a third facility in Rostov Oblast—which collectively process approximately 15.3 million tons of crude oil annually. Local authorities reported 145 drones intercepted, one civilian fatality, and widespread power outages in Samara Oblast. Russian President Vladimir Putin warned on November 24, 2026, that retaliatory action against Ukrainian strikes on refining facilities was ‘inevitable,’ framing the attacks as a violation of sovereignty despite Kyiv’s invocation of self-defense under Article 51. Ukrainian Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha dismissed Moscow’s objections: ‘If Putin doesn’t like Ukraine’s strikes on his oil refineries, he can order an end to the war.’ President Putin also acknowledged a ‘challenging’ fuel supply situation in Russia.

QNC News — Russian Strike on Zaporizhzhia Homes Leaves Two Dead and More Than 40 Wounded, Including Four Children

A Russian strike on residential areas of the Ukrainian city of Zaporizhzhia in the early hours of Tuesday killed two civilians and wounded more than 40 others, including four children, regional officials said. Explosions were heard at about 4 in the morning in the Voznesenivskyi district as the raid hit homes while residents slept. The strike destroyed a private house and partially destroyed a five-storey apartment building. Mykhailo Semikin, the head of the Zaporizhzhia Regional Military Administration, said two people were killed. The number of wounded rose through the morning from an initial figure of 11 to 22 and then 32, before the regional administration put the total at 40 by mid-morning; later reporting put the number of wounded as high as 44. Four children were among the wounded, the youngest a baby less than a year old, and the others aged four, six and 16.
