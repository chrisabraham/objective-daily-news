The Night in Brief

Nothing the week left open was closed in the night, and one presidency was. The open file at midnight — the larger Iran decision, the two Senate races, the leadership questions — stands open at 0400 in the same figures. What the night added is a second round of reports from Qeshm Island, a Qatari statement that the mediation between Washington and Tehran has not failed, and the first full accounts of Friday’s completed act in Asunción, where Paraguay’s Senate removed President Santiago Peña over the collapse of Ueno Bank, placed him under house arrest, and swore in Vice President Pedro Alliana to finish the term to August 2028. Beijing’s confirmation that talks are under way on a three-way meeting of Presidents Xi, Trump, and Putin at Shenzhen on November 18 and 19 stands beside them. The president, in Florida, added a sentence of his own: Russia and Ukraine, he said, want to settle, for the first time. The desk files the night in that order.

Qeshm Again: Two Projectiles Reported, a Drone Claimed, No Confirmation of the Impacts

Iranian media reported a second night of explosions on Qeshm Island, and no government has confirmed what the explosions were. Reuters’ Cairo file, filed below, reports that Tasnim said early Saturday that initial reports indicated two missiles had struck Qeshm Island, with no official information on possible damage, and that IRNA had earlier reported local witnesses saying two projectiles struck the Suza area after the sound of an aircraft was heard. A second account adds that state television reported two explosions in the Souza area late Friday with no casualties, that Fars reported explosions in Qeshm and in Bandar Khamir, and that none of the accounts has been independently confirmed and there was no immediate American confirmation of a new strike. A third file records the American position in one sentence: the United States military has not commented on the report. The reports arrive twelve hours after the president confirmed Friday evening’s limited strike on the same island, and the fullest account states plainly that the precise relationship between the reported impacts and the confirmed operation has not been independently established. That sentence stands at 0400.

Tehran’s second claim of the night is a drone, and it is the second in two days. The Iranian Army announced that its integrated air defense network destroyed an American MQ-9 Reaper drone over Qeshm, in the army’s account as carried in Tehran’s file, timed 0052 GMT on Saturday. A second file pairs it with Friday’s claim, in which the Revolutionary Guards said their air defenses destroyed an American MQ-1 drone over the same island. The account of the president’s remarks carries the only American sentence in the drone file: Central Command said the situation was “relatively calm” and declined further comment. The United States has not confirmed either claim, and no wreckage or imagery allowing independent verification appears in any file the desk took in.

The Mediation: Qatar Says the Failure Reports Are False

The diplomatic track produced the night’s clearest official sentence, and it came from the mediator. Qatar’s International Media Office on Saturday denied circulating reports that the United States and Iran had failed to reach an agreement to end their war, describing the allegations as false and misleading, in the Reuters file above and in the fuller statement carried in the Doha file. The statement’s own words are that the prime minister’s remarks “have been deliberately distorted by those who benefit from continued war and instability in our region,” and that the prime minister “stated today that both sides are continuing to work towards a deal.” Qatari mediators remain in close coordination with Washington and Tehran, the statement adds. The prime minister said at Oxford on Friday that mediation remains active and the crisis “cannot be left unresolved,” per the war file, which also carries the mediators’ reported two-day bridge proposal — the same draft the desk filed on Thursday. No agreement was announced in the night, by any side. What changed is the record about the talks themselves: reports that they had failed are denied, by name, by the office running them.

Asunción: A President Removed, a Successor Sworn In

Paraguay completed in one day the removal the night’s other capitals only discussed. The Senate on Friday removed President Santiago Peña for misconduct in office and poor performance of his duties, and authorities placed him under house arrest an hour later, in the Associated Press account filed below. Vice President Pedro Alliana took the oath to finish a term closing in August 2028. The vote is carried as 44 to nothing among the senators in attendance in the wire’s account, and as 44 to 1 in the account carrying CNN’s reporting and in a second full account; the desk prints the disagreement in the Cocked Hat. The lower house had voted 78 to nothing to impeach on Thursday. Mr. Peña had offered his resignation on Thursday and Congress refused it; the impeachment, unlike a resignation, stripped his immunity and the lifetime Senate seat a former president would otherwise hold. He is the second Paraguayan president forced out by impeachment since 1989. He denies wrongdoing.

The cause on the record is a bank, and its figures are the file’s fixed points. Regulators seized the digital lender ueno on November 3 after its solvency fell under half the required level. The bank served roughly 3 million customers — about 60 percent of the country’s adults — and held as much as $850 million in public money, $380 million of it from the state pension and health fund. Mr. Peña held shares in ueno’s holding company until last year, and the central bank chief he installed, Carlos Carvallo, had run the lender until 2023; the arrest order covered Mr. Carvallo as well. Prosecutors cited suspected breach of trust, with charges undetermined and possibly extending to influence peddling; the attorney general has opened three criminal cases. Mr. Peña must report to prosecutors on Saturday morning. The deposit guarantee covers about $38,000 per depositor, 97 percent of holders are estimated fully within it, and banking law requires payment within 15 days — the figures by which the new government will first be measured, beginning today.

Shenzhen and Ukraine: A Three-Way Meeting Under Discussion, a Settlement Claimed Near

Beijing has confirmed, in its own words, that a meeting of the three heads of state is under discussion. Foreign Ministry spokesperson Mao Ning told a briefing in Beijing this week that China “is in communication with relevant parties regarding a meeting between the three heads of state,” in the fullest account of the confirmation filed below — Beijing’s first public confirmation, with no final decision announced. The expected venue is the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit in Shenzhen on November 18 and 19. The idea is reported to have originated with Moscow: President Putin proposed a trilateral format to President Trump in a phone call on October 9, aimed at ending the war in Ukraine; Kremlin spokesperson Dmitry Peskov said on November 3 that work was under way to organise such a meeting, per the Ukraine war briefing. The three leaders have never held a joint meeting. The reported priorities do not describe the same meeting: Mr. Putin’s is Ukraine, without Kyiv at the table; the president’s, stated publicly, is nuclear arms control; and the analysts in both accounts expect the gathering to amount, in substance, to a photograph. Whether Mr. Putin attends has not been announced. The confirmation itself — discussions, in the open, eleven days before the summit — is the fact.

The president added his own bearing on Ukraine in the night, from Florida. Asked whether it feels different now, he said: “It does. They want to settle. Russia and Ukraine for the first time, I think, want to settle,” and “I think it’s going to be a very big settlement. I think it’s going to involve all of Europe,” in the transcript file filed below, which also records meetings by phone on Saturday and in person on Monday and Tuesday — the Abu Dhabi talks. The Reuters account of the same remarks records the limits beside the claim: substantial differences remain over territory and security guarantees. Europe’s answer arrived on Friday and stands: European leaders dismissed the drive for a new security agreement negotiated over Ukraine, and the European Union’s foreign policy chief said any agreement, to be implemented, will require the European Union to be part of it, per the Irish Times account in the Europe file. At 0400 the Ukraine file holds three positions, each in its owner’s words: Washington says both sides want to settle; Europe says no settlement architecture without Europe; and Moscow’s proposed room for the question is Shenzhen, with Kyiv not in it.

The Count at 0400: No Movement, One New Lawsuit

Neither Senate race moved in the night, and the wire’s figures at 0400 are Friday’s figures. In Mississippi the Associated Press file stands at 467,930 votes for the Democrat, Scott Colom, and 465,356 for the incumbent Republican, Cindy Hyde-Smith — a lead of 2,574, with all 1,736 precincts reporting, an estimated 96.92 percent of the vote in, and the race too early to call, per the wire’s Mississippi page. The ballots that can still move it are the ones the procedure names: mail ballots postmarked by Election Day arriving by Tuesday, November 10, affidavit ballots resolved by the same date, and county certification by November 13, in a state with no recount provision. In Alaska the file stands where it has stood since Wednesday: the Democrat, Mary Peltola, at 102,962 and the incumbent Republican, Dan Sullivan, at 102,325 — a lead of 637 at 73.95 percent of precincts reporting, too early to call, per the wire’s Alaska page. The next count is expected Tuesday and the ranked-choice tabulation is set for November 18.

The night’s one new count item is a filing, not a figure. Ms. Peltola’s campaign sued the Alaska Division of Elections on Friday, seeking the immediate release of records identifying absentee ballots rejected in the November 3 election and the reasons for each rejection, in the account of the suit filed below. The campaign says it requested the records on October 27 and again on November 4 under the Public Records Act, received no response, and was later denied immediate access; the state says the records are confidential preliminary data. It is the first major legal filing in the Alaska contest. Saturday is a counting day in neither state. The count speaks next on Tuesday, in both.
