Reuters — more than 3,700 Saturday cancellations, two airline ground stops of their own, and funds sought for an emergency

Major airlines on Saturday cancelled more than 3,700 flights in the United States and delayed 2,000 more as the country recovered from the longest nationwide shutdown of flights since the September 11, 2001 attacks. The Administration issued a nationwide ground stop on Friday that halted nearly all departing flights for almost five hours and forced airlines to cancel nearly 10,000 flights and delay more than 7,000 others. Tens of thousands of people were still stranded at airports on Saturday. United announced a ground stop for its own flights at Chicago O’Hare and Delta at New York LaGuardia, to handle congestion and gate issues, and many major airports said they could not handle more diversions. American said it was prioritizing international flights and smaller cities to position aircraft and crews. The stop followed an outage that appeared to affect a network handling critical radio frequencies used by controllers to communicate with pilots, according to two airline officials; the contractor overseeing the network, L3Harris, did not respond to a request for comment. Secretary Duffy said the trouble began at 3:36 p.m. Eastern, that switches and routers were overloaded with data and gave inconsistencies, that personnel never completely lost radar or communications, and that he seeks $50 billion to upgrade the system, up from $30 billion sought earlier: “This is an emergency.” Secretary Mullin said there was zero evidence so far of a cyberattack or a rogue artificial-intelligence system, neither ruled out, and that the National Security Agency was working with the Department of Homeland Security on the investigation. Because the Administration was responsible for the delays, airlines are not required to provide compensation to stranded passengers. The only other nationwide ground stop since September 11 came in January 2023, for about 90 minutes. Shares of American, United and Delta fell between 1 and 2 percent in after-hours trading on Friday, and JetBlue about 3 percent.

Fox Weather — more than 9,100 cancelled and 24,500 delayed on Friday, and the second-longest stop in the record

Thousands of flights were cancelled early Saturday in the wake of Friday’s nationwide ground stop, which lasted more than four hours—the longest shutdown of American airspace since September 11 and the second-longest nationwide ground stop in the country’s history, surpassed only by the roughly two-day shutdown after the 2001 attacks. More than 3,000 flights were cancelled early Saturday, according to FlightAware; all told, more than 9,100 flights were cancelled and more than 24,500 delayed on Friday. The Administration said it issued the stop, just before 4 p.m. with 3,500 flights in the air, out of an abundance of caution because of intermittent telecommunications outages. Secretary Duffy posted on Saturday that operations are slowly returning to normal, that planes are pushing back, taxiing and taking off, and that it is safe to fly, and announced a press briefing at 10:30 a.m. Eastern at the Department’s headquarters. Secretary Mullin said there was no indication of a nefarious attack: “This isn’t another 9/11.” Travellers at New York’s LaGuardia told correspondents they had slept on the terminal floor on Friday night, and Amtrak reported higher-than-normal call volumes as stranded passengers turned to the trains. The last nationwide ground stop before Friday was ordered on January 11, 2023, over the system that alerts pilots to flight hazards, and lasted about an hour and a half.

The Hindu BusinessLine — the ask boosted to $50 billion, and the $30.1 billion letter priced in parts

The Administration boosted its request for air traffic control funding to $50 billion late on Friday after a communications issue forced a nationwide ground stop that halted thousands of flights for hours. Secretary Duffy and the White House had earlier on Friday asked for $30.1 billion to upgrade the country’s aging airports and air traffic control system.
