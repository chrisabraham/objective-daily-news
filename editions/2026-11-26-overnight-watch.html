The Night in Brief: A Salvo Absorbed, a Rate Raised, a Refinery in Smoke

The night’s largest file arrived on Wednesday and was priced by Thursday morning. The Trade Representative proposed new tariffs on 14 trading partners after an eight-month investigation into excess industrial capacity, in the proposal account filed below, and Asia’s answer, in the wire account filed below, was a rally: the duties are capped by the trade deals already struck, they do not stack on the July levies, and implementation waits on a hearing the Morning Bid account dates to January 25. In Seoul the Bank of Korea raised its rate to 3.25 percent. In the Gulf, satellite images show smoke over Ras Tanura and no Saudi account confirms a hit. In the strait Tehran counted a third vessel.

What the night settled is the tariff proposal’s shape—14 partners, caps named, Mexico and Norway not proposed, China highest; the Bank of Korea’s decision and its 5-to-2 vote; Wednesday’s visible crossings in the strait, six in the counter’s corrected count; and the waivers standing over Thanksgiving Day travel. What it left open is the tariff rates as the Federal Register will print them; the refinery’s true state; Jeddah’s damage, claimed by one side and unconfirmed by the other; the third vessel’s name; and the Russian defence ministry’s word on its corvette.

The Tariff File: Fourteen Partners, Caps Named, and a Hearing in January

The proposal is now printed in full enough to be argued with. The duties follow an eight-month Section 301 investigation of 16 trading partners; 14 are proposed—China, Bangladesh, Cambodia, the European Union, India, Indonesia, Japan, South Korea, Malaysia, Singapore, Switzerland, Taiwan, Thailand and Vietnam—and Mexico and Norway are not, their reviews still open. China faces the highest rates: a proposed 20 percent duty on top of roughly 25 percent from the President’s first term, held under the trade truce cap expiring January 10. Caps for partners with deals stand at 15 percent for the European Union, Japan, South Korea and Taiwan, and 19 percent for Malaysia. The duties do not stack on the July levies, and about $30 billion of Chinese imports are excluded. A comment period and a hearing stand before any effect; the Morning Bid account dates the hearing to January 25 and the effect to February or March.

The answers arrived in the night from three directions. In Hanoi, the Pacific trade bloc’s ministers opened two days of talks with the Southeast Asian bloc and the European Union’s trade chief, in the Hanoi account filed below, with Australia’s trade minister naming fair and open trade and supply-chain resilience as the dialogue’s business. In Seoul, the Industry Ministry said it would hold further talks to safeguard its interests, in the Korean account filed below, which carries both a capped rate of 15 percent including existing duties and a higher proposed rate of 25 percent depending on follow-up consultations—two figures for one country, printed side by side in the Cocked Hat. In Beijing, the foreign ministry rejected the accusation of unfair industrial practices and stopped short of threatening retaliation, in the China account filed below, which also records the rare-earth suspension extended this week and the leaders’ fourth summit set for Miami next month. Settled: the proposal, its caps, and its calendar. Open: the Register’s rates, and Beijing’s answer beyond words.

Seoul at Dawn: A Third Hike, Five Votes to Two, and Growth Raised With the Rate

The Bank of Korea’s decision is the night’s cleanest file. The Monetary Policy Board raised the base rate 25 basis points, from 3.00 to 3.25 percent, at the year’s final meeting, in the bank’s own release filed below; five members supported the increase and two, Hwang Kunil and Lee Soohyung, voted to hold. It is the third increase of the tightening cycle, after July and August, with a pause in October, in the wire account filed below. The bank lifted its growth forecast for 2026 to 3.9 percent from 3.3 percent, and for 2027 to 4.1 percent from 2.9 percent, on a chip-driven export boom that keeps inflation and housing prices elevated. Settled: the rate, the vote, and the forecasts. Open: the timing of any further increase, which the board reserves to its reading of inflation and financial stability.

The Gulf at Dawn: Smoke in the Pictures, Interceptions in the Statements, and Ports on the Red Sea

The Ras Tanura file changed in kind overnight. The Houthi claim of Wednesday—an accurate and direct strike, fires at the facility—now stands beside evidence of a different sort: satellite images from NASA and Planet Labs showed thick black smoke over the installation, and a source with knowledge of the matter confirmed an attack on the refinery, in the refinery account filed below, which gives the complex at about 600 square kilometres and the refinery’s capacity at 550,000 barrels a day. The Saudi government and Aramco did not respond to requests for comment in that account, and no Saudi statement in the file confirms a hit on the refinery. The coalition’s confirmed Wednesday stands narrower, in the oil account filed below and in the ports account filed below: four drones intercepted and destroyed over Ras Tanura, fragments falling, no injuries in the first assessment.

The Red Sea half runs in sequence. On Wednesday the coalition warned civilians to leave Hodeidah and then said it had targeted operational capabilities at the port, with further targets in Saada and Amran. Within hours the Houthi spokesman warned that Jeddah and every Saudi Red Sea port would become targets, then announced operations against Jeddah and Jazan, saying both resulted in damage. Late Wednesday the coalition said it had intercepted ballistic missiles fired toward Riyadh. Oil closed the argument: Brent settled at $94.61 on Wednesday, up 3.5 percent, and rose again in early Asian trade, in the Thursday oil account filed below. Settled: the Jazan landing, the interceptions, and the Hodeidah strikes, each in the words of the side reporting it. Open: the refinery’s damage, Jeddah’s, and Hodeidah’s.

Settled: Wednesday’s six, in the one instrument, and Wednesday’s three, in Tehran’s statements as statements. Open, as all week: the tankers themselves.

Reuters—US proposes new tariffs on 14 trading partners to fight excess factory capacity

The administration on Wednesday proposed new tariffs on 14 major trading partners to combat structural excess industrial capacity, with the highest rates to hit China. The Trade Representative announced the duties after an eight-month Section 301 probe into 16 trading partners including Japan, the European Union and South Korea; no tariffs were proposed for Mexico and Norway, whose reviews continue. The proposed partners are China, Bangladesh, Cambodia, the European Union, India, Indonesia, Japan, South Korea, Malaysia, Singapore, Switzerland, Taiwan, Thailand and Vietnam. China’s proposed 20 percent excess-capacity duty would sit on top of roughly 25 percent first-term tariffs while keeping China under the 20 percent second-term cap of the trade truce expiring January 10. The duties would not stack on the 10 or 12.5 percent levies imposed in July, would respect caps of 15 percent for the European Union, Japan, South Korea and Taiwan and 19 percent for Malaysia, and would exclude about $30 billion of Chinese imports considered for reductions. A comment period and hearing stand before effect, possibly in January.

The Traveler—Thanksgiving storm: major airlines waive East Coast change fees

With a powerful coastal storm forecast to bring heavy rain and strong winds up the Eastern Seaboard on Thanksgiving Day, the four largest United States airlines have issued temporary weather waivers letting many travellers reroute or change Thursday flights at major East Coast hubs without standard change fees. Delta’s Northeast Weather Flexibility advisory covers nine airports: Baltimore/Washington, Boston Logan, New York Kennedy, New York LaGuardia, Newark Liberty, Philadelphia, Washington Dulles, Washington Reagan National and White Plains. United’s Northeast Coastal Storm waiver applies at least to Boston, Baltimore/Washington, Washington Reagan National, Newark, Washington Dulles, LaGuardia and Philadelphia. American has added a travel alert including parts of the Northeast, and Southwest is allowing adjustments for a group of East Coast airports on November 26. The waivers differ in dates, airports and eligible tickets, and each airline directs customers to its advisory page to verify eligibility.
