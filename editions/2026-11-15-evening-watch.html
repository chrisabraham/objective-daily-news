Bahrain at Evening: Five Capitals Answer in Writing, Tehran Answers Back, and Washington Does Not Answer

The first considered responses to Sunday’s morning are regional, written, and aligned. Saudi Arabia’s Foreign Ministry said the attacks constitute a violation of international law and the principles of good neighbourliness and stressed an immediate halt, in the condemnations account above; Qatar’s Foreign Ministry called the continuation of the attacks a dangerous escalation that will complicate efforts to contain the tension and undermine political and diplomatic endeavours, in the same account; Jordan and Egypt condemned the attacks and expressed solidarity with Bahrain, in the same account and in the second-attack account filed below. Manama’s own statement, in the ministry account above, holds Iran fully responsible, rejects the transformation of Bahraini territory into an arena for settling scores, cites Security Council Resolution 2817 and Bahrain’s right of self-defence under Article 51, in coordination with the Gulf Cooperation Council under its joint defence agreement, and calls on the Security Council, where Bahrain sits, to condemn the attacks and take decisive measures. The Defence Force’s account stands: its air defences intercepted and destroyed several Iranian aerial attacks before they reached their targets, in the Bahrain account filed below.

Tehran’s answer and Washington’s silence complete the evening file. The Guards say six drones of their Aerospace Force targeted the American air-defence radar at the Al-Riffa helicopter base, in a second phase of retaliation, in the Bahrain account above and in the drone account filed below; no damage and no casualties have been reported, and neither Washington nor Manama has confirmed a hit. Tehran’s Foreign Ministry spokesman, Mr. Baghaei, defended the strikes at a news conference as justified in self-defence and said Iran would keep responding to military aggression by the United States or other states, in the second-attack account above; Article 51 is therefore claimed in the same file by Manama for its defence and by Tehran for its strikes, for opposite acts, and the desk prints both claims as claims. TechSpot — nearly 150,000 people sign a petition requesting Mr. Huang back as chief executive

Three days after Mr. Huang stepped down as Nvidia’s chief executive, a campaign to bring him back is gathering momentum: more than 150,000 people have signed an open letter urging the board to reinstate its co-founder while an independent investigation runs its course. The organisers argue he was forced out without a fair opportunity to defend himself, and ask the board to do three things: reinstate him while the investigation continues, ensure he receives a fair hearing, and publicly explain the reasoning behind his departure. The chief financial officer has taken over as interim chief executive, and a longtime director has become the board’s chair. For now the board has announced no change to its succession plans; the next major event is Tuesday’s quarterly earnings report, whose results cover the period when Mr. Huang was fully in charge.

Le Monde — the November 14 demonstrations in Paris and Lyon, diverse but determined crowds

Across France on Saturday, November 14, demonstrations brought together a range of demands, from protecting children and increasing wages to providing more resources for education, continuing high school blockades and protesting police violence. According to the Interior Ministry, 106,000 people marched, including 24,000 in Paris, a sharp drop from previous weeks. A youth coalition of student unions claimed 200,000 demonstrators nationwide, with nearly 100,000 in the capital. Yellow Vest activists staged their fifth act against the high cost of living. The focus for many protesters was the bill called a licence to kill by its critics, scheduled to be examined by the Senate on November 19; the bill aims to introduce a presumption of self-defence after a police officer or gendarme fires a weapon.
