The claim stands in the Guards’ announcement filed below and in two further accounts, one of which carries satellite imagery said to show damage to radar domes at the naval base in Manama, in the imagery account filed below. The same Iranian file reports four tankers hit in the Strait of Hormuz; the independent record in the night’s reading confirms one vessel struck near Ras Al Khaimah, in the strait account filed below. On the talks, Mr. Kushner posted Thursday night that both sides had agreed to expand the next round to include the European partners and that Washington works toward the largest prisoner exchange of the war, in the wire account filed below; the Russian delegation’s head is reported in the Czech accounts to see nothing to talk about with the Europeans, in the Prague account filed below; and no European name, no prisoner number and no date or place for the next meeting stands in the file. In Washington, three House committees have asked Mr. Huang for closed-door questioning on December 1 and for his answer by the evening of November 13, in the congressional account filed below, and the Senate Banking chairman has asked Mr. Huang and Secretary Lutnick to testify on December 3, Mr. Lutnick’s spokeswoman saying he has agreed to appear voluntarily. Bahrain at 0400: A Radar Tehran Says It Destroyed, a Tower Tehran Says Was Struck, and Silence From Both Capitals Named

The Guards’ announcement is early, plain in its claim, and unaccompanied. The Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps announced early Friday that it targeted and destroyed an American early-warning radar installation in Bahrain, saying the strike was direct retaliation for an American airstrike on a telecommunications tower in Sirik, in Iran’s southern Hormozgan province near the Strait of Hormuz, in the Guards’ announcement above. The Guards said their aerospace force is fully prepared for an unlimited confrontation and warned that further American aggression would trigger retaliation without geographical restrictions. The same announcement’s file carries claims beyond Bahrain: damage reported to high-value radar systems across the Gulf, including the AN/FPS-132 early-warning radar at Al Udeid in Qatar, described as damaged rather than destroyed; an estimate of $3.15 billion in affected radar and satellite-communications systems across twelve installations, stated in the account to be asset values and not a confirmed repair bill; and more than ten missiles launched toward the strait and American naval assets. None of it had been independently verified as of early Friday, in the same account. It is, on the accounts before the desk, the first Iranian claim of a direct hit on an American asset in the Gulf since early September.

One account adds a picture where the others have only the claim. Satellite imagery released by Al Jazeera shows damage to several radar domes at the Naval Support Activity base in Manama, the headquarters of the United States Fifth Fleet, in the imagery account above. The desk prints that as it stands: imagery released by one outlet, described in one account, showing damage to domes at the base Tehran says it hit. It is a bearing, not a confirmation; no American or Bahraini statement, no casualty figure and no account of the weapon used stands beside it in the night’s reading. The Sirik end of the exchange is thinner still: Iran’s state agency reported enemy fire on the port city on Thursday without naming the attacker or giving targets, damage or casualties, in the strait account above, and residents near the tower are reported to have described shattered windows and interrupted communications, casualties unverified, in the imagery account above. The United States has not commented on the Sirik reports either. Settled: the claim was made, in Tehran’s words, early Friday, and the reason given for it. Open: everything the claim asserts.

The Strait at 0400: Four Tankers in Tehran’s Account, One Vessel in the Independent Record, and a Price That Barely Moved

Tehran’s shipping file grew by four hulls in the night’s reading, and the independent file grew by one. Iran’s military said four oil tankers had been hit by explosions in the strait: the Guards’ navy said two supertankers were disabled on Wednesday night after they ignored warnings against a route Tehran calls unauthorised, state media said the two hit naval mines and carried more than four million barrels of oil between them, and the army said two more tankers east of the strait caught fire after hitting a mine, in the strait account above. No names and no nationalities were given for any of the four. Against that account stands the single independent bearing in the file: the British agency UKMTO said a vessel was hit by an unidentified projectile about 14 nautical miles northwest of Ras Al Khaimah in the United Arab Emirates on Wednesday, while leaving the strait, with a fire in its engine room—the only independent confirmation of a ship struck in the area this week, in the same account. The Guards also warned companies working with the United States over oil shipments through the strait, and Central Command is reported to have confirmed ongoing enforcement of the naval blockades on Iranian ports, in the risk account filed below. Iran’s security chief is reported in the same account to have warned that military bases from West Asia to Europe could become targets if hostilities expand. The market’s answer, at the hours quoted below, was small: Brent eased in the Asian morning despite the Bahrain claim, a movement one account reads as traders waiting for confirmation before adding a risk premium. Both blockades stand, Tehran’s seven conditions stand unanswered in the file, and Friday holds the first American or Bahraini word on the radar, if either capital chooses to give one.

Dublin at 0400: The Chair Is Agreed in Washington and Kyiv, Opposed in the Russian Delegation’s Reported Words, and Empty in Dublin

Thursday night’s sentence from Washington is the largest the talks have produced, and it is a sentence about a table, not about the war. Mr. Kushner wrote that Russia and Ukraine agreed to expand the next round of talks to include European nations, and that Washington is also working toward what would be the largest prisoner exchange of the war, potentially reuniting thousands of people with their families, without giving details of the deal, in the wire account above. Expanding the round would let the talks take up broader postwar European security arrangements alongside the territorial issues, he said; Washington was encouraged, and significant differences remained. President Zelenskyy welcomed the European presence and said it was important the process had resumed, the Abu Dhabi round having been the first in nine months. Mr. Witkoff said President Putin agreed to let European representatives join the next round, and that the Russian president also urged Washington to work with its allies to understand whether they wanted peace or war in Ukraine. Kyiv and Moscow both said major differences remained, particularly on territory: Russia holds a fifth of Ukraine, and Kyiv refuses to give up the rest of its eastern Donbas region, in the same account. The desk notes what the sentence does not contain: no date, no place, no European name, and no number on the exchange that three governments now call the largest of the war.

The Russian delegation’s answer, in the Czech reporting, is a refusal wearing no diplomatic dress. The head of the Russian delegation, Mr. Medinsky, is quoted saying of the European leaders that they are basically participating in collectively deceiving the Ukrainian people and contributing to the mass dying of civilians, and that he therefore does not understand what there is to talk about with them, in the Prague account above. The same reporting carries the counterweight the desk printed at midnight and prints again because it is the only new machinery in the file: the American negotiators, joined by General Grynkewich, the American European Command chief, asked the Russian side to draft a new proposal addressing its security concerns alongside symmetric steps for de-escalation, in the same account. In the Kremlin, President Putin opened by proposing a humanitarian mission regarding the Ukrainian city of Kostiantynivka, the Russian agency TASS reported, in the same account. One reading of the week, filed from the war’s day-count, holds Mr. Kushner’s phrase meaningful progress implies no advance on a ceasefire, territory or security guarantees, and sets President Zelenskyy’s own three outcomes in order: the prisoner release, the European seat, and the preparation of the next meetings, in the day 1,723 account filed below. Europe’s own formats stand as the midnight edition left them—Ms. Kallas naming Mr. Costa and herself under the treaties, Chancellor Merz naming the E3 and a group of five, President Macron saying the format will be determined later—and the Coalition of the Willing meets in Berlin next week, the next occasion in the file for the chair to be filled. Settled: the expansion is announced by the American envoy and welcomed by Kyiv. Open: whether Moscow’s negotiators will sit with the Europeans they are quoted refusing, and who the Europeans would send if they did.

Nvidia at 0400: Two December Dates, an Answer Due by Evening, and a Petition Inside the Company

Congress has now written the Nvidia file into its December calendar, and its first deadline falls today. Three House committees—Intelligence, Foreign Affairs and the Select Committee on China—sent a joint letter asking Mr. Huang to take part in closed-door questioning on December 1, warning that they would issue a subpoena if he did not cooperate voluntarily, and he was asked to respond by the evening of November 13, in the congressional account above. In the Senate, the Banking Committee’s chairman, Senator Scott, sent letters asking Mr. Huang and Secretary Lutnick to testify voluntarily on December 3, warning that the committee was prepared to use all necessary measures, including subpoenas; senators of both parties are pressing Mr. Lutnick on national security and on chip sales to China, in the same account. A spokeswoman for the Secretary told NBC News that Mr. Lutnick has proactively agreed to appear voluntarily before the Banking Committee, in the employees account filed below. CNBC is reported in the same account to have said earlier Thursday that Mr. Huang had agreed to testify before Congress before Thanksgiving; the House letter’s December 1 date and its evening deadline stand in the file beside that report, and the desk prints both, as it found them. House Speaker Johnson signalled a hardening consensus in Congress on limiting chip exports to China, and senior White House officials are separately discussing whether Mr. Lutnick should be pushed out, in the Morning Bid account filed below. Lawmakers are also scrutinizing why the Commerce Department’s investigation into Megaspeed was stalled last year, in the employees account above.

Inside the company, the night brought the first organized answer from the floor. Several Nvidia employees have reportedly petitioned the company to bring back Mr. Huang: an internal suggestion-box post by a longtime engineer, submitted before the resignation and urging the board to consider employee sentiment before acting, has attracted more than 1,600 comments, many asking the board to find a way to bring him back, Wired reported, in the employees account above. The board’s position stands as Thursday left it: Mr. Huang resigned, Ms. Kress is interim chief executive, and the company says Mr. Huang has no relationship with it anymore, in the same account. President Trump contacted Mr. Huang several times this week to check on him and encouraged him to resist pressure to resign, a person familiar with the matter told CNN, and the President is reported concerned that the departure could affect the company and the market; whether Mr. Huang will join the President’s APEC trip to Shenzhen, where a meeting with President Xi is expected, is unclear, in the congressional account above. Beijing’s answer stands from Wednesday: the Foreign Ministry spokeswoman, Ms. Mao Ning, said the United States should stop fabricating stories about espionage as a pretext for technology blockades on China, in the same account. The Attorney General’s position stands from Thursday: the Justice Department has not reached a conclusion on whether Ms. Alice Huang is a spy. The shares were down 1.3 percent in overnight trading ahead of Friday, in the employees account above, against about 3 percent after hours in the midday Asian account below—two quotes at two hours, printed as found—and stand 11 percent down on the week as of Thursday and 30.5 percent below the October 6 record.

Spain at 0400: The Campaign’s First Day, a Prime Minister at 26 to 29 in Most Polls and at 33.3 in the State’s, and Housing on Every Tongue

Spain’s two-week campaign opened at midnight, and its first morning papers agree on the subject and divide on the score. Prime Minister Sánchez called the snap election last month betting that anger over housing and fear of the far right would rally the left behind his Socialists; interviews and polls suggest many of those voters are too furious at the status quo to oblige, his party’s backing hovering around the 26 to 29 percent recorded before he called the election, and without a shift in the next two weeks an alliance of the Popular Party and Vox is seen as the most likely next government, in the Madrid account filed below. Against that stands the state’s own survey, the largest its pollster has ever carried out for a general election—45,074 telephone interviews between October 7 and 30—giving the Socialists 33.3 percent, the Popular Party 25.5 and Vox 18.4, a commanding lead that contradicts almost every other poll in the country, in the poll account filed below. The desk prints the two readings side by side and calls neither the truth: the state survey’s interviews closed on October 30, before the campaign, and the private readings are reported without their fieldwork in the accounts before the desk. The issue under both is housing: the eviction of 87-year-old Maricarmen Abascal in September and her subsequent death, the protests, and Wednesday’s general strike, which unions said brought more than three million people onto the streets, in the Madrid account above—over 100,000 in the major cities in the campaign account filed below, the two counts standing as printed. Thursday’s decree stands as the evening editions left it: the Maricarmen measures carried 35 to 34 in the Permanent Deputation, the automatic-renewal decree defeated, and the Popular Party promising repeal if it wins. On the trail, Mr. Sánchez appeals for tactical votes with his phrase all on red, more than a million voters are still undecided, Podemos has refused the Broad Front, and Ms. Colau’s coalition campaigns out of Vallecas toward Seville on Saturday. Polls may be published until November 23; Spain votes on November 29. A second shadow stands in the file: the October 28 train crash, whose handling the Prime Minister is working to keep from derailing his message, in the campaign account above, and on Monday the Brazilian president visits Spain to lend his support to the Socialist campaign.

The Times — the state pollster gives Mr. Sánchez the lead as the campaign kicks off

Spain’s state pollster has given Prime Minister Sánchez a commanding lead on the eve of the campaign, in a survey that contradicts almost every other poll in the country. The Centre for Sociological Research predicted the Socialists would win 33.3 percent of the vote, ahead of the Popular Party on 25.5 percent and Vox on 18.4 percent, in the largest survey it has ever carried out for a general election: 45,074 telephone interviews between October 7 and 30. The campaign opens at midnight on Thursday, and polls may be published until November 23, six days before Spaniards vote on November 29. Parliament passed the new housing measure on Thursday by 35 votes to 34 in the Permanent Deputation: a two-year extension for rental contracts expiring before the end of 2028, a suspension of evictions of vulnerable tenants without alternative accommodation until December 2030, and restrictions on speculative purchases by investment funds. A second proposal, for automatic extensions of rental contracts, was defeated after the Basque PNV refused to support it. The Popular Party has promised to repeal the approved measures if it wins power.

EuropeSays — Mr. Sánchez faces a political test as a deadly train crash casts a shadow over the campaign

Prime Minister Sánchez hoped to leverage discontent over unaffordable housing when he called the snap elections in October. With the campaign officially underway, he is working to keep the train tragedy from derailing his message to voters, arguing that a right-wing government would be worse for Spaniards who need government help for housing, healthcare and infrastructure. The campaign of two and a half weeks will be dominated by domestic concerns—housing, immigration and corruption. Several opinion polls conducted before and after the October 28 train crash showed the Socialists behind the combined support of the Popular Party and Vox. On Wednesday a nationwide protest over rents and home costs drew over 100,000 people to Spain’s major cities, part of a general strike, the first in 14 years and the first whose main driver was housing. His government passed one of the two emergency housing decrees through the smaller interim parliament on Thursday. On Monday the Brazilian president will visit Spain and lend his support to the Socialist campaign.

SSBCrack News — Alaska’s Senate race tightens as Ms. Peltola’s lead over Senator Sullivan narrows

Alaska’s Senate race narrowed slightly in the latest results released Wednesday, with Ms. Peltola holding 48.7 percent to Senator Sullivan’s 47.1 percent. Her lead fell to 3,975 votes, down from 4,250 the previous day, and the margin is now smaller than the 4,100 votes cast for Daniel J. Sullivan Jr., the Petersburg candidate whose name resembles the incumbent’s. The results added 5,612 early, absentee and Election Day ballots, about 5,000 of them absentee ballots from House districts in the Matanuska-Susitna Borough. The Associated Press estimates 14 percent of the vote remains uncounted. Both campaigns said the outstanding ballots would decide the race and that counting is not complete. The next scheduled results update is Friday, the deadline for most mailed ballots to arrive; overseas ballots may arrive through November 18, which is also the final day for counting and the date of the ranked-choice tabulation in races where no candidate has more than 50 percent of first choices.

Finance Calendar — the producer price index release of November 13, 2026

The Bureau of Labor Statistics will release the producer price index for October 2026 on Friday, November 13, at 8:30 a.m. Eastern, following a surprise rise of 1.3 percent month on month in September and 6.3 percent year on year, with core producer prices up 0.7 percent on the month and 5.0 percent on the year. Consensus sees October slowing to 0.4 percent on the month and rising 6.6 percent on the year, with core at 0.3 percent on the month and steady at 5.0 percent on the year. A monthly figure above 0.6 percent, or a yearly rise above 7 percent, would surprise to the upside; below 0.2 percent on the month, or below 6 percent on the year, would surprise to the downside. The release is the first major inflation reading of the November calendar, arriving three days after the consumer price report of November 10, and together the two frame expectations for the Federal Reserve’s December meeting.
