The Midday in Brief: Two Hot Prints, a Tariff File Filed, and a Toll That Rose With the Morning

The midday file is the morning’s calendar, read whole. The Bureau of Economic Analysis printed October’s core PCE price index at 3.3 percent from a year earlier at 0830 Eastern—a tick above the 3.2 percent expected and up from September’s 3.1 percent—with headline PCE at 3.8 percent, in the wire account filed below. In the same hour the Bureau revised third-quarter product to a 4.3 percent annual rate, from the advance estimate of 3.4 percent, in the Bureau’s release filed below. Wall Street read the pair as heat on both dials and sold: the Dow down more than 800 points in the morning and the Nasdaq down 3.9 percent, in the live account filed below. Washington’s second morning act was a filing, not a figure: the Trade Representative made determinations against 14 economies in the excess-capacity investigations and proposed tariffs on their goods, in the Trade Representative’s release filed below. In the Gulf, the fuller wire form of the Ras Tanura account carries satellite imagery of black smoke over about 600 square kilometres, and no word from the refinery’s owner. In Ukraine, the night’s toll rose from the two dead the Overnight Watch filed to at least seven in six regions, in the strikes account filed below.

What midday settled is the 0830 block entire—inflation, product, durables and claims—and the 1000 housing print beside it; the tariff file’s shape, its 14 names and its two omissions; and Ukraine’s morning count of its dead, as its authorities file it. What it left open is the close, on the last full session before Thanksgiving; the Beige Book at 1400; the refinery’s state, which no Saudi account in the file describes; the tankers Tehran counts and never names; and the ministry’s word on the Merkury, unissued into a fifth day.

The Numbers at 1200: Core PCE at 3.3 Percent, Product at 4.3 Percent, Claims at 189,000

The inflation print is the correction the Midday Watch exists to file, and it corrects the expectation, not the morning’s hope. The PCE price index rose 0.3 percent in October after a 0.5 percent gain in September, and stood 3.8 percent above a year earlier, in the wire account; the core index, excluding food and energy, also rose 0.3 percent on the month and 3.3 percent on the year. The monthly figures matched the estimates in every account in the file; the annual core figure did not—3.3 percent against 3.2 percent expected, and against September’s 3.1 percent. One account in the file carries September’s headline year figure as revised, at 3.7 percent, where the wire carries it unrevised at 3.6 percent; the desk prints both in the Cocked Hat and notes that October’s 3.8 percent is the same in each. The print is the last inflation reading before the Federal Reserve’s December 8-9 meeting, and the futures markets’ reading of it is in the file in two forms: a December hike fully priced in one account’s futures tool, and the odds reaffirmed in the wire’s futures account.

Product ran hotter than prices. Real gross domestic product increased at a 4.3 percent annual rate in the third quarter, the fastest pace in three years, in the growth account filed below—an upward revision of 0.9 percentage point from the advance estimate of 3.4 percent, against forecasts of 3.5 to 3.6 percent, primarily reflecting upward revisions to investment and consumer spending, in the Bureau’s release; consumer spending itself was revised to a 4.2 percent rate. The two smaller prints of the hour ran in opposite directions: durable goods orders rose 0.2 percent in October, below the estimate of 0.5 percent in the live account and 0.7 percent in the calendar accounts, and jobless claims fell to 189,000, below the 200,000 to 201,000 expected, from 203,000 in the prior week in one account and a downwardly revised 202,000 in another. At 1000, new-home sales jumped 14.2 percent to an annual rate of 701,000 units in October, the highest since December 2025, in the housing account filed below—on a September figure revised sharply down in the same release, to 614,000 from the 676,000 previously reported, with the median new-house price at $391,300, down 3.2 percent from a year earlier. Settled: the five prints, with their revisions attached. Open: the Beige Book, and December’s meeting.

Washington at 1200: Determinations Against Fourteen, Proposals on the Table, and Two Names Left Off

At dawn the tariff file was a report: the Trade Representative preparing findings on excess industrial capacity among 16 trading partners, three people with knowledge of the matter telling the wire. By midday it is a filing. The Trade Representative determined under Section 301 that the acts, policies and practices of 14 economies relating to structural excess capacity and production in manufacturing sectors are unreasonable and burden or restrict United States commerce, and published an economy-specific report for each, in the release above. The 14 are China, the European Union, Singapore, Switzerland, Indonesia, Malaysia, Cambodia, Thailand, Korea, Vietnam, Taiwan, Bangladesh, Japan and India. The release states the negative in its own sentence: the Trade Representative has not made determinations with respect to Mexico or Norway at this time. Ambassador Greer’s words in the release name the practices the determinations rest on—industrial strategies built on targeting manufacturing, wage and labor suppression, financial and other support, and non-market economy inputs.

The rates stand in the file in two forms, and the desk keeps them apart. In the tariff account filed below, a senior official puts China’s proposed excess-capacity rate at 20 percent, in addition to the roughly 25 percent imposed during the President’s first term, keeping China above the 20 percent proposed for Vietnam; the duties would not stack on the 10 or 12.5 percent imposed in July; rates would respect the caps in negotiated deals, including 15 percent for the European Union, Japan, South Korea and Taiwan and 19 percent for Malaysia; and implementation could be pushed into January by the notice-and-comment period and a hearing. In the rates account filed below, the proposed ceilings run higher and by country: up to 35 percent on Vietnam, 25 percent on India with 17 percent available under a reciprocal trade agreement, 20 percent on China and Singapore with 10 percent available to Singapore under an agreement, 18 percent on Bangladesh, Cambodia, Indonesia, Malaysia and Thailand, and up to 15 percent for the European Union, Japan, South Korea, Switzerland and Taiwan; written comments and hearing requests are due by January 5, 2027, and the hearings commence January 25, 2027. The two forms differ on Vietnam by fifteen points; both are printed, and the Federal Register notice the official promised for Wednesday is the document that will settle which form governs. Settled: the determinations, the 14 names, and the two omissions. Open: the rates, in their final form, and Mexico and Norway.

The Strait at 1200: A Warning to Coordinate, Three Crossings by the Afternoon Count, and Two Tankers Still Unnamed

Tehran’s Wednesday statement reached the wires in its fuller form by midday, and the addition is a second voice. Beside the Guards’ Navy statement—two oil tankers struck naval mines, exploded and caught fire while attempting an unauthorised route, in the strait account filed below—stands a separate statement by Army spokesperson Mohammad Akraminia: the military has deployed new systems and upgraded existing ones to strengthen surveillance and control of the strait, and vessels passing through without coordinating with Iranian forces have faced greater risks and struck mines in recent weeks. The same account carries Tehran’s standing position, that normal shipping will not be restored until its conditions are met, and the Qatari-mediated proposal under which Tehran offered to reopen the waterway within 48 hours of Washington meeting its conditions. The Guards’ own wording, in the Tehran account filed below, warns crews of ships with navigation and positioning systems switched off against American officials’ statements. No vessel is named in either statement, no crew is accounted for, and Central Command has filed no answer at the stamp.

The visible counter’s day moved from the Overnight Watch’s zero to three. Three AIS-visible commercial ships had crossed on Wednesday as of 14:42 UTC—three inbound, none outbound, two of them tankers, the most recent the oil and chemical tanker MIRAAN, inbound at 13:47 UTC—in the strait count filed below. Tuesday stands corrected at four crossings in the same counter, one inbound and three outbound, one a tanker; the seven days stand at 49 crossings, about 8 percent of the normal rate. The wider monitor’s 24-hour count stands in the file at 27 to 28 ships, as the midnight and overnight editions printed it. Settled: Wednesday’s three, in the one instrument, and the warning, in the spokesman’s words. Open, as it has been open all week: the tankers themselves.

Ukraine at 1200: Seven Dead in Six Regions, a Gas Plant and a Power Plant Stopped, and Three Refineries Hit in Return

The Overnight Watch filed the air force’s arithmetic—112 drones in the night, 88 shot down or suppressed, hits at 18 locations—and left open what the 18 locations cost. The morning’s reports answer in part, and the answer is a toll. At least seven people were killed in six regions, in the strikes account above: one in Bila Tserkva in the Kyiv region, where critical infrastructure, an Epicentr shopping centre and a Nova Poshta postal branch were damaged; one killed and seven injured in the Kharkiv region, where a strike hit a minibus on a highway; two killed in the Sumy region, aged 76 and 69, and another wounded; the two killed in the Odesa region on Tuesday evening, a man of 41 and a boy of 17, now with their ages in the file; and one killed and three wounded in the Zhytomyr region, with casualties also reported in the Chernihiv and Donetsk regions. The air force’s count stands confirmed in its fuller form, in the air-force account filed below: 112 strike drones from 1800 on November 24, 66 of them jet-powered, with Gerbera and Parodiya decoys among them, launched from five sites in Russia and from Donetsk region and Crimea; 88 downed or suppressed by 0800, 47 of them jet-powered; and the attack still ongoing, with several drones still in Ukrainian airspace, when the statement was published.

The energy file of the night is two stoppages and a count of the dark. A Naftogaz gas production facility in the Poltava region was struck, caught fire and suspended operations, its acting head saying Russia was deliberately targeting gas production; a DTEK thermal power plant was seriously damaged and stopped generating, the third large-scale strike on the company’s plants in November, the company said; and more than 9,000 customers were left without electricity in the Odesa region after Tuesday evening’s attacks. Kyiv’s answer in the same night is three refineries and an airbase, in the strikes account: the Kuibyshev and Syzran plants in the Samara region, both Rosneft’s, with fires at both—the regional governor confirming the attack without naming the facilities, reporting one person killed, power cut in several districts, and 145 drones shot down over the region in the authorities’ count—and the Novoshakhtinsk plant in the Rostov region, where the governor confirmed a combined strike of more than 50 drones and missiles lasting six hours before the plant suspended operations. The General Staff also reported strikes on the Savasleyka airbase in Nizhny Novgorod region, with three MiG-31 and two Su-34 aircraft damaged. The Samara fires stand described in the refinery account filed below, which places the Syzran impact area near the plant’s primary distillation and catalytic cracking units and records that the full extent of the damage has yet to be established. Dymer’s toll stands at 12, unchanged. Settled: the seven, as the authorities file them, and the three refineries, as the governors confirm the attacks. Open: the damage at each plant, and the night’s final count.
