Wall Street Buys the Morning, the Autopsies Begin in Kraków, and the Mediator Goes In at Noon

The news at midday: the morning’s file corrected itself in three places before noon, and the corrections are the lead. Kraków’s wounded now stand in a governor’s hospital count, not in a weekend tally. Alaska’s next count stands on Tuesday, not Monday. Asia’s rebound closed smaller than its morning print, and Seoul closed down. Against the corrections, the day’s two scheduled acts ran on time: Mr. Miller took the chief of staff’s office in Washington, and the G7 ministers sat in Paris. At noon exactly, the mediator went into the Oval Office. What he carries out is not yet in any file. Wall Street, handed the same record, bought it: the Dow up 540 points at the open and oil down five percent.

This edition continues the log from Edition No. 24, the Overnight Watch, collected at 0400 today, and from the Organ Donation special report, filed at 0805 today. The Midday in Brief

Midday corrected the morning in Kraków, in the count, and in the markets, and it confirmed the day’s schedule everywhere else. In Kraków, Governor Krzysztof Klęczar told reporters that fifteen people injured in the attack remain in hospitals, fourteen of them in stable condition and one, a police officer who took part in the arrest, in serious condition, in Reuters’ Monday file filed below; the overnight edition’s figure was seventeen injured in the prosecutors’ morning count, and both are printed in the Cocked Hat. The suspect died at about 4:30 a.m., the governor said, after at least three operations; prosecutors said autopsies of all nine victims began Monday and would take about two days, and Justice Minister Waldemar Żurek said the investigation continues, to establish whether the man acted alone or was inspired by a group. In Washington, Mr. Miller’s first day as chief of staff opened with one departure reported beneath him, deputy chief of staff Richard Walters leaving the administration, in the departure account filed below. At noon the president’s meeting with Qatar’s prime minister began behind closed doors; no readout had been issued at collection time. In Paris the G7 foreign ministers opened two days of talks, moved at short notice from Marseille, with Secretary Rubio attending both days on his first trip abroad since the midterms. In New York the Dow rose 540.52 points at the open and Brent crude fell about five percent to $93.38 a barrel, in Reuters’ market file and the morning market account filed below. The desk files the day in that order.

The Corrections at Noon: Three Morning Figures, Restated

The first correction is a hospital count. The weekend’s Kraków wounded ran fifteen, sixteen and seventeen in successive files, the seventeen a prosecutors’ count carried in Monday’s morning accounts. The midday figure is of a different kind: fifteen people remain in hospitals, the governor of the province said at a Monday press conference, fourteen stable and one officer serious. A count of the injured and a count of those still in hospital are not the same instrument, and the desk prints the governor’s figure as the midday state of the hospitals without striking the earlier tallies, which counted the wounded at other hours. On the two officers the accounts still divide: one in a coma in one Monday account, one in serious condition in the governor’s words, the second officer’s state given as regained consciousness in Sunday’s file and not restated at midday.

The second correction is a date. The overnight edition carried Alaska’s next count as due Monday or Tuesday. The count guide checked Monday morning against the Division of Elections puts the next scheduled count on Tuesday, November 10, carrying early votes cast October 30 to November 2 and questioned ballots, in the count guide filed below. Nothing moves in Alaska today. The ranked-choice tabulation stands on November 18.

The third correction is a close. Tokyo’s morning print, up 3.44 percent at 59,411 in the overnight file, closed at 58,775.50, up 2.33 percent, in the Asia close account filed below. Seoul’s morning gain, up between 1.4 and 1.8 percent in the overnight accounts, closed down 0.81 percent at 5,018.85. The morning’s rebound was real at the morning hour and smaller at the close; both prints stand, each under its hour.

Kraków at Noon: The Autopsies Begin, and the Case Narrows to the Evidence

Poland’s file acquired its official Monday shape before noon, in statements from the governor, the prosecutors and the justice minister. Washington at Noon: A Chief of Staff In, a Deputy Out, and the Mediator Behind a Closed Door

The handover ran on its announced date, and its first personnel fact is a departure. Stephen Miller, 41, took the chief of staff’s office Monday as the White House’s 33rd holder of the post, succeeding Susie Wiles, who moves to the Trump Political Operation; the president’s Friday announcement is the record of the change, in the handover account and the record of his stated positions filed below. Deputy chief of staff Richard Walters is leaving the administration, reported Sunday as the first major departure expected in the shakeup; Mr. Miller called him an invaluable member of the White House and said he will keep helping the administration from outside the building, in the departure account above. No statement from the new chief of staff describing his plans for the office was in the file at noon.

At noon the day’s central meeting began, and at noon it is a beginning and nothing more. Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al Thani, Qatar’s prime minister and foreign minister, went into the Oval Office at 1200 Eastern in a session the White House schedule marks closed press, having visited Tehran last week and with a separate meeting with Vice President Vance on the day’s schedule, in Reuters’ Monday file filed below. What he carries is restated in the midday accounts in its fuller form: an end to the American blockade of Iran’s ports, the lifting of some sanctions and a halt to bombing, in return for Tehran stopping its own closure of the Strait of Hormuz, with talks on the nuclear programme and other sanctions to follow. Tehran’s foreign ministry spokesman, Esmaeil Baghaei, defended the plan on Monday, saying talks could resume within two days if its conditions were met, in the live file filed below. Against it stand the American sentences of the weekend, unwithdrawn at noon: the president’s, that fresh attacks are highly unlikely if talks produce a strong agreement, and Secretary Rubio’s, in the fuller form he gave it on his way to Paris, that there had been no progress and that Iranian demands were in part, in his words, unacceptable, outrageous and not realistic. Senior American and Gulf officials cast doubt on an imminent deal, Reuters reported. The White House has released no readout. The desk files the schedule, the proposal as both sides state it, and the absence of a readout, and nothing beyond them.

Paris at Noon: The Ministers Sit, and the Subject Is Another Table

The G7 foreign ministers opened in Paris on Monday morning, and the meeting’s business is the week’s other rooms. The gathering, among the last major events of France’s G7 presidency, was moved at short notice from Marseille to Paris, for scheduling reasons in one account and for security reasons after weeks of protests in another; both accounts are filed below, in the Paris account and the diplomats’ account. Secretary Rubio attends both days, his first appearance before the allies since the midterms, and ministers from Ukraine, Saudi Arabia, Egypt, South Korea and Brazil attend alongside the seven. The stated agenda runs to continued support for Ukraine in military capability, finance and energy, the Middle East, and the securing of energy supplies; four G7 diplomats told reporters the meeting is the allies’ first chance to gauge whether the president’s midterm defeat alters the administration’s approach. On the sidelines, the French finance ministry hosts a donor conference Monday for the repair of the Chernobyl shelter dome, damaged in a strike last year. France has indicated the meeting is not expected to produce a joint communiqué, in the Brussels account filed below. The Abu Dhabi table itself sits Tuesday and Wednesday; Paris is its antechamber, and at noon the antechamber is in session and the table is not.

Two passenger trains travelling south from Cairo were involved in a fatal collision on November 8, which killed seven and injured 125. Train 134 from Cairo to Fayoum ran into the rear of inter-city train 2010 from Cairo to Aswan at Al-Ayyat, around 74 kilometres south of Cairo; train 2010 is understood to have been stationary, and the driver of train 134 is reported to be one of the dead.
