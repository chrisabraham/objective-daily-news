Shenzhen Answered: Berlin’s Reply, and the Call That Three Accounts Tell Three Ways

The day’s second room has now spoken, and its answer to the first room is written down. The APEC leaders adopted by consensus the 2026 Leaders’ Declaration, issued as the Shenzhen Outlook on building an Asia-Pacific community, with three outcome documents on trade, connectivity and innovation, at the 33rd APEC Economic Leaders’ Meeting chaired by President Xi on Thursday morning in Shenzhen, in the APEC account filed below. The document was issued as an outlook rather than a declaration, a change of wording made by China as host. China announced $20 million for APEC practical cooperation, and Vietnam will host APEC in 2027, in the same account. The trilateral declaration itself stands as the White House published it, in the declaration text filed below: United Nations reform, more refined petroleum products, the immunities of state officials and the limits of the International Criminal Court, and a dialogue on reducing nuclear risks. It names neither Ukraine nor Iran, sets no arsenal cuts and no timetable, and announces no lifting of sanctions. The President, speaking to NBC after the meeting, rated the summit a 12 on a scale of zero to 10, in the wrap account filed below, and said on his way back to Washington that it was a great and highly consequential trip, with a news conference to come at noon on Friday.

Berlin’s reply came in the form of a joint statement by Germany, France, Britain and Ukraine, and it is the evening’s considered response. The four called on Russia to agree to an energy ceasefire throughout the winter as a first step toward ending hostilities, and to a ceasefire allowing food exports from the Black Sea region, in the Berlin account above. They reaffirmed long-term support—political, diplomatic, military, financial and civilian—and said Russia would not be able to outlast the coalition’s support and Ukraine’s resilience. Strengthening air defence, above all systems able to intercept ballistic missiles, is the priority, with extra efforts ahead of winter. Any future ceasefire must include robust, legally binding security guarantees, defensive in nature and closely coordinated with the United States, which could include a European-led Multinational Force Ukraine supported by the United States. The statement reaffirmed NATO allies’ commitments to provide 70 billion euros in military assistance in 2026 and at least the equivalent in 2027. The European Commission’s proposal of a 45 billion-euro loan for Ukraine in 2027 stands beside it, and Britain announced a 260 million-pound winter package including more than 1,000 long-range strike drones, in the package account filed below—though one account carries the British pledge as 1,000 long-range cruise missiles, and the desk prints the disagreement in the Cocked Hat. The American envoys, Mr. Witkoff and Mr. Kushner, attended, and Mr. Kushner said Russia is “comfortable” with NATO-like, Article 5-based security guarantees for Ukraine, in the envoy account filed below; no Russian official was quoted accepting or addressing such guarantees, and the claim rests on his characterisation alone. Mr. Kushner also announced a new prisoner exchange between Russia and Ukraine in the week of November 23, after talks resumed in Abu Dhabi under American mediation. Chancellor Merz said he expects new peace talks soon, with Europe at the table alongside the United States, Russia and Ukraine, on neutral ground, with the United Arab Emirates a possible venue, in the talks account filed below.

The first responses from the capitals run in both directions from the declaration. President Macron, in Berlin, said the Shenzhen declaration would not lead to a reorganisation of the world, and that the critical factor in Europe’s reorganisation is the meeting in Berlin; he said the allies should reopen the question of Ukraine’s accession to NATO, in the NATO account filed below. Chancellor Merz called Shenzhen “above all a photo op” and said a Yalta-style format will not bring peace. Prime Minister Frederiksen of Denmark called it incomprehensible that the President met President Putin while Moscow continues its war. President Zelensky said the meeting showed Russia had failed to divide the European allies and saw a “huge chance” for peace if the United States really put pressure on President Putin. Ukraine’s foreign minister, Mr. Sybiha, wrote that Shenzhen is not the Yalta of President Putin’s dreams, and that Russia has practically lost its bridge to the real Yalta. The Senate Minority Leader, Mr. Schumer, called the President’s calling President Putin his friend despicable. The President telephoned the Berlin meeting from Air Force One after Shenzhen, and the call is the evening’s sharpest disagreement: one account, citing sources, reports he asked why the allies keep spending millions and billions on military support for Kyiv; a second reports he said his conversation with President Putin had gone well and that President Putin wants to end the war; a third, citing one official present, reports he told the leaders they would not need to spend such sums propping up Ukraine’s defences if they put more effort into ending the conflict—“Enough already, you’re spending so much money on the war and you need to be spending it on your own countries.” Prime Minister Burnham, by contrast, said the President was very clear that the war is a lose-lose for everybody, and disputed the first account, in the speech account filed below: “That was not the phone call that we heard from the president this afternoon. I would say quite the opposite.” President Putin, for his part, conceded at his Shenzhen press conference that the declaration did not set ambitious goals, and added that nobody would be forced to comply with its decisions. He said he had suggested, half-jokingly, nominating the President for the Nobel Peace Prize, in the prize account filed below, and said he would consider attending the Group of 20 meeting in Miami in December. On the trade truce, the file stands as it stood at midday: an extension reported, with no length and no terms, against a second reading that no extension was announced. The truce as agreed runs to January 10, 2027. Settled: Berlin’s text, the pledges, and the disagreement about the call. Open: the truce’s length, the talks’ date, and the guarantees’ shape.

The Bridge at 1800: A Third Strike, a Vow, and a Conveyor Belt Broken

The war did not wait for either room, and the bridge is the day’s third front. A third Ukrainian aerial attack in four days on the Kerch Bridge halted rail traffic on Thursday, a Russian official said, after earlier strikes shut down the road section, in the bridge account filed below. Train traffic stopped after the overnight strike while authorities assessed the damage, and was to be restored from around 4 p.m. local time on Thursday, said Mr. Aksyonov, the Moscow-appointed head of Crimea. Ukraine’s Defence Ministry confirmed hits with domestically produced Neptune missiles near the arch and the railway tracks, in the Kyiv account filed below, and called the bridge a “war conveyor belt” transporting materiel to Russian forces in occupied southern Ukraine: “That conveyor belt has now broken down.” A large section of the road deck and an arch collapsed in this week’s attacks, the culmination of weeks of preparatory strikes over seven stages, according to President Zelensky. Whether the trains run tonight is itself in dispute: one account says rail services continue on schedule while road traffic remains halted, and another says trains are still able to transit the bridge—against the Defence Ministry’s statement that all train traffic has been halted for now. President Putin, at his press conference in Shenzhen, said that after the strike on the bridge there would be no agreement on a ceasefire or on not hitting energy infrastructure, and that Russia’s response would be “much more painful” than what Kyiv had done. He said the crossing is used only for civilian purposes and not to transport military equipment or petroleum products, and said he had ordered the bridge closed to the transport of military hardware—against the accounts that describe it as a key military supply route. Russia’s Investigative Committee opened a criminal terrorism probe into the damage.

The day’s strikes ran wider than the bridge. Russia launched anti-ship missiles and 98 drones at Ukraine, with hits recorded at 18 locations, and at least 10 people were wounded as strikes caused damage in 12 regions. A daytime drone attack on Kyiv killed one person, a 26-year-old man in the Podilskyi district, and injured five; the Dutch and Austrian embassies were damaged, St. Volodymyr’s Cathedral had its windows shattered, and cars and a security post were damaged. The Netherlands summoned the Russian ambassador. Russian strikes hit central Odesa three times, killing four people and injuring nine, and a strike on a market in Zlatopol, in Kharkiv Oblast, injured 11. Naftogaz facilities in Dnipropetrovsk and Poltava oblasts were attacked. Russia’s Defence Ministry said its air defences intercepted 53 Ukrainian drones over 10 Russian regions overnight. On the front lines there is no verified territorial change in the file: Ukrainian forces reported gains north of Sumy toward the Russian border, and the Russian Defence Ministry claimed control of Belytske in the Pokrovsk direction and further advances in Zaporizhzhia Oblast. Settled: the third strike, the vow, and the day’s dead. Open: whether the trains run, and what the promised response strikes.

Washington at 1800: The War-Powers Vote, Alaska’s House, and the Week’s Root Cause

The Senate rejected the Iran war-powers resolution on Thursday, the first Iran vote in either chamber since the November 3 midterms. The resolution, which had passed the House, failed 48 to 51 in the vote account above. Three Republicans voted for it—Senators Collins of Maine, Murkowski of Alaska and Paul of Kentucky—and Senator Fetterman of Pennsylvania was the only Democrat to vote against it. The war has cost the American military at least $47 billion as of October 16, according to the Pentagon, in the same account. The Senate also passed, by voice vote, the Fair Leave Act, and resumed consideration of the permitting bill. In Alaska, the House race is settled and the Senate race waits. Representative Nick Begich III won re-election in the ranked-choice runoff conducted on Wednesday evening, 51.35 percent to 48.65 percent over the independent Mr. Hill, in the Alaska account filed below; Mr. Hill conceded on Wednesday night. Certification is still scheduled for November 25, and the results are unofficial until then. The Division of Elections director said she plans to certify the election as the law requires, confirmed to include the Senate race won by Ms. Peltola, despite the governor and other Republicans saying the Senate race might not be certified over allegations that Ms. Peltola’s campaign recruited spoiler candidates. The Senate race stands at about 144,302 votes for Ms. Peltola, 50.12 percent, against 131,844 for Senator Sullivan, 45.80 percent; Mr. Sullivan has not conceded. The Justice Department’s appeal of the dismissal of its suit to bar certification is pending in the Ninth Circuit, with its response reported due on Thursday; no report of the response actually being filed was found in the file by the evening. A separate criminal probe continues, with more than 200 subpoenas issued over whether Ms. Peltola’s staff or others recruited a spoiler candidate; no finding of criminal wrongdoing has been reported.

In the air, the week’s root cause is promised, not yet delivered. The Federal Aviation Administration’s administrator said on Thursday that the government expects to announce this week the root cause of the router outage that led to the nationwide ground stop on Friday, November 13, which disrupted more than 18,000 flights and a million passengers, in the aviation account filed below. The Transportation Secretary has said a Juniper router installed by L3Harris in a remote Arkansas facility triggered the nearly five-hour stop, the longest since September 11, 2001, and the FAA has paused Juniper router installations while testing continues. The Senate Commerce Committee is owed its technical answers by November 23. On Thursday afternoon there were no ground stops anywhere in the United States, with ground delay programmes at San Francisco and Las Vegas. No United States-wide cancellation total for Thursday was found in the file. Settled: the vote, the House race, and the promise of a cause. Open: the certification, the Justice Department’s response, and the router’s verdict.

The World at 1800: The Hague’s Word on Myanmar, a Boat Off Senegal, and a Lockdown in Dhaka

The International Court of Justice ruled on Thursday that Myanmar’s security forces’ killings and widespread sexual violence against the Rohingya in 2017 amounted to state-sponsored genocide, in the Court account filed below—the first time the court has held a state responsible for genocide, and only the fourth time since the Holocaust an international court has found the genocide threshold met, after Rwanda in 1994, Srebrenica in 1995 and the Khmer Rouge. The case was brought by Gambia in 2019; the 2017 offensive drove at least 700,000 to 730,000 Rohingya into Bangladesh. The court ordered compensation, the amount to be determined later, and a safe, dignified return and family reunification. The ruling is final and cannot be appealed, but the court has very limited means of enforcement. Myanmar’s government did not immediately respond. Off Senegal, a pirogue capsized off Yenne, south of Dakar, at about 2 a.m. local time on Wednesday, and dozens are missing, in the boat account filed below. The numbers disagree: one survivor told police about 100 people were aboard, another told Reuters about 50; bodies recovered stand at one, two and four in different accounts. Nearly 600 people have died this year trying to reach the Canary Islands from West Africa, according to the United Nations refugee agency. In Bangladesh, the banned Awami League’s nationwide dawn-to-dusk lockdown brought crude-bomb blasts in Dhaka, arson and clashes in 28 of 64 districts, in the lockdown account filed below. At least four people have been killed over the past two days. The ancestral home of the former interim chief, Mr. Yunus, in Chattogram was set on fire late on Wednesday night; the families on the upper floors were safe. At least 37 Grameen Bank branches have been targeted in arson or attempted fires since November 10, and the police arrested 565 people in a 24-hour operation before the protests. Settled: the Court’s word, and the lockdown’s toll. Open: the boat’s count, and the compensation’s sum.

France and Spain at 1800: A March Counted in Thousands, a Bill Unvoted, a Debate Unjudged

France’s Thursday ends with its counts still apart and its bill still unvoted. The retirees marched in Paris from the Montparnasse station forecourt to Place Vauban near Les Invalides, at the call of the retirees’ inter-union, the Group of Nine, demanding indexation of all pensions to inflation from January 2027 and the keeping of the 10 percent income-tax allowance for retirees. The organisers had aimed for about 30,000 in Paris. The only turnout figure in the file is far below it: more than a thousand people, mostly retirees, marched, in the march account filed below. No Interior Ministry figure and no union count for Thursday was found in the file, and no incidents were reported. The Senate’s debate of the police firearms bill—which would give officers a presumption of legitimate self-defence when they use firearms—had not reached a vote by the evening, in the Senate account filed below. The bill could be put to the vote late in the evening, unless the many speeches from the left push the ballot back to Friday evening; the government said examination could if needed continue on Friday evening. The Interior Minister, Mr. Nuñez, said the presumption will simply lift the automaticity of police custody for the officer or gendarme who used his weapon. A senator of the left said that tomorrow it will no longer be for the officer to justify his shot, but for the courts and the families to prove he should not have fired. Outside the Senate, several hundred opponents gathered, and a mock cemetery of 35 tombs was installed before the Luxembourg Gardens gates, representing as many victims of fatal police shootings since 2017, according to the organisers. A petition against the bill has gathered 732,086 signatures.

Spain’s Thursday is one room, and at the evening stamp the room is still talking. Mr. Sánchez and Mr. Feijóo face each other at 10 p.m. Madrid time in the only head-to-head debate before the general election on November 29, organised by the Television Academy at the Madrid Content City studio in Tres Cantos and moderated by its president, Ms. Casado, in the debate account filed below. The debate lasts about two hours, Mr. Feijóo opens and Mr. Sánchez closes, and the five blocks run housing, the economy and taxes, social policy and public services, corruption and democratic regeneration, and migration and foreign policy. At the stamp the file carries the opening and the first two blocks: Mr. Feijóo opened with “Spain deserves a decent president. And he is not one”; in the housing block Mr. Sánchez defended rent regulation and Mr. Feijóo questioned it; in the economy block Mr. Feijóo framed the argument around pay slips, the fridge and people’s lives, and Mr. Sánchez replied that Spain is the large economy creating the most jobs. The later blocks, the closing minute, any verdict and any audience figure are not in the file at 1800, and the desk prints no winner. The polls ahead of the debate disagree with each other: the 40dB tracking gives the Popular Party 32.2 percent and 134 seats against 30.2 percent and 120 for the Socialists, with Vox at 17.6 percent and 58 seats and the Popular Party–Vox bloc at 192 seats, in the tracking account filed below; the SW Demoscopia survey for La Séptima gives the Socialists 31.9 percent and 134 seats against 28.9 percent for the Popular Party, with the right-wing bloc short of a majority on its own, in the survey account filed below; and the survey of surveys stands at 31.3 percent for the Popular Party, 27.5 for the Socialists and 19.0 for Vox. Mr. Sánchez remains the preferred candidate to lead the government, 31.8 percent against 23.1 for Mr. Feijóo. In Brussels, the European Commission confirmed on Thursday that it was targeted by an AI-generated cyber campaign, detected on October 22 and confirmed after investigation on October 26, in the cyber account filed below. Several internal applications were taken offline as a precaution and most restored; whether staff data was compromised is not established. Settled: the march’s size as reported, the debate’s hour and order, and the campaign’s confirmation. Open: the Senate’s vote, the debate’s verdict, and the breach’s extent.
