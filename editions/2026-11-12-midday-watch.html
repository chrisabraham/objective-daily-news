Nvidia at Noon: The Silence Ends in a Resignation, and the Filing Gives No Reason

The absence the overnight edition printed at its full size ended on Thursday morning in three paragraphs of filing. Nvidia’s Form 8-K, filed Thursday and read in the filing account above, records that on November 11 the board accepted the resignation of Mr. Huang as president, chief executive officer and member of the board, effective immediately. The board appointed Ms. Kress, 59, the chief financial officer since 2013, to serve as interim chief executive while it conducts a search that will consider internal and external candidates; she keeps the finance job in the meantime. The board also appointed Mr. Jones, 73, a director since 1993 and a former chief executive of Synopsys, as chair of the board, a position the company has never had before. The release attached to the filing carries the two statements of confidence the form allows: Mr. Jones said the board has full confidence in Ms. Kress and the leadership team and expects the company’s operations, customer commitments and product roadmap to continue without interruption, and Ms. Kress said she is committed to building on the foundation Mr. Huang created over the last 33 years. The company’s results stand where they stood: Tuesday, November 17, at about 1:20 p.m. Pacific, with the call at 2 p.m., now the first report the company will deliver with Ms. Kress holding both of the top jobs.

What the documents do not contain is the week. Neither the filing nor the release gives a reason for the resignation. Neither mentions the independent investigation the board announced on November 8, whether it continues now that Mr. Huang has left, or anything it has found. The verb is the file’s open seam: the company says he resigned, and the Journal’s account uses the same word against its own reporting that the board was preparing to remove him; Bloomberg’s news account said he had been ousted, in the market account filed below. The documents do not say whether he was asked to go. The allegations stand where they stood at midnight—two reported relationships, one of them with a woman American authorities are reported to have concluded was a Chinese intelligence asset, in the timeline account filed below—as allegations; no finding has been made by the board, by any committee or by any court, and no criminal charge against Mr. Huang stands in the release or in any account in the file. Mr. Huang holds about 3.61 percent of the company and is its fourth-largest investor, in the wire account filed below; the release does not say his shares are for sale.

Washington’s track is unchanged in form and changed in address. No subpoena has been issued in any account in the midday file. The three committees’ request for a closed-door briefing on November 18 was addressed to Mr. Huang personally; whether the briefing now happens, and who attends for Nvidia, is unstated in the filing account above. A second congressional paper entered the file on Wednesday: Senators Warren and Coons wrote to Secretary Lutnick, Secretary Rubio and Mr. Miller asking them to preserve documents related to Nvidia chip exports to China and to the reported intelligence matter, and requesting written confirmation by Tuesday, November 17 and a briefing, in the brief account filed below. On the sell side, Bernstein cut its price target on the shares in half on Thursday morning, from $400 to $200, in the market account above. Settled: the resignation, the interim chief, the chair, the earnings date. Open: the reason, the investigation, the search, and November 18.

Moscow at Noon: Two Hours in the Kremlin, a New Meeting Set, and the Numbers Still to Come

The meeting the overnight edition filed as the day’s first fact has been held, and its announced product is a process: the trilateral restarted, a new meeting set, and Europe promised the chair Abu Dhabi left empty. Mr. Witkoff and Mr. Kushner went straight to the Kremlin and briefed President Putin in a meeting that lasted about two hours, in the Associated Press account above; Mr. Ushakov, Mr. Dmitriev and Mr. Medinsky sat on the Russian side, in the meeting account filed below. On departure Mr. Witkoff said, in the departure account above: “I think progress was made. I think it was made earlier today. People didn’t think that we were going to restart trilateral. We’ve restarted it. We’ve set a new meeting.” Mr. Kushner added that the discussions had been very fulsome and detailed on all the topics, and said in Moscow that the delegations had agreed on many of the principles and are still apart on some of the way to accomplish peace, in the Associated Press account above. No date or place for the new meeting stands in the midday file.

On Europe, the envoys carried the question in and carried an answer out, and the answer is printed beside the week’s contrary sentences, unjoined. Mr. Witkoff confirmed that European representatives would join the next talks—“They’re our partners, and it’s important for them to be involved”—in the departure account above. President Putin said Russia had never objected to pursuing dialogue with Europe, and said, in the same account: “You want this peace to be a durable and lasting one, and I confirm that I subscribe to that, and I’d say more. I want this peace to be eternal, for good.” Mr. Dmitriev said there is a growing consensus that working on European security is an important step. Against those sentences stand the ones the file already holds: Mr. Peskov said on Wednesday that no contacts with European representatives were planned in Abu Dhabi and none took place, and Minister Lavrov criticized the European presence there; the assessment filed below records that the Kremlin stated on November 11 that its officials currently have no vision of a negotiating format that includes European representatives, in the assessment account filed below. President Putin’s other sentences in the meeting, in the Associated Press account above, run the other way: any European troops sent to Ukraine after a ceasefire would be absolutely unacceptable and would be regarded as legitimate targets—in his words, the war would resume immediately—and Russia regards its prospective border with Ukraine as extending to the four Ukrainian regions Moscow annexed in 2022. The envoys fly to Dublin on Thursday evening to debrief President Zelenskyy, who is attending the European Political Community summit there.

The prisoner numbers were due today, on Mr. Kushner’s word at Abu Dhabi, and at 1200 no delegation has published a figure. President Zelenskyy said in Dublin on Wednesday that he was waiting for more information from the American team and expected Moscow to agree to the humanitarian release of up to 1,200 prisoners, in the envoys account filed below; he described a possibility to exchange 1,000 to 1,200 prisoners of war, in the Dublin account filed below. The Russian general’s figure in the assessment account above puts an exchange of 1,000 prisoners within the next two to three weeks. The ranges the week has carried—more than 1,000 a side on the Russian delegation’s figure, 1,000 or 1,500 on the president’s earlier announced plans—stand in the Cocked Hat against the new ones. Settled: the meeting, its length, the new meeting, and Europe’s promised place. Open: the numbers, the land, and the ceasefire—and now a date for the next round.

The War at Noon: One Dead in Kyiv, 123 Drones in the Air Force’s Count, and a Colonel Killed in St. Petersburg

The night’s exchange of fire, which the overnight edition filed as explosions heard in one capital and five drones counted down toward the other, carries a Kyiv toll at midday. At least one person was killed in Kyiv and several others injured in a Russian drone attack on Thursday morning, local authorities said, in the departure account above. The Ukrainian Air Force’s count of the night, in the Associated Press account above, is missiles and 123 drones launched, 73 of them jet-powered, with hits recorded at 17 locations. Russia’s Defense Ministry said its air defenses downed 71 Ukrainian drones overnight and that its forces struck a drone-manufacturing plant in Kyiv and three other facilities producing missile fuel and drone components in the area around the capital, in the same account. The five drones downed on the road to Moscow stand where the overnight edition printed them; no account of damage in Moscow stands in the midday file.

Kyiv’s second file of the morning is a satellite service, and the desk prints it as Kyiv’s account, confirmed by no American official in the file. President Zelenskyy said on Thursday that the Starshield system—the military-grade version of the Starlink service, critical to Ukraine’s operations—had faced significant disruptions in recent days, with speed restrictions limiting Ukrainian drones to 161 kilometres per hour, and that a military investigation established the restrictions were not random errors but systematic, in the Associated Press account above and the departure account above. He said Kyiv is trying to establish all the circumstances with the American side. The account in the departure file records that the broadcaster has sought comment from the White House, the Pentagon and the company that owns the service, and that in October Mr. Musk acknowledged the company had throttled the service in Ukraine for about a week. Separately, a Russian serviceman was killed in St. Petersburg early on Thursday when his vehicle exploded, Russia’s Investigative Committee said, without identifying the victim or saying who was behind the bombing; the Fontanka outlet said the victim was a colonel and that a bomb in his car exploded as he drove off, in the Associated Press account above.

Spain at Noon: One Decree Stands by One Vote, the Other Falls by One Vote

Thursday’s Spanish fact is settled, and it is the count the overnight edition published, run in both directions. The standing body of 69 caretaker deputies passed the broad housing decree by a one-vote margin, 35 to 34 on the count in the decree account filed below, and rejected the second measure, also by one vote, in the vote account above. The decree that stands—in force since October 8 and now validated—includes rent caps, a temporary ban on evictions and limits on corporate purchases of residential property, in the vote account above; the decree account’s detail adds a cap on annual increases of 2 percent through the end of 2027 and none where rent stands above the official reference index, extensions of up to two years for leases ending before the end of 2028, suspension of evictions of vulnerable households without an alternative to the end of 2030, room rentals brought under the main tenancy law, seasonal contracts of one to twelve months requiring a provable temporary reason, and a 10 percent value-added tax on tourist rentals. The rejected decree would have renewed rental contracts automatically—automatic five-year renewals for leases past their minimum term, due to start on November 15, in the decree account above. The midday accounts describe the second decree in those terms; the overnight edition described it as compensation for tenants whose owners decline to renew, and the desk prints the midday description as the file’s corrected one, the overnight edition standing unrevised as a log stands. The PNV voted with the government on the first text and against it on the second, beside the PP, Vox and Junts. Mr. Feijóo’s pledge of Wednesday, to repeal the decrees if he governs, stands. The official campaign for the November 29 election opens on Friday, and the housing deficit behind the week stands at about 750,000 homes on the central bank’s figures.

The Spanish parliament’s standing committee, the only body able to legislate while parliament is dismissed for the election, approved one of two housing decrees on Thursday by a one-vote margin while rejecting the other, also by one vote. The caretaker government does not expect to win the snap election called for November 29, and the opposition leader Mr. Feijóo said on Wednesday he would repeal the decrees if he becomes prime minister.

SpainGuru — the Spain housing decree of 2026: what changes

The caretaker Congress validated the broad decree 35 to 34 on November 12 and rejected the separate decree on automatic five-year lease renewals by one vote, the Basque Nationalist Party voting against the second text alongside the PP, Vox and Junts. The validated package, in force since October 8, makes seasonal rentals of one to twelve months require a provable temporary reason, brings room rentals under the main tenancy law with combined room rents capped at the whole-flat rent, extends leases ending before December 31, 2028 by up to two years, caps annual increases at 2 percent until the end of 2027 and at zero where the rent exceeds the official reference index, suspends evictions of vulnerable households without an alternative until the end of 2030, limits some home purchases by companies, funds and large holders until 2030, grants tenants an income-tax deduction of up to one month’s rent, and applies a 10 percent value-added tax to tourist rentals. The rejected decree would have begun automatic five-year renewals for leases past their minimum term on November 15, with fines for landlords who break them.
