The wire account — a return to normal expected on Monday, and 55 of Monday’s flights cancelled as of Sunday evening

United States air travel was expected to return to normal on Monday after three days of widespread disruption following the no-takeoff order issued on Friday. As of Sunday evening, more than 55 flights within, into or out of the United States scheduled for Monday were cancelled, according to the tracker FlightAware, a dramatic shift from the more than 1,960 flights cancelled on Sunday in the same account. The Administrator had said on Saturday that travel would return to normal by the close of business on Sunday; the Secretary said on Sunday morning it would take longer to get planes, crews and travellers where they need to be, and that the system would be probably fully normal on Monday.

The timeline account — 9,000 cancelled on Friday, 144 of Monday’s down as of Sunday afternoon, and the routers at El Dorado and Amarillo

The Administration grounded departing flights at every United States airport on Friday, November 13, from 3:54 p.m. Eastern, with about 3,500 aircraft in the air, until the stop was lifted at 8:15 p.m. About 9,000 flights were cancelled on Friday, roughly 6,400 more on Saturday, and cancellations carried into Sunday, when about 2,300 flights were cancelled and 18,689 delayed, according to FlightAware. Officials say the cause was network equipment, not a cyberattack: routers at Administration sites in El Dorado, Arkansas, and Amarillo, Texas, flooded air traffic control systems with data during a copper-to-fiber upgrade, and the root cause is still being investigated. The Administrator says there is zero evidence of anything nefarious. Cancellations for Monday fell sharply, with 144 reported as of Sunday afternoon, and airlines expect near-normal operations on Monday with occasional knock-on cancellations possible.

Reuters — more than 11,500 flights affected on Sunday, the installation went smoothly in two accounts, and the $50 billion request

United States air travel disruptions continued for a third day with more than 11,500 flights affected on Sunday, but the Transportation Secretary predicted a return to normalcy by Monday as airlines scrambled to get back on schedule following the systemwide ground stop.
