Oil at Noon: A Surplus Found by the Agency, Exports at 74 Percent, and Four Prices Before Dinner in London

The day’s oil fact is not a price; it is a balance, and it reverses the war’s. The International Energy Agency’s November report, in the report filed below, says global supplies surpassed demand for the first time since the outbreak of the war: observed inventories rose by 52 million barrels in October as Gulf exports recovered much faster than demand, supplies surged by 2.3 million barrels a day to 105.4 million a day in October, and fourth-quarter supply estimates were raised by 3.3 million a day. The wire’s account of the report, in the surplus account filed below, puts fourth-quarter supply at an average of 105 million barrels a day, 1.7 million a day above forecast demand, against a deficit forecast a month ago; Gulf exports have rebounded to 74 percent of pre-war levels; 71 million barrels from the emergency-reserve release pledged in March will be made available by the end of February, including 15 million barrels of diesel; demand falls by 2.7 million barrels a day this year to 102.3 million, and the 2027 surplus is put at about 3.8 million a day. The report’s own caution stands in both accounts: the severe tightness in product supply, especially diesel, remains the overriding concern, and attacks on shipping undermine the recovery the balances assume.

The price spent the European morning ignoring the balance in both directions, and the desk prints its prints with their hours. Brent rose 0.6 percent to $96.43 in early European trading, in the European briefing above; Brent fell 37 cents, or 0.4 percent, to $95.45 at 1015 GMT, in the weekly account filed below; and Brent reversed its earlier gains after the agency report at 0900 GMT to stand down 0.1 percent at $95.71, in the surplus account above. West Texas Intermediate prints at $88.89, $88.06 and $88.59 across the same accounts. On the week, both crudes are headed for a second weekly decline of nearly 3 percent, in the weekly account above, after the previous week’s falls of 16 and 13 percent. The market’s reading, in the wire’s sentence, is that the continued blockade eased fears of renewed direct conflict while shipping attacks remained a supply risk. Settled: the surplus, on the agency’s balances. Open: whether the strait lets it stand.
