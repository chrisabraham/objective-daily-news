The Airlines at Midnight: Nearly 6,500 Cancelled on the Recovery Day, Nearly 900 of Sunday’s Already Down, and a Cause With One Question Left in It

The recovery day cancelled more flights than the evening edition’s count, and the midnight count is the day’s last. By 2200 Eastern the carriers had cancelled nearly 6,500 flights on Saturday, in the tracker account above, on the tracker’s data: more than 16,000 flights cancelled since Friday, more than 9,000 of them on Friday alone, and nearly 900 flights for Sunday already cancelled by 2200. The day’s earlier counts stand beneath it as counts at their hours: more than 5,000 cancelled by 1300, in the cause account; more than 4,000 cancelled and 3,000 delayed at the evening count, in the wire’s account filed below; and more than 10,000 delayed or cancelled together on the day, in the newspaper’s account filed below, which also carries the administrator’s estimate of as many as 5,000 of the day’s 41,000 scheduled flights cancelled. No second stop was ordered on Saturday; departures ran all day. The shape of Sunday, at midnight, is uneven and stated plainly in the tracker account: one carrier had cancelled roughly a quarter of its planned Sunday operation by late Saturday night, cancellation rates on the other carriers were trending far lower, and one airline’s officer wrote to customers Saturday evening that the night’s work was positioning crews and aircraft for a strong start. The promise on the record remains the airlines’ own, carried in the newspaper’s account: full schedule integrity by the close of business on Sunday. At midnight that promise is six hundred cancelled flights short of whole and has one day to run.

The cause gained its last fact on Saturday morning and its open question at the same press conference. As many as 17 of the 22 en route centers were affected, and none of them actually went dark, the administrator said, in the cause account: frequencies went in and out, facilities switched between primary and backup, and the stop was ordered when the count of affected facilities reached a size the agency could not explain. About 30 percent of the system’s copper wire has yet to be replaced with fiber. The Secretary’s account of the work stands: routine maintenance at a remote location, a conversion from copper to fiber that has been done across the country for decades, and a router that flooded the network. His unanswered question stands beside it, in his own words: what did the contractor do, did they make mistakes, or was it a faulty router—and the department will find out, and will hold people accountable. The contractor’s statement, in the same account, says it is supporting the investigation and takes the stop and its impact seriously. On attack, the file closed Saturday where Friday left it: zero evidence of anything nefarious, and zero evidence of anything connected to an artificial intelligence tool, in the administrator’s words. The money moved one step: the outgoing Speaker said Saturday that his party will use the remaining 50 days of its majority to authorize more spending on the agency, in the cause account, against the $50 billion ask the Secretary carried into the weekend. Settled: the stop, its mechanism in outline, and Saturday’s cost at nearly 6,500 cancellations. Open: whose error, if anyone’s, and Sunday’s close.

Shenzhen at Midnight: Confirmed in Washington and Moscow, Unconfirmed in Beijing, and an Agenda Argued in Advance

The week’s largest appointment acquired its second confirmation on Saturday and kept its missing third. The Kremlin confirmed the meeting in the spokesman’s words, in the confirmation account above: an agreement has been reached to hold a trilateral meeting on the 19th between President Putin, President Xi and President Trump, in Shenzhen, on the sidelines of the APEC summit—and he gave no agenda. The White House announced the meeting on Friday. No Chinese official has confirmed the three-way meeting on the record, in the same account, and the host’s official word in the wire’s account filed below is that it is in communication with relevant parties regarding a meeting between the three heads of state, and no more. The itinerary stands in the itinerary account filed below: the President leaves Washington on Monday, attends the ASEAN summit in the Philippines on Tuesday, and holds a working lunch and bilateral talks with President Xi on Thursday, their fourth meeting since he returned to office, with the trilateral as the final event of the trip; no separate meeting with President Putin is planned, and export controls on advanced chips will not be discussed, American officials said. The APEC leaders’ meeting itself runs November 18 and 19 in Shenzhen, with President Xi in the chair.

The agenda is stated three ways, and the desk prints them apart. Washington’s version: nuclear arms at the top, the war in Iran to be raised, and Ukraine not on the agenda, because those talks run on a separate track—with the senior official’s standing sentence that the United States does not expect to get to any kind of agreement, in the wire’s account and the European account filed below. Moscow’s version: the Kremlin says President Putin intends to speak to President Trump in Shenzhen about efforts to end the war in Ukraine, in the European account, and his aide said on November 12 that the settlement would be discussed with President Trump in Shenzhen, in the confirmation account—against the American official’s statement that a separate meeting between the two presidents was not expected. Beijing’s version is its academics’, carried in the wire’s account: three-way nuclear talks are a nonstarter for China, whose stated position, in the vacuum account filed below, is that it is neither fair, reasonable nor realistic to ask China to join a replacement for the expired treaty. The vacuum is a fact and not a metaphor: the New START treaty, the last pact limiting the two largest arsenals, expired on February 5, and the Secretary-General of the United Nations said the two countries were then without binding limits for the first time in more than half a century, in the European account. The arsenals themselves stand apart by source, in the Cocked Hat. Settled: the meeting, its date, its place, and two of its three confirmations. Open: the host’s word, the agenda, and whether Ukraine is discussed in a room Washington says it will not be discussed in.

Dublin and the Front at Midnight: A Membership Proposal Stated in Kyiv, a Demands Paper Reported From Abu Dhabi, and No Date for a Next Round

The talks file’s new sentences on Saturday came from Kyiv, and they concern what was proposed in Abu Dhabi, not what happens next. President Zelenskyy stated on November 14 that the American delegation at the November 10 and 11 talks proposed that Ukraine gain NATO membership in exchange for a Ukrainian withdrawal from the entirety of the Donbas region, and that he would hold a referendum on any future war termination stipulations should the United States and NATO support such proposals, in the Institute’s November 14 assessment filed below. Against that proposal stands the other side’s paper, as reported: an unofficial paper of demands circulated by the Russian delegation at Abu Dhabi, reported by a newspaper on November 13 and by a German outlet the same day, in the same assessment—the alliance to pull back its forces and bases from every state that joined since 1997, to cease expansion, and to refrain from military activity in Eastern Europe, the South Caucasus and Central Asia; France and the United Kingdom to refrain from using their nuclear weapons to protect other European states; and Ukraine’s membership in the European Union approved, but not its participation in the bloc’s common defense policy. Neither document is an agreement; both are positions, and the distance between a membership offered for the Donbas and a paper demanding the alliance withdraw to its 1997 lines is the distance the next round, whenever it sits, has to cross. Europe’s place in that round stands where the evening edition left it: the team will sit as national security advisers at the technical level, in the advisers account filed below; the Kremlin will decide when Europe’s intentions are clear, in its spokesman’s position; and the subject was never discussed in Moscow at all, in the Foreign Ministry account filed below. The war under the diplomacy ran on Saturday at its stated tempo: 47 attacks in the Dobropillya direction on November 13 and 14 in the Ukrainian General Staff’s count, a disputed proposal for a local ceasefire at Kostyantynivka, a sixth day of satellite speed restrictions reported by Kyiv, and 96 drones launched at Ukraine overnight, all in the Institute’s assessment. The prisoner exchange agreed in principle at Abu Dhabi still carries no number in any account, and no date or place for a next round stands in the file at midnight.

France at Midnight: 219 Arrests at the Close, 106,000 in the Ministry’s Count, and a Strike Called for Tuesday

France’s fifth Saturday closed with its final numbers, and they are smaller than the fourth Saturday’s. The Interior Ministry’s closing count stands at 219 arrests across the country, in the agency account at the top of this report—against 121 at 1800 in the evening edition’s file—with incidents in Marseille, Grenoble and Lyon, bins set alight and objects thrown at police, and the ministry’s judgment that the situation remained manageable overall. The turnout stands apart by counter, as it has every Saturday: 106,000 demonstrators nationwide in the ministry’s count, 24,000 of them in Paris, across 137 events, against about 200,000 in the organizers’ count, in the same account. The count account filed below adds the day’s detail: 10 police officers slightly injured, and 52 of the arrests in Nantes alone, nearly a quarter of the national total. The movement’s direction is single-valued in both counts: down from 146,000 in the ministry’s count the Saturday before, and down from 565,000 a month ago, in the agency account. Its origin stands as the file has carried it: a school movement demanding staff, funding and repairs, joined in mid-October by the cost-of-living protests. The next date is not a Saturday: the unions have called a strike for Tuesday, in the agency account. Settled: the fifth Saturday, its size, and its direction. Open: whether a weekday strike carries what the Saturdays are losing.

A newspaper reported November 13 that it obtained an unofficial paper of demands the Russian delegation circulated at Abu Dhabi: the alliance to pull back forces and bases from all states that joined since 1997, to cease expansion and to refrain from military activity in Eastern Europe, the South Caucasus and Central Asia; France and the United Kingdom to refrain from using their nuclear weapons to protect other European states; and Ukraine’s membership in the European Union approved, but not participation in its common defense policy. The assessment also records a disputed proposal for a local ceasefire at Kostyantynivka, mechanized assaults in the Dobropillya direction, a sixth day of speed restrictions on a satellite network over parts of Ukraine, and 96 drones launched at Ukraine overnight. A White House spokeswoman stated November 13 that the American delegation ensured Europe will be included in future talks, and a report the same day designated the national security advisers of Germany, France, the United Kingdom, Italy and Poland and a representative of the Union to represent Europe at the technical level.
