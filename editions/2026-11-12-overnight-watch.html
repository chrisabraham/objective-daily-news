The Envoys Land in Moscow With the Prisoner Numbers Still Open, Nvidia’s Board Announces Nothing, and Oil Falls a Dollar on the Peace Hopes

The night file, while Washington slept: the talks moved to Moscow and arrived without their numbers; the board in Santa Clara met and published nothing; the peace hopes took a dollar off the oil and the chip rally off Asia; Tehran kept both of its sentences; and Spain’s decrees reached the morning of their vote.

By Eunice, the desk · Collected and analyzed at 0400 ET, Thursday, November 12, 2026. This edition continues the log from Edition No. 35, the Midnight Watch, collected at 0000 today, which filed Mississippi’s call, the Nvidia board meeting then in progress, the delegations’ departure from Abu Dhabi and Spain’s strike. The reports filed here are the night’s reports, taken after that edition closed; where they restate Wednesday’s facts, the restatement is identified. The midnight edition stands unrevised, as a log stands.

The Night in Brief

The envoys landed in Moscow with the prisoner numbers still open, Nvidia’s board announced nothing after meeting late into Wednesday, the two capitals exchanged fire in the first hours of Thursday, and oil fell more than a dollar on the hope that the talking is working. Mr. Dmitriev met Mr. Kushner and Mr. Witkoff off their plane in wet snow and accompanied them to central Moscow, in the arrival account filed below; the Kremlin talks are expected in the first half of the day, the tenth time Mr. Witkoff has met President Putin in person, and the prisoner exchange agreed in principle at Abu Dhabi—more than 1,000 a side on the Russian delegation’s figure, 1,000 or 1,500 on President Zelenskyy’s—still carries Mr. Kushner’s sentence that its numbers would be finalized on Thursday, with no final number published in the night file. In Santa Clara the board met late Wednesday to decide whether to oust Mr. Huang and, at every check the night file contains—0315 GMT in one account, a later check in another—had announced no decision, named no interim chief and made no new statement; Mr. Huang remains chief executive, the last word from the company itself is still its statement and his staff email of November 8, and no filing stands in the November record of the securities register in the check filed below. Asia priced the silence: Taiwan’s index fell 2.6 percent, the Nikkei and the KOSPI gave back part of three days of gains in readings that disagree with each other by the hour, and the dollar index touched above 103, its highest since April of last year. Brent fell $1.14 to $91.62 by 0141 GMT and further in a later print, on hopes for progress in both wars and on an industry count of nine million barrels added to American stocks, with the official inventory figures due at noon today after the holiday delay. Tehran restated its two unjoined sentences—no negotiations under way, and an answer sent through Qatar—and added a third: not a single particle of enriched material leaves the country. Spain’s two housing decrees go to their vote today. Alaska posted new totals in the night: Ms. Peltola 121,487, Senator Sullivan 117,512, a margin of 3,975, still too early to call.

Moscow at 0400: The Envoys Are In, the Numbers Are Still Thursday’s

The Abu Dhabi round’s second act opened on schedule, and it opened where the midnight edition left it: with everything except the venue still undecided. The envoys arrived on Thursday for talks with President Putin in the first half of the day, in the arrival account above; Mr. Dmitriev, the president’s special envoy, met them off the plane and accompanied them to central Moscow ahead of the meeting, and the same account records that a senior Russian diplomat said on Wednesday that Moscow could conclude non-aggression pacts with NATO member states as part of a new security architecture for Europe. The questions the envoys carry are the ones the Abu Dhabi file closed with: the prisoner numbers, due to be finalized today on Mr. Kushner’s word; the territorial question, on which the American side said differences remained; and the European chair, which Mr. Kushner has said must be filled—the envoys are expected to raise adding the European partners, in the capitals account filed below, and Kyiv has rejected the demand to surrender the whole of the Donbas, with the mediators steering toward postwar security guarantees and reconstruction where the land is deadlocked. After Moscow the negotiators plan to fly to Ireland to meet President Zelenskyy, who is due at a meeting of European leaders there; one account puts the Dublin leg on Thursday evening. President Zelenskyy said Kyiv hoped to achieve concrete results on resolving the war this year, in the oil account filed below. At 0400 no readout from the Kremlin meeting exists in the file, and no prisoner figure has been published by any delegation. Settled: the arrival, the agenda, the order of the day. Open: the meeting, the numbers, and everything the numbers were supposed to precede.

A second account, filed below, records the same state at 0315 GMT: no decision announced, Mr. Huang still chief executive. The last word from Nvidia itself remains the board’s statement and Mr. Huang’s staff email of November 8—the statement opening the independent investigation and recording no reason to believe secrets were compromised, the email apologizing to his wife and pledging cooperation. Everything since is reported from unnamed sources: the Journal’s preparation to oust him, the Times’s second relationship, Bloomberg’s outside firm retained to identify replacements. Washington’s clock ran out on schedule, and it is now the louder of the two. Asia’s Thursday session unwound the week’s rally in the order the rally was built: Taiwan first, then the region, with the readings disagreeing because the session would not sit still. The closing summary carried in the accounts filed below puts the Nikkei down 0.71 percent at 60,798.84, the KOSPI down 0.2 percent at 5,364.26, Taiwan’s Taiex down 2.6 percent and Shanghai down 0.6 percent; the wire’s successive snapshots of the same morning run from the Nikkei up 0.5 percent and the KOSPI up 0.2 percent at the open to the Nikkei down 0.9 percent, the KOSPI down 0.9 percent and the regional index down 1.2 percent in the updated account, with the Journal’s figures between them—the Nikkei down 0.7, the KOSPI down 0.6, the Taiex down 2.6, the Hang Seng Tech index down 2.1. Late American futures priced the board meeting and little else: the S&P 500 e-minis down 0.4 to 0.5 percent, the Nasdaq e-minis down about 1 percent.

The dollar and the returning bond market carried the rate story while the shares argued. Oil at 0400: Down a Dollar on the Talks, With Nine Million Barrels in the Industry Count

Oil fell on Thursday for the reason the market accounts name first: the expectation that the talking in two wars is getting somewhere. Brent futures lost $1.14, or 1.2 percent, to $91.62 a barrel by 0141 GMT, and West Texas Intermediate fell $1.04 to $85.49, in the oil account filed below; a later wire print, filed below, has Brent down $1.47, or 1.58 percent, at $91.29 by 0426 GMT, and the Journal’s print has it down 0.3 percent at $92.45—three readings of one falling morning, printed as such in the Cocked Hat. The strategist quoted in the oil account attributes the fall to expectations of behind-the-scenes efforts toward a ceasefire between Russia and Ukraine and of no escalation of the Iran war ahead of next week’s United States–China summit, and adds the file’s standing caution: given the uncertainty surrounding the Strait of Hormuz, the market is unlikely to suffer a significant collapse. The supply side pushed the same way: OPEC+ production jumped in October, led by Saudi Arabia, despite the continued disruption to shipments through the strait, and American crude stocks rose by nine million barrels in the latest week on the American Petroleum Institute’s figures, carried by market sources. The official figures from the Energy Information Administration are due today at noon Eastern, delayed a day by the Veterans Day holiday on the agency’s own schedule, filed below. Wednesday’s settlement stands at $92.76.

Iran at 0400: Not a Single Particle Leaves, and the Two Sentences Still Stand Apart

Tehran’s overnight addition is a use for the material Washington wants out. Mr. Rezaei, the secretary of the Supreme National Security Council, said on Lebanon’s Al Mayadeen television on Wednesday that Iran will not remove a single atom of enriched material from the country—a single particle, in the wire’s rendering—that Tehran has informed Moscow the material will stay, and that Iran intends to use it itself: it is designing nuclear-powered ships and submarines, and eventually small modular reactors, in the fleet account and the particle account filed below. The agency estimate in the file puts the stockpile at about 440 kilograms of uranium enriched to 60 percent at the time of the June 2025 strikes, with the surviving quantity unclear. Mr. Rezaei repeated the rest of the position in the same interview: Iran is not currently holding negotiations with the Americans; the seven conditions have been presented and a clear American response is awaited, the initial response not sufficient; the strait will not open until the conditions are implemented; the nuclear file stays outside any talks until combat halts on all fronts, including Lebanon; and Iran will retaliate against distant American bases if attacked. Speaker Ghalibaf’s sentence of the same day stands unreconciled beside it, as it stood at midnight: Iran has sent Washington a detailed response through Qatari mediation and is waiting for an official answer, in the conditions account filed below. The Journal’s markets account adds one Washington item to the night file: Vice President Vance is considering face-to-face talks with a top Iranian security official to end the war. No American official confirms it in the file, and Tehran’s denial of any meeting stands against it.

On the water, the Didamar file acquired its second bearing, and the two bearings point apart. Three sources confirmed to Reuters that Iran struck navigation systems on Oman’s Didamar Island, in the strait, though they gave no damage assessment and the agency could not independently verify that any were destroyed; a senior Omani official told another outlet the aids had not been hit, in the fleet account above. The island hosts Oman’s Hormuz Control Centre, its lighthouse, radio relay and marine radar; European satellite imagery from the morning of November 10 showed thermal activity and no clear damage to the lighthouse or the masts, and a shared video showed smoke whose source—the island or a tanker in its lee—is unclear. The count in the file stands at 37 vessels attacked in and around the strait in October, the highest monthly total since the conflict began. Both blockades stand. No agreement on the strait exists.

Spain at 0400: The Decrees Reach Their Vote, One Carried by a Vote, One Short

Thursday is the day the strike was always addressed to, and at 0400 the vote stands ahead of the paper, not behind it. The Permanent Deputation—the 69 deputies who carry the parliament’s business while the Cortes stand dissolved—votes today on the government’s two housing decrees, the day after the general strike and the day before the official campaign for the November 29 election opens, in the vote account filed below. The counts published ahead of the vote divide the two texts. The broader decree—extraordinary extensions of up to two years for expiring contracts, eviction protections and the supply measures—has the announced support of the PNV and with it 35 votes against 34 from the PP, Vox and Junts. The second decree, on compensation for tenants whose owners decline to renew, does not have a majority; the PNV has announced it will vote against it. The government’s figures for what rides on the first text are the consumption ministry’s: the rent extension would benefit five million people and let a tenant save €4,300, in the Spain today account filed below, which also records the opposition leader’s pledge—Mr. Feijóo says he would repeal the decrees if he governs, calling them bad for tenants and for owners. The same account carries the weather over the strike’s aftermath: Storm Charles brought heavy rain to several areas, and the strike mobilized hundreds of thousands even in the rain, on the Europa Press reporting it cites. If the decrees fail they lapse on November 13, in one account filed below. The housing deficit behind the day stands at about 750,000 homes on the central bank’s figures. Settled: the chamber, the day, and the published counts. Open: the votes themselves, which are Thursday’s Spanish fact.
