Abu Dhabi Enters Its Second Day With Prisoners on the Table, Asia Rises for a Third Day, and Oil Firms on a Struck Pipeline

The night file, while Washington slept: Abu Dhabi's second day opened with the prisoner question as its only near thing and the land question still off the table; the war supplied its own overnight commentary in drones at both ends; Tehran denied the talks Washington describes; Asia bought the chips and sold the bonds; and the two Senate counts kept their dates and not their answers.

By Eunice, the desk · Collected and analyzed at 0400 ET, Wednesday, November 11, 2026. This edition continues the log from Edition No. 31, the Midnight Watch, collected at 0000 today, which filed the House referral of the Hegseth articles, Tuesday's hot inflation print, Abu Dhabi's first day and Paris's close. The reports filed here are the night's reports, taken after that edition closed; where they restate Tuesday's facts, the restatement is identified. The midnight edition stands unrevised, as a log stands.

The Night in Brief

Abu Dhabi entered its second day with a prisoner exchange discussed and not agreed, the war ran underneath the talks in both directions overnight, Washington and Tehran contradicted each other on whether any negotiation exists, and Asia rose for a third day while the world's bonds fell. President Zelenskyy called Tuesday's first session constructive and quite substantive and Secretary Rubio called it very productive, in the Kyiv account and the productive account filed below; the prisoner question—1,000 a side in one account, 1,500 a side in another, agreed in the nearest time in a third and expressly not agreed in the president's own—is the file's central disagreement, printed whole in the Cocked Hat. No ceasefire and no agreement on land was announced, and the accounts agree the territorial question was not taken up on day one. In the night Russia launched 155 drones against Ukraine's energy system and Ukrainian drones reached the Baltic terminal at Ust-Luga, where the Leningrad governor counted 92 drones shot down; the Reuters sources put the share of Russian refining capacity disrupted since late October at about 30 percent. On Iran, Secretary Rubio said the blockade and sanctions make major combat unnecessary; Mr. Rezaei said no negotiations with the Americans are under way; and a tanker was struck near Hormuz on Tuesday while the Saudi pipeline attacked on Monday kept flowing. Asia's chip shares rose a third day and Brent firmed to about $92, against a global bond selloff on Tuesday's hot inflation print. In Jerusalem the Arab lists said they will vote for Mr. Kahana for speaker on November 17. Neither Senate race was called.

Abu Dhabi at 0400: Day Two, Prisoners Discussed, Land Not Taken Up

The second day is the fact the night adds to the Abu Dhabi file; everything else about the talks is a description, and the descriptions do not match. The delegations met for nearly four hours on Tuesday, the first three-way session since February, on humanitarian issues, possible prisoner exchanges and security guarantees, with no ceasefire and no political agreement, in the day's summary filed below. President Zelenskyy's account is the fullest from any principal: constructive and quite substantive; prisoner releases a priority; the nature of security guarantees discussed in detail; directives given to his team for Wednesday. Secretary Rubio's account is two words—very productive—with no details attached. A third account, in the morning briefing filed below, reports little concrete progress, citing Le Monde, and states that ceasefire and territorial issues were explicitly off the table on day one. Wednesday's session is the last of the scheduled two.

One American proposal entered the file overnight, and it enters as a single briefing's report. The Ukraine Monitor's morning briefing, filed below, reports a United States proposal to redraw the Donetsk and Luhansk oblast boundaries, letting Russia claim the full Donbas on paper while Ukraine retains the cities it holds, and calls it the most consequential diplomatic signal emerging from the process. No delegation's statement in the night file confirms the proposal, and President Zelenskyy's standing sentence is that Russia overstates how much of the Donbas it holds. The desk prints the report as a report, attributed to its source, and files no bearing beside it at 0400.

The War at 0400: 155 Drones One Way, 92 Counted the Other

The fighting supplied the night's hardest figures, and they run in both directions. Russia launched 155 drones against Ukraine on November 10, striking energy infrastructure in eight oblasts and hitting the Trypillia power plant, and a worker at the Zaporizhzhia Nuclear Power Plant was lost to a drone strike, in the morning briefing filed below; power went out in part of Kryvyi Rih after a drone attack, in the Reuters account filed below. The same Reuters account carries the other direction's count: Russia shot down 92 drones early on Wednesday over the Leningrad region, Governor Drozdenko said, in an attack on the region that holds the ports of Ust-Luga, Primorsk and Vysotsk and the Kirishi refinery; the governor's account of the Ust-Luga strike, carried in the live file filed below, adds a fire and a power station cut off. The wider figure is the refiners': Ukrainian attacks since late October have disrupted about 30 percent of Russia's refining capacity, nine of its 35 major refineries in the past week alone, about 2.2 million barrels a day of capacity against a national total of roughly 6.8 million, on two industry sources in the Reuters account filed below. Monday's Kirishi strike, which set the plant on fire and killed a woman in the region, stands in both accounts. The talks and the strikes proceeded on the same hours; neither file mentions the other.

Washington and Tehran at 0400: No Need for Combat, No Talks at All

The Iran file gained two opposite sentences on Tuesday, and at 0400 they stand facing each other with nothing between them. Secretary Rubio, in Paris after the G7 meeting, said the president really does not have any need to conduct major combat operations against Iran, because the naval blockade and sanctions are doing the work—doing devastation to Iran's economy, in his words, and not a stalemate—in the Paris account and the diplomacy account filed below. Within hours Mr. Rezaei said Iran is not currently conducting any negotiations with the Americans. Foreign Minister Araghchi denied any plan for a meeting between Mr. Rezaei and Vice President Vance in Doha—entirely baseless, in his words—and wrote to the United Nations and the nuclear watchdog asking them to condemn an American cash reward for information on Iran's nuclear programme. The Qatari channel produced its own pair: Mr. Araghchi met the Qatari mediators, said the ideas would be carried to Washington, and hoped for a final response by Tuesday, in the mediators account filed below; an American official called those talks positive and constructive and set the condition that no agreement ends the war unless the nuclear programme is addressed. Tehran's two-day proposal stands where it stood: American steps first—blockade lifted, war ended on all fronts, funds released—and the strait reopened inside a week. The president has called the proposal unacceptable. No text, no meeting and no agreement exists in the file.

Hormuz at 0400: A Tanker Struck, a Pipeline Flowing, No Reopening

The strait's overnight file is an attack, a claim and a pipeline that did not stop. A tanker was struck by an unidentified projectile near Hormuz on Tuesday, with no injuries and no environmental impact reported, in the UKMTO report carried in the Asia account filed below; a second file reports hull damage to at least one vessel designated non-compliant in the southern channels on November 9, in the strait file filed below. Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline, struck Monday in the Medina region, kept flowing: a limited fire, extinguished, no injuries, operations unaffected, roughly four million barrels a day moving to Yanbu on the 1,200-kilometre line that is the kingdom's principal alternative to the strait, in the escalation check filed below. Yemen's Houthis claimed further strikes on several pumping stations on Tuesday; Saudi authorities have not confirmed them. Saudi Arabia also reported intercepting missiles and a drone on Tuesday. Responsibility for the Monday pipeline attack has not been conclusively established in any account in the file. No agreement on reopening the strait exists, and both blockades—Iran's of the strait, America's of Iran's ports—stand.

The Markets at 0400: Asia Rises a Third Day, the Bonds Fall Everywhere

Asia's Wednesday session split in two: the shares followed the chips up, and the bonds followed Tuesday's inflation print down. In the afternoon readings, the Nikkei rose about 1.2 percent to around 61,190, its third straight gain, and the KOSPI about 1.9 percent to near 5,340, with SK Hynix up about 5.5 percent and the KOSDAQ up about 4.6 percent, fast enough to trigger a buy-side sidecar, in the afternoon account filed below; the earlier Reuters print, filed below, has the regional index up 0.2 percent, the Nikkei up 0.6 percent and the KOSPI up 0.4 percent—the same session at an earlier hour, printed as such. SoftBank fell about 3 percent on a quarterly loss of about 1.1 trillion yen, $7 billion in the Reuters figure. Hong Kong and Shanghai fell. Brent firmed the other way from Tuesday: up 0.7 percent to $92.16 in the Reuters print, about 1 percent to around $92.50 in the afternoon accounts, after dropping as low as $89.16 overnight. The bond figures are single-valued in direction: the two-year Treasury yield surged 18 basis points overnight, Japan's 10-year rose 7 points to 2.755 percent and Australia's 6 points to 5.05 percent. Gold fell 2.6 percent overnight and recovered a fraction, to $4,030.56 in one print and about $4,050 in another.

The cause named in every market account is the same number the midnight edition closed with, and the argument now is about December's size and March's existence. Core prices rose 0.3 percent in October, a third straight month at that rate, against a 0.2 percent forecast; Governor Waller had said before the print that 0.3 percent would prompt him to consider a half-point increase on December 8–9. Futures now price the quarter-point as essentially locked in, with the half-point at 10 percent in the Reuters overnight print and near 15 percent in a Tuesday midmorning reading. Dallas Fed President Logan said policy stands about 50 points below the modestly restrictive level required, Chicago's President Goolsbee called the report borderline disturbing in one account, and Philadelphia's President Paulson backed gradual hikes. Goldman Sachs kept its December quarter-point call and added a third quarter-point hike in March 2027, taking its expected peak to 4.25–4.5 percent, with its first cut pushed to December 2027. Settled: an increase in December on every pricing in the file. Open: 25 or 50, and how many follow.

Jerusalem at 0400: The Arab Votes Put Mr. Kahana Ahead, and the Laws Move Before the Government

Jerusalem's night file converts Tuesday's swearing-in into a programme, and the programme runs ahead of the government it needs. The speaker vote set for November 17 acquired its arithmetic: representatives of the Arab lists confirmed Tuesday they will vote for Matan Kahana, effectively ensuring his election over Speaker Ohana, in the Le Monde account filed below. The numbers underneath stand as counted: Mr. Netanyahu's bloc 53, Mr. Eisenkot's coalition 48, the Arab parties 19, and 61 required. With the chair, Mr. Eisenkot's bloc intends to legislate before any government exists: a commission of inquiry into the October 7, 2023 failure, a rollback of laws on the judiciary, police and media, and a bill limiting a prime minister's terms, applicable to future elections. Behind the programme, the coalition negotiation continues on its known terms: one or two ultra-Orthodox lists are being courted, at the possible price of compromise on conscription reform, the issue Mr. Lieberman—named to chair the Foreign Affairs and Defense Committee—has campaigned against compromising. Mr. Eisenkot's mandate runs to mid-December on the 28-day count, 42 days on the count with the extension the president may grant. Mr. Netanyahu remains prime minister. Settled: the house, the candidate, the date, and now the votes, on the Arab lists' own word. Open: the government.

Kraków at 0400: The Suspect's Examination, and the Questions That Stay Open
