The Midnight in Brief: What Monday Settled, What It Left Open

Monday settled the president’s word, the markets’ close and a sentence in London, and it left the war it described, the coalition it requires and the count it awaits all open, and the desk files the day in that order. The evening edition closed with no readout from the Oval Office; by midnight a readout exists, and it is the president’s own. In a Truth Social post and then in a surprise appearance in the briefing room, President Trump said “Iran, militarily, is effectively over,” that there is a “good chance” of a deal, that Tehran is “talking very politely,” and that renewed strikes are “quite unlikely,” in the Washington account and the running account filed below. The same sentences keep the naval blockade and Operation Iron Gate in place and keep American forces “locked and loaded.” Tehran answered in two voices: President Pezeshkian’s, that Iran will negotiate with dignity and not surrender to force, and Mohsen Rezaei’s, that the talks are imaginary. No agreement, no text and no Iranian confirmation exists in the file. In New York the close stood—the S&P 500’s best day since April—and after settlement Brent fell more than seven dollars to its lowest since late August. In Poland the national mourning ended with no ideology established for the dead gunman. In Jerusalem a speaker from Yashar was agreed, and one partner declined the replacement at Tuesday’s opening. In London Mr. Brand was sentenced to ten years and six months. Neither Senate race was called. Tuesday holds the Abu Dhabi table, the inflation report, the Alaska count, the Mississippi deadline, the Knesset’s first sitting and the secretary of defense’s press conference.

Washington at Midnight: A Readout at Last, and It Is the President’s Own

The meeting that produced no word by 1800 produced, by midnight, the day’s largest sentence, and the desk files it as a statement, not a settlement. Qatar’s prime minister presented Tehran’s two-day proposal in Washington, met the president and the vice president, and wrote afterward that he was grateful for positive discussions on reaching a deal, in the Reuters and Al Jazeera account filed below. The president’s readout names Qatar, Saudi Arabia, the Emirates, Bahrain and Kuwait as preferring the status quo—continuation of the naval blockade and of Operation Iron Gate, land and sea—and records agreement, in his words, that Iran cannot have a nuclear weapon. None of the governments he named has confirmed that account in the file. A diplomat briefed on the meeting, cited by one reporter and unnamed, said the president told the Qatari prime minister in private that he wants a deal but could reverse course if Tehran is not serious; that description rests on a single unnamed person. Secretary Rubio’s position stands unmoved: negotiators are not on the verge of a breakthrough, and Tehran’s demands are outrageous and not realistic. Settled: what the president said, in public, twice. Open: whether any other party says the same.

Tehran at Midnight: Dignity From the President, “Imaginary Talks” From the Security Council

Iran answered Monday in two registers, and midnight is the hour to print them side by side without joining them. President Pezeshkian said the Leader had instructed officials to end the state of neither war nor peace, that Iran can negotiate with dignity, and that it will not surrender to force, in the Tehran account filed below. Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of the Supreme National Security Council, wrote that Washington should fold its hand and “stop manufacturing fake news about imaginary talks,” and accept the seven conditions Iran has repeatedly set out, in the conditions account filed below. Three of the seven are named publicly: an end to the war on all fronts, the release of frozen funds and an end to the naval blockade. Tehran says it will not reopen Hormuz to normal traffic until the conditions are met. The two capitals describe the same evening in opposite words—momentum in Washington, imaginary talks in Tehran—and the blockade both sides invoke is the one physical fact they share.

Kraków at Midnight: The National Mourning Ends, the Limit Stands

Poland’s file closed Monday where its prosecutors placed it: with a death, a limit and an investigation that continues without the man it concerns. The suspect died at about 0430 after at least three operations; autopsies of the nine victims began Monday and will take about two days; fifteen wounded remain in hospital, fourteen stable and one officer in serious condition. Prosecutor’s spokesman Przemysław Nowak said investigators do not have findings that would let them state responsibly that the gunman was inspired by any particular ideology, and that no obvious symbols associated with a particular political movement have been found, in the prosecutors’ account filed below. Those sentences stand against the interior minister’s weekend reading, which stands unwithdrawn; the desk prints the limit as the newest bearing. The Cocked Hat

Three bearings never give a point; they give a small triangle, and this paper prints the triangle. On the war’s state, the president’s effectively over, the secretary of state’s not on the verge of a breakthrough, President Pezeshkian’s negotiate with dignity, and Mr. Rezaei’s imaginary talks—four bearings, no two the same, and no agreement beneath any of them. On the private meeting, one unnamed diplomat’s account against the public record. On the close, the two prints differing in the third figure, the rally single-valued and its exact size not. On Monday the American Special Envoy to Iraq, Tom Barrack, condemned in the strongest possible terms the missile attack targeting the Khor Mor gas field in the Kurdistan Region. On Sunday evening the field, in Chamchamal district of Sulaymaniyah Governorate, was subjected to an attack by a suicide drone; a massive fire followed and production and supply operations halted completely. The operator Dana Gas announced that emergency teams had extinguished the fire, with no human casualties reported, and that all production and supply operations remain temporarily suspended pending a full assessment of damage and verification of safety.
