The Midnight in Brief: What Wednesday Settled, What It Left Open

Wednesday settled its longest count, its holiday and its strike, and it left its newest crisis—a board meeting in California—undecided at midnight, and the desk files the day in that order. The Count at Midnight: Mississippi Called at 1758, a Concession Within the Hour, and Alaska Alone

The count that began with 43 votes ended with a call and a concession, and both are Wednesday’s facts. The Associated Press called the race at 5:58 p.m. Eastern, in the wire account, the call account and the totals account filed below, which agree on the hour. NBC News and CNN’s decision desks projected the same winner separately, and Reuters reported that Senator Hyde-Smith had lost her re-election bid. The unofficial totals the accounts publish stand at 473,336 votes, 49.2 percent, for Mr. Colom, 468,667, 48.7 percent, for Senator Hyde-Smith, and 20,234, 2.1 percent, for the independent candidate, Mr. Pinkins—a margin of 4,669 votes out of nearly a million cast. The wire’s published explanation, carried in the explanation account filed below, says the decisive update came from Jackson County, which reported another 2,173 votes, and estimates fewer than 12,000 ballots still requiring county review. Less than an hour after the call, Senator Hyde-Smith conceded: she telephoned Mr. Colom, on his campaign’s account, and then posted a video captioned “Thank you, Mississippi,” saying, in the concession account filed below, “While this is not the outcome we worked for or hoped for, I respect the decision of Mississippi voters.”

What the call makes of the Senate is arithmetic. Mr. Colom’s is the Democrats’ eighth pickup of the midterms; the caucus that convenes on January 3 stands at 55, and Alaska decides whether it stands at 55 or 56. The man carries two firsts into the chamber, both on the record of the accounts filed below: he is Mississippi’s first Black senator since Reconstruction, and the first Democrat the state has elected to the Senate in 44 years; the senator he defeated is the senator who blocked his nomination to a federal judgeship in 2023, in the history account filed below. The result remains unofficial: counties submit certified results by Friday, November 13, and the state certifies on December 3, and Mississippi law carries no automatic recount. The margins the day printed on its way to the call—3,041 at the evening’s tally, 4,404 on Wednesday afternoon in one account, 4,645 on Wednesday night in another, and 4,585 in the at-call totals one program published—stand in the Cocked Hat against the wire’s 4,669.

Alaska is now the whole of the Senate file. Ms. Peltola leads Senator Sullivan by 4,250 first-choice votes—118,926, or 49 percent, to 114,676, or 47.2 percent—on Tuesday’s update, and no new count posted on Wednesday. One account puts 84 percent counted and about 40,000 ballots remaining; another puts the remainder at about 46,000. The next count is scheduled for Friday, November 13; if no candidate passes 50 percent of the first choices, the ranked-choice tabulation runs on November 18, and the target certification date is November 25. The legal file stands where the week left it: a judge dismissed the administration’s suit over certification, and the Justice Department appealed, in the count account filed below. In the House, the wire’s results page for Florida’s 27th District carries the status Called for Representative Salazar, 124,519 votes to Mr. Rodriguez’s 123,433 after the machine recount, in the results page filed below; the canvassing board certifies on Friday and the state on November 17, in the recount account filed below. More than a dozen House races stood too early to call at the evening. Settled: Mississippi, and the size of everything except Alaska and the House margin. Open: one seat, and the paperwork.

Nvidia at Midnight: The Close Reversed, the Evening Reversed Back, and a Board Out Late

The day’s largest movement in any file came after the bell, in a company whose earnings are not due until Tuesday. Nvidia closed the regular session at $173.73, up 2.92 percent on the figures the evening edition printed, as the instrument of the chip rally that reversed Tuesday whole. About an hour after the closing bell, The Wall Street Journal reported that the company’s board is preparing to oust its chief executive and co-founder, Mr. Huang; minutes earlier in the evening’s sequence, The New York Times reported that Mr. Huang had also had a relationship with a company executive who reports to him, identified in one account of the Times report as the vice president of health care. The shares fell about 8 percent in after-hours trading in the wire account and the futures account filed below, 8.6 percent to $158.76 by 1959 ET in the price account filed below, and more than 9 percent in the ouster account filed below; all three figures stand in the Cocked Hat. A second wire service, CBS News, citing two sources, also reported the board preparing to oust him, and the board was meeting late Wednesday to decide, on a source familiar with the matter in the Reuters account above. Bloomberg is reported to have said the company has been working with an outside firm to identify possible replacements and is considering a temporary chief. At midnight the file holds no decision, no interim appointment and no company statement: the running file’s last update before this edition closed, stamped 0043 GMT, records none announced, in the running account filed below. Mr. Huang remains the chief executive. The allegations in this file are allegations, printed as the accounts print them; no finding has been made by the board, by any committee or by any court.

The record the board is deciding on is a week old and is printed here once, in order. On Sunday Mr. Huang admitted a relationship with a woman whom American officials suspect of working with Chinese intelligence, in the pressure account filed below. On November 8 the board opened an independent investigation and retained a former federal prosecutor, Mr. Kim, and the firm Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz to lead it. On Monday Bloomberg reported that the company’s general counsel had intervened in the Commerce Department’s investigation into Megaspeed, its largest Southeast Asian customer, asking whether American authorities were involved in the woman’s detention in Singapore. The stock fell 9.5 percent across Monday and Tuesday to $168.89. On Capitol Hill the pressure ran on its own track: Senator Cramer of North Dakota repeated his demand that Mr. Huang resign, calling his actions a national security risk; Representative Moolenaar, who chairs the House Select Committee on China, gave Mr. Huang until the end of Wednesday to answer a request for a closed-door briefing, with a subpoena likely if he did not agree; and a second committee is reported to be preparing one. The company reports its quarterly results on Tuesday, November 17. Settled: the close, the fall, and that the board met. Open: the decision, which is Thursday’s first fact.

The dollar carried the rate story while the bonds were shut. Friday’s producer price report is the next inflation fact in the file. Settled: Wednesday’s close and the dollar’s level. Open: whether the chip rally survives its own largest company.

Abu Dhabi at Midnight: The Delegations Go Home, the Numbers Go to Thursday, and the Capitals Trade Fire

The talks’ first night at home produced paper from one delegation and fire from both armies. Mr. Umerov, who headed the Ukrainian delegation, published the delegation’s own closing account: two days of negotiations concluded, the main result an agreement on a large-scale prisoner exchange, the negotiators returning to their capitals to finalize the details, in the closing account filed below. Mr. Kushner’s closing sentences in the same account stand beside it: there is currently no trust between the two sides, which he described as expected after such a conflict, and territorial issues were discussed and would be the most difficult to resolve. The numbers are Thursday’s work, as they were when the evening edition closed; the exchange is expected to exceed 2,000 prisoners in total on the Russian general’s figure of more than 1,000 a side, with the swap itself in two to three weeks, in the swap account filed below. The itinerary is unchanged: Mr. Kushner and Mr. Witkoff meet President Putin in Moscow on Thursday, where they are expected to raise adding the European partners to the talks, and then fly to Dublin to meet President Zelenskyy.

The war’s own file added its night entry in the small hours. Russia and Ukraine exchanged attacks on each other’s capitals early on Thursday, in the Reuters account at the top of this report: explosions were heard in Kyiv in the early hours, a Reuters witness said, and it was not immediately clear whether the city had incurred damage; Moscow’s mayor, Mr. Sobyanin, said on Telegram that five drones heading for Moscow were downed, without additional details. Wednesday’s counts stand as the evening edition printed them—three killed and 11 injured in the daytime attack on the market in the Odesa region, seven wounded overnight in Zaporizhzhia and Kharkiv, and the drone counts unreconciled between the ministry and the governor. The talks and the strikes proceeded on the same hours for two days in Abu Dhabi; on the first night after them, the strikes proceeded alone. Settled: the round is over, the exchange is agreed in principle, the delegations are home. Open: the numbers, the land, the ceasefire, and Europe’s chair—all of it now addressed to Moscow.

Iran at Midnight: No Speck Leaves, an Answer Sent by Way of Qatar, and the Strait Shut

Tehran spent the president’s holiday restating its position in three voices, and midnight collects the three without joining them. Mr. Rezaei, the secretary of the Supreme National Security Council, said in interview excerpts released Wednesday that no enriched nuclear material will leave the country, that Tehran had communicated the decision to Moscow—which has offered to receive the material, and whose foreign ministry spokeswoman confirmed on Wednesday that the offer stands—and that reports of a recent meeting between Iranian and American representatives in Qatar were fabricated: no negotiations are currently taking place, in the Tehran account filed below. Speaker Ghalibaf, Tehran’s chief negotiator, said in an interview published the same day that Iran had sent Washington a detailed response through Qatari mediation and was waiting for an official answer, and that the waterway will never return to its pre-war arrangements, in the conditions account filed below. The two sentences—no channel, and an answer sent through one—stand in the file side by side, unreconciled by either speaker. The seven conditions themselves remain unpublished as a text; the demands the accounts list are sanctions relief, the release of frozen assets, the end of the American blockade of Iran’s ports and an end to the fighting across the region.

On the water, the day added claims and one reported attack, and no confirmation of either claim in the file. The Revolutionary Guard claimed on Wednesday that two more tankers struck naval mines and caught fire attempting to leave the strait by an unauthorized route under American fighter protection; a maritime operations centre had received a report on Tuesday evening of a tanker struck by a projectile, in the Tehran account above. Reuters reported on Wednesday, citing three sources, that Iranian forces had attacked navigation systems on Oman’s Didamar Island, in the strait account filed below. Against the whole file stands the president’s Arlington sentence of the same day—“We won”—and the desk prints the two records as it finds them: a war claimed won from an amphitheater, and a strait claimed shut until conditions are met, with both blockades standing and no agreement in the file. Settled: the positions, restated by every authorized voice on both sides. Open: the strait, the material, and the war’s end, whenever either capital dates it.

Spain at Midnight: The Strike Ends, the Count of It Does Not, and the Decrees Go to Thursday

Spain’s first general strike in 14 years ran its 24 hours and ended, and the argument the day leaves behind is about its size. The unions’ figure stands at four million workers; the employers’ confederation put average participation at about 5 percent, and Madrid’s regional government said 9 percent of its public employees joined—three figures measuring three different things, printed as they were given, in the strike account filed below. Barcelona’s march drew 400,000 in the housing activists’ count and 55,000 in the city’s. The Interior Ministry’s running totals at 1700 stood at 919 incidents and 52 arrests, against the 476 incidents and 40 arrests the midday accounts carried; the newspaper figures carried in the Madrid account filed below put more than 280 flight cancellations on the day and at least 42 arrests across several cities. The electricity figures divide by their bases: consumption up to 1100 ran 7.5 percent below the grid operator’s forecast for a day without the strike, on the wire service EFE’s figure, while demand between 1000 and 1100 fell by as much as 15.5 percent on the figures the strike-day account filed below carried. Madrid’s Metro ran 42 percent below the same peak a week earlier; Barcelona’s ran only at the commuting peaks.

The strike’s politics move to parliament on Thursday, on the government’s own scheduling. Prime Minister Sánchez defended the strike and backed its demands; his government brings two housing decrees to a vote on November 12, and a property portal’s reading of the smaller chamber, carried in the Madrid account above, gives the broader package enough support and the lease-renewal decree no majority yet. The official campaign for the November 29 election opens on Friday, November 13. The housing deficit that made the day stands at about 750,000 homes on the central bank’s figures, growing past a million by 2028. Settled: the strike happened, on a national scale no account disputes, and the election is 17 days out. Open: how large it was, and Thursday’s votes.
