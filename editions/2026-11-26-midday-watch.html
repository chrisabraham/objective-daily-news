The Midday in Brief: A Rally Corrected, a Strait Recounted, a Parade Moved

The midday’s work is correction, and the first correction is the morning’s own headline. Asia did rally on Thursday, as the Overnight Watch reported; it did not rally by the morning wire’s margins. The closes, in the closing account filed below and in the wire’s own closing form filed below, put the Nikkei up 0.86 percent and the Kospi up 0.29 percent—against 1.6 and 1.9 percent in the morning’s accounts of the same session. Europe then took the same tariff file and rose at midday by more than Asia kept. In the strait, Tehran counted two more tankers on mines and the counter counted two crossings out. At Ras Tanura, a shipping source says loadings stopped. In Ukraine, the dark regions rose from five to seven. In New York, the parade will march on Saturday.

What midday settled is Asia’s closes in the two fullest closing accounts; Europe’s direction and its leaders at the half-day; Wednesday’s strait count, closed at six in the counter; the parade’s postponement and the storm’s first counts; and the tariff answers on the record from Brussels, Tokyo, Singapore and Jakarta. What it left open is everything the morning left open—the refinery’s state, the claimed hulls’ names, the ministry’s word on the Merkury—plus the size of the strike Kyiv says is being prepared.

The Tariff File: The Answers Are Filed, and the Rate Table Carries Two Vietnams

The proposal of Wednesday now has its answers on the record. The European Commission’s spokesman said the Union strongly rejects the basis of the proposal, is not a source of structural excess capacity, and expects Washington to comply in full with the 2025 agreement and its 15 percent all-inclusive cap, in the collective account filed below. Japan on Thursday called the proposal regrettable and rejected the excess-capacity allegation. Singapore expressed deep disappointment and disagreed with the Trade Representative’s findings. Indonesia is reviewing a proposed rate of up to 18 percent. Bangladesh’s commerce minister said he hoped Washington would not decide anything that harmed the trade between the two countries. No government in the file has announced a countermeasure.

The rate table itself, as the collective account prints it, runs: India 17 percent with an agreement and 25 percent without; Bangladesh, Cambodia, Indonesia, Malaysia and Thailand at 18 percent; Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, the European Union and Switzerland at 15 percent; Singapore up to 20 percent; Vietnam at 35 percent, potentially 20 percent with an agreement; and China at the proposed 20 percent over its first-term duties. Switzerland’s answer is the file’s most particular: its business association rejected the finding and said the Swiss surplus with the United States is driven primarily by pharmaceuticals and gold, and its government expects any new duties to stay within its framework deal, in the fourteen-economies account filed below. The calendar stands as the desk printed it at dawn: written comments and hearing requests by January 5, the hearing on January 25, 2027, and nothing in force before then. Settled: the answers, as answers. Open: the Federal Register’s text, and whether any capital moves from words to duties of its own.

The Strait at Midday: Two More Hulls in Tehran’s Count, Two Crossings in the Counter’s

Thursday added two hulls to Tehran’s count before the American morning. The Guards said two tankers exploded and caught fire after hitting sea mines north of Oman’s Khasab port, crossing by an unauthorised route; one was a supertanker carrying more than two million barrels of crude, in the Khasab account filed below. The Guards’ spokesman said the force is using heavy firepower to keep the strait closed, and its navy commander said Iran holds full operational control of the waterway and that safe passage depends on respect for Iran’s rights, in the fuller form filed below. Neither vessel is named, and no crew is accounted for, as no vessel has been named all week.

The other instruments count differently, as they have counted differently all week. The maritime trade operations agency reported one incident for Thursday: an explosion caused by an unknown projectile near a tanker about 50 nautical miles west of Dubai. The visible counter, in its Thursday update filed below, counts two crossings for the day as of 1559 UTC—none inbound, two outbound, both tankers, the latest the products tanker Asha outbound at 1040 UTC—and it closes Wednesday at six crossings, confirming the corrected figure the Overnight Watch printed, with the seven days now at 51 crossings and the daily average at 7.3, about 9 percent of normal. Whether either of the counter’s two outbound tankers is either of the Guards’ two claimed hulls, no account in the file says. Settled: Wednesday’s six, and Thursday’s two crossings in the one instrument. Open: the two claimed hulls, unnamed like the three before them.

Ras Tanura at Midday: A Terminal Reported Stopped, an Owner Still Silent, and Oil Up a Third Day

The refinery file gained one fact at midday and kept its central silence. A shipping source told the American network on Thursday that oil loadings at the Ras Tanura terminal were suspended after the attack, and that it is unclear whether they have resumed, in the satellite account filed below. That is the first statement in the file about the terminal’s operations, and it comes from a source, not from the operator. Aramco, approached for comment, had filed no word in any account at the stamp; the Saudi government likewise. The coalition’s confirmed account stands where Wednesday left it: four drones destroyed over the town, debris fallen, no injuries in the first assessment, a projectile at Jazan, a drone downed near Jeddah.

The market priced the uncertainty rather than the damage. Brent rose 35 cents to $94.96 a barrel at 1211 GMT in the London oil account filed below, after settling up 3.5 percent on Wednesday; the Dow Jones midday form put Brent down 0.2 percent at $94.48 and West Texas Intermediate up 0.3 percent at $83.93, and the desk prints both readings in the Cocked Hat. Gulf stock markets were subdued in early Thursday trade, in the Gulf markets account filed below. The producers’ ministers meet on November 29 to set 2027 output policy, with the refinery’s state still undescribed by anyone who owns it. Settled: the suspension, as one source’s statement. Open: the resumption, the damage, and Riyadh’s word.

Ukraine at Midday: Seven Regions Dark, Hourly Blackouts Ordered, and a Larger Strike Said to Be Coming

The grid file widened in the morning. Russian strikes on energy infrastructure overnight caused outages in seven regions—Dnipropetrovsk, Zhytomyr, Zaporizhzhia, Mykolaiv, Odesa, Kharkiv and Chernihiv—the Deputy Energy Minister said on Thursday, in the outages account filed below; Wednesday’s file had counted five. The grid operator ordered scheduled hourly outages for all consumers, households included, of between half a queue and three and a half queues, with industry capped, the restrictions to run to the end of Thursday, in the operator’s account filed below. Crews are working where the security situation allows; no hour for full restoration is given.

The two capitals’ statements stand side by side. Moscow’s defence ministry said its forces carried out a massive overnight strike hitting military-industrial facilities in Kyiv, in the live file filed below. Kyiv’s president said Russia launched ballistic missiles and 113 drones overnight, that the air force reported hits at 20 locations, and that Ukrainian intelligence indicates Moscow is completing preparations for a new massive strike on energy, logistics and lifeline infrastructure, in the presidential account filed below; crews are restoring power in six regions in his count, against the ministry’s seven dark. Kyiv also filed its own night’s work: a military airfield and an oil pumping facility struck in Russia, oil reservoirs at the Transneft facility in Bashkortostan named in the later form, and a fire reported at the Nevinnomyssk thermal power plant in the running file, where the regional governor said a drone attack was repelled with debris falling near industrial and energy sites. Settled: the seven regions and the blackout schedules, in the operator’s and the ministry’s words. Open: the strike Kyiv says is coming, and the winter margin behind it.

Libya at Midday: No Word From the Ministry, and a Harbour Sealed With Boats

The Merkury file’s midday adds works, not words. Reporting in the file says Moscow has moved to seal Benghazi’s harbour entrance with a makeshift wall of boats to protect the vessels remaining there, and that forces loyal to the eastern commander have vowed reprisals for Sunday’s strike, while the Kremlin has said publicly that Libya has the right to respond, in the Benghazi account filed below. The same account records that two Libyan naval personnel wounded in the strike died of their injuries on November 24. Of the word the file waits on there is still none: Russia’s defence ministry, to which the Kremlin’s spokesman referred every question on Monday, had issued no statement on the corvette by the midday stamp—not a confirmation, not a denial, not a list of her crew. The paper’s own counts of the silence differ by the day they start from: the fifth day since the sinking on Sunday, the fourth since the referral on Monday; the desk states both rather than choosing. Settled: the harbour works, as reported. Open: the ministry’s word, the crew, and the joint inquiry.

Thanksgiving at Midday: The Storm on the Coast, 357 Cancellations by Morning, and a Parade on Saturday

The holiday’s file is now counts, not forecasts. Airlines reported 405 delays and 357 cancellations by Thursday morning, after more than 1,000 delays on Wednesday, in the storm account filed below, which counts more than 70 million people under flood warnings and advisories and 77.7 million under wind alerts, with New York and New Jersey under declared states of emergency. The weather service’s own morning figures, in the same file’s reporting, put about 36 million people under coastal flood warnings and 32 million under high wind warnings. The waivers the Overnight Watch printed stand over the day’s travel; the storm’s worst hours for the Northeast were still ahead of the stamp, from Thursday afternoon into the night.

The day’s emblem is a parade that will not march on its day. The 100th Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade was postponed on Wednesday to Saturday, November 28, on the forecast of rain and damaging winds—an unprecedented change in the event’s century-long history, in the travel account filed below, and the city urged residents to limit travel. The storm is a two-coast file: in California, king tides destroyed a section of the Pacifica pier on Wednesday and coastal flooding threatens record levels around San Francisco, and winter storm warnings stand in Missouri, Illinois, Montana, Washington and Alaska. Settled: the postponement, the emergencies, and the morning’s counts. Open: the afternoon’s sky over the I-95 corridor, and the counts it makes by evening.
