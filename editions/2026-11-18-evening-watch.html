The Fed at the Close: “Almost All” for Further Hikes, Some for October Itself, a Couple Open to More Than a Quarter-Point, and Two Governors Speaking the Same Day

The instrument the midday file waited for published at 1400, and its wording is the evening’s central document. The minutes of the October 27 and 28 meeting record that “almost all” participants judged that, under their baseline outlooks for inflation and the labour market, further increases in the target range would likely be appropriate in order to ensure a timely return of inflation to the 2 percent objective, in the wire’s minutes account and in the minutes account filed below. Some participants had favoured an increase of 25 basis points at the October meeting itself, in the same accounts; the meeting held the range at 3.75 to 4.00 percent by 10 to 2, with Governor Cook and President Hammack dissenting for a quarter-point hike. A few participants said inflation could prove more persistent and require a shift to a more restrictive stance than currently expected, in the wire’s account, and a couple said a hike larger than 25 basis points could warrant consideration at the December 8 and 9 meeting if incoming inflation data were unfavourable, in the buildout account filed below. Many participants treated the buildout of artificial intelligence capacity as a material source of inflation risk, in the same account. The minutes’ own caution stands in that account: their descriptions rest only on what the committee knew at the meeting, and they predate the October jobs, prices and retail reports published in November.

The day’s two governors spoke on either side of the release and in the same direction. Governor Waller told CNBC in the morning that he is “very, very concerned” about inflation, that about 70 percent of core services items are rising at a rate above 3 percent, and that a half-point hike in December is probably needed unless the committee signals a faster pace to come, in the broker’s minutes account filed below. Governor Barr, speaking at an event in New York on Wednesday, also said he saw the need for further rate hikes, in the minutes account above. Against them stands the week’s other voice, unchanged: New York President Williams said on Tuesday that another hike is likely and dismissed the suggestion that a large hike needs consideration, in the wire’s account. The market’s pricing after the release stands in the broker’s account: a quarter-point hike in December fully priced, with more than 80 basis points of tightening priced through September 2027, and minimal odds on more than a quarter-point in December. The administration’s position stands in the wire’s account: that elevated inflation is temporary and the Federal Reserve should ignore it. Settled: the direction, in the committee’s own record, is up, and the argument is now over size. Open: December 8 and 9.

Anthropic at the Close: Priced at $83, Above the Range, the Offering Enlarged, and Trading Thursday as ANT

The listing the week waited for priced after the close, and it priced above the range. Anthropic priced its offering at $83 a share, above the targeted $70 to $78, in the pricing account above, a Reuters account sourced to a person familiar with the matter who said the size of the offering had also increased; the company had initially planned to sell 825 million shares, and the company declined to comment, in the same account. Bloomberg News first reported the pricing, in the same account. At $83, the original 825 million shares raise about $68.5 billion in gross proceeds, and the value stands at about $1.5 trillion on a fully diluted basis, in the pricing account and the calculation account filed below; excluding options and restricted shares, the $78 figure in the earlier accounts falls closer to $1 trillion, and at $83 on the shares outstanding in the amended filing the value stands nearer $1.04 trillion in the upsize account filed below. Both bases are printed in the Cocked Hat, unblended. The demand figures stand as reports: institutional indications of about $400 billion and retail indications approaching $100 billion, neither binding, in the upsize account; retail requests close to $100 billion in the calculation account, with underwriters setting aside up to 20 percent of the shares for individual investors in the Reuters reporting it carries. The separate $26 billion placement of non-voting shares to seven suppliers—AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia and TSMC—stands in the pricing accounts as filed. The offering is the second large artificial-intelligence listing of the year, after June’s SpaceX sale, in the pricing account; whether it tops that record depends on the enlarged size, which no evening account states as a final number. Trading begins Thursday on the Nasdaq under the ticker ANT. Settled: the price, $83. Open: the final size, and Thursday’s first trade.

The Housing File at the Close: Pending Sales at 63.0, the Lowest Since 2001, Against a Forecast Fall of 2.0 Percent

The afternoon’s other official print completed the morning’s two-way housing file in one direction. Contracts to purchase previously owned homes fell 4.7 percent in October, and the Pending Home Sales Index fell to 63.0, the lowest level since the series began in 2001, in the wire’s housing account above and in the association’s release filed below. Economists polled had forecast a fall of 2.0 percent, in the wire’s account. Pending sales fell 16.7 percent from a year earlier, and fell in the month in all four regions and in the year in all four regions, in both accounts; the regional falls in the month run from 0.8 percent in the Northeast to 6.9 percent in the West, in the analysis account the wire’s figures accompany. October was the second straight month of large decreases, in the wire’s account, and the association reported last week a drop in home resales to the lowest level in more than 16 years in October, in the same account. The association’s chief economist, Dr. Yun, said October could mark the low point in contract signings for this housing cycle, as mortgage rates appear to have topped out, in the release. The rate stands where the midday file left it: the 30-year fixed mortgage at 7.18 percent last week, after reaching near 7.40 percent in mid-October, in the wire’s account. Read with the morning’s print—starts up 9.9 percent, permits down 7.4 percent—the housing file at the close is a building trade still starting houses and a contract market at its recorded floor. Settled: the index, at 63.0. Open: whether Dr. Yun’s low point is October’s or a later month’s.

Measles at the Close: The First Considered Responses—Grief From the Checkers, Resignation Calls From the Hill, and a Recommendation From the Secretary

The Evening Watch’s remit is the first considered responses, and the measles decision of Tuesday drew them on Wednesday from every side of the question. The chairman of the verification body’s advisory committee, Dr. Andrus, told the Guardian there was no question the decision was emotional, because the work had run for decades, in the responses account filed below; public health leaders in the same account called the loss significant damage and renewed calls for the Secretary’s removal, with Senator Cassidy’s Sunday sentence—that the Secretary’s tenure has been “a series of failures”—carried in it, and Senator Warnock’s criticism beside it. Representative Crockett blamed the administration and the Secretary by description, in the blame account filed below, which also carries the Secretary’s own timing point: the chain that cost the status began on January 20, 2025, before his confirmation on February 13, a point he made to a Senate committee in April in his own words—“the measles epidemic began before I came into office.”

The administration’s answer stands as the morning file carried it, with one addition. The Secretary posted on Tuesday that the United States “will stop measles outbreaks, protect American families, and work to regain U.S. measles elimination status,” and added: “I continue to recommend MMR vaccination as the most effective way to prevent measles,” with his department working to close vaccination gaps and rebuild public trust, in the responses account and in the decision account filed below. The CDC’s Director said the designation is not what protects Americans and vaccination does. Nearly 150 health care organisations wrote to the Secretary and the Director on Tuesday urging stronger action, in the status account filed below. An investigation published Tuesday, carried in the decision account, found in data from 13 states and New York City a larger pullback in vaccination among younger children in 2025 than in any year since records of that kind, including the pandemic years; the department declined the investigators’ request to interview the Secretary, and a White House spokesman said the Secretary and the President had encouraged vaccination and worked with states on outbreak response, in the same account. The counts stand where the morning file left them: 5,412 confirmed cases in 2026 through November 12, the highest since 1991; the deaths stand apart at six in the CDC’s 2026 count and at ten and thirteen in other accounts, in the Cocked Hat. The corrective action plans are due in the coming weeks. Settled: the status, and every side’s first answer to it. Open: the plans, and the twelve months without transmission that winning the status back requires.

France and Spain at the Close: The Police March on Paris for Their Own Allowance, and a Second Instrument Puts Spain’s Right 16 Seats Apart From the First

France’s Wednesday produced the week’s reversal: the police marched. Several hundred officers, called out by the Alliance Police Nationale union, marched in Paris on Wednesday toward the Élysée and the Ministry of the Interior at Place Beauvau, demanding an immediate increase in the special police allowance, in the march account filed below. The allowance, the ISSP, stands at 28.5 percent of base salary and has not been increased since 2019; the unions demand 35 percent in stages, and the Minister’s offer of November 13—five points in three stages to 33.5 percent by 2029—was rejected by the unions, in the same account. The day before, more than 1,200 riot police requested medical checkups, a course the account describes as a way around the ban on police strikes, and the figures presented put 1,600 officers and gendarmes injured in the recent weeks of protests and urban violence, in the same account. No major demonstration was confirmed in central Paris on Wednesday, in the schedule account filed below. Thursday carries two gatherings in the capital—a pensioners’ march from Montparnasse toward Les Invalides and a protest outside the Senate against the police firearms bill, which the Senate debates Thursday afternoon—and the roundabout occupations from Friday, in the same account. The strike file behind the day stands where the morning file left it: 146,000 demonstrators counted by the Interior Ministry on Tuesday against more than 500,000 by the CGT, and a strike rate of 2.59 percent in the state civil service, in the tracker accounts the morning file carried.

Spain’s evening gain is a second instrument, and the desk prints the two side by side. The 40dB tracking’s sixth instalment stands as the midday file printed it: the Popular Party at 32.2 percent and 131 seats, the Socialists at 30.7 and 122, Vox at 17.0 and 55, its lowest of the campaign. Sondaxe, published the same Wednesday, puts the Popular Party at 29.0 percent and 121 seats, the Socialists at 27.0 percent and 111, and Vox at 19.4 percent and 71 seats, in the useful-vote account and the average account the midday file carried. The two instruments agree on the order and on the right’s majority, and disagree on Vox by sixteen seats; both stand in the Cocked Hat. Thursday carries the campaign’s single head-to-head debate; polls may publish until November 23; Spain votes November 29.

The fine print, in brief. Objective, as this paper uses the word, means the desk is not driven by an agenda, is not an advocate, is not a politician, is not modeling behavior, and is not training up citizens: it is reporting the news as if it were objective. Subjective means the item comes from a source with its own position, as all sources have; it is printed because it is useful information, weighed by the reader. Objectivity is relative; everything is relative, even relativity is relative; absolutism is wrong. No source in these pages is more reputable than a press release issued directly from Langley.
