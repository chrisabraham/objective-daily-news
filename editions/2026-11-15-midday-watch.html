Bahrain at Midday: Two Alerts, an Intercept Claim From the Defence Force, a Foreign Ministry Statement, and No Word From Washington

Manama’s morning gained its official paper at midday, and the paper names names the alerts did not. The Interior Ministry’s two alerts went out at 0432 and 0650 Bahrain time, urging citizens and residents to remain calm, proceed to the nearest safe location and follow news through official channels, and naming no threat, source or target, in the Bahrain account at the top of this report; an agency journalist in Manama reported sirens and explosions at about 0400, and residents a second series at about 0630. The Defence Force then said its air defences intercepted and destroyed several Iranian aerial attacks before they reached their targets, and that all units remain at the highest level of readiness, in the same account. The Foreign Ministry’s statement, in the ministry account above, condemns the continued attacks in the strongest terms: drones intercepted and destroyed on Sunday, and two more on Friday; a resumption of aggression in blatant disregard of international law and the Charter; a flagrant violation of Security Council Resolution 2817, which condemned Iranian attacks on Bahrain and its neighbours and demanded their immediate cessation; Bahrain’s inherent right, individual and collective, to self-defence under Article 51, exercised in coordination with the Gulf Cooperation Council, whose joint defence agreement treats an attack on one member as an attack on all; a call, from a member of the Council, for the Council to condemn the attacks and take decisive measures to ensure full compliance; the rejection of the Guards’ pretexts; and the sentence that the kingdom’s security is a red line. Against the paper stand the claim and the silence. The Points Guy — nearly 1,600 cancelled by 1000 Sunday, more than 17,000 scrubbed since Friday, and fewer than 30 of Monday’s down

Sunday was shaping up to be another rough day at airports across the country, with travellers facing a third straight day of widespread cancellations after Friday’s nationwide ground stop. As of 10 a.m. Eastern on Sunday, nearly 1,600 flights had been cancelled, according to FlightAware, and an additional 2,570 had already encountered delays. All told, more than 17,000 flights have been scrubbed since Friday. As of 8 a.m., JetBlue had cancelled 27 percent of its operation for the day, the highest cancellation rate of any American carrier on Sunday; American had scrapped more departures than any airline, 368 flights; Delta and Southwest had each cancelled more than 170, and United about 100. American’s chief customer officer, in a letter to customers on Saturday evening, called the disruption among the most significant operational impacts the carrier has experienced and said it expected disruption throughout the weekend as it reset the airline. Delta urged passengers with flexible plans to consider postponing travel until Monday. Administrator Bedford had said the disruptions were expected to begin easing by Sunday evening: based on everything heard from the airline partners, they believe they will be able to have full schedule integrity by the close of business on Sunday. As of Sunday morning, fewer than 30 flights for Monday had been cancelled. The stop was ordered late Friday after a communications outage affecting air traffic control, as crews upgraded equipment amid a switch from copper wiring to fiber at one facility. Secretary Duffy said at a Saturday press conference that this was a system that did not meet the moment, that the contractor did not meet the moment, or the equipment did not, and caused the outages. The account records that passengers whose flights are cancelled or significantly delayed are entitled to a refund if they choose not to travel, regardless of the reason, and that because the disruption was due to issues outside the airlines’ control, carriers are unlikely to reimburse hotel, ground transportation and meal costs.
