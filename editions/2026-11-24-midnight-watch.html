TechTarget — FAA meltdown raises a $50 billion question for CIOs

On November 13 the Federal Aviation Administration ordered a nationwide ground stop after network instability disrupted radar and communications feeds; the problem affected 17 of the agency’s 22 air route traffic control centers. The trouble emerged during a routine network upgrade: a new router from Juniper Networks, installed by contractor L3Harris at a remote facility in El Dorado, Arkansas, is among the equipment under investigation, with equipment in Amarillo, Texas, also identified as a possible source; the legacy network was overwhelmed by roughly 1,000 connection attempts every four to five seconds. The agency paused additional router installations and disabled the functionality under investigation on more than 60 other routers as a precaution. Investigators are still determining precisely what went wrong. The account also carries the September warning of the Government Accountability Office that the accelerated modernization program lacked a reliable lifecycle cost estimate and an integrated master schedule.
