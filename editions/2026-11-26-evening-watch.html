The ECB Minutes: A Large Number Ready in October, a Unanimous Hold, and December Priced Above Ninety Percent

The afternoon’s document is the account of the European Central Bank’s meeting of October 28-29, published Thursday. Its operative lines, in the Dow Jones account filed below, are these: a large number of members said the data since September had confirmed the case for further tightening and that they could have supported raising rates at the October meeting itself; all members supported the hold, on the understanding that the communication would convey that another increase would likely be necessary unless the inflation outlook improved significantly; and waiting to reassess in December was, in the account’s words, a reasonable decision. The deposit rate stands at 2.50 percent, after increases in June and September. The pause’s reasons are stated in the same account: underlying inflation broadly stable, no material second-round effects in wages, and financial conditions already tighter.

The market’s reading moved in one direction. Money markets priced a 91 percent probability of a December increase in the midday briefing the Midday Watch filed, and 93 percent in one afternoon account of the minutes; the euro rose 0.10 percent to about 1.0986. The council meets on December 16-17, with new staff forecasts. Settled: the October council’s mind, in its own account. Open: December 17.

BeInsure — Thanksgiving storms hit US coasts as 70 mn people face flooding risks

Airlines reported 405 delays and 357 cancellations by Thursday morning, after more than 1,000 delays on Wednesday. More than 70 million people were under flood warnings and advisories and 77.7 million under wind alerts, with New York and New Jersey under declared states of emergency. In California, king tides destroyed a section of the Pacifica pier on Wednesday and coastal flooding threatens record levels around San Francisco, and winter storm warnings stand in Missouri, Illinois, Montana, Washington and Alaska.

The fine print, in brief. Objective, as this paper uses the word, means the desk is not driven by an agenda, is not an advocate, is not a politician, is not modeling behavior, and is not training up citizens: it is reporting the news as if it were objective. Subjective means the item comes from a source with its own position, as all sources have; it is printed because it is useful information, weighed by the reader. Objectivity is relative; everything is relative, even relativity is relative; absolutism is wrong. No source in these pages is more reputable than a press release issued directly from Langley.
