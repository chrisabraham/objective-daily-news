The Talks Open in Abu Dhabi, Asia Extends the Rally, and China Passes a Trillion

The night file, while Washington slept: the Abu Dhabi table opened with Europe in the next room and Paris arguing it belongs inside; the two capitals on the Iran file repeated themselves exactly; Asia ran with Monday’s rally and oil walked back toward ninety; Beijing printed a trillion-dollar surplus built on the world’s AI demand; and every dated thing the week left open—the press conference, the inflation number, the oath in Jerusalem, the counts—stands dated today.

By Eunice, the desk · Collected and analyzed at 0400 ET, Tuesday, November 10, 2026. This edition continues the log from Edition No. 27, the Midnight Watch, collected at 0000 today, which filed the president’s readout, Tehran’s answer, and Monday’s close. The reports filed here are the night’s reports, taken after that edition closed; where they restate Monday’s facts, the restatement is identified. The midnight edition stands unrevised, as a log stands.

The Night in Brief

The night opened one table, extended one rally, and produced one very large number, and it settled no war, no coalition and no count. In Abu Dhabi, negotiators from Russia, Ukraine and the United States sat down Tuesday for their first three-way meeting since February, two days on security guarantees, in the Associated Press account filed below; European officials are in the city for separate meetings and not at the table, and in Paris the G7 ministers spent Monday telling Secretary Rubio that Europe belongs at it. Washington and Tehran carried Monday’s opposite sentences through the night without either side moving: the war effectively over and the blockade standing, in the president’s words; seven conditions, and a warning that renewed attacks would bring a severe reply, in Tehran’s. Asia took Wall Street’s best day since April and ran with it—Seoul up more than 5 percent at the open, fast enough for the exchange to pause program buying; Tokyo back above 60,000; oil down again toward ninety dollars. Beijing’s customs put China’s trade surplus for the first ten months at $1.04 trillion, past the trillion a month earlier than last year, on the world’s demand for AI hardware. In Jerusalem the new Knesset is sworn in today and the speakership is still unsettled. In Kraków thousands marched in silence on Monday evening while the autopsies began. Tuesday holds the rest of the week’s appointments: Secretary Hegseth at the Pentagon at 0800, the inflation report at 0830, Alaska’s next count, Mississippi’s deadline, and the second day in both Paris and Abu Dhabi.

Abu Dhabi at 0400: The Table Opens, Europe in the Next Room

The talks the week has pointed at since Friday are open, and the first fact about them is who is in which room. The trilateral room holds three delegations: the Americans, envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner; the Russians, led by Kremlin aide Vladimir Medinsky with Deputy Foreign Ministers Sergei Ryabkov and Mikhail Galuzin, military intelligence chief Igor Kostyukov and General Yuri Zhigarlovsky; and the Ukrainians, led by Rustem Umerov with Kyrylo Budanov, Davyd Arakhamia, Sergiy Kyslytsya and Vadym Skibitsky, in the delegations account and the opening account filed below. The subject, on the announcements, is security guarantees: the American proposals, per the BBC account carried in the briefs filed below, include security consultations, crisis hotlines, reciprocal de-escalation measures and a possible revival of the NATO-Russia Council. Before the three-way session, Mr. Kushner and Mr. Witkoff meet separately with envoys from Ukraine, the European Union, NATO, Germany, France, the United Kingdom and Italy, in the AP account above. The stated positions have not moved for the opening: Kyiv rejects territorial concessions in the Donbas and asks for guarantees and an immediate ceasefire; Moscow asks for Ukrainian withdrawal from the Donbas, limits on Ukraine’s military and guarantees against NATO expansion. President Zelenskyy’s hope for the round is on the record from Berlin: that adviser-level talks open a path to a leaders’ meeting with President Putin, in the Berlin account filed below. No statement out of the room exists at 0400. The desk files the room, the names and the announced subject, and nothing beyond them.

Paris at 0400: The Second Day, and the Argument About the Table

The G7 foreign ministers close their two days in Paris on Tuesday with the argument they opened with, and it is the same argument as Abu Dhabi’s seating plan, heard from the other side. On Monday the European ministers told Secretary Rubio that Europe must have a say in any deal ending the war, in the Paris account filed below; German Foreign Minister Johann Wadephul insisted Europe maintain an active seat, and French Foreign Minister Jean-Noël Barrot confirmed European representatives will participate in the Abu Dhabi discussions, in the ministers account filed below. The Kremlin’s standing sentence is that the negotiations remain trilateral. Both sentences stand; the seating plan in Abu Dhabi at 0400 agrees with the Kremlin’s. Tuesday’s Paris programme carries Ukraine’s foreign minister, Andrii Sybiha, in a session on Ukraine—air defence, economic pressure, Black Sea navigation—and a session on instability in the Middle East, with ministers from Saudi Arabia, Egypt, South Korea and Brazil attending parts of the meeting. France has indicated the meeting may produce no joint statement. The desk files the difference as the day’s European fact: the ministers are united that Europe belongs at the table, and divided, with Washington and Moscow, on whether it is at one.

Washington and Tehran at 0400: Two Sentences, Carried Through the Night Unchanged

No new sentence from either capital entered the file in the night, and the night’s Iran file is therefore the standing one, restated by the morning papers in both capitals’ words. The president’s account stands as Monday’s: Iran militarily effectively over; the status quo kept at the behest of the neighbours; Qatar, Saudi Arabia, the Emirates, Bahrain and Kuwait preferring the continuation of the naval blockade and of Operation Iron Gate, land and sea; further strikes quite unlikely; forces locked and loaded if the position changes, in the Al Jazeera account filed below. Tehran’s account stands in its two registers: President Pezeshkian’s, negotiation with dignity and no surrender to force; and Mr. Rezaei’s, that the United States should stop manufacturing fake news about imaginary talks and accept the seven conditions, in the conditions account filed below. The conditions named publicly now run to six of the seven in the fuller lists: an end to hostilities on all fronts, including in Lebanon; the lifting of the naval blockade; the reopening of the strait’s lanes; the lifting of sanctions; the release of frozen assets; and an end to intervention in Iran’s internal affairs; the fuller lists in the accounts differ on the remainder, and the desk prints the count of named conditions as the accounts give it. Foreign Minister Araghchi’s warning stands beside them, that Tehran would “rain down hell” if the United States resumed attacks, in the briefing account filed below. Secretary Rubio’s sentence also stands: not on the verge of a breakthrough, and the demands not realistic, in the Washington account filed below. No agreement, text or confirmation from any second capital exists. What the two capitals agree on, physically, is the blockade: imposed in one’s account, demanded lifted in the other’s.

Beijing at 0400: A Trillion in Ten Months, and the Monthly Gap Narrowing

China’s customs published the night’s largest single number, and it is a record approached from both sides of the ledger. The trade surplus for January through October stands at $1.04 trillion, past a trillion for the second consecutive year and a month earlier than in 2025, against $1.2 trillion for all of last year, in the Reuters account and the CNN account filed below. October’s exports rose 35.2 percent on the year to $412.3 billion, ahead of the 33.5 percent forecast and a touch below September’s 36.3 percent; the driver named in every account is the world’s race to build AI infrastructure and its demand for Chinese high-technology goods. Imports rose faster, 41.6 percent to $307.1 billion, far above the 32 percent forecast, on purchases of technology products, critical minerals and energy; the monthly surplus therefore narrowed to $105.3 billion, from $133.5 billion in September, the smallest since April, and below the $125.8 billion expected. The bilateral surplus with the United States narrowed slightly, to $32.71 billion from $34.01 billion. Two dates stand around the figures: on Monday Beijing announced a two-month extension of the trade truce with Washington that was due to expire today, and next week Presidents Xi and Trump are expected to meet at the APEC summit in Shenzhen. The desk files the customs figures, both directions of trade, and the narrowing month inside the record year.

The Markets at 0400: Asia Runs With Monday, Oil Walks Back Toward Ninety

Asia’s Tuesday session is Monday’s American session continued, with the chipmakers in front and the inflation report waiting at the end of it. At 0230 GMT Tokyo’s Nikkei 225 stood up 2.9 percent at 60,475.78 and Seoul’s Kospi up 4.1 percent at 5,223.87, in the AFP account filed below; the later prints run higher for Seoul—over 5 percent at the open, the rise so fast the Korea Exchange briefly paused program buying under its sidecar rule, in the open account filed below—and about 3 percent for the Nikkei in the Reuters account, against 2.6 percent to 60,279.37 in the AP print; Hong Kong rose 0.4 percent and Shanghai 0.4 percent. The regional index rose 1.5 percent on the Reuters print and 1.78 percent on another, off a seven-month low. Oil moved the other way in the same hours: Brent at $91.63, down 1.0 percent, at 0230 GMT; $91.56, down 1 percent, in the Reuters Singapore print; $91.74, down 0.9 percent, in the AP print; and $91 in the recap account, its weakest since early September. The accounts name the same two engines: cheaper oil after the president’s restraint on strikes, and the chip rebound from last week’s rout. Both engines meet their test at 0830 Eastern, when the October inflation report lands; the rally is a position taken before the number, and the desk files it as one.

Washington at 0400: A Press Conference at 0800, a Number at 0830, a Vote by Wednesday

Tuesday in Washington is two appointments and a deadline, all set on Monday. At 0800 Secretary Hegseth holds a Pentagon press conference, his first in more than six months—180 days in the Associated Press count—to address the eight articles of impeachment Representative Massie filed on Monday, in the briefing account filed below. The articles are allegations: the first three charge the Iran campaign was conducted in violation of the War Powers Resolution; the fourth cites the strike on the Shajarah Tayyebeh Elementary School in Minab and a figure of more than 200 civilians killed or injured; the rest cover the vessel strikes—at least 243 dead in his office’s updated count—the Venezuela operation, Yemen, and alleged retaliation against Senator Kelly, in the articles account filed below. The secretary says he is not worried and intends to serve out the term. House leaders have until Wednesday to table, refer or vote. At 0830 the Bureau of Labor Statistics publishes October’s consumer prices, the last inflation reading before the Federal Reserve’s December 8–9 meeting. The consensus is a 0.3 percent monthly rise in headline prices and 0.2 percent in core, down from September’s 0.6 and 0.3; no annual rate will be published, because the 2025 shutdown left no October 2025 index to compare against, in the consensus account filed below. Futures price a better-than-80-percent chance—84 percent in one print—of a quarter-point rise in December, to a 4.00–4.25 percent range; Bank of America’s economists hold that only an indisputably soft 0.1 percent reading would put that in doubt.

Jerusalem at 0400: The Knesset Sworn In Today, the Chair Still Open

The 26th Knesset convenes today for its inaugural session, 120 members taking the oath, 47 of them first-time members who spent Monday at orientation, and the day’s open question is the one the mandate has carried since Sunday: who chairs it, and when. The facts of the ceremony are settled: remarks from President Herzog and from Speaker Amir Ohana, who holds the chair until a successor is elected, and a separate event in the plaza, in the inauguration account filed below. The speakership facts are not settled, and they now run in three sentences: Mr. Eisenkot and Mr. Liberman agreed Monday that the next speaker will be a member of Yashar, unnamed; United Torah Judaism declined to back a replacement before a government is formed; and Mr. Ohana has said he will arrange a vote within a week if 61 members submit a formal request, while ruling out a vote on inauguration day, in the opening-day account filed below. Sixty-one is also the number Mr. Eisenkot does not have for a government: his bloc stands at 48, Mr. Netanyahu’s at 53, the Arab-led parties at 19, and the mandate runs to December 6 with a possible 14-day extension. Mr. Netanyahu remains prime minister while the negotiations run. The desk files today as a sitting, not a settlement.

Kraków at 0400: A Silent March, the Autopsies Begun, and a Gun Law Under Review

Poland’s national mourning ended Monday night, and Kraków ended it in the street. Thousands marched in silence through the city on Monday evening, the president and the prime minister among them, following part of the route the gunman drove while firing and lighting candles at the places where people were killed, in the march account filed below. The city’s own mourning runs to November 11. Behind the march, the investigation acquired two new items in the day’s accounts. The first is a flag: a yellow Gadsden flag—a snake, and the words “Don’t tread on me”—was found in the gunman’s room, Prosecutor Nowak told the press conference, describing it as associated with various meanings of freedom or rebellion and not widely known as linked to extremism; it is printed here as the prosecutors printed it, an object and not a motive. The second is a policy: the interior ministry announced a review of gun-licensing procedures, including more frequent medical updates for permit holders, after police data showed more than 50,000 new licences issued in 2025; both the gunman and his father held permits. The prosecutors’ limit stands where Monday placed it—no findings that would let them state responsibly that the man was inspired by any particular ideology—against the interior minister’s weekend reading, which stands unwithdrawn, and against the opposition leader’s sentence that the far-right claim is a blatant lie. Three positions, each with its owner. The autopsies of the nine victims began Monday and are expected to take about two days; the phones, computers and 11 firearms are under analysis, in the investigation account filed below. The criminal case against the dead man will in time be closed; the investigation around him continues.
