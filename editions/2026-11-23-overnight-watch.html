The Night in Brief: A Claim Made in Kyiv, a Second Survivor in Dymer, and a Council Convening in Brussels

The night’s file is a claim the desk has waited two days to print in the claimant’s own word, a rescue that produced a second survivor while the count of the dead rose past it, and an alliance that convenes this morning on missiles now named in the record. President Zelensky said on Monday that Ukraine sank the corvette Merkury at Benghazi with seven naval drones, destroyed a Ka-27 helicopter with her, and hit a second ship he names as the dry cargo vessel MV Angara, in the claim account filed below. Russia has not yet commented in any account in the file. In Dymer, the toll reached 11 in the regional governor’s Monday word, in the toll account filed below, after a night in which rescuers pulled a 79-year-old woman alive from the rubble at 0328, in the rescue account filed below. The North Atlantic Council meets in Brussels today, with Poland presenting its assessment of the three objects its prosecutors confirmed on Sunday were Kh-101 cruise missiles, in the confirmation account filed below. The desk takes the files in that order.

What the night settled is narrow and real: that Kyiv claims Benghazi, in its president’s words, with the drones numbered and the second target named; that Dymer’s search gave back two survivors in about thirty hours and has not finished; that the Sparta II file is closed in Finland, her rescue ended and her restrictions lifted; and that Monday’s first strait crossing is a tanker inbound. What it left open is the rest: Russia’s answer to the claim, the second ship’s true name, Dymer’s final count, the council’s decision, the radars at Al-Riffa and Awali, and the cash open on Wall Street, which has not yet spoken to Asia’s bounce.

Libya at 0400: Seven Drones in the President’s Word, a Helicopter Added, and a Second Ship Under Three Names

The Benghazi file changed its character in the night: what stood at midnight as a sinking without a claimant stands at the stamp as a claimed operation, described by the claimant. Ukraine sank a Russian warship docked in Libya and disabled a cargo vessel off the North African coast, President Zelensky said Monday, in the claim account above. The warship, the 344-foot corvette Merkury, was attacked with seven maritime drones, sinking the ship and destroying a combat helicopter, Mr. Zelensky said in a post to Telegram, and he released footage showing sea drones speeding towards the ship as it erupted into a giant ball of flames in the Benghazi dock. The wire form of the same statement adds the particulars the desk files with it: seven Ukrainian-made naval drones, a ship commissioned in 2023, and a Ka-27 combat helicopter destroyed along with the corvette, in the wire account filed below. The president’s own sentences, as one paper prints them, run: “Our warriors deployed seven Ukrainian-made naval drones. Despite all the enemy’s efforts to counter the attack, the Russian corvette Merkury was sunk,” in the statement account filed below.

The second target is where the night’s file divides, and the desk prints the division whole. Mr. Zelensky’s second target was the dry cargo vessel MV Angara, which he said Russia used to transport weapons: “Result: target hit,” in the claim and statement accounts. The open-source identification carried in the specialist file names a different ship: the roll-on/roll-off cargo vessel Ascalon, struck off Benghazi the same evening, an identification not independently confirmed, in the defence account filed below and in the campaign account filed below. The wire file of Sunday evening names no ship at all: a Maltese-flagged commercial ship hit by a sea drone off the coast, her injured crew rescued, in the sinking account filed below. Three forms—a named Russian dry cargo ship in Kyiv’s word, a named roll-on/roll-off vessel in the open-source file, and an unnamed Maltese-flagged ship in the wire file—stand in the Cocked Hat, unmerged. On the corvette herself the counts also differ in the small: seven drones deployed in the president’s word, against two that struck and at least four intercepted in the Libyan officials’ initial investigation, in the defence account. The eastern government’s standing words stand: a joint investigation with Russia, a formal request to the United Nations for an international investigation, and the statement that Libya is not a party to the war and will not allow its territory to be turned into an arena for settling scores, in the claim and sinking accounts. Russia has not yet commented on the attacks, in the claim account; that silence is itself the file’s open end. Settled: the claim, made and quotable. Open: Moscow’s answer, and the second ship.

Dymer at 0400: Eleven Dead in the Governor’s Monday Word, a Woman Alive at Twenty-Two Hours, and a Search Still Running

Dymer’s night ran in both directions at once, and the desk files the two movements in the order they landed. At 0328 the rescuers pulled a 79-year-old woman alive from under the rubble, in the rescue account above; the emergency service names the two survivors as the seven-year-old boy Davyd, rescued on Sunday after nearly fourteen hours, and the woman Valentyna, trapped for twenty-two hours, in the wire account filed below. Against the rescues, the count rose through the night in the governor’s own postings: nine in Dymer in the earlier regional count, in the relayed account filed below, which also counts at least twelve dead across the Kyiv region; ten, with four more bodies recovered, at the hour of the woman’s rescue, in the rescue account; and eleven on Monday, after rescuers pulled another body from the rubble, regional governor Tymur Tkachenko said, in the toll account above and in the regional account filed below. Rescuers were combing the rubble of the residential building after retrieving several bodies overnight, and it was not immediately clear how many were feared trapped, in the wire account. Constant air raid alerts and the threat of drones hampered the work by interrupting the search, the emergency service spokeswoman Viktoriia Ruban said, in the same account—the same interruption the Sunday file recorded, running now into a second day. The building stands as the file has carried it: five storeys in Dymer, about forty-five kilometres north of Kyiv, its entrance and stairwell destroyed by the drone that struck at about 0500 on Sunday while residents slept. Settled: two survivors, and a toll of eleven in the governor’s word. Open: the number still under the rubble, which no account at the stamp states, and the final count when the search ends.

Brussels and the Baltic at 0400: Three Kh-101s in the Prosecutors’ Word, a Council Convening, and a Finnish File Closed

The alliance file gained its fact before it gained its meeting. Poland has confirmed that all three objects that crashed on its soil on Friday were Russian Kh-101 cruise missiles, not two missiles and a drone, its prosecutors confirmed on Sunday, in the confirmation account above. The objects came down at Nowy Lipowiec in Lublin province, about sixty-two kilometres from the Ukrainian border, at Rudnik nad Sanem, about one hundred kilometres from the border, and at Mołodycz, about thirty-eight kilometres away, in the same account. The extraordinary meeting of the North Atlantic Council convenes at Brussels headquarters today, Monday, confirmed by Poland’s Permanent Representation, in the meeting account filed below. Warsaw’s programme for the room stands in three parts, in the proposals account filed below: its own assessment of the circumstances and of why the objects crossed the border; clear confirmation of political support and solidarity from all thirty-two member states; and specific proposals to strengthen the air defence of the eastern flank. Poland will not invoke Article 4, seeking expanded help through the existing Eastern Sentry mission instead, in the confirmation account. No readout from the council stands in the file at the stamp; the meeting is today’s business, and the desk files it as convening, not concluded.

The Baltic file, by contrast, closed in the night, and the desk records its closing as a closing. The Sparta II was towed out of Finland’s economic zone on Sunday, ending the Border Guard’s rescue mission, and the maritime and aviation restrictions imposed around the operation were lifted, in the departure account filed below. President Stubb said late Sunday that the ship had left Finnish territorial waters after posing a serious danger there, in the tow account filed below. The Russian rescue vessel Murman led the tow, supported by the Finnish patrol vessel Uisko, Estonia’s Kindral Kurvits and commercial rescue tugs, in both accounts; flames were no longer visible from outside, thermal cameras showed some containers still warm, the ship listed slightly, and no fuel or other hazardous leaks had been detected, in the tow account. Finland’s completion announcement, posted at 2017 Finnish time on Sunday, ends Finland’s operation; it does not announce the ship’s arrival at a Russian port, establish her cargo, or say that all fire inside her containers is out, in the completion account filed below. Settled: the departure, the lifted restrictions, and the end of the Finnish mission. Open: the cargo, and the arrival.

The Gulf and the Strait at 0400: No New American Word on the Radars, and a Tanker First on Monday

Bahrain’s file did not move in the night, and the desk records the standing without dressing it. The Guards’ claim stands in its own words—two Patriot radars at the Al-Riffa and Awali bases struck in a combined missile and drone operation—in the Manama account filed below. Manama’s account stands against it: a civilian industrial facility afire, and two injured—a Bahraini citizen in stable condition and a Pakistani resident in critical condition—in the same account. The American word in the file remains the one word: no damage to American personnel or facilities in the region, in an official’s statement carried in the same accounts and in the roundup account filed below, which is not a statement about the radars. The one denial Washington has issued in this file answers a different claim: Central Command said Iran had not attacked an American F-16 and that all American fighter jets in the Middle East were safe and accounted for, against the claim of a damaged aircraft forced to an emergency landing, in the denial account filed below. Bahrain’s appeal to the Security Council and its invocation of Article 51 stand as filed. Settled: the two injured and the condemnation. Open, for a second night: the radars themselves.

The strait opens Monday the way Sunday closed, at the crisis’s working level, with its instruments apart. The visible counter’s latest brief records one crossing so far on Monday: the oil products tanker Musik, inbound into the Gulf at 0437 UTC, one inbound and none outbound, the crossing a tanker, in the strait count filed below. Sunday closed at two crossings in the same counter, one in and one out, no tankers. The week’s figure moved again in the night’s readings—fifty-five at the midnight stamp, fifty-one in the brief’s earlier Monday reading, fifty-two in its latest, with thirty-three inbound and nineteen outbound, fifteen of them tankers, a seven-day average of 7.4 a day, about nine percent of the pre-crisis 84.8—and the desk prints the three readings in the Cocked Hat as one instrument revising itself, not as three facts. The second instrument counts its wider sea: twenty-five ships in the twenty-four hours to 0731 UTC, sixteen in and nine out, with tankers carrying an estimated 0.82 million barrels out, in the monitor account filed below, whose table again lists the tanker Khat Shekan outbound late Sunday—the name the desk struck from the record on Sunday as a spoof transmission, not restored on the monitor’s word. Settled: Sunday’s two, and Monday’s first, a tanker inbound. Open: the week’s true figure, and the true count of the strait.

The Markets at 0400: A Bounce Held in the Futures, a Sidecar in Seoul, and a Margin Cut in China

Asia’s Monday is two markets in one session, and the desk files them as they stand. The futures held the bounce the Sunday reopening gave them: S&P 500 futures rose about 2.2 percent and Nasdaq 100 futures close to 3 percent, holding near session highs through the Asian session, in the session account filed below; the wire’s earlier reading had the same contracts up about 1 percent and 2 percent at the open, in the wire account filed below, and the later desk reading stands at 2.1 and 2.8, in the update account filed below. The cash markets of Asia did not follow. Seoul fell almost 6 percent at its low, triggering a sidecar halt on program selling, before paring the loss to around 3.2 percent, with Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix each down about 3.6 percent at that reading, in the session account; the wire file puts the Kospi’s fall at about 4 percent to a ten-month trough before support, in the regional account filed below. The support measures stand in the file as the night’s other market fact: China’s three exchanges will cut the minimum margin-financing requirement to 80 percent from 100 percent, effective November 30, in the update account, after state funds poured a record sum into exchange-traded funds on Friday; Taiwan’s regulator tightened short-selling rules on Saturday and its index jumped 2.2 percent on Monday, in the same accounts; Seoul readies a stabilisation fund unused since 2008, worth around $8 billion, in the session account. Shanghai and Hong Kong ran in both directions across the readings—down about 1 percent and 0.7 percent early, the Shanghai Composite and ChiNext up 0.2 and 3.6 percent later—and Japan was closed for a holiday throughout, in the session and update accounts. Gold rose about 2 percent to near $3,610 as the forced selling eased, in the session account; Brent stands near $90 in one reading and at $92.86, up 0.3 percent, in the update account’s ICE figures.

The coin spent the night above the line its week had taken from it, and its morning carries a dated test. Bitcoin’s latest price in the data service’s reading is $61,457.16 at 0758 UTC, up 3.91 percent on its day, in the exchange account filed below; the overnight brief had it at $61,465 at 0030 Eastern, up 4.2 percent in twenty-four hours and 11.5 percent off Saturday’s $55,130 low, in the brief account filed below. The brief’s base case for the day is a sideways range of $59,800 to $63,200, the rebound running on light volume and needing confirmation from the American open at 0930. The Deribit daily expiry fell at 0300 Eastern, inside this watch: 4,964 bitcoin, max pain at $60,500, the report price 1.6 percent above it, in the same account. The week’s large expiry stands ahead on November 27: 112,026 bitcoin, max pain at $72,000, in the same account. Settled: the futures’ direction through Asia, Seoul’s fall, and the measures taken against it. Open: the cash open at 0930, which decides whether the bounce was a market or a futures reading.

Spain at 0400: Rival Rallies in One City, a Final Poll Nine-Tenths Apart, and the Poll Ban at Dawn

Spain’s campaign spent its last Sunday in one city and its last poll nine-tenths of a point apart, and it wakes Monday to silence from the pollsters by law. The two leaders held rival rallies in Murcia on Sunday: Prime Minister Sánchez at the Príncipe de Asturias pavilion in the morning, and Mr. Feijóo about half an hour later in the Plaza del Cardenal Belluga, before about 7,000 people by the organizers’ count, urging voters not to relax despite favourable polls, in the rallies account filed below and in the Murcia account filed below. The final poll of the campaign, by 40dB from 2,000 interviews taken between November 18 and 22 and published Monday, gives the People’s Party 31.8 percent and 132 seats, the Socialists 30.9 percent and 122, Vox 17.3 percent and 56, and Frente Amplio 4.4 percent and seven, in the final poll account filed below; on those figures the two parties of the right together hold 188 seats, above the 176 needed for a majority, though neither governs alone. The averages stand wider than the final poll: twenty institutes at 31.0 for the People’s Party, 28.2 to 28.3 for the Socialists, and 18.7 to 18.8 for Vox across the Sunday and Monday printings, in the Murcia and rallies accounts, and Sunday’s four polls ran from a half-point Ipsos margin to a five-point Hamalgama margin, in the Sunday polls account filed below. Monday is the last day polls may legally be published; a five-day ban now runs until voting ends on November 29, the campaigns close Friday, and Saturday is the day of reflection, in the final poll account. Mr. Feijóo campaigns in Cádiz today. Settled: the final numbers, in their disagreement. Open: the vote itself, six days out, with no new poll permitted to move it in print.

Maritime and aviation restrictions imposed around the operation had been lifted, and the ship had left both Finland’s economic zone and the area for which Finland was responsible for preventing environmental damage. Finnish Interior Minister Mari Rantanen said the authorities had stabilized the situation and avoided environmental damage, according to Yle. The Russian rescue vessel Murman began towing Sparta II on Sunday afternoon; within Finland’s maritime rescue responsibility area the operation was supported by the patrol vessel Uisko, Estonia’s Kindral Kurvits and rescue tugs operated by Alfons Håkans. The Border Guard said it had not detected fuel or hazardous-substance leaks.
