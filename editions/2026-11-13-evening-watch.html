The oil market is showing tentative signs of rebalancing, with global oil supplies surpassing demand for the first time since the outbreak of the war in Iran. Observed oil inventories rose by 52 million barrels, or 1.7 million barrels a day, in October as Gulf exports recovered much faster than global oil demand. North Sea Dated prices plunged by more than 30 percent from a high of $136 a barrel on October 8 to around $93 at the time of writing, but remained $22 above pre-war levels. Global supplies surged by 2.3 million barrels a day to 105.4 million a day in October, lifting output by 5.3 million a day since August. Fourth-quarter supply estimates were raised by 3.3 million a day; the balances suggest a return to surplus in the fourth quarter, with a more substantial build in 2027, while the severe tightness in product supply, especially diesel, remains the overriding concern.

Australian Associated Press — Europe to have a voice in the peace talks

Europe now looks set to play a role when peace negotiations resume between Russia, Ukraine and the United States, after a round of shuttle diplomacy by Mr. Witkoff and Mr. Kushner, who held talks with President Zelenskyy in Ireland directly after meeting President Putin in the Kremlin. Mr. Kushner indicated on Thursday night that President Putin is willing to accept the bloc’s involvement. The American-brokered session in Abu Dhabi this week brought the first direct discussions between Ukrainian and Russian representatives in nine months; it produced no major breakthrough aside from agreement to swap a large number of prisoners, the exact figure still to be announced. Mr. Kushner said one goal of the Kremlin meeting was to get the Russian leader’s blessing for European representatives to take part, and that the talks are looking at the continent’s broader security arrangements. The United States and President Zelenskyy want Europe to appoint a single person to negotiate with Moscow. Russia did not want European countries involved in the direct negotiations, complaining they finance and supply Ukraine’s military effort. President Putin reaffirmed to the envoys that any European troops sent to Ukraine after a ceasefire would be absolutely unacceptable and would be regarded as a legitimate target.
