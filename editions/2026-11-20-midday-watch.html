Kyiv at Noon: Seven Dead in the Updated Count, Three in the Wire Count, and One Bridge Reopened

The correction of the day is a count, and the desk prints both counts. Mayor Vitali Klitschko’s updated toll, carried in the updated account above, stands at seven people killed and 17 injured in Kyiv, with 16 of the injured in hospital; the Kyiv Patrol Police reported the same figures, seven killed and 17 injured, in the same account. The wire count, filed in the overnight edition and carried again in the wire account above, stands at three people killed and 15 injured in Kyiv, in what the wire calls the biggest attack on routes connecting the left and right banks of the capital since the start of the war. The overnight file’s own breakdown—three killed in Kyiv and two in Lviv, for a headline count of at least five—stands between the two. The desk does not reconcile the three figures at noon; it prints them, in the Cocked Hat, and notes what is common to all of them: the three bridges, the Podilskyi, the Darnytskyi and the Pivdennyi, were damaged, and three of the dead were employees of the city’s water company, Kyivvodokanal, in the wire account.

One fact in the file moved the other way, toward repair. Traffic from the right bank to the left bank on the Podilskyi crossing, suspended in the morning because of the damage, resumed during the day, in the updated account. Damage on the Darnytskyi Bridge was still being assessed by specialists, in the same account, and traffic on the Pivdennyi Bridge remains restricted, as in previous days. The morning’s wider toll stands where the wire left it: five people injured in the Dnipropetrovsk region after drones hit apartment buildings, and four wounded in Kharkiv after drones hit a hospital, in the wire account. The State Emergency Service reported a man injured in a morning drone strike in the Podilskyi district, a passenger car burned, and a shop, nearby buildings and 14 cars damaged, in the updated account, and city services were continuing to deal with the aftermath. Settled: the strike, the three bridges, and the reopening of one crossing. Open: the true count of the dead, which the two counts in the file do not agree on, and the Darnytskyi assessment.

Washington at Noon: A News Conference Convening, and a Root Cause Still Unnamed

The President’s news conference convened at noon at the White House as this edition collected, and the midday file therefore carries its occasion and not its contents. The President is set to hold the conference following his return from a four-day trip to Asia, where he sat down for a three-way meeting with President Xi Jinping and President Vladimir Putin in Shenzhen, in the conference account above; he did not formally take questions while in China at the end of his trip. He said he will be discussing the historic meetings in China as well as trade agreements reached during the earlier part of the trip in the Philippines, in the same account, and, in his own announcement, the trade deals with Thailand, the Philippines and others. On his arrival on Thursday evening he told reporters on the South Lawn to brace for a “really historic” meeting, and described the trip on his social platform as “a great and highly consequential trip,” in the reporters account filed below. The gathering produced a joint declaration pledging dialogue on reducing nuclear risks, maintaining stable energy supplies and reforming the United Nations, but stopped short of announcing major breakthroughs on Ukraine or Iran, in the same account. What the President says at noon—on the declaration, on Kyiv’s bridges, on the strait—is the Evening Watch’s file, and the desk does not anticipate it here.

The week’s other Washington promise stands unkept at noon, and the desk records the correction plainly. The Federal Aviation Administration said on Thursday that the government expected to announce this week the root cause of the November 13 ground stop, as the midnight file carried it. The agency’s updated question-and-answer page, carried in the probe account filed below, names the equipment—it believes it addressed the initial cause by disabling the functionality of the originating Juniper routers, it disabled the same functionality on more than 60 other Juniper routers as a precaution, and it has paused installing them—and still says the agency and L3Harris are investigating the root cause. Hewlett Packard Enterprise, which owns Juniper Networks, said the equipment had operated according to its installed configuration, in the same account. The probe centres on a newly installed router at a remote radio site in El Dorado, Arkansas, where data flooded the network at 1,000 connection attempts every four to five seconds, destabilising 17 of 22 en route centres, in the same account. Bernstein puts four airlines’ bill at $345 million before tax, in the same account. The Senate Commerce Committee is owed its technical answers by November 23. Settled: the equipment, and the precaution. Open: the root cause itself, which no announcement in the midday file supplies.

The Gulf at Noon: Oil Gives Back Its Morning Gain, and the Week Stands at a Third Straight Fall

Oil’s midday reading corrects its morning reading, and the desk prints the turn. Brent crude futures rose in the early hours to $92.22 a barrel, and to $92.77, up 79 cents or 0.9 percent, in the rise account filed below, on supply concern after the Houthi attacks on Riyadh and reports of tanker disruptions in the strait. By the later readings the gain was gone: Brent fell 0.7 percent to $91.36 a barrel and West Texas Intermediate declined 1 percent to $82.40, in the retreat account filed below, as traders assessed improving global supply conditions; a Friday-afternoon European reading put Brent at about $90.50, down roughly 1.6 percent on the day, in the slide account filed below. Both benchmarks are on course for a third straight weekly fall, Brent down about 3.5 percent on the week in the overnight file’s reading and about 5.5 percent in the slide account, and West Texas down about 6 to 7 percent. Brent has fallen more than 20 percent from its late-October high near $118, in the slide account.

The reason the file gives for the fall is flow, not quiet. Oil shipments through major Middle Eastern chokepoints rose 19 percent in the third quarter from the previous three months, regional crude production reached 23.1 million barrels a day in October, the highest since the conflict began, and shipments through the Strait of Hormuz have largely recovered, in the retreat account and the rise account; the Energy Information Administration expects Brent to average $86 a barrel next year as recovering supplies rebuild inventories, in the retreat account, and the International Energy Agency projects an oversupply of 1.7 million barrels a day in the fourth quarter, widening to about 3.8 million next year, in the same account. American commercial crude inventories rose 3.4 million barrels to 417.8 million in the week to November 13, in the rise account. Against the flows stand the strait’s own visible count, which the overnight file carried and the midday file does not supersede: one commercial ship crossed so far on Friday as of 0548 UTC, six on Thursday, and 41 in seven days, about 7 percent of the pre-crisis average, with the status severely restricted. The two readings of the strait—flows recovered through alternative routes and ship-to-ship transfers, and visible transits near shut—stand side by side in the file, and the desk prints both. The OPEC+ ministers meet on November 29. Settled: the settlement’s direction, and the week’s fall. Open: the missing men, the mines, and the November 29 meeting.

The World at Noon: Paris Resumed and Unvoted, and Madrid’s Instruments Unmoved

France’s Senate resumed its examination of the police firearms bill on Friday, and at the midday stamp no vote stands in the file. The Senate suspended its consideration on Thursday evening after sharp tensions, the discussion suspended at 0030, and senators were due to resume on Friday at 1700 Paris time, which is 1100 Eastern, in the suspension account filed below; during the day the upper house is also examining the finance bill for 2027, in the same account. The bill, which has passed the National Assembly, would establish that officers who use their weapons are presumed to have acted within the framework of the law, reversing the burden of proof: it would no longer be for the officer to demonstrate the legality of the shot, but for the complainant to provide proof of improper use, in the same account. The left tried three motions of rejection on Thursday afternoon, then multiplied speeches to delay the ballot, in the same account. Outside the Senate, protesters gathered near the Luxembourg Gardens, in the rally account filed below, and the Defender of Rights said it had received 106 complaints implicating the police, including 39 for armed violence, while the police watchdog had opened 83 inquiries at the end of October, in the suspension account’s companion file. Senators of the Socialist, Ecologist and La France Insoumise groups have said they will jointly refer the bill to the Constitutional Council if adopted. Settled: the resumption, and the text. Open: the vote, which Friday still holds.

Spain’s file stands where the overnight edition left it, and the desk records the standing as a standing. Both camps claimed victory in Thursday night’s debate, the expert panels mostly favoured Mr. Feijóo, and the quick polls split, as the overnight file printed them. The instruments stand unmoved at noon: the People’s Party at 29.5 percent against 27.2 percent for the Socialists in the PF Global poll for POLITICO, and at 32.2 percent and 134 seats against 30.2 percent and 120 in the 40dB tracking, with the two right parties together at 192 seats against 176 needed. Mr. Feijóo travels to the Canary Islands on Friday and to the Valencia bullring on Saturday; a second debate is agreed for around November 26; Spain votes November 29. Settled: the split verdicts. Open: the first post-debate polls, and which instrument Spain believes.
