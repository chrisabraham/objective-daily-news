The Night in Brief: Two Bilaterals Held, a Benchmark Down 4.4 Percent, and Three Tankers Claimed

The night ran its two Shenzhen bilaterals and left the three-way room still waiting, sold Taiwan down 4.4 percent into a regulator’s curbs, and added three unnamed tankers to Tehran’s mine arithmetic. The President and President Xi held their bilateral meeting at the Kylin Villa in Shenzhen on Thursday and the President and President Putin held a one-on-one there, in the summit account filed below; the formal trilateral session stood scheduled for later on Thursday, and no agreement or joint statement had been reported by midday in Shenzhen, in the same account. Taiwan’s benchmark closed down 4.4 percent and its financial regulator announced new short-selling curbs on Thursday, in the wire account filed below. Iran’s army said three more oil tankers caught fire after striking naval mines east of the Strait of Hormuz on Wednesday evening, in the army’s account filed below; no vessel was named in the claim, and no party not making it has confirmed it. The desk takes the files in that order.

What the night settled is the bilaterals, Taiwan’s close and its curbs, and a ratings outlook in New York. What it left open is the rest of the file: the trilateral and its statement, the fire and the missing man in the strait, the radar in Bahrain, and everything Thursday in Washington still holds—a retailer’s report at 0600 Central, claims at 0830 Eastern, and a first trade on the Nasdaq.

Shenzhen at 0400: A Plenary Opened, Two Bilaterals Held, a Hormuz Consensus Claimed on One Wire Chain, and the Trilateral Still Ahead

Thursday in Shenzhen opened in the plenary hall. President Xi opened the APEC leaders’ meeting on Thursday morning and pledged $20 million to support practical cooperation among the member economies, setting out a programme of economy and trade, connectivity, innovation and development, in the pledge account filed below; the plenary began just before 10:00 local time, the leaders posing for a group photograph before taking their seats, in the opening account filed below. President Putin’s plenary words, carried by TASS in the live coverage the summit account draws on, were that the Western sanctions imposed over the invasion of Ukraine had “failed” and that the need was for “unity rather than division, and dialogue rather than ultimatums,” in the summit account above. Leaders were expected to adopt a Shenzhen Declaration before the meeting closed, with Moscow’s support for the draft text confirmed by President Putin, in the pledge account.

The two bilaterals followed, and each produced one sentence that carries the day. At the bilateral meeting with President Xi, held at the Kylin Villa, the President said the two share a strong consensus that oil transport through the Strait of Hormuz should take place, and asked President Xi to do more for American farmers, in the summit account above, which grades the sentence as the President’s, reported by Reuters and carried by Yonhap, on a single wire chain, with no Chinese or American official reaction quoted in that report; the President also called President Xi his friend and said the two leaders have a very special relationship, in the same account. Earlier, the President and President Putin met one on one in Shenzhen, in the same account’s live coverage: the President told President Putin that a lot of wonderful things could come out of the meeting and, in a separate White House video, pointed at him and called him his friend. Against the meeting stands the wire’s reading of the photograph: the two men shared a brief handshake and a few words at a photo opportunity, Russia’s Interfax agency said they did not have a substantive conversation there, and Russian officials said no full-scale bilateral negotiations were planned at the one-on-one, in the wire account filed below. Before the meetings, the President told Fox News he would welcome an agreement with China and Russia to cut nuclear arsenals, put the American stockpile at about 4,000 warheads against 3,800 for Russia and 400 to 500 for China, and said he would accept inspections in all three countries, in the summit account.

The three-way room itself had not reported by the night’s last bearing. The formal trilateral session stood scheduled for later on Thursday and no agreement or joint statement had been reported by midday in Shenzhen, in the summit account; the Kremlin aide Mr. Ushakov said on Monday that the three countries were in intensive consultations over a draft joint statement and that Russia had conveyed its position to China, and it remained unclear whether a statement would be adopted, in the Yonhap account filed below. The expectations printed ahead of the session stand unamended: no major agreements expected, in the wire account and in the Yalta account filed below, which records that no agenda has been published and no joint statement announced. On Ukraine, the signals conflict in the open: Washington had signalled the war was not expected to be addressed in the three-way meeting before the President said it would, in the summit account, and White House officials have said he is not expected to raise it there, in the wire account. The reactions from outside the room stand in the summit account: Ukraine’s foreign minister, Mr. Sybiga, said in Kyiv he hoped Presidents Xi and Trump would put “pressure on Russia to end the war,” and Canada’s Prime Minister, Mr. Carney, in Shenzhen for the summit, said sanctions on Russia would “continue to increase as long as this war goes on.” On the fourth chair, no meeting with Mr. Kim is planned on this trip in American officials’ repeated words, in the wire account. After the trilateral, the President is scheduled to depart Shenzhen for Washington, in the same account. Settled: the plenary, the pledge, and both bilaterals. Open: the room, and what, if anything, it signs.

Asia at 0400: Taiwan Down 4.4 Percent at the Close and 7.22 on the Smaller Board, Tokyo and Seoul Higher, Hong Kong Down Two, and Australia’s Jobless Rate at 4.9

Asia’s Thursday session split on the same line as Wednesday’s: the chip markets that host the trade fell or rose with it, and Taiwan fell hardest. Taiwan’s benchmark ended down 4.4 percent, in the wire account above, at 38,426.89 in the exchange account filed below, which carries the island’s smaller-company index, the TPEx, down 7.22 percent at 285.33, Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing down 3.45 percent and MediaTek down 5.58 percent in the reports it cites. The session’s path ran through a fall of 3.73 percent at 0351 GMT in the early account filed below and a drop of as much as 4.8 percent in the slide account filed below; the closing figures printed elsewhere stand apart from the wire’s and are carried in the Cocked Hat. Japan’s Nikkei 225 rose 0.70 percent to the 59,450 level and South Korea’s Kospi rose 0.55 percent to about 5,095.30, while Hong Kong’s Hang Seng fell 2.19 percent to about 22,790 and the Shanghai Composite fell 0.69 percent to 3,563.56, in the divergence account filed below; a second account of the chip markets puts the Nikkei up about 1 percent over 59,334, with SoftBank Group and Tokyo Electron each up about 3 percent, the Kospi up 0.79 percent at 5,079.54, SK Hynix up 4.19 percent and Samsung Electronics up 2.31 percent, and the Hang Seng down 1.12 percent at 22,816.43 with its technology index down 2.5 percent to a four-month low, in the rally account filed below. The two readings are successive and rival at once, and both stand.

Taiwan’s answer came from its regulator the same day. The Financial Supervisory Commission said on Thursday it will enact new short-selling curbs after the benchmark’s fall, in the wire account above: where a stock closes down 3.5 percent or more on a given day, investors may not sell it short the next trading day, through margin short selling or through borrowed securities. The island has already put a $15 billion stock stabilisation fund into operation, alongside previously announced curbs, in the same account; the regulator noted sharp swings in share prices and said stock fundamentals remain fairly solid, listed companies have posted record revenue, and the capital market is resilient, in the same account. The wire’s sentence for the cause stands as its sentence: investors unnerved by the future of artificial intelligence after Mr. Huang resigned as Nvidia’s chief, in the same account.

The day’s two data prints came from Canberra and Tokyo. Australia’s seasonally adjusted unemployment rate jumped to 4.9 percent in October from 4.7 percent, and total employment dropped by 43,500 to a five-month low of 14.74 million, in the divergence account above; the Australian dollar slipped below $0.67 to its lowest level since December, and Australia’s S&P/ASX 200 fell 1.04 percent to 8,730.40, in the same account. Japan’s core machinery orders slipped 0.3 percent on the month in September, against a forecast fall of 0.5 percent, and Governor Ueda told a parliamentary committee that underlying inflation must remain anchored around 2 percent and that the Bank of Japan will continue raising interest rates as economic and price conditions warrant, in the same account; the yen traded around 159.4. Settled: Taiwan’s close, its curbs, and both prints. Open: whether the curbs meet a second falling day on Friday.

The agency’s senior vice-president, Mr. Joshi, said an upgrade is unlikely over the next 12 to 18 months despite the company’s strong market position and finances, in the agency account, which also carries the agency’s projections—revenue up 96 percent in fiscal 2027 and $746 billion in fiscal 2028—and its figure of $118 billion in guarantees, chiefly connected to a lease guarantee for a data centre campus in Portsmouth, Ohio. The guarantee figure stands against the $164.5 billion of total financial commitments the week’s file carried from the company’s own report, and the two are printed apart in the Cocked Hat. The cost of protecting the company’s debt against default was quoted at 121 basis points late Wednesday, about 20 basis points lower than a week earlier, in the outlook account filed below. The interim chief, Ms. Kress, said on Tuesday the company would maintain a “disciplined” and “rigorous” approach to future investments, in the same account. No word from the board on a permanent successor stands in the overnight file.

The Gulf at 0400: Three More Tankers Claimed on Mines East of the Strait, None Named, and No Word on the Radar for a Sixth Day

The strait’s arithmetic grew again on Wednesday evening, on a claim. Iran’s army said three violating tankers caught fire and ground to a halt after striking naval mines in the eastern Strait of Hormuz on Wednesday, after entering an unauthorized maritime area despite repeated warnings, with their navigation systems switched off, in the army’s account above; a companion account attributes the same evening claim to the Guards, relayed through the state broadcaster, and carries the sentence that passage through the waterway must be coordinated with Iranian authorities, in the claim account filed below. The claim arrived with no vessel name, flag, owner or cargo, no casualty figure and no position more precise than east of the strait, and Reuters could not independently confirm it, in the claim audit filed below, which sets the week’s claims in a table against what has been confirmed and records that the evening claim may be one announcement reported two ways, by the Guards and by the regular army, the two naval forces Tehran fields. The wire’s agency version stands in the agency account filed below: three more oil tankers struck mines and caught fire, the ships unable to manoeuvre, the claims not independently verified at this stage. The desk prints the claim as a claim, at its full size and with its full absence of names.

The named ship in the strait gained no ending in the night. Searches continued on Wednesday to locate the missing seafarer from the Tara Gas and officials in Oman were still trying to put out the fire aboard her, in the wire account filed below; the night’s fullest restatement of the ship’s file, published at 0003 UTC on Thursday, carries the same position—24 of 25 crew rescued, 21 Indian seafarers ashore at Khasab, two in hospital, one still missing, the fire’s footage widely shared and not independently verified—in the restatement account filed below. The injury counts stand apart as they have stood: eleven injured in the operator’s statement, two in hospital in the authorities’, unblended. On the mechanism that struck the ships, the file’s split stands where the week left it: mines in Tehran’s accounts, an unknown projectile in the maritime centre’s report of the Tuesday strike, in the claim audit above.

Bahrain and Kuwait took Wednesday’s round, and the radar question took another day of silence. Bahrain’s defence force reported it had intercepted another round of Iranian aerial attacks on Wednesday and its interior ministry issued two more threat alerts—the fourth attack wave on Bahrain in six days—and Iran’s regular army said on Wednesday its drones had targeted Patriot systems and launchers at the Ali al-Salem base in Kuwait, with no casualties or damage confirmed in the initial reporting, in the Bahrain account filed below. Qatar’s foreign ministry condemned the renewed attacks on Bahrain and Kuwait in its November 18 statement as violations of their sovereignty, in the condemnation account filed below, which also carries Bahrain’s call for full implementation of Security Council Resolution 2817. On the radar the Guards said they destroyed on November 13, no assessment of the machine itself—hit, missed or intact—stands from any capital in the overnight file, for a sixth day; the intercepts are reported, the machine is not, and the desk records the silence as a silence. Settled: Wednesday’s intercepts, and the condemnation. Open: the fire, the missing man, the three claimed tankers, and the radar.

The fine print, in brief. Objective, as this paper uses the word, means the desk is not driven by an agenda, is not an advocate, is not a politician, is not modeling behavior, and is not training up citizens: it is reporting the news as if it were objective. Subjective means the item comes from a source with its own position, as all sources have; it is printed because it is useful information, weighed by the reader. Objectivity is relative; everything is relative, even relativity is relative; absolutism is wrong. No source in these pages is more reputable than a press release issued directly from Langley.
