The Consumer at Midday: Three Beats, Three Tariff Refunds, and a Housing Print That Points Both Ways

The morning’s three retailers reported before the bell, and the midday file reads the three reports as one document. Target earned $2.65 a share against $2.05 expected, on revenue of $27.1 billion against $26.49 billion expected, with comparable sales up 5.6 percent against 3.8 expected—its best sales growth in five years, driven by a 5 percent rise in traffic while the average ticket rose 0.6 percent—in the signals account and the watch account filed below. Inside the beat stands a $301 million tariff refund, worth about $0.50 a share in the watch account; the company raised its full-year outlook, to earnings of $10.65 to $11.40 in the watch account and by 1 percent in the premarket account. Lowe’s earned $3.10 a share against $2.86 to $2.90 expected, on sales of $22.76 billion, up 9.3 percent, with comparable sales up 1.8 percent against 0.4 expected and online sales up 17.4 percent, in the same accounts; tariff refunds added $0.34 a share in the watch account and about $250 million in the signals account, and gross margin fell 124 basis points to 32.95 percent even with them. TJX earned $1.34 a share adjusted, a penny ahead in the premarket account and in line in the watch account, which also prints $1.39 unadjusted, on sales of $16.0 billion, up 6 percent, with comparable sales up 4 percent against its own plan of 2 to 3 percent, in the same accounts; it raised its full-year comparable-sales guide to 4 percent and guided its fourth quarter below the estimate. The common sentence in the three reports is the refund: strip it out, in the signals account’s arithmetic, and margins fell.

The morning’s other official print pointed in both directions at once. Housing starts rose 9.9 percent in October to an annual rate of 1.309 million, against a consensus of 1.245 to 1.25 million, while building permits fell 7.4 percent to 1.295 million, against 1.377 to 1.38 million expected, in the starts account, the wire account and the data account filed below. Single-family starts rose 4.0 percent to 864,000; single-family permits fell 8.7 percent to 772,000. The Census Bureau’s own caution stands in the starts account: the 90 percent interval on the monthly change is plus or minus 16.8 points and includes zero. The rate that prices the houses stands in the wire account: the 30-year fixed mortgage averaged 7.18 percent last week, up 120 basis points since the war began in late February, and builder sentiment in October fell to its lowest since April 2012 in the builders’ survey of Tuesday. Walmart reports Thursday, in the signals account. Settled: the consumer spent, and the refunds flattered the spending’s keepers. Open: permits, which are the forward half of the print.

Shenzhen at Midday: Free Trade From the Host, Sanctions From the Guest, a Fifth Meeting of the Two, and a Photograph of the Three

The two then met on Wednesday, their fifth meeting this year, with the Chinese readout emphasising the durability of the relationship and the carrying-out of commitments already reached, and Mr. Xi placing the meeting at the thirtieth anniversary of the two countries’ strategic partnership, in the bilateral account filed below.

The Gulf at Midday: One Ship Where the Morning Printed Two Statements, Eleven Injured Against Two in Hospital, and a Search Continuing

The morning file printed two statements side by side and left the identity of the ship between them unresolved. The midday accounts join them. The shipping account filed below states that the Tara Gas is operated by ADNOC Logistics and Services, the operator whose Wednesday statement reported one crew member missing and eleven injured on an unnamed vessel; the wire account filed below carries the two statements in one story—the operator’s, and Oman’s emergency authorities’ report that they rescued 24 crew members from the Panama-flagged Tara Gas while the search continued for one missing person. The databases’ entries stand in the wire account as they stand: the ship listed under the ownership of a Marshall Islands company and the management of an Emirates-based firm. The desk prints the join as the accounts make it: one ship, the Tara Gas, struck twice—first on Monday about seven nautical miles north of Khasab in the British agency’s report, and again on Tuesday, when the crew of 25 abandoned her—and now drifting about 4.5 nautical miles southwest of Jazirat Um Alganam in the navigational warning of November 17 carried in the shipping account.

The injury count did not join. On the Bahrain radar, the file gains nothing for a fifth day: no assessment of the machine from any capital stands in the midday file, and the desk records the silence as a silence. Settled: the ship has one name in the midday file, and one operator. Open: the injured count, the missing man, and the radar.

The fine print, in brief. Objective, as this paper uses the word, means the desk is not driven by an agenda, is not an advocate, is not a politician, is not modeling behavior, and is not training up citizens: it is reporting the news as if it were objective. Subjective means the item comes from a source with its own position, as all sources have; it is printed because it is useful information, weighed by the reader. Objectivity is relative; everything is relative, even relativity is relative; absolutism is wrong. No source in these pages is more reputable than a press release issued directly from Langley.
