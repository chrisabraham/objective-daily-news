The Midday in Brief: A Declaration Signed, a Debut Indicated, and a Beat Sold

The midday’s corrections run against the morning’s expectations in Shenzhen and with them in New York. The three presidents met for one hour and 45 minutes in Shenzhen and signed a four-point declaration, in the outcome account filed below: reform of the United Nations, more refined petroleum products, limits on the International Criminal Court’s reach, and a dialogue on reducing nuclear risks. The declaration names neither Ukraine nor Iran, sets no arsenal cuts and no timetable, and announces no lifting of sanctions, in the declaration account filed below. In New York, Anthropic’s shares were indicated to open at $100 against an $83 offering price, in the indication account filed below, and the first trade’s price stood unsettled at noon, with the morning’s accounts disagreeing on when the trade would come. Walmart reported revenue of $188.8 billion and adjusted earnings of $0.65 a share, both ahead of the estimates the overnight file carried, and raised its full-year guidance, in the earnings account filed below; its shares slipped about 1 percent after the report, in the morning account filed below. Jobless claims printed 203,000 against 197,000 to 198,000 expected, and the Philadelphia Fed index fell to 6.3 from 20.9, in the claims account filed below. The desk takes the files in that order.

What the midday settled is the declaration’s text, the retailer’s quarter, and the morning’s two prints. What it left open is the rest of the file: the first trade of ANT, the evening’s debate in Madrid, the Senate’s bill in Paris, and the fire and the missing man in the strait.

Shenzhen at 1200: Four Points Signed in One Hour and 45 Minutes, Neither War Named, and a Truce Extension Reported Without a Length

The room the overnight file left waiting has now met, signed, and emptied. The three presidents met for about one hour and 45 minutes at the Kylin Villa in Shenzhen, on the sidelines of the APEC summit, after starting nearly two hours behind schedule, in the outcome account above; the session had been due to start at 2:30 p.m. local time, began at 4:16 p.m. and ended at 6:01 p.m. The joint declaration was published at 6:55 p.m. by China’s state news agency Xinhua, in the same account, and the text was published by the Kremlin and by China’s foreign ministry, in the declaration account. The President left Shenzhen for Washington soon after the talks ended, without speaking to reporters, in the outcome account. The declaration is the first thing the three leaders have ever signed together, in the same account, and it settles none of the wars or the arms questions that filled the agenda.

The four points stand as printed. First, the three countries, as permanent members of the United Nations Security Council, committed to reform the United Nations so it can respond more effectively to global challenges and conform to its fundamental mission of international peace and security. Second, they plan to increase the production and supply of refined petroleum products for the benefit of all nations, and reaffirmed the importance of stable energy flows. Third, they recognised that the principle of state consent and respect for the immunities of state officials are of paramount importance, and concurred that the International Criminal Court and other similar bodies must not abuse their jurisdiction. Fourth, they reaffirmed that a nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought, and will conduct a dialogue on reducing nuclear risks and preventing the proliferation of nuclear weapons. The full text stands in the live account filed below. What the text does not carry is stated in the accounts with the same plainness: it does not specify an agreement to reduce arsenals, numerical targets or a disarmament timetable, in the declaration account; it provides no volumes or implementation dates on the energy point and announces no lifting of sanctions, in the same account; and it contains no agreement to end the war in Ukraine or Iran, or a new tariff arrangement, in the same account. Kyiv was not mentioned in the joint declaration, in the statement account filed below, and the Kremlin’s spokesman, Mr. Peskov, said Ukraine was not the main topic of the leaders’ discussions, in the press account filed below.

Beside the signed text stands a reported agreement that no side has put in writing in the file. The United States and China agreed to extend their trade truce, decided in a private meeting between the President and President Xi, according to the Globe and Mail, CNBC and Bloomberg, in the outcome account; neither side said how long the new extension runs, or on what terms, in the same account, and the Chinese readout mentioned no extension details, in the same account. The truce agreed in Busan in October 2025 had been due to run until January 10, 2027, in the same account. The bilateral that produced it stands in the overnight file: the President said the two share a strong consensus that oil transport through the Strait of Hormuz should take place, and asked President Xi to do more for American farmers. The other bilateral stands in the midday file with its subject named by the Kremlin aide, Mr. Ushakov: the war in Ukraine was the main topic of the preceding 40-minute meeting between the President and President Putin, in the Kyiv account filed below, which also carries the President’s greeting to President Putin on entering the room—“Who is he? He’s my friend.”

President Putin spoke after the room, and his words run the other way from the declaration’s. He said Russia would retaliate for Ukrainian strikes on the bridge linking Russia and Crimea and that Moscow’s response would be “much more painful,” in the press account above, and that after repeated attacks on the bridge, “no negotiation will take place” on a possible ceasefire, in the same account; Ukraine has attacked the bridge three times in the past week, in the live account above. He said the crossing is used only for civilian purposes and not to transport military equipment or petroleum products, in the press account, and described the Shenzhen discussion as “open” and “very useful,” in the same account. He defended the comparison of the meeting to the Yalta Conference, saying there was nothing wrong with either event and that Yalta merely codified the existing reality, in the Yalta account filed below, and said he would consider attending the December Group of 20 meeting in Miami after discussing it with the President, depending on “the situation in Russia,” in the press account. Ukraine’s foreign minister, Mr. Sybiha, mocked the comparison, writing that Shenzhen is not the Yalta of President Putin’s dreams and that Russia has practically lost its bridge to the real Yalta, in the Yalta account. The declaration’s most substantial point, in the outcome account’s reading, is its last one: a promise to talk about reducing nuclear risks, with no date set for the dialogue it promises and nothing said about when, or whether, the three will meet again. Settled: the text, the hour, and the departure. Open: the dialogue’s date, the truce’s length, and everything the declaration left unnamed.

Berlin at 1200: The Allies Meet Hours After Shenzhen, With Air Defence First and a Funding Hole Still Open

While the declaration was being published in Shenzhen, the other room was assembling in Berlin. More than 30 leaders gathered there on Thursday for a meeting of the Coalition of the Willing, hosted by German Chancellor Friedrich Merz with President Macron of France and Prime Minister Andy Burnham of Britain, in the Berlin account filed below, and President Zelensky arrived for the summit after President Putin had wrapped up his press conference in Shenzhen, in the live account above. The American envoys, Mr. Witkoff and Mr. Kushner, attended, in the same account, and President Zelensky said they had promised to brief Kyiv on the President’s discussions in China, in the Kyiv account above. His priorities, stated on his arrival, were strengthening air defence, energy and financial support, and diplomatic efforts to end the conflict, in the live account. The meeting’s agenda covers long-term security guarantees and continued military and financial support, in the Berlin account, and German officials said it would focus on reaffirming a commitment to robust and legally binding security guarantees, which would come into force following a ceasefire, in the wire account filed below.

The money stands in the file as pledges and as a hole. The European Commission proposed an additional 45 billion euros in financing for Ukraine in 2027, in the funding account filed below, and a 25 billion-euro hole in Ukraine’s 2027 budget remains open even if that loan passes, in the same account. Germany and Norway each announced $300 million for NATO’s Prioritised Ukraine Requirements List, a programme that purchases American weapons for Kyiv, with the joint contribution including Patriot missiles, in the same account; Prime Minister Burnham announced a £260 million winter support package, including 1,000 long-range strike drones, in the same account, and Britain will announce long-range cruise missiles capable of reaching targets inside Russia beyond the front lines, in the Berlin account. President Zelensky has said Kyiv needs 40 additional F-16 fighter jets, in the funding account, and has asked European partners to release Patriot and IRIS-T interceptors from existing stocks. The war itself did not wait for either room: a Russian drone crashed near the Dutch Embassy in Kyiv on Thursday, damaging cars, a security post and shattering windows, in the Kyiv account, with a second drone circling above the embassy neutralised, in the live account; it is not yet clear whether the drone targeted the area or was downed, in the Kyiv account. The Dutch arms manufacturer Destinus had said its Ruta missiles were used in Ukraine’s strikes on the Kerch Bridge, in the live account. Settled: the attendance, the pledges, and the priorities. Open: the hole, the guarantees, and what the envoys carry back from Shenzhen.

The Markets at 1200: A Morning Bought on the Debut, With the First Trade’s Price Still Unsettled

The morning file carried the futures; the midday file carries New York’s morning. At 0931 Eastern the S&P 500 stood at 7,397.35, up 1.01 percent, the Nasdaq Composite at 24,809.76, up 1.55 percent, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average at 50,433.9, up 0.47 percent, in the morning account filed below. A later reading, at about 1008 Eastern, put the S&P 500 up 1.13 percent, the Dow up 0.30 percent and the Nasdaq 100 up 2.00 percent, in the climb account filed below, which carries the day’s direction plainly: the indexes are sharply higher after Anthropic priced its record offering, reviving optimism in artificial-intelligence infrastructure spending. The pre-open had pointed the same way: Nasdaq-100 futures up 2 percent, S&P 500 futures up 1.1 percent and Dow futures up 299 points, or 0.6 percent, at 0848 Eastern, in the pre-open account filed below. Against Wednesday’s close—the Dow at 50,198.17, down 561.25 points, the S&P 500 at 7,323.63, down 0.23 percent for a fifth straight decline, and the Nasdaq at 24,429.88, up 0.42 percent—the morning reads as a reversal of the week’s direction, not yet as its settlement.

The debut is the morning’s event, and at noon its price is still a set of indications. Anthropic priced 980 million shares at $83 on Wednesday, above the $70 to $78 range, raising about $81.3 billion, with a further $26 billion private placement at the same price, in the debut account filed below. Early indications pointed to an open at $100 a share, roughly 20 percent above the offering price, in the indication account above and in the surge account filed below; a second reading put the expected open at $99.60, and a third at $103, 24 percent above the price, with grey-market pricing in a $100 to $107 range, in the debut account. The pre-open account puts the first trade at around noon to 1 p.m. Eastern, once the opening auction clears, in the pre-open account; the debut account, published at 0958 Eastern, says the shares began trading on the Nasdaq under ANT, that the exchange made them eligible for trading from 10 a.m., and that the first trade came out of the opening auction. The two timings stand apart in the Cocked Hat, and the desk prints no first-trade price at noon, because no account in the file carries one it can stand behind. The chief executive, Mr. Amodei, and the president, Ms. Amodei, rang the opening bell, in the debut account. The chip file followed the debut up: Seagate up more than 7 percent, Intel, SanDisk and Western Digital up more than 6 percent, and Alphabet up more than 4 percent, in the climb account, with Nvidia up more than 3 percent in the same account—against the quote account’s $152.96 at 1057 Eastern, up 1.32 percent from Wednesday’s $150.96 close, on an open of $152.92 and a high of $158.13, in the climb account’s own figures. The ten-year Treasury yield stood at 5.08 to 5.11 percent in the morning’s readings, in the pre-open account and in the morning account above. Settled: the morning’s direction, and the indications. Open: the first trade’s price, which will set the market price the $83 offering price only proposes.

The Consumer and the Data at 1200: A Beat and a Slip, Claims at 203,000, the Philadelphia Fed at 6.3, and the Leading Index Down

Walmart’s quarter beat the estimates the overnight file carried and the market sold it anyway. Revenue rose 5.2 percent to $188.8 billion, against the $187.24 billion the overnight file carried as consensus and the $187.27 billion in the earnings accounts, and adjusted earnings came in at $0.65 a share, up from $0.62 a year earlier and ahead of the $0.64 consensus, in the earnings account above and in the wire account filed below. The company’s American net sales reached $125.0 billion, up 3.6 percent, with comparable sales up 2.9 percent excluding fuel, in the earnings account; global e-commerce sales rose 17 percent, the American figure 23 percent, marketplace sales about 50 percent, and the advertising arm grew 36 percent, in the same account. Net income fell 19.5 percent to $4.95 billion, and GAAP earnings were $0.62 a share, in the trade account filed below, on investment losses against the prior year’s gains. The company raised its full-year guidance for the third time this year, in the same account: adjusted earnings of $2.84 to $2.89 a share, up from $2.80 to $2.87, and constant-currency net sales growth of 4.9 to 5.2 percent, in the earnings account. The shares were down about 1 percent after the report, in the morning account above. The correction to the overnight file is a small one and the desk prints it: the consensus the file carried was $187.24 billion on revenue and $0.64 on the share; the print beat both, and the shares fell regardless.

The morning’s two prints both came in softer than the forecasts. Initial jobless claims rose to 203,000 in the week ending November 14, up 1,000 from a revised 202,000, against forecasts of 197,000 to 198,000, in the claims account above; continuing claims jumped 70,000 to 1.763 million in the week ending November 7, from a revised 1.693 million and against about 1.703 million expected, in the same account, with the four-week average of initial claims at 197,750, in the release account filed below. The Philadelphia Fed’s manufacturing index fell to 6.3 in November from 20.9 in October, its lowest reading since May, against about 20 expected, in the claims account; new orders turned negative at minus 0.4, from 4.3, while the prices-paid index jumped to 58.2 from 43.2, in the market account filed below—slower factories with faster-rising costs, in the same account’s reading. The Conference Board’s Leading Economic Index fell 0.3 percent in October to 99.3, against no change expected and a flat September, in the Board’s release filed below, mostly on fewer building permits, in the same release; the coincident index was unchanged at 115.1 and the lagging index unchanged at 121.3, in the same release, and the leading index rose 0.1 percent over the six months to October, in the summary account filed below. For the Federal Reserve, the pricing stands where the midnight file left it: a quarter-point hike in December priced near 95 percent, a half-point near one in ten, in the market account, with one bank’s economists reported to see hikes at the next three meetings, in the claims account’s reading. Settled: the beat, the slip, and the three prints. Open: whether the soft data moves December’s size, or only its argument.

The Gulf at 1200: Wednesday’s Round Stands, the Fire Has No Thursday Word, and Oil Barely Moves on the Declaration

Thursday has added nothing to the Gulf file at noon, and the desk records the silence as a silence. Wednesday’s round stands where the overnight file left it. Iran’s military said its drones targeted Patriot systems and launchers at the Ali al-Salem base in Kuwait on Wednesday, in the Gulf account filed below; Kuwait’s military said its air defences intercepted the drones and reported no damage, in the Kuwait account filed below, and Kuwait’s foreign ministry called the attack criminal, in the same account. Bahrain’s defence force said it intercepted another round of Iranian aerial attacks on Wednesday, its fourth in six days, in the Gulf account. The base at Ali al-Salem has been struck at least 30 times in the war, damaging or destroying at least 15 aircraft hangars, in an Associated Press analysis of satellite imagery carried in the wire account filed below, and a broader review found more than 115 drone or missile strikes had damaged or destroyed structures at 26 military bases and other sites holding American equipment, in the expansion account filed below. The sequence stands as the accounts state it: an American strike on a missile site on Qeshm Island on Tuesday, itself an answer to an Iranian strike on an American radar installation in Bahrain, answered on Wednesday in Kuwait and Bahrain.

In the strait, the named ship has no Thursday word in the midday file. The Tara Gas stands where the overnight file left her: 24 of 25 crew rescued, 21 Indian seafarers ashore at Khasab, two in hospital, one still missing, the fire still being fought on Wednesday, in the restatement account filed below; Oman evacuated the 24 about two nautical miles off the Musandam Peninsula, in the rescue account filed below. A further tanker was struck by an unknown projectile in its engine room about six nautical miles north of Khasab on Wednesday evening, at 1645 UTC, in the Gulf account above. The three tankers Iran’s army said caught fire after striking mines east of the strait on Wednesday evening stand unnamed and unconfirmed, in the claim account filed below, which attributes the same evening claim to the Guards. On the radar the Guards said they destroyed on November 13, no assessment of the machine itself stands from any capital in the midday file; the intercepts are reported, the machine is not. Oil barely moved on the declaration’s refined-products pledge: Brent rose 0.18 percent and American crude 0.15 percent on the pledge, which set no production targets, delivery dates or distribution details, in the oil account filed below, and Brent stood at $92.19, up 24 cents or 0.26 percent, with West Texas at $83.31, up 5 cents, at 0953 GMT, in the wire account filed below, against Wednesday’s settlement of $91.95 and $83.26. The wire account also carries the week’s other oil figure: Ukrainian drone attacks have reduced Russian oil refineries’ combined crude processing volumes by 45 percent since late October. Settled: Wednesday’s intercepts, and the morning’s prices. Open: the fire, the missing man, the three claimed tankers, and the radar.

France and Spain at 1200: The Retirees March Into the Afternoon, the Senate Takes the Police Bill, and the Debate Stands at 2200

France’s Thursday is under way and its counts are not yet in the file. The Prime Minister, Mr. Lecornu, holds talks with political parties about the protests on Thursday, in the day-ahead account filed below, and the contested police bill, which would give officers a presumption of legitimate self-defence when they use firearms, is debated in the Senate on Thursday, in the same account and in the protests account. Tuesday’s counts stand behind the day: the interior ministry counted 146,000 demonstrators nationwide, including 28,000 in Paris, against more than 500,000 in the unions’ count, in the day-ahead account, and coordinated cost-of-living demonstrations are called in seven European countries for November 20, 21 and 22, in the same account. Settled: the day’s order, and Tuesday’s counts. Open: the march’s size, and the Senate’s vote.

Spain’s Thursday is one room, at 2200, and the instruments ahead of it have moved a fraction. The fine print, in brief. Objective, as this paper uses the word, means the desk is not driven by an agenda, is not an advocate, is not a politician, is not modeling behavior, and is not training up citizens: it is reporting the news as if it were objective. Subjective means the item comes from a source with its own position, as all sources have; it is printed because it is useful information, weighed by the reader. Objectivity is relative; everything is relative, even relativity is relative; absolutism is wrong. No source in these pages is more reputable than a press release issued directly from Langley.
