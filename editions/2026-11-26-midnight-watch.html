The Midnight in Brief: What Wednesday Settled, What It Left Open

Wednesday settled its figures, its close, and its peak travel day—and left the holiday itself to a storm, the strait to two counts that do not meet, and a sunken corvette to a ministry that has not spoken in five days. The futures are up in the night: Dow futures up 0.4 percent, S&P 500 futures up 0.7 percent and Nasdaq-100 futures up 0.9 percent in the overnight account filed below, with the cash market shut Thursday for Thanksgiving and a half session to come on Friday. The morning’s full slate of figures stands printed and unrevised: core inflation at 3.3 percent, growth revised to 4.3 percent, claims at 189,000. In the strait, Tehran’s services claimed two more tankers on mines on Wednesday, and a third in a second service’s account, against four crossings in the visible counter. In Ukraine, the night’s work was against the power grid: a thermal plant halted, a gas site burning in Poltava, five regions without power in places, and the Zaporizhzhia nuclear plant cut from external power for the 17th time this year. In Benghazi’s file, Kyiv’s security service has now formally confirmed the operation and named its method, and two Libyan officers are reported dead of their wounds.

What Wednesday settled is the data slate entire; the whipsaw close, taken back from a fall of more than 1,100 Dow points to a gain at the bell; Tuesday’s 52,000 flights, the most in a single day in 16 years; and the Benghazi operation’s authorship, in Kyiv’s own formal word. What it left open is Friday’s half session and the rally attempt it may confirm; the December 9 meeting, for which the market now prices a rate increase at 97.2 percent; Thanksgiving Day under a coastal storm; the tankers Tehran names by number and never by name; the response to the refinery strikes, promised in Moscow’s word as inevitable; and the Russian defence ministry’s account of its corvette, unissued into a fifth day.

The Markets at Midnight: A Whipsaw Close, and the Futures Up Into a Closed Market

The close stands as the Evening Watch printed it: the Dow up 164.25 points at 50,182.95 after falling more than 1,100 points in the morning, the S&P 500 down 0.25 percent and the Nasdaq down 0.62 percent, in the last full session before the holiday. The overnight account adds the session’s shape: the Nasdaq fell more than 4 percent at its low before cutting the loss, the Dow and the Russell 2000 both staged upside reversals, and Wednesday was the third day of a rally attempt that stands valid while the indexes hold above last Friday’s lows. The night’s futures rose in the one full reading—Dow up 0.4 percent, S&P 500 up 0.7 percent, Nasdaq-100 up 0.9 percent—and stood near flat in an earlier evening reading, in the steady account filed below, taken at 1814 Eastern. There is no session Thursday to receive them: the stock and bond markets are closed for Thanksgiving, Dow futures trade until 1300 Eastern and reopen at 1800, and Friday’s half session is the next cash verdict.

The chip file closed mixed and argued. Nvidia fell 3.9 percent on Wednesday after 5.9 percent on Tuesday, and stands up 18.9 percent for the week of Mr. Huang’s return. Micron fell 4.5 percent and undercut Friday’s lows. Anthropic rose 1.1 percent to $82.79, reversing from a record low of $73.85 in the session. The overnight account describes that close as back above the $83 offering price; $82.79 is below $83, and the desk prints the figure and the description side by side, as filed, in the Cocked Hat below. United States crude rose 2 percent to $83.68 a barrel in the New York reading, against Brent’s settle at $94.61 in the evening’s file, and the 10-year Treasury yield fell nearly six basis points to 4.845 percent. Settled: the close and the calendar. Open: whether Friday confirms the rally attempt, on holiday volume.

The Figures at Midnight: 3.3 Percent, 4.3 Percent, and 189,000—the Slate Stands Whole

Wednesday’s 0830 slate was the week’s fullest, and at midnight every number in it stands as printed at midday, confirmed in the evening accounts. October’s PCE price index rose 0.3 percent in the month and 3.8 percent from a year earlier, matching the 12-month high last set in May; the core index, without food and energy, rose 0.3 percent in the month and 3.3 percent on the year, against 3.2 percent expected and 3.1 percent in September—the largest annual core increase since November 2023, in the figures account filed below. The second estimate of third-quarter product rose to a 4.3 percent annual rate, up nine-tenths from the first estimate of 3.4 percent and the strongest since the third quarter of 2025. Personal income rose 0.7 percent against 0.4 percent expected, spending rose 0.3 percent, durable goods rose 0.2 percent with core capital goods up 1.2 percent, and initial jobless claims fell to 189,000 against 200,000 expected, with continuing claims at 1.697 million.

The slate’s meaning for December is priced, not decided. The Beige Book, printed at 1800 in the Evening Watch, counts eight districts growing, costs outrunning prices, and wages up a notch, on information collected on or before November 16. The market’s measure, in the data account filed below, puts the probability of a rate increase at the December 9 meeting at 97.2 percent, down from 100 percent on Tuesday—the week’s only softening, and a small one. Yields fell on the day the hot figures printed: the two-year down 3.7 basis points to 4.66 percent and the 10-year down 5.8 to 4.87 percent in that account, against 4.845 percent for the 10-year in the overnight market account. Settled: the figures. Open: the meeting, twelve days out, and the November jobs report on December 4 that stands between the desk and it.

Thanksgiving at Midnight: 52,000 Flights Flown, and the Storm Still Coming Up the Coast

The travel file closes its peak and opens its holiday in the same hour. More than 52,000 flights operated nationwide on Tuesday, the most in a single day in 16 years, Secretary Duffy said on Wednesday, in the traffic account filed below; weather caused 50.5 percent of Tuesday’s delays and volume 24.1 percent. The Administration’s forecast for the nine days from November 23 through December 1 stands at about 417,000 flights, the busiest Thanksgiving in the skies since before the 2010s. On the ground, AAA projects 81.5 million travelers at least 50 miles from home between Tuesday and Monday, nearly 73 million of them by car and close to 6 million by air, and the security administration expects to screen about 17.6 million passengers in the same span, in the travel account filed below.

Wednesday’s own count, at mid-morning, stood at more than 1,100 delays and several dozen cancellations nationwide, with peak security waits of roughly 80 minutes at Miami and 40 minutes at Newark and Portland. Miami expects its busiest Thanksgiving ever, about 1.95 million passengers across its 11-day period, and the waiver map now covers Miami and nine Northeast and Mid-Atlantic airports for travel on Thursday, in the Miami account filed below. What the holiday itself holds is weather: a strong coastal storm tracking up the Eastern Seaboard from Wednesday into Thanksgiving Day, heavy rain, gusty winds and coastal flooding from the Carolinas through New England, with reduced arrival and departure rates possible at the New York airports on Thursday and into Friday morning—and, in the Miami account, the open question of whether the parade balloons in New York fly at all. Settled: the peak day, flown. Open: the holiday’s own sky.

The Strait at Midnight: Two Tankers in One Service’s Word, a Third in Another’s, and Four Crossings in the Counter

Wednesday added tankers to Tehran’s count and none to its names. Two oil tankers struck naval mines, exploded and caught fire in the strait on Wednesday, the Guards’ Navy said in a statement carried by state media, in the wire account above; the vessels were on a route Tehran calls unauthorised, off the southern and eastern coasts, with fires in their engine rooms, in the explosions account filed below. A second Iranian service adds a third hull to the day: an oil tanker hit a naval mine and caught fire in a restricted zone on Wednesday, the Army said in a statement carried by the Fars agency, in the Thursday oil account filed below, which carries both statements without merging them. No vessel is named in either. The visible counter’s Wednesday stands at four crossings, as the Evening Watch read it. The conditions Tehran attaches to a reopening stand in the wire account in full: the lifting of the naval blockade of Iranian ports and an end to the war on all fronts, with a Qatari-carried offer to reopen the waterway within 48 hours of Washington meeting them.

Oil took the file at its word. Crude rose in early Asian trade on Thursday, extending Wednesday’s gains, on supply concerns joining the strait claims to the Houthi claim against Saudi Arabia’s largest refinery at Ras Tanura, in the Thursday account; Brent had settled at $94.61 on Wednesday, up $3.21, with government stocks up 1.1 million barrels against a trade count of 126,000, as the Evening Watch printed. The producers’ own calendar now stands in the file: OPEC+ ministers meet on November 29 to decide 2027 output policy, with the Emirates no longer in the group and Iraq and Kazakhstan pressing for higher capacity estimates. Settled: Wednesday’s announcements, as announcements, and the counter’s four. Open: the tankers themselves, the refinery’s true state, and Sunday’s meeting in Vienna’s long shadow.

Ukraine at Midnight: The Grid Struck in Five Regions, and the Refineries Answered in Samara

The war’s Wednesday file is an exchange of infrastructure, stated in both capitals’ words. A Russian attack on the power sector overnight damaged energy and gas infrastructure and caused outages in five regions, Ukrainian officials said, in the wire account above: generation halted at a thermal plant of the DTEK company, its location undisclosed and its equipment badly damaged; a large fire at a gas production site of the Naftogaz company in the Poltava region, its operations stopped; and external power to the Russian-occupied Zaporizhzhia nuclear plant cut for the 17th time this year, Deputy Energy Minister Nekrasov said. In Zaporizhzhia city a morning strike on a residential building injured six, including two children, on top of Tuesday’s building strike in the same city, whose injured count the governor’s file carries at 48 in one account and at least 50 in the wire’s. One person was killed in the Kyiv region, Prime Minister Koretskyi said, and in Odesa a residential complex was hit and two women wounded by shrapnel.

The day count behind the grid file stands in the daily briefing filed below: 96 drones launched, 64 of them jet-powered, 73 brought down or jammed, and nine dead and 81 injured nationwide over the past day, with the Zaporizhzhia building’s two dead and 48 injured inside that count, a wheat warehouse of nearly 1,500 tons struck, and Odesa’s dead a man of 41 and a boy of 17. Kyiv’s answering file is in its ministry’s running record, in the ministry account filed below: Ukrainian forces attacked the Syzran and Kuibyshev refineries in Russia’s Samara region overnight on November 25, with fires and reported damage to processing equipment. President Putin’s word that retaliation for the refinery strikes is inevitable, and Kyiv’s foreign minister’s answer to it, stand as the Evening Watch printed them. Settled: the damage, in each side’s own statements. Open: the winter grid’s margin, and the retaliation’s date.

Libya at Midnight: Kyiv Confirms the Mothership, Two Libyan Officers Are Reported Dead, and the Ministry Is Silent Into a Fifth Day

Benghazi’s file gained its last missing voice on Wednesday—Kyiv’s, formally. Ukraine’s security service has formally confirmed the operation, saying seven domestically produced naval drones were launched from a mothership against the two Russian vessels, and President Zelensky released footage showing a drone striking the warship at anchor, in the November 25 update to the running file filed below. The human count moved in the same day’s update cycle: two Libyan naval officers wounded in the strike have died, the first deaths reported from the attack, in the officers account filed below, which spells the corvette Merkur in the President’s post as carried there and Merkury in its own text. The eastern government’s position stands: the ship had docked to refuel and take on supplies under a cooperation agreement, a joint investigation with Moscow stands opened, and Tripoli’s government has condemned the strike and asked Kyiv to explain, with the United Nations mission calling for an independent investigation.

Against all of it, the one word the file has waited for since Sunday stands unissued. The Kremlin’s spokesman said on Monday that Russia had taken note of the claim and that any information would come from the Defence Ministry; the ministry has confirmed nothing, denied nothing, and published no account of the ship or her crew, and at the midnight stamp that silence enters its fifth calendar day. The desk’s log count of the hull, printed at the last midnight, stands as printed: the 25th Russian hull recorded lost since February 24, 2022, in the naval events log, and the first outside the Black Sea basin. Settled: the operation’s authorship and method, in the author’s own confirmation, and the first Libyan deaths. Open: the ministry’s word, the inquiry’s findings, and the launch waters’ proof beyond Kyiv’s statement.

On the corvette’s name in the officers account: Merkur in the President’s post as carried, Merkury in the account’s own text; the desk keeps its standing spelling, Merkury. On the futures at the turn: up 0.4, 0.7 and 0.9 percent in the late reading, near flat at 1814 in the evening reading—two readings of one night, in one direction by the later one. Inside the triangle is where the midnight honestly stands, and the log turns on it. The market is shut for Thanksgiving; the Overnight Watch collects at 0400.
