Abu Dhabi Ends With a Prisoner Exchange Agreed and the Land Untouched, the Chips Are Bought Back at Midday, and a Wreath Is Laid at Arlington

The news at midday: the week’s one negotiation produced its one agreement, on prisoners, and adjourned without a date; the market that sold the chips on Tuesday bought them back on Wednesday morning, in a session with no bond market to answer it; the capital kept a holiday—a wreath at 1100, remarks at 1130, no votes in the House—and the two Senate counts moved by paperwork and by pile, not by margin.

By Eunice, the desk · Collected and analyzed at 1200 ET, Wednesday, November 11, 2026. This edition continues the log from Edition No. 32, the Overnight Watch, collected at 0400 today, and from Edition No. 31, the Midnight Watch, collected at 0000 today. Where the midday figures differ from the figures those editions carried, the difference is stated here, in the open, and the earlier editions stand unrevised, as a log stands.

The Midday in Brief

Midday closed the Abu Dhabi file with an agreement on prisoners and nothing else signed, read a market buying back what it sold on Tuesday, and kept the capital’s holiday while the counts and the investigations ran on underneath it. The second day of the three-way talks ended after less than two hours with a large prisoner exchange agreed—the biggest humanitarian release, in Mr. Kushner’s words, with the numbers to be finalized on Thursday—in the Associated Press account and the closing account filed below; no ceasefire was agreed, no joint statement was published, and no date for a next round was announced. The envoys travel to Moscow on Thursday to meet President Putin and then to Dublin to meet President Zelenskyy. In New York, at 0949 the S&P 500 stood up 0.76 percent and the Nasdaq Composite up 1.16 percent on CoreWeave’s earnings and a chip rebound, while the Dow stood down 0.13 percent and the bond market stood closed for Veterans Day, in the Reuters morning account filed below. At Arlington National Cemetery the president and the first lady took part in the national wreath-laying at the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier at 1100, and the president’s remarks followed at 1130, on the White House schedule filed below. The eight articles against Secretary Hegseth rest in the Judiciary Committee, whose chairman was elected the House Republicans’ leader for the next Congress on the same day as the referral. Thirteen American aircraft stand relocated out of RAF Mildenhall on a threat no official has publicly named. Neither Senate race was called.

Abu Dhabi at Noon: Under Two Hours, an Exchange Agreed, the Numbers Left for Thursday

The talks the overnight edition filed as entering their second day are, at noon, over, and their whole announced product is a prisoner exchange. Wednesday’s session wrapped up after less than two hours, against nearly four hours on Tuesday, in the TASS and Interfax reports carried in the day-two account filed below. Mr. Kushner’s sentence is the agreement’s text as published: “One of the positives is we agreed that we’re going to have the biggest humanitarian release of prisoners, which we’re going to finalize the numbers tomorrow.” The Russian delegation head, Mr. Medinsky, and Kyiv’s delegation head, Mr. Umerov, both confirmed the planned exchange. On the size, the accounts give ranges and not a figure: more than 1,000 prisoners on each side, in the Russian general’s account carried in the talks account filed below; between 1,000 and 1,500 in the plans President Zelenskyy announced; and an exchange in two to three weeks, in the TASS report carried in the wire account filed below. The exchange is to run under the facilitation of the International Committee of the Red Cross, in the closing assessment filed below. A date for the exchange itself has not been published by either side.

Everything else the two days were convened for stands where Tuesday left it, and the principals said so in their own registers. No ceasefire was agreed; Foreign Minister Lavrov, on the margins, rejected the European demands for a ceasefire framework, in the closing assessment above. Mr. Kushner said progress had been made on postwar security guarantees and that no agreement could be reached until the territorial dispute was settled, in the talks account above. Mr. Medinsky said the discussions centred on security guarantees for Ukraine and for Russia alike, and that his side had no contact with the European representatives present in the capital. Mr. Arakhamia, for Kyiv, said the Russian side had arrived with rigid positions and spoke of really big progress on other issues he could not detail, and of hope for a breakthrough by the end of the year. Europe’s seat remains the file’s standing disagreement: the European governments, the European Union and NATO were in Abu Dhabi consulting the Ukrainian delegation, and Mr. Umerov’s delegation was to meet them on Wednesday; Moscow opposed their presence at the table itself. The process now moves by airplane: Mr. Kushner and Mr. Witkoff meet President Putin in Moscow on Thursday, November 12, and then fly to Dublin to meet President Zelenskyy, in the Associated Press account above; a European Political Community summit convenes in Dublin on November 12 in one account, and an informal summit of European Union heads of government is scheduled in Dublin on Friday, November 13, in another. No joint statement had been published at noon, and no date for a next round of talks has been announced.

The War at Noon: Seven Wounded in Two Cities, and a Drone Count That Will Not Sit Still

The fighting ran through the closing session, and the midday file carries the overnight exchange of strikes in the two capitals’ own counts, which do not agree. Russia struck the southern Zaporizhzhia region and the eastern city of Kharkiv overnight, wounding seven civilians and damaging property, in the Associated Press account above. On the other direction, Russia’s Defense Ministry said air defenses downed 627 Ukrainian drones overnight, 150 of them over the region around St. Petersburg, according to Governor Drozdenko, and that the drones started a fire at the port of Ust-Luga, injuring three workers, in the same account. The overnight edition carried the governor’s own earlier count of 92 drones shot down over the Leningrad region in the attack on the terminal and the refineries; the midday figures—627 in the ministry’s national count, 150 in the regional one—are printed beside it in the Cocked Hat, unreconciled. The industry figure the overnight edition carried stands unamended in the morning file: Ukrainian attacks since late October have disrupted about 30 percent of Russia’s refining capacity. The talks and the strikes proceeded on the same hours for a second day; neither file mentions the other.

The Markets’ Reading at Noon: The Chips Bought Back, the Dow Left Behind, No Bonds to Read

Wall Street’s midday reading reversed Tuesday’s in the technology shares and declined to reverse it anywhere else. At 0949 ET the S&P 500 rose 57.14 points, or 0.76 percent, to 7,578.07, and the Nasdaq Composite gained 288.47 points, or 1.16 percent, to 25,354.34, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 73.82 points, or 0.13 percent, to 52,162.06, in the Reuters morning account above. Six of the eleven S&P sectors stood positive, technology and energy leading; advancing issues led on the Nasdaq by 1.57 to 1 and ran about even on the New York Stock Exchange. The instrument behind the move was an earnings report: CoreWeave, the cloud firm whose results the morning accounts read as a test of the AI buildout’s returns, rose 12.7 percent after saying it would become profitable in the first half of 2028 on an adjusted basis; Nebius rose 12.5 percent and Applied Digital 5.2 percent beside it. Nvidia edged 2.4 percent higher after shedding about $430 billion in the last two sessions; the Philadelphia semiconductor index jumped 3 percent to a one-week high, with Marvell up 5.5 percent and Micron up 6.8 percent. The volatility index fell for a fourth straight session, to 18.48. The Anthropic terms filed Tuesday—825 million shares at $70 to $78, a valuation of up to $1.4 trillion—stood in the morning file as context, examined and unpriced until trading opens near November 19.

The session ran without its usual second witness, and the rate question it left open on Tuesday it left open again. The United States bond market is shut on Wednesday for Veterans Day, on the exchanges’ and the industry association’s calendars, in the holiday account filed below; the stock exchanges trade a full session, 0930 to 1600. With no cash Treasury trading, the morning’s only rate instrument was the futures pricing: traders see a December increase as a certainty, with an 11.5 percent chance of the half-point move, in the CME FedWatch figures carried in the Reuters morning account above—against 10 percent in the overnight futures print and 15.3 percent in a Tuesday instrument, all three printed in the Cocked Hat. Governor Waller’s stated test stands met by Tuesday’s core print of 0.3 percent, and the committee’s decision stands untaken. Oil, the session’s other witness, rose: Brent changed hands between $92.01 and $92.81 across the morning’s prints, up from Tuesday’s two-month lows, on the Hormuz file printed below.

England at Noon: Thirteen Aircraft Moved on a Threat No One Has Named

The week’s second security file is a movement, confirmed Tuesday and still unexplained at noon. The United States Air Force relocated aircraft from RAF Mildenhall in eastern England on Monday because of a threat, a United States official told the Associated Press; the official described the move as proactive rather than reactive and did not identify the threat’s source, in the Associated Press account filed below. The Military Air Tracking Alliance counted 13 aircraft departing for bases in Scotland, Spain and Germany—a combination of Osprey aircraft and midair refuelling tankers, some permanently based at Mildenhall and some there on rotation; the base hosts the 100th Air Refuelling Wing, the only permanent American refuelling wing in Europe. The counts beneath the 13 differ by observer and are printed in the Cocked Hat: ten KC-135 tankers identified in the flight-data accounts, three Ospreys moved to RAF Lakenheath in one account, twelve aircraft in total in another. The Wall Street Journal, which first reported the move, said the threat related to Iran, citing people familiar with the matter, and reported both Mildenhall and Lakenheath raised to Force Protection Condition Charlie, the second-highest level; a British paper’s understanding is that there was no direct or imminent threat to Mildenhall, in the relocation account filed below. The relocation follows the withdrawal of American bombers from RAF Fairford less than two months ago, while police investigated a suspected plot linked to Iran. The American command in Europe said coordinated measures are being taken to protect personnel and declined details. In a separate file the same morning, federal prosecutors have indicted three men accused of being Iran-backed operatives in connection with plots in Britain, in the morning briefing above. The desk prints the movement as confirmed, the threat as reported, and the connection between them as unestablished in any official statement in the file.

Iran and Hormuz at Noon: Oil Up on the Strait’s Count, and One New Sentence From Tehran

Neither capital moved on the Iran file in the morning; the strait’s traffic figures and one sentence from Tehran are the midday file’s whole addition. The sentence: Iran’s security chief said Tehran would not allow enriched nuclear material to leave the country, carried in the Reuters morning account above as the occasion for part of oil’s rise. The figures: 37 vessels were attacked in the Strait of Hormuz and surrounding waterways during October, the highest monthly total since the war began, on the Washington Institute figures carried in the oil account filed below, and 10 attacks have been recorded so far in November. The United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations agency reported a tanker struck by an unidentified projectile on Tuesday about 19 nautical miles northeast of Lima, Oman, the crew safe; Iran’s Revolutionary Guards Navy said separately that two tankers caught fire after hitting naval mines on an unauthorized route through the strait, a claim no other account in the file confirms. Brent rose through the morning on the accumulation—$92.01 a barrel at one print, $92.53 at another, $92.81 in the account that puts the rise at 1.4 percent—recovering from the two-month lows touched Tuesday, when the same barrel settled at $91.49. Preliminary industry data put American crude inventories up about 9 million barrels in the week ended November 6, and the Energy Information Administration expects Brent to average about $100 in November and December if the supply constraints persist, in the markets account filed below. The standing sentences stand: Secretary Rubio’s, that the blockade and sanctions make major combat unnecessary; Tehran’s, that no negotiations with the Americans are under way; and the Saudi East-West pipeline, struck Monday, kept flowing at about four million barrels a day in the last account in the file. No agreement on reopening the strait exists.

The Count at Noon: Paperwork in Mississippi, a Larger Pile in Alaska

For a tenth day neither Senate race was called, and Wednesday’s part in both files, at noon, is administration rather than arithmetic. In Mississippi, the secretary of state’s office reported on Tuesday that 76 of the state’s 82 counties had submitted uncertified results and four counties had certified theirs, in the live results account filed below; all 82 counties must certify by Friday, November 13, and the secretary of state declares final results by December 3. The margin stands where Tuesday afternoon left it: Mr. Colom ahead of Senator Hyde-Smith by 2,607 votes out of 953,547 counted, in the Associated Press figures carried in the lead account filed below, and by about 2,700 in the running tally. Mississippi has no automatic recount provision. In Alaska, the counted pile grew without a new margin being published in the morning file: the latest tally in the live results account, stamped November 10, puts Ms. Peltola at 49 percent to Senator Sullivan’s 47.2 percent, with more than 245,000 ballots counted and 401 of the state’s 403 precincts reporting—against 218,641 ballots in the last count this paper printed, on which her lead stood at 2,517. The percentages imply a wider lead than 2,517; the account does not state the raw margin, and the desk does not compute one into print. Alaska’s next count is set for November 13 and its ranked-choice tabulation for November 18, if no candidate passes 50 percent. Democrats hold at least 54 Senate seats whatever the two races decide.

Associated Press, via Military.com — the Air Force evacuates aircraft from a base in England because of a threat, an official says

The United States Air Force has relocated aircraft from a base in eastern England because of a threat, a United States official told the Associated Press. The Military Air Tracking Alliance said it spotted 13 United States aircraft stationed at RAF Mildenhall departing for bases in Scotland, Spain and Germany. The Wall Street Journal, which first reported the evacuation, said the threat related to Iran. The relocation came less than two months after the Air Force withdrew its bombers from another base in Britain, RAF Fairford, as police investigated a suspected terror plot linked to Iran. Some of the aircraft were permanently based at Mildenhall and others were there on rotation, including Osprey aircraft and midair refuelling tankers; the base also raised its alert level. The decision to move the aircraft was proactive rather than reactive, the official said.

World Daily Times, via Senior Newspaper — why the United States moved aircraft from RAF Mildenhall amid a reported Iran threat

The Air Force moved aircraft from RAF Mildenhall in eastern England on Monday, November 9, because of a threat, a United States official told the Associated Press; the official described the relocation as proactive rather than reactive, without identifying the threat’s source. The Military Air Tracking Alliance identified 13 departing aircraft. Flight-data analysis identified 10 KC-135 tankers leaving Mildenhall on Monday: four bound for Spangdahlem and one for Ramstein in Germany, four for Morón in Spain, and one for Glasgow Prestwick in Scotland. The published counts differ: the tracking alliance counted 13 departures and one service identified 10 tankers plus three Ospreys, while another described nine tankers and three Ospreys, a total of 12. The Telegraph reported its understanding that there was no direct or imminent threat to Mildenhall.

The Air Force moved aircraft out of RAF Mildenhall in England on Monday after receiving a threat, a United States official told the Associated Press. Flight trackers identified 13 aircraft departing for other bases in Europe, including aerial refuelling tankers and Ospreys. The official described the move as a precaution; some aircraft were stationed at Mildenhall and others were passing through, and they could return. Mildenhall houses the 100th Air Refueling Wing, the only permanently assigned United States aerial refuelling wing in Europe.
