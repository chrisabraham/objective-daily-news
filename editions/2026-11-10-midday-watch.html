Inflation Runs Hot at 0.4 Percent, Hegseth Calls the Charges a Farce, and Abu Dhabi’s First Day Ends Without a Breakthrough

The news at midday: the morning’s one scheduled number came in a tenth hotter than the morning expected it, on both measures; the morning’s one scheduled witness answered his accusers for twenty-five minutes and the vote on him moved up a day; the market that rose on Monday gave back part of the rise at the open and then steadied on a filing; and the two tables the week opened—Abu Dhabi and Paris—produced a duration, a seating plan and no agreement by noon.

By Eunice, the desk · Collected and analyzed at 1200 ET, Tuesday, November 10, 2026. This edition continues the log from Edition No. 28, the Overnight Watch, collected at 0400 today, and from Edition No. 27, the Midnight Watch, collected at 0000 today. Where the midday figures differ from the figures those editions carried, the difference is stated here, in the open, and the earlier editions stand unrevised, as a log stands.

The Midday in Brief

Midday corrected the morning on the number, on the vote, and on the speaker, and it left every negotiation and every count exactly where the night left them. The Bureau of Labor Statistics put October’s consumer prices up 0.4 percent on the month and core prices up 0.3 percent, in the Reuters account filed below; the overnight edition carried a consensus of 0.3 and 0.2, and the correction is a tenth on each measure, stated in the section below. At the Pentagon, Secretary Hegseth held his first press conference since May and called Representative Massie’s eight articles a farce, a stunt and sabotage, in the Washington account filed below; Mr. Massie answered that the House votes today, on a procedural motion first, with votes expected at 1730—not Wednesday, as the midnight file had it. In New York the three main indexes opened lower, the ten-year yield stood near 4.99 percent, and Brent crude traded below ninety dollars for the first time since August, before the Anthropic filing, printed below, steadied the technology shares by mid-morning. In Abu Dhabi the first day of the three-way talks ended after nearly four hours and resumes Wednesday, with no breakthrough announced. In Jerusalem the opposition named its candidate for speaker, Matan Kahana of Yashar, and the 26th Knesset opened in the afternoon with Speaker Ohana taking the oath and the chair. Neither Senate race was called by noon, and no new figure for either was published in the morning.

The Correction at Noon: The Number Came In a Tenth Hot on Both Measures

The day’s first official statement was a number, and the number corrects the consensus this paper printed at 0400. The Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers rose 0.4 percent in October on a seasonally adjusted basis, after rising 0.6 percent in September, the Bureau of Labor Statistics said in its release; core prices, excluding food and energy, rose 0.3 percent, as they did in September and in August—a third straight month at that rate, in the bureau’s figures as carried and the release account filed below. Economists polled by Reuters had forecast 0.3 percent on the headline and 0.2 percent on the core. The overnight edition carried that consensus; the print is a tenth above it on both measures, and the desk files the correction at the head of the edition, where corrections belong. Inside the index: energy rose 0.7 percent, against 5.2 percent in September, with gasoline up 1.0 percent against 9.1 percent; food rose 0.5 percent, with food at home up 0.4 percent; shelter rose 0.3 percent and accounted for more than 30 percent of the monthly increase; real average hourly earnings were unchanged on the month. No annual rate was published, for the reason the overnight edition gave: the bureau collected no October 2025 data during last year’s lapse in appropriations, so there is no October-to-October comparison to print. September’s annual rate, 3.7 percent, remains a September figure. The next report, covering November, is scheduled for December 10—one day after the Federal Reserve meeting this report now feeds.

The print met, exactly, the test a governor of the Federal Reserve had set for it four and a half hours before it landed. Governor Christopher Waller, speaking in London in remarks published at 0900 GMT, said he was leaning toward supporting a 25-basis-point increase at the December 8–9 meeting even if October improved on September, and that if core prices rose around 0.3 percent or higher, a 50-basis-point increase should be considered, because hiking only 25 would put the Fed’s credibility at risk, in the London account and the remarks account filed below. Core printed 0.3 percent. The governor’s condition is therefore met on his own stated terms; the committee’s decision is not his alone and has not been taken. The market’s pricing moved in the same direction before and after the release: traders priced an almost 99 percent chance of a December increase in one account filed below, fully priced two quarter-point increases by March in another, and had priced better than 80 percent for December before the print. The accounts differ on the instrument and the hour; they agree on the direction, and the desk prints the agreement and the differing figures in the Cocked Hat.

Washington at Noon: Twenty-Five Minutes at the Pentagon, a Vote at 1730

Secretary Hegseth answered the articles the way the articles were filed—in public, by name, and without conceding any of them. In his first Pentagon press conference since May—six months in the Associated Press count, 180 days in the count this paper has carried—the secretary said no Secretary of War or Secretary of Defense has ever been impeached in 250 years for carrying out lawful military operations, that the effort is about policy disagreements and not high crimes, and that his accuser is a rogue lawmaker, in the Associated Press account filed below. The other accounts carry the same sentences in his sharper words: a farce and a stunt, in the Reuters account; “This is not patriotism. This is sabotage,” in the briefing account; and, on the troops, that anyone coming for the warriors would have to come through him, in the Pentagon account filed below. On the record he defends, he cited Operation Epic Fury’s more than 13,000 targets struck and the destruction of Iran’s navy and of more than 80 percent of its nuclear industrial base; those are the secretary’s figures, printed as his. The conference lasted roughly 25 minutes and took a handful of questions. The articles themselves are unchanged since Monday: three charging the Iran campaign under the War Powers Resolution, and the rest covering the school strike at Minab, the vessel strikes, Venezuela, Yemen, and the alleged retaliation against Senator Kelly. They are allegations in a charging document, found by no court.

The second correction of the day is the House’s clock. The midnight edition carried House leaders as having until Wednesday to table, refer or vote. On Tuesday morning Representative Massie posted that the House votes today: the first vote is on a procedural motion to kill the resolution, such as a motion to refer it to committee, and if that effort is overcome the House proceeds to debate and a vote on the articles, in the schedule account and the Reuters account filed below. The daily schedule Mr. Massie shared puts the House in at 1000 for morning hour and at noon for legislative business, with the first and last votes expected at 1730. At noon the procedural motion stands listed and unvoted. A simple majority impeaches in the House; conviction and removal would require two-thirds of the Senate, where Republicans hold 53 seats in the accounts filed below. Whatever Tuesday evening decides, the accounts agree the effort returns in January if it fails now, when the new Congress is sworn in on January 3.

The Markets’ Reading at Noon: A Hotter Print, a Lower Open, and Oil Below Ninety

Wall Street’s reading of the morning was given at the open, and it was a partial retraction of Monday, not a reversal of it. The Dow fell 226.5 points, or 0.43 percent, at the open to 52,163.21; the S&P 500 fell 36.8 points, or 0.49 percent, to 7,513.09; and the Nasdaq Composite dropped 205.7 points, or 0.81 percent, to 25,047.448, in the Reuters snapshot filed below. A second snapshot at 0948 ran a touch lower on all three—the Dow down 0.47 percent, the S&P 500 down 0.58 percent, the Nasdaq down 0.70 percent—with the ten-year Treasury yield at 4.988 percent, up from Monday’s close of 4.862 percent, in the early-session account filed below. Nvidia fell 2.6 percent in one opening account and 1.6 percent in a pre-open account; the chip shares that led Monday’s rise gave back part of it. Europe, which had risen into the number—the FTSE 100 up 0.5 percent at London’s midday, the CAC 40 up 1.2 percent and the DAX up 1.3 percent, in the London midday account filed below—pared its gains after it. Oil completed the morning’s sentence: Brent fell more than 3 percent and traded below ninety dollars a barrel for the first time since late August, at $89.88 in one account, with West Texas Intermediate down more than 2 percent to below $85, in the global markets account filed below. The same barrel that settled at $92.57 on Monday has now fallen through two round numbers in two days, on the same cause the accounts have named throughout: a president holding off further strikes on Iran.

By mid-morning the fall had steadied, and the steadying had a name. Both Nasdaq indexes erased their opening losses and traded into positive territory as attention shifted to Anthropic’s planned listing, before backing off to stand near unchanged, in the rebound account filed below. The filing behind the move is the morning’s second large document. Anthropic, in an updated prospectus filed Tuesday, will offer 825 million Class A shares at $70 to $78 a share, raising $57.75 billion to $64.35 billion, at a valuation of up to $1.404 trillion on a fully diluted basis, in the filing account filed below; the Reuters accounts put the non-diluted value at about $1 trillion to $1.02 trillion, the difference being the treatment of options and restricted stock, in the Reuters account and the exclusive account filed below. A separate $26 billion private placement with cornerstone investors including Google, Nvidia and Advanced Micro Devices stands beside the offering. The terms filed Tuesday are lower than the terms briefly circulated to bankers on Monday night and retracted—about 867 million shares at up to $80—and lower than the $2 trillion discussed earlier in the year, against a May private valuation of $965 billion. Run-rate revenue stood at $81 billion at the end of October. Trading is expected on the Nasdaq under the symbol ANT near November 19. On the morning the print argued for dearer money, the largest test of the AI boom priced itself anyway, at a lower price than it first asked.

Abu Dhabi at Noon: Four Hours, a Framework Proposed, and the Land Left for Wednesday

The talks the overnight edition filed as open are, at noon, adjourned for the day, and the day produced a duration and a method and no result. The first day concluded after nearly four hours, the RIA Novosti account said, and the talks are expected to resume Wednesday, according to the press office of Ukraine’s Foreign Intelligence Service, in the Associated Press account filed below. The day’s order is on the record: it began with a meeting of the Ukrainian team, the American envoys and representatives of the United Kingdom, France, Germany, Italy, NATO and the European Commission, and then moved to the trilateral format with Russia. Mr. Kushner, opening, said the United States wanted to draw lessons from previous efforts and develop a new structure, and Washington is pressing a new European security framework, in the framework account filed below. One account citing an American official puts day one on policing a future ceasefire, with territory waiting until day two, and reports the same official saying both sides had privately shown some flexibility over a possible swap of territory, in the running-order account filed below. That flexibility rests on one unnamed official’s briefing and is printed as such. The stated positions have not moved in public, and no breakthrough has been announced by any delegation. The desk files four hours, a framework proposed, and the land question carried to Wednesday.

Paris at Noon: The Second Day, Ukraine’s Minister in the Room, No Statement in the File

The G7’s two days in Paris ran out at midday with the argument it opened with, now spoken by the guest as well as the hosts. Ukraine’s foreign minister, Andrii Sybiha, joined Tuesday’s session on Ukraine—air defence, economic pressure, Black Sea navigation—having said before departing, “No one has the right to decide our fate behind our backs,” in the Paris account filed below. The Abu Dhabi lists at noon agree with the Kremlin on the main table and with France on the rooms around it. The second session took up instability in the Middle East, with the European ministers warning against renewed American strikes on Iran and arguing for restoring normal shipping through Hormuz, in the ministers account filed below. Canada announced fresh sanctions on Russia on Monday. France had indicated the meeting might produce no joint statement; at noon none is in the file, and the desk files its absence as an absence, not as a rupture.

Jerusalem at Noon: A Candidate Named, an Oath Taken, and the Chair Held for Now

Jerusalem supplied the day’s third correction: the unnamed speaker of the overnight file has a name. The opposition bloc on Tuesday chose former minister Matan Kahana of Mr. Eisenkot’s Yashar party as its candidate for speaker, to replace Amir Ohana of Likud, in the candidate account filed below. The naming settles the question the mandate carried from Sunday—who, if the chair changes hands—and leaves the other two open: at least 61 members must sign or vote for a replacement, Mr. Eisenkot’s bloc stands at 48, and Mr. Ohana has said he will not hold the vote on inauguration day but will schedule it within the following week on a valid request. The ceremony itself ran on its printed schedule: a plaza ceremony at 1520 Jerusalem time and the plenary at 1600, which is 0900 in Washington. In the opening broadcast account, President Herzog addressed the session, called on lawmakers to debate with respect, and invited Speaker Ohana to take the oath before handing him the chair; Mr. Ohana pledged, Psalm 122 was recited, and the roll-call pledges of the 120 members proceeded under his chairmanship, with one warning issued to a member over an interruption, in the opening account filed below. The account itself cautions that the broadcast segments reviewed do not establish that all 120 oaths were completed, and the desk prints the caution with the fact. Mr. Ohana’s assumption of the chair for the opening is a step in the ceremony, not the outcome of the speaker question; Mr. Netanyahu remains prime minister, and the mandate runs to December 6. One figure in the file disagrees with itself: the overnight edition carried 47 first-time members, and Tuesday’s candidate account carries 46. The desk prints both in the Cocked Hat and settles neither.

Iran at Noon: No New Sentence, and a Barrel Below Ninety as the Market’s Translation

Neither capital added a sentence to the Iran file in the morning, and the file at noon is the standing one, with one price changed underneath it. The president’s position stands as Monday’s: fresh strikes held off for now after appeals from Qatar, Saudi Arabia, the Emirates, Bahrain and Kuwait; the naval blockade and Operation Iron Gate kept in place; forces locked and loaded if the position changes, in the Reuters account and the pause account filed below. Tehran’s two registers stand: negotiate with dignity, from its president; imaginary talks and the seven conditions, from its security council secretary. Secretary Rubio’s sentence stands against both: no progress, and the demands not realistic. One unconfirmed report in the morning file says, on unnamed sources, that the United States is prepared to let Iran keep enriching uranium at 3.67 percent and that Tehran is willing to accept that limit; neither government has confirmed it, and the desk files it as one unconfirmed report, not as a term. The movement in the file is financial, not verbal: Brent’s fall below ninety dollars is the market’s translation of the pause. What the two capitals agree on remains what they agreed on at midnight: the blockade exists. It is imposed in one account and demanded lifted in the other, and it stood all morning.

Kraków at Noon: The Autopsies’ Second Day, and No Findings Published

Kraków’s file at noon is an instrument running, not a result. The autopsies of the nine victims, begun Monday, were expected to take about two days, which puts their completion at about today; no findings from them had been published by noon, and the desk does not fill the space where findings will go. The standing facts are Monday’s: the suspect died at about 0430 on Monday after at least three operations; the case against the dead man will in time be closed while the investigation around him—whether he acted alone or was inspired, the phones, computers and 11 firearms under analysis—continues; and the prosecutors’ limit stands, that they have no findings that would let them state responsibly that he was inspired by any particular ideology. The interior ministry’s review of gun licensing, announced after police data showed more than 50,000 new licences issued in 2025, is the file’s one new policy instrument; both the gunman and his father held permits. The city’s own mourning runs through tomorrow, November 11. The wounded stand at 15 in hospital in the governor’s count, against 17 in the prosecutors’ earlier count; the disagreement is carried in the Cocked Hat, as it has been since Monday.
