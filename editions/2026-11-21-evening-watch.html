The Sparta II, a Russian cargo ship bound from St. Petersburg for Kaliningrad, was struck by two drones in international waters west of Gogland island between 0840 and 0850 local time, caught fire, drifted into Finnish waters at 1227, anchored south of Loviisa at 1545, and was still burning late on Saturday with her 16 crew evacuated to a Russian ship, in the Baltic account filed below and in the Gulf of Finland account filed below. The Baltic at Evening: The Sparta II at Anchor, Her Crew Off, and a Helicopter Inside Finnish Airspace

Saturday’s new front opened in the morning in the Gulf of Finland and was still open at the stamp. The Sparta II, a Russian-flagged roll-on/roll-off cargo ship of 121 metres owned by Oboronlogistika and bound for the Russian exclave of Kaliningrad, was damaged in international waters west of Russia’s Gogland island, in the Baltic account above, corroborating an earlier account by Russian officials. Finnish authorities detected the attack at approximately 0840 to 0850 local time, when the vessel was roughly 50 kilometres west of the island, in the Gulf of Finland account. The Leningrad region’s governor said on Telegram that a cargo vessel had been attacked by two drones in international waters about three nautical miles from Russia’s border, and that 80 drones were shot down over the Leningrad region in the night, in the same accounts; Estonia’s border guard said preliminary information indicated explosions on board, in the Helsinki account filed below. The damaged ship lost manoeuvrability, drifted into Finnish territorial waters at 1227, and dropped anchor at 1545 south of Loviisa, with damage to her bridge and cargo area and smoke continuing to rise, in the Gulf of Finland account.

The evening’s settled facts are the crew and the fire. The 16-member crew evacuated onto lifeboats, then to a nearby Russian ship, and is on its way back to Russia, the Finnish Border Guard’s commander told the public broadcaster late on Saturday, in the Baltic account. The blaze was ongoing late on Saturday, Finland is leading the rescue operation as the ship lies at anchor in its waters, and Finnish authorities warned that the uncontrolled fire could lead to further explosions or the loss of the vessel, in the same accounts; one account puts five units working to extinguish the fire with water cannons and warns it could rage for days or weeks, in the night account filed below. There is no confirmed information about what cargo is on board, and it cannot be ruled out that there is military materiel on board, the Border Guard’s commander said, in the Baltic account. The vessel is subject to United States sanctions and is linked to Oboronlogistika, according to the Treasury Department, in the same account.

Attribution stands where Finland placed it, and no further. “It is likely that Ukraine is behind the attack,” Finland’s armed forces spokesman told a press conference, adding that it was not clear why the vessel had been targeted, in the Baltic account; the evening press conference repeated the judgement with the qualification that authorities had no conclusive evidence, and Ukraine has not claimed responsibility, in the Gulf of Finland account. Finland’s prime minister wrote on X that the war is spilling over into neighbouring areas and that Ukraine has the right to fight against the aggressor, in the Helsinki account. Helsinki imposed temporary aviation and maritime restrictions in the eastern Gulf of Finland, in the same account. A second incident rides on the first: a Russian helicopter was suspected of briefly violating Finnish airspace during the rescue operation, reaching about 4.1 kilometres inside Finnish territory shortly after midday near Loviisa, in the Baltic and Gulf of Finland accounts; the defence minister said the suspected violation may have been connected to rescue or surveillance operations involving the ship, and the Border Guard is investigating, in the Gulf of Finland account. Settled: the strike’s hour and place, the drift, the anchorage, the evacuation, and the fire at nightfall. Open: responsibility in any confirmed word, the cargo, and whether the ship can be towed home or will be lost.

Ukraine at Evening: Kryvyi Rih’s Toll, the Bridges’ Two States, and an Argument Answered in Numbers

The evening’s second Ukrainian file is Friday night’s second strike, counted on Saturday. A strike on the centre of Kryvyi Rih, the President’s hometown in central Ukraine, killed two people and wounded 60, local officials said Saturday, in the wire account above. A 7-year-old boy and a 20-year-old woman were killed in the attack Friday night, the head of the city administration wrote on Telegram; the head of the regional military administration said 60 people were wounded, including six children, in the same account. The strike caused four fires and damaged about 25 apartment buildings, a hospital, schools and an industrial facility, as well as businesses and vehicles, in the wire account. The sequence stands in the city administration’s own words: “First, two ballistic missiles. And then, when the emergency and rescue operation began, three more rockets hit doctors, rescuers and utility workers,” in the same account; one account identifies the woman killed as a paramedic responding at the scene and the boy as having died in intensive care of head injuries, in the night account. Elsewhere on Saturday, the State Emergency Service said 11 people, including two children, were wounded when a drone hit a five-storey apartment building in the Kharkiv region, in the toll account filed below; a separate overnight tally in the night account puts the night’s civilian wounded across Ukraine at 83. The air force’s overnight figures—three ballistic missiles and 148 drones launched, 117 destroyed or jammed—stand in the file beside the Kryvyi Rih count. Settled: the toll, the wounded, and the damage list. Open: nothing in the file revises them; the counts from other cities are still arriving.

Kyiv’s own file did not move past the midday standing, and the desk records it as a standing. The toll from Thursday night’s barrage stands at at least nine killed nationwide—seven in Kyiv and two in Lviv—with at least 28 injured, in the BBC account filed below, and the package stands at more than 400 drones and over 110 missiles, 27 of them ballistic, in the same account’s report of the President’s figures. On the bridges the file still holds two states: all three struck crossings closed to traffic, in the BBC account; traffic from the western to the eastern bank restored over the Podilsko-Voskresenskyi bridge by mid-afternoon, with the Darnytskyi assessment still under way, in the accounts the midday file carried. No account in the evening file gives the Darnytskyi’s structural damage or a repair timetable for any crossing.

What the evening adds to Kyiv is the argument, made in person. President Zelensky, speaking in Kyiv ahead of a meeting with Britain’s defence secretary, justified the strikes on the Crimean Bridge as a “fair response” to 109 separate Russian attacks on bridges in Ukraine—distinct attacks, not individual munitions—in the briefing account and in the 109 account filed below. He rejected the Russian president’s claim that the bridge is exclusively civilian in use: “There are facts, not just the words of Putin. We can see,” he said, pointing to images of tanks and other military hardware crossing it, in the briefing account. The intensified attacks that followed the bridge strikes were, in his words, a signal that President Putin does not want to stop and wants a reason to escalate, in the same account. The repair figures now stand opposed in the file: Russia could take several months to a year to remove the collapsed section, the defence minister said at the same briefing—several months simply to lift and tow away the collapsed arch and span—against a Russian deputy prime minister’s estimate of November 19 that some road sections might reopen within one to two months, in the 109 account. The visiting secretary’s own errand was stated to the President’s face: he was in Kyiv to show that Europe was united in its support, and “the more Putin tries to divide us, the more resolute we become,” in the briefing account. In London’s other voice the same day, the defence secretary described Friday’s missiles in Poland as an “outrageous provocation,” in the London account filed below. Warsaw’s file stands as the midday left it: the guarantee spoken in person, the adjustments promised, and the North Atlantic Council meeting in Brussels on Monday, in the Warsaw account filed below. Settled: both sides’ bridge numbers, spoken on the record in the same city on the same day. Open: which repair figure is right, and what the Council does on Monday.

The diplomacy’s evening state is two positions stated within a day of each other and a meeting in Berlin that produced no disclosed outcome. On the other side of the table: Iran wants a deal but not one acceptable to him, the President said on Friday, demanding a “real deal, not a fake deal,” and on Saturday he said any deal he makes will be “permanent”—a deal a future president could not undo—in the talks account and in the real-deal account filed below. America at Evening: A Death in Virginia, a Court Reaching Back to 2022, and the Chipmaker’s Standing File

Virginia’s file is one paragraph long and complete. The department disclosed no further details, including the child’s vaccination status, citing the family’s privacy, and will not provide any further information, in both accounts; its dashboard updates next on Tuesday, November 24, in the release. The context stands in the department’s own data: 220 cases in the state so far in 2026, 40 of them among children aged 0 to 4 and 75 among those aged 5 to 12, as of November 19, in the wire account, which also records that Virginia has not had a measles-related death since 1978, on an immunologist’s word. Settled: the death, the age band, and the caseload. Open: nothing the department intends to add.

The courts’ file reaches back four years. The House unconstitutionally passed the $1.7 trillion funding bill of 2022 through the pandemic-era proxy rule, the Fifth Circuit ruled on Friday, 9 to 8, upholding an injunction that Texas’s outgoing attorney general secured against enforcement of the pregnant workers provision the bill carried, in the ruling account filed below. The figures are the ruling’s own: the bill passed 225 to 201, with 226 members voting by proxy—a majority of the House present only by proxy—in the same account. The majority held that the Quorum Clause requires physical presence and that its constraints cannot be circumvented by congressional fiat, in the opinion account filed below, which quotes the opinion’s ground: the clause’s text, context, original understanding and unbroken historical application. The relief is narrow—the Justice Department and the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission are barred from enforcing the provision against Texas—and the same account records the standing warning in the commentary around the case: narrow relief that places a target on broad legislation, since the holding concerns how the entire bill was passed. The ruling is certain to be appealed, and it arrives weeks before the Congress chosen in the midterms takes office, in the docket account filed below, which also notes that the office that brought the challenge changes hands in January. Settled: the holding and its scope. Open: the appeal, and how many laws passed on proxy quorums the holding reaches.

The chipmaker’s file stands at evening where it stood at noon, and the desk records the standing once, plainly: California’s investigative subpoena of November 19, the Commission’s reported accounting inquiry, the criminal investigation of Megaspeed beside the company rather than on it, no charges on any front, no answer from the company in the file, and a board that has said it will not reinstate its former chief executive, in the subpoena account filed below and the accounts the midday file carried. Reuters — US House unconstitutionally passed spending bill in 2022, court rules

The House of Representatives unconstitutionally passed a $1.7 trillion government funding bill in 2022 through a pandemic-era rule allowing lawmakers to vote by proxy rather than in person, a federal appeals court ruled on Friday. The Fifth Circuit voted 9 to 8 to uphold an injunction blocking enforcement of a provision of that measure that gave pregnant workers stronger legal protections. The bill passed the House on a 225 to 201 vote, with 226 members voting by proxy. The majority held that the proxy rule violated the Constitution’s Quorum Clause, which the court said requires a majority of House members to be physically present to pass legislation. The relief granted was narrow: the Justice Department and the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission are barred from enforcing the pregnant workers provision against Texas.

Reason — En Banc Fifth Circuit Declares House COVID Proxy Voting Rule Unconstitutional

In Texas v. Blanche, the full Fifth Circuit concluded that the proxy voting rule the House adopted during the pandemic violated the Quorum Clause. The opinion, by Judge Wilson, holds that the enrolled-bill rule does not bar review, and that the clause’s text, context, original understanding and unbroken historical application indicate that members must be present for quorum purposes. The account notes that, while purporting to offer narrow relief confined to a limited portion of the bill in Texas, the court places a target on broad legislation.
