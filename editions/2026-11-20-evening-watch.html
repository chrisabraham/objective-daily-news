New York at 1800: A Seventh Straight Fall, Two Indexes in Correction, and a Debut Five Dollars From Its Price

The Nasdaq 100 closed at 25,028.98, almost 20 percent below its October peak, on the verge of bear-market territory, in the same account. The S&P 500’s loss is its seventh straight, its longest losing streak since February 2020, in the year account above; the Dow’s is its fifth straight loss of more than 1 percent, and the Dow fell 8 percent on the week, its worst week since March 2020, in the same account. The Dow and the S&P 500 closed in correction, down more than 10 percent from their peaks of August 5 and October 6; the Nasdaq, which closed in correction earlier this month, stands 18 percent below its October 6 record and down more than 2 percent for the year, its 18.7 percent year-to-date gain erased, in the same account. Washington at 1800: A News Conference, One Announcement, and the Rest in Asides

The President’s noon news conference, whose occasion the midday file carried, produced one announcement and a wide-ranging statement around it. President Trump, flanked by Vice President Vance and Secretary of State Rubio, said the United States entered the Asia trip “from a position of unmatched economic and diplomatic strength,” in the conference account above, and described the trip as a resounding success. The account’s own summary is plainer: the trip resulted more in agreements to continue discussions than in concrete changes, and the Shenzhen meeting produced an agreement to continue discussing the reduction of nuclear risks. In Manila the President signed a trade agreement with Thailand whose document marked a “substantial conclusion” of negotiations, with details still being finalized, in the same account. On oil, he noted that prices have been dropping and tied them to how matters stand in Iran, in the wire account filed below. The opening statement ranged across Iran, the Federal Reserve, his quest for a Nobel Peace Prize, and domestic manufacturing, in the conference account.

The announcement came in the middle of the remarks. The President said he and President Xi have agreed to establish “joint guardrails” on artificial intelligence—a change from his standing position that regulation would inhibit American innovation—and that President Xi likes his term “super intelligence” for the technology: “Problem is, it may be too super. So, President Xi and I agree that we’re going to do some joint guardrails, the two of us,” in the conference account. No details of what the guardrails would cover, when they would be negotiated and released, or how they would apply between the two countries have been announced, in the same account. The President said he would continue to push for data centres to be built in the United States—“Make them pay your community, but take advantage. Don’t shut them off”—and announced penalties against companies that build data centres abroad: “We’re not going to allow our companies to build data centers outside of our country,” in the same account. On chips he said he wants only “older chips” sold to China rather than the most advanced technology, and that he would accept no sales at all, while noting the cost: “China makes chips too. And if we’re not going to sell them, they’ll make them themselves,” in the same account. The account records a divide inside the cabinet on China—the Commerce Secretary in favour of selling chips, the Treasury Secretary more hawkish—and that the President has acknowledged both views; it also records that the President has replaced the White House cyber director amid the technology outages. Settled: the conference, and its one announcement. Open: the guardrails’ text, the Thailand agreement’s final terms, and any instrument on Ukraine or Iran, of which the conference produced none.

Kyiv at 1800: Nine Dead in the Corrected Count, Two Missiles Down in Poland, and One Bridge Reopened

The night’s count has finished rising, and the desk prints the finished figures with their disagreements. At least nine people were killed in the overnight attack, in the latest account above: seven killed and 17 injured in Kyiv, in Mayor Klitschko’s updated toll, and two more killed in other regions, in Prime Minister Koretskyi’s account, both carried there; the Interior Minister, Mr. Vyhivskyi, put the injured across Ukraine at 30, with more than 500 rescuers and police still at work at 1 p.m., in the same account. A second full account puts the dead at at least eight and the injured at nearly 30, in the blitz account filed below, and a third at nine killed and at least 28 injured, in the strike account filed below—the desk prints the spread in the Cocked Hat. The Air Force’s figures stand in the latest account: 84 cruise missiles and 404 attack drones launched, with President Zelensky adding 27 ballistic missiles, and air defences downing or suppressing three ballistic missiles, 79 cruise missiles and 380 drones, with strikes recorded at 23 locations. President Zelensky called the attack one of the biggest of the war and, speaking to the strike account, the biggest this year: “It was very difficult, we had losses, dead civilians, wounded people, absolutely terrible night.” He said the missiles came in groups at intervals of 20 to 30 minutes to complicate firefighting and recovery, and that Ukrainian forces struck three Russian ballistic missile launchers overnight, destroying one, in the latest account.

The attack’s second front is in Poland, and it is the evening’s sharpest new file. Two Kh-101 missiles came down at Nowy Lipowiec and Rudnik nad Sanem and a jet-powered drone at Molodycz, in the latest account, on the word of Defence Minister Kosiniak-Kamysz; no damage to infrastructure was reported at any of the three locations, in the blitz account. Prime Minister Tusk convened his ministers and security chiefs and called the fall of the weapons “an intentional provocation” of the alliance: “It does not look like an accident,” in the provocation account filed below. Mr. Kosiniak-Kamysz said the timing, during Coalition of the Willing exercises in Poland, was deliberate, and asked for additional NATO aircraft, airborne early warning aircraft and F-35 fighters, in the latest account; two Swedish Gripen fighters and a Dutch refuelling aircraft supported Poland’s air defence during the attack, in the same account. The NATO Secretary General, Mr. Rutte, condemned the attack and the violation of Polish airspace, in the provocation account; the European Commission President, Ms. von der Leyen, expressed full solidarity with Poland, in the strike account; and France’s foreign minister, Mr. Barrot, wrote that Russia will have to answer for the attack and called for a meeting of the Security Council, in the provocation account, which also records that Russian weapons hit the Austrian embassy in Kyiv and landed near the Netherlands embassy. President Zelensky’s answer runs against the conference in Washington: Russia, he wrote, is compensating for its military and diplomatic failure, and the attempt in Shenzhen to redraw the world had failed; asked whether describing President Putin as a friend gives him leave to keep attacking, he said a man so described “doesn’t feel that he is a killer, he doesn’t feel enough pressure,” in the strike account. The President’s own first word on the Polish falls, spoken aboard Air Force One on Thursday, was that President Putin “has been landing drones in Poland and a couple of little missiles, by mistake,” in the latest account. In Kyiv the repair stands where the midday file left it, confirmed in the latest account: traffic across the Podilskyi crossing was restored at about 3 p.m. local time, the Darnytskyi assessment continues, and the Pivdennyi remains partly restricted. The attack also struck the Western Ukrainian 750-kilovolt substation, the largest in Europe, and forced the nuclear plants to reduce generation, with parts of Kyiv and ten regions without electricity, in the latest account; Ukrainian forces struck the Druzhba oil pipeline pumping station in Tatarstan on Thursday, damaging three storage tanks, in the same account. Estonia will close all its road border crossings with Russia on December 1, in the same account. UNICEF’s figures for the year stand beside the night’s: 1,144 children killed or injured in Ukraine between January and October, 114 killed and 1,030 injured, in the same account. Settled: the count, the falls in Poland, and the reopening. Open: the Darnytskyi damage, the Security Council’s meeting, and the alliance’s answer.

The Gulf at 1800: A Fourth Tanker Burns Off Khasab, Eight Crossings on the Day, and Oil Settles Higher

The strait added a ship to its week on Friday afternoon. A tanker sailing outbound was struck by an unknown projectile eight nautical miles north of Khasab at about 1500 UTC, and its company security officer reported a fire on board, in the fire account filed below; no casualties or environmental impact were reported, and authorities are investigating. The strike hit the vessel’s port bow, and the warning stands as Warning 214-26, in the tanker account filed below. The Guards said shortly afterwards that an oil tanker had caught fire after hitting a mine while attempting to transit near Khasab, in the fire account; the mine explanation has not been independently verified, and the vessel’s name, flag and cargo stand in no account in the file, in the tanker account. The incident is the latest in a run of strikes near Khasab this week, after the engine-room strike of Wednesday evening and the attacks on the Tara Gas and the Navig8 Messi earlier in the week, in the same account.

The day’s count of the strait is fuller than the morning’s and still a fraction of normal. Eight commercial ships crossed on Friday as of 1802 UTC, five inbound and three outbound, two of them tankers, against six on Thursday, three of them tankers, and 47 in the last seven days, an average of 6.7 a day against 84.8 before the crisis, about 8 percent of normal, in the strait count filed below; the status stands severely restricted and the threat level severe, in the same account. A second tracker counts three crossings on Thursday, two outbound and one inbound, one of them dark, in the midday file’s companion readings—the desk prints the two trackers’ disagreement in the Cocked Hat, as it has all week. Oil’s Friday settlement reverses its intraday direction, and the desk prints both. Brent settled at $92.57 a barrel, up 59 cents, or 0.64 percent, and December West Texas Intermediate at $83.98, up 77 cents, or 0.93 percent, in the settlement account filed below. The intraday readings ran the other way: Brent down 67 cents, or 0.7 percent, at $91.31 at 1414 GMT, and December West Texas down 36 cents at $82.85, in the weekly account filed below, on strong export flows from the Middle East. On the week Brent is on track for a fall of near 5 percent and the December contract for 6.5 percent, a third straight weekly decline, in the same account. The OPEC+ ministers meet on November 29. Settled: the strike, the count, and the settlement. Open: the tanker’s name and state, the weapon, and the missing men of the week’s earlier ships.

France and Spain at 1800: A Law Passed in Paris, 205 to 110, and a Verdict Delivered in Madrid

France’s file, open since Thursday, closed on Friday. Senators gave final approval to the law presuming that police officers who use their weapons are acting lawfully, by 205 votes to 110, in the Paris account filed below; the lower house had passed the bill in July, in the passage account filed below. The law gives an officer who has shot someone the benefit of the doubt unless there is proof to the contrary, in the Paris account. The Interior Minister, Mr. Nuñez, supports it as an important measure to protect officers who work in dangerous conditions, in the same account, and Senator Josende, opening Thursday’s debate, said it provides legal protection tailored to the conditions in which officers intervene and lets them avoid being held in custody for questioning, in the same account. Against it stand the left-wing groups, which plan to refer the law to the Constitutional Council in a last effort to halt it, in the same account; the United Nations special rapporteur on extrajudicial executions, Mr. Tidball-Binz, wrote ahead of the vote that the burden of disproving lawfulness should not fall on victims and their families, especially where the authorities control the key evidence, in the same account; and a protester outside the Senate whose nephew was killed by an officer in 2021 said the quest for truth will become even more impossible, in the same account. Critics’ name for the law stands in the Paris account’s headline, and the desk prints it as their name for it. Settled: the vote, and the text. Open: the Council’s word.

Spain’s debate, unjudged at Thursday’s stamp, has its verdict, and Friday’s instruments agree where Thursday’s quick polls split. Mr. Feijóo emerged as the perceived winner of Thursday night’s face-off, in the Madrid account filed below: 51 percent to 29 percent in the Sigma Dos poll and 43 percent to 33 percent in the 40dB poll, the remainder naming neither man or a tie. The audience was 10.2 million, nearly double the 2023 debate’s, on the Barlovento consultancy’s figures, in the same account. Housing stood at the centre of the debate, with rival plans on rents and affordable supply; the two men also sparred over migration after the arrivals in Ceuta and the Canary Islands, and traded accusations over corruption scandals attached to their parties, in the same account. An analyst quoted there puts the debate’s effect as indirect—it can change the campaign’s tone through the post-debate consensus, which is why candidates try above all not to err. The standing instruments are unchanged in direction: an overwhelming majority of polls show an advantage for a bloc of the People’s Party and Vox, in the same account, and the second debate stands agreed for about November 26, as the midday file carried it. Spain votes November 29. Settled: the verdict, on two instruments. Open: whether the verdict moves the vote share, which no post-debate voting-intention poll in the file yet measures.

The Air at 1800: The Week Ends Without Its Root Cause

The week’s Washington promise stands unkept at the evening stamp, and the desk records the standing plainly. The Federal Aviation Administration said on Thursday that the government expected to announce this week the root cause of the November 13 ground stop; Friday’s file contains no announcement. What the file contains is a narrowed field. The agency’s investigation points to two sites—a radar site at Amarillo, Texas, and a radio site at El Dorado, Arkansas, where the contractor L3Harris had been installing a new router in a copper-to-fibre upgrade—and to data flooding the telecommunications network at 1,000 connection attempts every four to five seconds, destabilising 17 of the 22 en route centres, in the funding account filed below and in the week account filed below. Secretary Duffy’s formulation stands as the file’s firmest: “We think it was the installation or the router that was the bad actor,” in the week account; the two routers are at an L3Harris laboratory in Florida for examination, in the same account, and whether the fault is the equipment, the installation or the configuration is still under investigation, in the same account. There is no evidence of a cyberattack, in the same account. The agency has paused the router installations and disabled the broadcasting function on the new routers, as the midday file carried it. The administrator said the Secretary would brief the public on the investigation, in the update account filed below. Congress is questioning the Secretary’s request for $50 billion for modernisation as the inquiry proceeds, in the funding account. The Senate Commerce Committee is owed its technical answers by November 23, and the Thanksgiving travel week stands ahead of the file. Settled: the equipment, the two sites, and the precaution. Open: the root cause itself, now a week outstanding.
