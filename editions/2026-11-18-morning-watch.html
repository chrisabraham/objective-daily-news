The Morning in Brief: A Senate Called, a Quarter Priced, and a President Landed

The morning calls the last Senate race, prices the chipmaker’s quarter, and lands the President in Shenzhen. The Press called Alaska’s Senate race for Ms. Peltola at 10:19 p.m. Eastern on Tuesday, on a count of 144,302 votes to 131,844—50.12 percent of first choices, past the majority line, so the ranked-choice tabulation set for today will not be run for the Senate; the Senate of the 120th Congress stands 56 to 44. Nvidia reported revenue of $115.8 billion for the quarter ended October 25, up 103 percent on the year, and guided the current quarter to $130 billion, in the results account filed below; Asia’s Wednesday session bought the chip stocks back, Seoul closing up 2.0 percent. The Quad’s leaders, meeting in Manila on Tuesday, warned against economic coercion in a statement that named no country, in the Manila account filed below. The President landed in Shenzhen on Wednesday afternoon, China time, and was met on the tarmac by President Xi in person, in the arrival account filed below; President Putin, arrived earlier, was met by an aide. In the strait, a gas carrier abandoned off Oman has a seafarer missing, and an Emirati operator counts eleven injured on a second vessel. The desk takes the files in that order.

What the morning settled is the Senate, the quarter, and the order of the Shenzhen week—Manila on Tuesday, the arrival today, the bilateral and the first trilateral on Thursday. What it left open is the rest of the file: the House tabulation in Juneau, the cause of Friday’s stop, the price of the Anthropic listing tonight, and whether the strait’s missing seafarer is found.

Alaska, at 1006: 144,302 to 131,844, Past the Line on the First Count, and the House to a Tabulation

Alaska’s count ended on Tuesday night, two weeks after Election Day, and it ended on the first count. The Division of Elections’ Tuesday evening count, with all 401 precincts reporting and 290,003 ballots cast, stands at 144,302 votes for Ms. Peltola, 50.12 percent, to 131,844, 45.80 percent, for Senator Sullivan—a lead of 12,458—with Mr. Heikes at 6,105, Mr. Daniel J. Sullivan Jr. at 4,760 and 885 write-ins, in the call account filed below. The Press called the race for Ms. Peltola at 10:19 p.m. Eastern on Tuesday and NBC News projected her the winner the same night, in the same account. Because she cleared half of the first-choice votes, the ranked-choice tabulation scheduled for today will not be run for the Senate seat; no ballot is redistributed.

The margin answers the file’s longest-running question by itself. Hand the Senator every vote cast for the other Mr. Sullivan, every vote for Mr. Heikes and every write-in, and he would still finish 708 votes behind Ms. Peltola, in the call account—the arithmetic that the recruitment case, in which a retired teacher of the Senator’s surname was alleged to have been recruited to divide the vote, will now be argued against in the courts; the Justice Department is appealing a dismissal and the criminal investigation remains active, in the majority account filed below. Ms. Peltola, 53, is Yup’ik and becomes the first Alaska Native to serve in the United States Senate, in the same account.

The seat is the ninth the Democrats flipped in the midterms, the most since 1958, and it makes the Senate 56 to 44, counting the two independents who caucus with the party, against the 53 to 47 the Republicans carried into the elections, in the call account. The state’s House seat stands open: Mr. Begich leads the independent Mr. Hill 47.54 percent to 41.77, short of a majority, so that race goes to the ranked-choice count today, in the same account. Overseas ballots may arrive through today; the state finishes counting today and plans to certify on November 25, in the accounts filed below. Settled: the Senate, on the first count, by 12,458 votes. Open: the House seat, in today’s tabulation, and the court file, which outlives the count.

Nvidia, at 1006: $115.8 Billion Reported, $130 Billion Guided, and No Return for Mr. Huang

Nvidia’s third quarter, the first reported since Mr. Huang’s resignation on November 12, beat the company’s own guidance by more than the guidance’s margin. Revenue for the quarter ended October 25 was $115.8 billion, up 20 percent on the quarter and 103 percent on the year, against the company’s outlook of $108.0 billion, plus or minus 2 percent, and a consensus of about $109 billion, in the results account filed below. Data Center revenue was $107.6 billion, up 110 percent on the year and about 93 percent of the total; earnings were $2.68 a share on the adjusted basis against about $2.47 expected, and $2.70 on the reported basis, with net income of $65.3 billion, up 105 percent; gross margin was 74.8 percent reported and 74.9 adjusted, in the quarter account filed below. The filing was signed by Ms. Kress in her capacity as interim chief executive and chief financial officer, in the read-back account filed below.

The guidance is the report’s second fact. For the current quarter the company expects revenue of $130.0 billion, plus or minus 3 percent, against a consensus near $125 billion, with gross margin of 71 to 72 percent, in a 14-week quarter that includes an extra week, and on the stated assumption of no data-center compute revenue from China, in the quarter account. Ms. Kress raised the outlook for fiscal 2028 growth to about 75 percent, from about 70, and put the cloud industry’s revenue backlog at roughly $2.5 trillion, up from about $2 trillion a quarter ago, in the same account. The chief financial officer’s commentary carries the supply file forward: supply commitments rose from $279 billion last quarter to $390 billion, primarily for multi-year procurement of memory supply, in the read-back account. The board added $150 billion to the buyback authorization in September, leaving about $227.3 billion available, in the quarter account.

The leadership question gained its plainest sentence in the earnings coverage: the board said it will not reinstate Mr. Huang and is searching for a permanent successor, in the quarter account. Monday’s file had the board’s position at three removes: reported not weighing a return, and not in talks, by a person familiar with the matter. The shares rose in extended trading on Tuesday evening—by 2 to 3 percent in one account, about 4 in another and 4.7 in a third; all three readings stand in the cocked hat. The quarter itself predates the resignation: the numbers show the company Mr. Huang ran, and the guidance the one Ms. Kress runs. No finding has been made by the board, by any committee or by any court on any allegation in the leadership file.

Manila, at 1006: A Statement Against Coercion That Names No Country, and a Minerals Claim Without a Text

The four leaders met in Manila on Tuesday—the President, Prime Minister Modi of India, Prime Minister Albanese of Australia and Prime Minister Takaichi of Japan—the first Quad leaders’ meeting in more than two years, the first of the President’s second term, and the first held in a country that is not a member, in the summit account filed below. Their joint statement opposed “any destabilizing or unilateral actions that seek to change the status quo, including by force or coercion,” and expressed “grave concerns” about “economic coercion, including through arbitrary export restrictions,” in the statement account filed below. The statement named no country.

The announced work is technical and maritime. An inaugural Quad Business Leaders’ Forum will meet in early 2027; the four support the Super Intelligence Accelerator announced by their foreign ministers in Atlanta, backed by more than $85 million in the Manila paper’s account; a new Indo-Pacific Maritime Surveillance Collaboration will share information, and in 2027 the four countries’ P-8 and P-3C patrol aircraft will fly a joint exercise for the first time under the Quad name; Australia expands its contribution to the cable partnership to electricity cables and to undersea projects in the Indian Ocean; and cybersecurity cooperation is enlarged, citing the rapid increase in incidents in the region, in the statement account. The statement uses “Super Intelligence” where the other three governments say artificial intelligence, with a footnote explaining the equivalence, in the same account.

One claim stands in the President’s words and not in the statement. At the meeting the President said the four had signed several important new critical minerals and rare earths deals to secure supply chains against coercion by any single foreign supplier, and that the United States would match China’s rare earth output within 10 months—“We’re going to catch them within 10 months”—in the minerals account filed below. No details of the agreements—minerals, companies or values—were made public, and the leaders’ joint statement did not mention critical minerals, in the same account. A separate American-Philippine technology pact will not be signed on this trip, in the statement account. The President’s Tuesday closed with the ASEAN-United States special summit and dinner in the overnight file; on Wednesday morning he was received by President Marcos Jr. at Malacañang Palace for the welcome ceremony of his state visit to the Philippines, in the arrival accounts filed below. Settled: the statement, the forum, the exercise. Open: the minerals, which stand as a claim without a text.

The Gulf, at 1006: The Tara Gas Abandoned, Eleven Injured on an Unnamed Ship, and No Word on the Radar for a Fourth Day

The strait’s file gained names and a missing man. The Panama-flagged gas carrier Tara Gas, 53,200 tons, was struck twice off Oman’s Musandam Peninsula—first on Monday evening, disabling the ship and firing its engine room, and again on Tuesday, when a second strike set it ablaze and its crew of 25 abandoned it—in the shipping account filed below. Twenty-four of the crew were rescued, 21 of them Indian nationals brought ashore at Khasab; one Indian seafarer is missing and two others were injured, in the same account. The British maritime agency had reported the first attack at about seven nautical miles north of Khasab, in the overnight file and the same account.

A second operator’s statement does not obviously describe the same ship. ADNOC Logistics and Services, the shipping arm of Abu Dhabi’s state energy company, said on Wednesday that one crew member was missing and 11 injured after one of its vessels was attacked in the strait on Tuesday, that the rest of the crew had been safely evacuated, and that search and rescue continued; the company did not name the vessel or say what struck it, in the operator account filed below. A compiled account names a second gas tanker struck on Tuesday and identifies the Tara Gas as that vessel, in the vessels account filed below; whether the ADNOC ship and the Tara Gas are one ship or two stands unresolved in the file, and the desk prints both statements as they stand. The same compiled account names the Liberia-flagged crude tanker Al Dhanna, its stern steering gear room damaged in the Gulf before reaching the strait, turned back toward Dubai. The On Phoenix, struck by an unidentified projectile while leaving the strait early on Monday, stands in the overnight file as the week’s first named ship.

The two militaries traded fire on Tuesday on the file’s own terms. American fighter aircraft struck a Revolutionary Guards missile launch site on Qeshm Island after Guards drones targeted an American radar system at an air base in Bahrain; Central Command said the strike was self-defence, that the drones were intercepted, and that there was no damage to American equipment or personnel, in the vessels account. The Guards claimed several tankers struck naval mines on routes Tehran considers unauthorised, and claimed to have shot down an American MQ-1 Predator drone over Qeshm, the 18th such interception claimed since the war began; neither claim has been independently confirmed, in the same accounts. The Guards’ standing arithmetic—six tankers caught fire and sunk since Sunday—remains a claim without a named or confirmed ship, in the vessels account. The United Nations’ maritime agency has recorded 136 security incidents involving vessels in and around the strait between March 1 and November 11, 35 of them in October, in the same account; satellite tracking counted an average of 0.4 tankers a day through the strait in the week to November 15, against 40.4 a day before the war, in the strait account filed below. On the Bahrain radar itself—the machine at the centre of the week’s oldest open question—Central Command’s Tuesday sentence says drones targeted a radar system and were intercepted with no damage; the earlier file’s question, what Friday’s attack did to the radar at Al-Riffa, gains no assessment from any capital for a fourth day.

The Air File, at 1006: The Router Model Disabled, the Cause Recreated but Not Found, and $4 Billion Questioned

The cause file for Friday’s ground stop gained a mechanism and kept its mystery. The Secretary, after sitting in on the Senate Republicans’ weekly lunch on Tuesday, told reporters that investigators have recreated the failure but cannot yet say what caused it—“We do not yet have clarity on the root cause of what happened”—in the cause account filed below. The router at the centre of it was the 69th of its kind installed, after 68 others worked without problems; it began broadcasting into the network, and the agency believed its broadcasting feature had been disabled and does not know how its configuration changed, the Administrator said at the same briefing, in the same account. The failure reached communications and radar systems at 17 of the country’s 22 regional control centres, in the installations account filed below.

The agency has acted on the machine, if not on the cause. The Federal Aviation Administration said on Tuesday it would pause installation of new routers, has suspended use of that router model, made by Juniper Networks, and has disabled units already in service while investigators examine the problem, in the cause account and the installations account. The contractor’s copper-to-fibre work at El Dorado, Arkansas, and the second site at Amarillo stand in the earlier file. No second nationwide stop stands in any account the desk read, and the Secretary said the problem has been isolated, that the airspace should operate normally through the Thanksgiving travel days, and that he would halt flights again if needed, in the cause account.

The money argument acquired its Republican questions. The Secretary said the $12.5 billion approved last year is committed, and that the department pays contractors only when equipment is delivered; he would accept any amount Congress approves and urged the $50 billion, in the cause account. Senator Cassidy questioned the agency’s handling of the money, put the unspent figure at about $4 billion—the figure the Majority Leader used on Monday and the Democrats’ account rendered as spent or committed—and suggested an independent investigation could help determine the cause, in the funding account filed below. The President’s sentence stands in the earlier file: very, very old equipment that had a heart attack. Settled: the model is suspended and the installs are paused. Open: the root cause, which the recreation did not supply, and whether Congress pays before December 11.

Measles, at 1006: Elimination Withdrawn From Three Countries, 5,412 Cases, and Twelve Months to Win It Back

The hemisphere’s measles map was redrawn on Tuesday. The Pan American Health Organization’s Director, Dr. Barbosa, accepted the recommendation of its verification commission, which met from November 10 to 13, that endemic measles transmission had been reestablished in the United States, Mexico and Bolivia, and withdrew the three countries’ elimination status, in the status account filed below. The test is the same one Canada failed a year ago: the same genotype and lineage circulating without interruption for twelve months or more. The United States had held the status since 2000, Mexico since 1996.

The counts behind the decision are the worst in a generation. The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention counted 5,412 confirmed cases in the United States in 2026 through November 12, the highest annual total since 1991, across 45 states and the capital district, with more than 590 hospitalizations, and 2,289 cases in 2025—more than 7,700 across the two years, in the status account and the decision account filed below. The region counted 58,213 confirmed cases and 82 deaths in 2026 as of November 13, in the same accounts. The deaths stand apart in the file’s readings: thirteen dead since 2025 in one account, six deaths in the CDC’s 2026 count in another; both stand in the cocked hat.

The American answers were given the same day. The CDC’s Director, Dr. Schwartz, said the designation is not what protects Americans and that vaccination does, and that the loss would not change the agency’s approach; the Secretary, Mr. Kennedy, promised in a social media post on Tuesday that the administration will stop the outbreaks and work to regain the lost status, in the decision account. To regain it, each country must submit a corrective action plan—expected in the coming weeks—and then document twelve months without endemic transmission, in the status account. Bolivia’s Health Minister said her country’s cases had dropped 83 percent this year, crediting vaccination, tracing, border controls and community surveillance, in the setback account filed below.

France and Spain, at 1006: A Strike Day Smaller Than Its Billing, 15,000 Detentions Counted, and a Tracking at 131 to 122 to 55

France’s strike day came in smaller than its billing. Turnout at Tuesday’s one-day public sector strike fell sharply and the day passed largely peacefully, in the detentions account filed below; the police had expected 220,000 to 300,000 nationwide and 40,000 to 80,000 in Paris, in the strike account filed below. The Education Ministry’s participation figures are the day’s hardest numbers: 3.14 percent of its employees struck, 3.76 percent of teachers and 9.28 percent of school support staff, in the strike account. The movement’s ledger gained its official total on the same day: the Justice Minister told parliament on Tuesday that about 15,000 people have been arrested or detained across the cost-of-living and school protests, 2,661 of them minors from the cost-of-living marches, more than 3,000 charged and 195 sent directly to prison, in the detentions account. The anniversary week runs on: roundabout and toll-booth occupations are called from Friday to Sunday, a sixth Saturday of marches on November 21, and the police bill goes to the Senate on Thursday, in the same accounts.

Spain’s campaign moved a few seats and no order. The fifth instalment of the 40dB daily tracking, published Wednesday on fieldwork from November 13 to 17, puts the Popular Party at 131 seats, the Socialists at 122 and Vox at 55—Vox three fewer than Tuesday’s instalment, the two right parties together at 186, ten above the 176 needed—and keeps Mr. Sánchez ahead of Mr. Feijóo by ten points in the preference to lead the government, in the tracking account filed below. The average of 17 institutes, kept by the same paper, stands at 31.4 percent for the Popular Party, 27.3 for the Socialists and 18.9 for Vox, in the same file. The week’s other trackings stand apart from each other in the cocked hat’s keeping. Thursday brings the campaign’s single head-to-head debate, at 22:00 at the Television Academy, moderated by its president, Ms. Casado, across five blocks—housing, the economy and taxes, social policy, corruption, and migration and foreign policy—with Mr. Feijóo opening by lot and Mr. Sánchez holding the last word, in the debate account filed below. Spain votes on November 29; polls may publish until November 23.

The fine print, in brief. Objective, as this paper uses the word, means the desk is not driven by an agenda, is not an advocate, is not a politician, is not modeling behavior, and is not training up citizens: it is reporting the news as if it were objective. Subjective means the item comes from a source with its own position, as all sources have; it is printed because it is useful information, weighed by the reader. Objectivity is relative; everything is relative, even relativity is relative; absolutism is wrong. No source in these pages is more reputable than a press release issued directly from Langley.
