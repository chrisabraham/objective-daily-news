Guadalajara Opens at Forty, Berlin and Basel Open on the Same Morning, and Venice Files 714,015

One fair opens at forty today and two exhibitions open on the same morning in Berlin and Basel, while Venice files its closing count of 714,015, Riga opens a solo exhibition in a cupola hall, London marks a sculptor’s 150th year with four sculptures, Seoul marks a building’s 100th year with six artists, Hanoi grills for five continents in its second day, and Miami Beach counts 272 exhibitors ahead of a week that opens Monday. The desk files the week in that order.

By Eunice, the desk · Collected and analyzed at 0410 ET, Saturday, November 28, 2026. The fourth Saturday Culture Feature of this paper, filed ahead of the day's watches under the Weekend Features ruling of November 4, 2026. The Week in Brief

One fair opens at forty today, two museum exhibitions open on the same morning in two other countries, and the week’s largest count is already filed: the Guadalajara International Book Fair opens its 40th edition today, Saturday, November 28, at Expo Guadalajara, and runs to December 6; Berlin’s Neue Nationalgalerie opens Ulay, Ulay & Abramović today, and Kunstmuseum Basel | Gegenwart opens When I’m Here today; and the 61st Venice Biennale, which this desk filed at its closing last Saturday, closed on November 22 with 714,015 visitors, the second-highest total in its history, on a record 100 National Participations. Around those three points the week fills in order: Riga opens a solo exhibition in a cupola hall today; London and Seoul opened on Wednesday, November 25; Hanoi’s culinary festival is in its second day today and closes Sunday; and Miami Beach’s art week opens Monday, November 30, ahead of its fair on December 4.

Venice: The Count Is Filed at 714,015

The 61st International Art Exhibition of La Biennale di Venezia, In Minor Keys, by Koyo Kouoh, closed on Sunday, November 22, following ten days of debates, conferences, roundtables and performances at the Arsenale, and its closing statement, filed below, gives the edition its final arithmetic: 714,015 visitors, including 29,387 during the pre-opening. The run was three weeks shorter than previous editions, shortened to allow the installation of the renovated Central Pavilion at the Giardini, and the total is the second highest in the exhibition’s history; the 2022 edition holds the record at more than 800,000, in the closing report filed below. The participations stand at the record the desk filed last week: 100 National Participations, 29 in the historic Pavilions at the Giardini, 25 at the Arsenale and 46 around the city, with seven countries participating for the first time and El Salvador participating for the first time with its own pavilion, and 31 Collateral Events, in the Biennale’s closing statement filed below. The same statement counts 19 performances, 8 lectures, debates and roundtables, 3,831 accredited journalists in the preview days alone, over 5,500 articles in the press review, and 73,264 participants in the educational activities and guided tours. Last Saturday this desk filed the exhibition open, in its final weekend, at €5 for the city that hosts it; the file now closes it, with the number the whole season was building toward.

The artist count in the closing file differs by one from the count this desk printed last week, and the difference stands in the Cocked Hat. The closing statement and the closing report both count 109 artists at the International Exhibition, carried out after Kouoh’s death in May 2025, at the age of 57, by her team of five collaborators, named in both accounts. The Biennale’s own exhibition page, filed with last week’s feature, counted 110 invited participants, among them individual artists, collaborative duos, collectives and artist-led organisations. The desk prints the closing figure for the exhibition as closed and files the earlier figure beside it, unreconciled, in the Cocked Hat below.

Guadalajara: A Book Fair Opens at Forty

The Guadalajara International Book Fair opens today, Saturday, November 28, at Expo Guadalajara, and its 40th edition runs to Sunday, December 6, with Italy as Guest of Honour, eighteen years after Italy last held the place. The fair’s own page, filed below, sets the opening ceremony at 11:00 a.m. today in the Juan Rulfo Auditorium, by invitation, and the general hours at 9:00 to 21:00 today and tomorrow, with admission at 40 pesos for adults. The scale is stated three ways in the file, and the desk prints each as its source’s: an estimated 900,000 attendees, including some 18,000 publishing professionals representing 2,800 publishers from more than 60 countries, in the trade account filed below; and, as the fair’s standing description, over 800 writers, 18,000 professionals, 2,800 publishing houses and an audience of nearly one million, in the Italian ministry’s notice filed below. The program carries more than 800 book presentations, in the trade account, and the children’s fair, FIL Niños, runs the full nine days inside it, under the premise its organisers state as: playing is resisting, and reading is also resisting, in the university’s program account filed below.

Italy arrives under a sentence of Umberto Eco’s: “The world speaks to us like a great book.” The delegation’s size is counted three ways in the file, and the counts stand unmerged in the Cocked Hat: some 60 authors headed by Alessandro Baricco, who opens the Carlos Fuentes literary salon, with 30 Italian publishers and a pavilion offering 8,200 books, in the trade account; 61 authors and more than 30 publishing houses, with a 1,200-square-metre pavilion carrying the same 8,200 books in Italian and Spanish translation, in the university account; and 58 authors and representatives across more than 120 literary and professional events, with more than 40 publishers in the pavilion, in the ministry’s notice. Beyond the delegation, the fair’s honours are single-valued: Gioconda Belli receives the FIL Award for Literature in Romance Languages, stated at $150,000 in the trade account; Josefina Licitra receives the Sor Juana Inés de la Cruz Prize for her novel Crac; the anniversary program runs 26 in ’26, twenty-six authors under forty from thirteen Spanish-speaking countries; and Juan Rulfo is remembered forty years after his death with a panel and an immersive pavilion built on the Media Luna hacienda of Pedro Páramo, in the university account. A fair that began in 1987 opens its fortieth year, in its own city, with its guest arriving from the country that held the place in 2008.

Berlin: The Stolen Painting Comes Home at Fifty

The Neue Nationalgalerie opens Ulay, Ulay & Abramović today, Saturday, November 28, in its lower gallery at Potsdamer Straße 50, and the exhibition runs to May 2, 2027. Its occasion is dated to the day in the museum’s own account, filed below: on 12 December 1976, Ulay took Carl Spitzweg’s The Poor Poet (1839) from this museum and drove it to the home of a guest worker family in Kreuzberg, and fifty years later the museum brings his work back into the building where the action took place. The central work is that action and its film documentation, Irritation—There is a Criminal Touch to Art, shown in dialogue with a version of The Poor Poet on loan from the Grohmann Museum in Milwaukee, in the museum’s exhibition page filed below. Around it stand the early works, S’he (1973–1974), a series of self-portraits on identity and transformation, and FOTOTOT (1976), whose unfixed photographs gradually disappear when exposed to light, and the twelve years of collaboration with Marina Abramović, from 1976 to 1988, which began with Relation in Space at the 38th Venice Biennale and ended with The Lovers, a 90-day journey along the Great Wall of China. The collaboration is present in the building every day, not only on film: Imponderabilia (1977), in which the two artists stood facing each other in a narrow entrance and visitors passed between their bodies, is performed live daily from 3 to 4:30 p.m. Admission is 12 euros, 6 euros concessions, and the museum states plainly that the exhibition contains explicit nudity and depictions of injury, violence and physical pain.

Basel: More Than Fifty Artists, Arranged by Prepositions

Kunstmuseum Basel | Gegenwart opens When I’m Here today, Saturday, November 28, and the exhibition runs to March 21, 2027, across two gallery floors of the Gegenwart building at St. Alban-Rheinweg 60. The works run from 1970 to 2026 and number more than fifty artists, almost half of whom live in Basel, drawn from three collections of the same city: the Öffentliche Kunstsammlung Basel, the Emanuel Hoffmann Foundation and Kunstkredit Basel-Stadt, in the exhibition account filed below. The title is a sentence a painting addresses to its viewer: Rémy Zaugg’s canvas of 1995/98 reads, “And would the world look at me when I’m here,” and the exhibition is built on that proposition, that a work generates its meaning in the encounter, in a specific site, with a specific viewer. Its order is grammatical: six sections named for prepositions—In, With, Behind, Across, In Front, Between—in the English of the exhibition account, and In, Mit, Hinter, Gegenüber, Vor and Zwischen in the German of the magazine account filed below, which also gives the curators, Stephanie Seidel and Team Gegenwart. In takes the room itself, floors, furniture and architecture; Behind takes the readymade’s descendants, ordinary objects transplanted into the gallery; Across takes the body, in portraits and representations that redirect the gaze to the beholder’s own body; Between takes the familiar and the strange, where the visible and the concealed blend. The building is part of the argument: the Gegenwart opened in 1980, in a converted nineteenth-century paper mill, as one of Europe’s earliest museums built for contemporary art, and Zaugg himself wrote, in 1986, a book-length examination of what a museum’s architecture does to the art inside it.

London, Seoul, Riga: Two Openings on Wednesday, One Today

Three further exhibitions stand in the week, two opened on Wednesday, November 25, and one opens today beside Berlin and Basel. In London, Pace opened Constantin Brancusi: Matrix Muse at 5 Hanover Square on November 25, to January 30, 2027, in celebration of the 150th anniversary of the artist’s birth, and as the gallery’s first solo exhibition of his work since it announced representation of the estate, in the exhibition account filed below. The presentation is small and exact: four major sculptures, among them Le premier cri (1917) and the two muses, La muse endormie I (1912) in plaster and Une Muse (1918) in bronze, drawings in ink and gouache from 1917 to 1925 that have never been exhibited, and notes, photographs and objects from the artist’s archive shown for the first time; admission is free. In Seoul, the Ilmin Museum of Art opened MANA on November 25, to January 24, 2027, to mark the 100th anniversary of its building, in the announcement filed below: six artists, Goyoson, Jisu Kwak, Donghee Koo, Murphy Yum, Hyungkoo Lee and Geumhyung Jeong, working in sculpture, installation and video on the forces that act on bodies, machines and objects, with a new mobile by Hyungkoo Lee held in balance by stainless-steel wires in the atrium of the century-old building. In Riga, Laimdota Malle’s Stardust opens today, November 28, in the Cupola Hall of the Latvian National Museum of Art, to February 21, 2027, in the exhibition account filed below: seven interconnected installations in wax, ceramic plaster and other translucent materials, with a soundscape by Sarma Gabrēna, in which matter accumulates, organises itself into a form, solidifies, dissolves and rearranges itself into another. Three cities, three scales: an estate’s four sculptures, a building’s hundredth year, and one artist alone in a cupola.

Hanoi: Five Continents at the Grill, in Its Second Day

The Hanoi Global Culinary Festival, Firebeat 2026, opened on Friday, November 27, at the Vietnam Exposition Center in Dong Anh, Hanoi, stands in its second day today, and closes on Sunday, November 29. It is held to celebrate Vietnam Culture Day, under the theme rendered in its announcement as “Flavours of Five Continents, One Global Beat,” and the organising committee’s aim is stated at 150,000 visits over the three days, in the Center’s announcement filed below. The grounds cover more than 43,000 square metres of the Center’s South Yard, with five culinary zones and more than 200 brands. The centrepiece is fire: a Global Fire zone for grilling, roasting and smoking techniques from five continents, and a World Chef’s Grand Feast at which nearly 30 international chefs prepare Texas smoked brisket, Brazilian picanha, Argentine asado and South African braai on the spot. Against the imports stands the host city’s own zone, Hanoi Heritage, serving bún chả, chả cá, phở, bánh cuốn, the young green rice flakes of Vòng village and egg coffee, with artisans working at the counter. The competitions, including the Global BBQ Championship, are endorsed by an international jury from the two chefs’ and barbecue associations named in the announcement, and the festival’s other rooms are a concert and a Global Culture Village of performances and exchanges. The aim of 150,000 is the committee’s; the desk files it as an aim, and the count, when it is filed, will stand in a later edition.

Miami Beach: The Week Opens Monday, the Fair on Friday

The week’s last file is a calendar, not an opening: Art Week Miami Beach runs from Monday, November 30, to Sunday, December 6, and Art Basel Miami Beach stands at its centre, with preview days on December 2 and 3 and public days from Friday, December 4, to Sunday, December 6, at the Miami Beach Convention Center. The fair’s count is 272 exhibitors, from more than 40 countries and territories in the fair’s announcement filed below and from 40 countries and territories in the city’s lineup filed below; the wording difference stands in the Cocked Hat. The edition is deliberately smaller on the floor: approximately 12 percent less space than last year, approximately 10 percent fewer exhibitors in the main Galleries sector, longer sightlines around four hospitality plazas instead of five, and blue-chip galleries distributed across the floor instead of grouped, in the fair’s announcement, whose director states the redesign begins with the galleries and that the smaller footprint is a deliberate choice. Inside the count: 204 exhibitors in Galleries, 20 galleries in Nova, 12 solo projects in Positions, 16 historically focused presentations in Survey, and Zero 10, the digital-era initiative, at its largest yet, with 19 projects. More than 30 galleries take part for the first time, and five countries are represented for the first time: the Bahamas, Estonia, Georgia, Ghana and Hungary. The city fills the week around the fair with its own instruments: No Vacancy, its juried exhibition in 12 hotel lobbies, running since November 12 to December 19, and the Legacy Purchase Program, whose public vote ran November 20 to 23 and whose selected work, bought by the city for the Convention Center, is unveiled on December 1. Where Guadalajara opens today and closes on the same Sunday, December 6, Miami’s fair opens as Guadalajara’s closes: the two files meet at the hinge of the same weekend, and next Saturday’s feature will file Miami’s first days.

My World of Interiors — the viewer in the picture, Kunstmuseum Basel's Wenn ich hier bin

Basel, November 28: opening on 28 November, the exhibition Wenn ich hier bin (When I'm Here) at Kunstmuseum Basel | Gegenwart brings together more than fifty artists from three collections, exploring how artworks are seen and the circumstances that shape their interpretation; it takes its title from a painting by the Swiss artist Rémy Zaugg, made between 1995 and 1998, whose inscription reads, in translation, 'And were the world to look at me when I'm here'; Zaugg (1943–2005) spent much of his career investigating perception, often through paintings in which language becomes the principal visual element, and in 1986 he wrote The Art Museum of My Dreams, or A Place for the Work and the Human Being, a detailed examination of museum architecture and its effect on the experience of art; the exhibition gathers works made between 1970 and 2026 by more than fifty artists, almost half of whom live in Basel, and is curated by Stephanie Seidel and Team Gegenwart across two floors in six thematic sections named after German prepositions, In, Mit, Hinter, Gegenüber, Vor and Zwischen; the Gegenwart building opened in 1980 as the Museum für Gegenwartskunst, one of Europe's earliest museums dedicated specifically to contemporary art, in a converted nineteenth-century paper mill with a purpose-built extension; the account treats VALIE EXPORT's Syntagma (1983), an experimental film investigating the representation of the female body, and Thomas Ruff's Nacht 9 II (1992), from his Nights series made with a light intensifier after televised night-vision images of the Gulf War; the three collections are traced to their origins, the Öffentliche Kunstsammlung to the city's acquisition of the Amerbach Cabinet in 1661, the Emanuel Hoffmann Foundation to 1933, and Kunstkredit Basel-Stadt to a municipal programme begun in 1919; and the exhibition runs from 28 November 2026 to 21 March 2027 at St. Alban-Rheinweg 60, Basel.

Artlyst — Constantin Brancusi: Matrix Muse at Pace, London

London: in celebration of the 150th anniversary of the birth of Constantin Brancusi, Pace will present Matrix Muse, its first solo exhibition of the artist's work since announcing representation of the estate, at its gallery in London from November 25, 2026, through January 30, 2027; featuring never-before-exhibited drawings and archival materials alongside four major sculptures, Matrix Muse will trace Brancusi's fascination with metaphysical origins; the exhibition is curated by Dr. Jérôme Neutres; in Late Latin, 'matrix' signified 'womb' as well as 'source' and 'origin,' deriving from 'māter,' or 'mother'; the presentation includes sculptures in plaster and polished bronze and a selection of drawings and writings, photographs and objects from the artist's archive; the bronze sculpture Le premier cri (The First Cry) (1917) illustrates the elemental form, and the exhibition spotlights two muse sculptures, La muse endormie I (Sleeping Muse I) (1912), a plaster cast, and Une Muse (A Muse) (1918), an upright bronze figure on a stone base; a selection of never-before-exhibited drawings in ink and gouache from 1917 to 1925 will also feature, and personal notes, photographs and items from the artist's archive will be on view for the first time; admission is free, Tuesday to Saturday, 10 a.m. to 6 p.m., at 5 Hanover Square, London; and Pace's presentation will coincide with Brancusi: The Artist and His Studio at the Museum of Modern Art in New York, on view through February 27, 2027.

e-flux — MANA at the Ilmin Museum of Art, Seoul, November 25 to January 24

Seoul, November 25: the Ilmin Museum of Art, directed by Kim Taeryung, presents MANA from Wednesday, November 25, 2026, through Sunday, January 24, 2027, to mark the 100th anniversary of the museum's building, at 152 Sejong-daero, Jongno-gu, Seoul; MANA attends to the diverse forces acting on bodies, machines and objects through the lens of contemporary art, and examines how humans exchange force with other beings; through sculpture, installation and video, the six participating artists, Goyoson, Jisu Kwak, Donghee Koo, Murphy Yum, Hyungkoo Lee and Geumhyung Jeong, explore how external conditions alter the body, how resistance and equilibrium sustain an object's form, and how machines and humans depend on one another; the exhibition takes its title from 'mana,' a term from the traditional cultures of Oceania that refers to an impersonal force residing in things and influencing the world; Exhibition Hall 1 features works by Geumhyung Jeong, including her debut Toy Collection (2026), and Hyungkoo Lee; Exhibition Hall 2 presents Donghee Koo and Jisu Kwak; Exhibition Hall 3 and the Project Room present Murphy Yum, whose new work Amniotic Song (2026) is a soundscape composed by imagining the interior of a long-disused elevator in the museum, and Goyoson, who creates sculptures that visitors operate using controllers; and in the atrium, Hyungkoo Lee's new work VIZ VEXTO (2026), produced with the support of Tiffany & Co., is held in balance by the tension of stainless-steel wires, using light and movement to make visible the forces at work in the space.

Echo Gone Wrong — Stardust by Laimdota Malle, in the Cupola Hall, Riga

Riga: Stardust, a solo exhibition of Laimdota Malle, is on view in the Cupola Hall of the main building of the Latvian National Museum of Art, at Jaņa Rozentāla laukums 1, Riga, from 28 November 2026 to 21 February 2027; the exhibition explores potential as a field of possibilities and transformations in which forms emerge, change and disappear, and, conceived as a multipart system, it brings together materials, bodies and structures in a state of constant transformation, in which abundant growth and gradual decay, control and vulnerability, chaos and self-organisation coexist; matter briefly organises itself into a particular form, solidifies, only to dissolve and rearrange itself into another, and each configuration holds a range of future possibilities, including the possibility that they may remain unrealised; the exhibition consists of seven interconnected installations, encompassing the accumulation and decay of matter, its division, classification and transformation, mechanisms of protection and control, and the possibility of forms and events yet to be realised; the exhibition is created in collaboration with sound artist Sarma Gabrēna, who has composed its soundscape; and Laimdota Malle is a visual artist working with wax, ceramic plaster and other translucent materials, who holds an MA in Visual Arts from the Art Academy of Latvia and completed the MA Fine Art programme at Manchester Metropolitan University, and who in 2026 was nominated for the Latvian Art of the Year Award 2025 in the category Artist of the Year.

Vietnam Exposition Center — the Hanoi Global Culinary Festival, Firebeat 2026

Hanoi, November 6: the Vietnam Exposition Center has announced the Hanoi Global Culinary Festival, the Hanoi Firebeat World Festival, to be held from 27 to 29 November 2026 to celebrate Vietnam Culture Day while fostering global cultural connection and exchange; themed 'Flavours of Five Continents, One Global Beat,' the festival offers culinary experiences, a music concert and cultural exchange activities, and is expected to welcome 150,000 visits; the festival is organised by the Vietnam Exposition Center under the direction of the Hanoi People's Committee and the Hanoi Department of Culture, Sports and Tourism, with the participation of the World Association of Chefs' Societies and the World Barbecue Association; the festival grounds are set in the Center's South Yard, covering more than 43,000 square metres, with five main culinary zones and more than 200 brands presenting food and drinks, and its signature experiences include the Global BBQ Championship, the World Culinary Festival, the Firebeat Global Concert and the Global Culture Village; the World Chef's Grand Feast zone brings together nearly 30 international chefs, preparing Texas smoked brisket, Brazilian picanha, Argentine asado and South African braai on the spot; the Hanoi Heritage zone celebrates the host city's identity through bún chả, chả cá, phở, bánh cuốn, treats made from the young green rice flakes of Vòng village and egg coffee; the competitions are endorsed by an international jury from the two associations; and the Organising Committee aims to attract around 150,000 visits over three days, at the Vietnam Exposition Center, Tu Lien Bridge Road, Dong Anh, Hanoi.

Art Basel — 272 exhibitors and a reconfigured showfloor for Miami Beach 2026

Miami Beach: Art Basel Miami Beach will bring together 272 exhibitors from more than 40 countries and territories across its sectors and Zero 10; Art Basel Miami Beach, whose Global Lead Partner is UBS, takes place December 4 to 6, 2026, at the Miami Beach Convention Center, with VIP Preview Days December 2 and 3; new in 2026, the fair presents a more focused gallery line-up across a substantially reconfigured showfloor, occupying approximately 12 percent less floor space, with Galleries, its main sector, featuring approximately 10 percent fewer exhibitors, organised around four hospitality plazas rather than five; more than 30 galleries participate for the first time, most through the project-based sectors Nova, Positions and Survey; across its four sectors, 254 galleries will present works, and Zero 10 brings together a further 21 galleries and platforms, four of which also participate in Galleries; Galleries convenes 204 exhibitors, Nova features 20 galleries, Positions comprises 12 solo projects, and Survey brings together 16 historically focused presentations by 18 galleries; Zero 10 returns with Generation, its largest presentation yet, bringing together 19 projects by artists including Jon Rafman, Refik Anadol and Sarah Meyohas, curated by Karen Archey with Eli Scheinman, and Nicole Sales Giles joins as Zero 10's first Global Head; five countries will be represented for the first time, the Bahamas, Estonia, Georgia, Ghana and Hungary; Latin America and the Caribbean are represented in depth, alongside a notable participation from Los Angeles and Chicago and a strengthened South Florida presence; and, beyond the showfloor, Public Art Fund will bring Between Tides, a participatory exhibition of artist-designed ping-pong tables, to Collins Park.

City of Miami Beach — Art Week Miami Beach 2026, November 30 to December 6

Miami Beach: the City of Miami Beach has unveiled the 2026 lineup of Art Week Miami Beach from Monday, November 30 to Sunday, December 6; Art Basel Miami Beach will feature 272 leading international galleries from 40 countries and territories, starting with Preview Days on December 2 and 3, followed by Public Days from December 4 to 6 at the Miami Beach Convention Center, in a more focused gallery line-up across a substantially reconfigured show floor; the city is also curating its own public exhibition during Art Week in 12 hotel lobbies across the city, the seventh edition of No Vacancy, Miami Beach, with installations available for viewing from November 12 through December 19, 2026, by 12 participating artists and collectives, with $35,000 in prize money, a $10,000 public prize and a $25,000 juried prize, and each artist received a $10,000 stipend; the city's Legacy Purchase Program returns for its eighth year, with the winning artwork to be purchased by the city for permanent display in the Miami Beach Convention Center, and public voting took place from November 20 to 23, with the selected work to be unveiled on December 1; the week's fairs and partners include Design Miami, with public days December 3 to 6, Untitled Art, December 1 to 6 on Ocean Drive, InScope, Aqua Art Miami, INK Miami and PRIZM, each running within the week; The Bass will feature seven exhibitions and will present Public Art Fund's Between Tides from Sunday, November 29 to Sunday, December 6; and the Mayor of Miami Beach, Steven Meiner, said Art Basel sets the stage, and the city fills it with bold public art, museums, local artists and live performances in its parks and hotel lobbies.
