Asia’s Tuesday session followed Wall Street’s correction lower, Seoul’s Kospi closing down 2.6 percent to 4,968.48 in the Press account filed below, ahead of the one report the week waits on: Nvidia’s third-quarter results after Tuesday’s close, the first since Mr. Huang’s resignation, with consensus revenue at about $109 billion. The petition for his return was read at 188,548 names on Monday evening in the petition account filed below, against a board reported to be focused on its investigation and not in talks about a return. The President flies into Manila late Tuesday afternoon for the Quad’s first leaders’ meeting in two years, in the wire account filed below, with Thursday’s room in Shenzhen behind it. Alaska’s count did not move in the night: it stands at 141,637 to 130,162, Ms. Peltola 52 votes short of a majority on the current total, with about 6,500 ballots estimated still to count, in the Juneau account filed below. In the Gulf, a tanker has been named off Oman against six sunk in a claim. In France, a strike day opens. The desk takes the files in that order.

What the night settled is short: the selling is a second day and not a turn, the board’s reported position hardened without the board speaking, and every dated thing on Tuesday’s calendar—retail sales at 0830, the Secretary’s lunch, the Quad at 1830 in Manila, the count in Juneau, the report after the close—still stands where Monday left it. The Count at 0400: 141,637 to 130,162 Stands, 52 Votes to the Line, and Tuesday’s Mail Is the Next Fact

Alaska’s count spent the night where Monday afternoon left it, and the overnight file adds three readings of the same numbers and one statement. The Press’s count as of 0252 UTC on Tuesday prints the state’s Monday figures at a rounded 50.0 percent for Ms. Peltola—141,637 votes to Senator Sullivan’s 130,162, on 283,377 votes in the contest, with about 98 percent estimated counted—and the Press has not called the race, in the Press count account filed below. On the full denominator the share is 49.98 percent, and a majority on the current total is 141,689, leaving her 52 votes short, in the Juneau account above. NBC News estimates about 6,500 ballots remain to be counted, in the same account; more ballots arriving by mail will be counted on Tuesday, and overseas ballots may arrive through Wednesday.

The other side has now said how it will wait. Senator Sullivan’s spokesman, Mr. Adams, said on Monday night that Alaskans deserve a complete and accurate count, that the campaign will let the process run its course until every legal vote is counted and certified, and that it will not comment on individual ballot updates or outside projections in the meantime; the campaign will have more to say by the end of the week, in the Juneau account. One outside projection stands in the file as a model and nothing more: a newspaper’s independent tabulation of the Division’s scanned ballot record, covering 282,698 ballots, puts Ms. Peltola ahead 143,127 to 132,985, or 51.83 percent, with two candidates left, in the threshold account filed below, which records that the record leaves out ballots counted only by hand and that the state’s tabulation is conducted separately. The same account notes the arithmetic of Wednesday: the 874 write-in votes are excluded at the start of the ranked-choice process, which on its own would put her past half of the remaining first-choice votes. Certification is targeted for November 25, and results can be challenged until December 5, in the Juneau account. Settled: the governorship, by concession on Monday, and the distance—52 votes. Open: the call, which no desk has made, and whether Tuesday’s mail finds the 52 before Wednesday’s tabulation does.

Nvidia at 0400: A Petition Read at 188,548, a Board Reported Focused on Its Investigation, and the Report After the Close

The petition passed another set of readings in the night, and the board’s reported position gained a detail and kept its form: it is reported, and it is not stated. The campaign site’s public counter read 188,548 names at 5:13 p.m. Pacific on Monday, up from 181,025 at 8:13 a.m., with self-identified current employees at 9,451 and shareholders at 98,855, the affiliations unverified, in the petition account above. Against it, a person familiar with the matter said the board is not considering bringing Mr. Huang back and is not in talks about a return; the board is focused on its investigation, led by the law firm Wachtell Lipton, examining his conduct and whether it compromised national security or the company’s secrets, and a British broadcaster was told the same, in the same account. The board’s only public step remains the search: a representative said on Friday it was choosing a search firm, in the same account. Mr. Gerstner, who co-signed the investors’ letter, said on Monday that he made no demands of the board, that Mr. Huang is owed a full hearing, and that the interim chief, Ms. Kress, is very capable, in the same account. The President’s Monday words stand in the same file, in both directions at once: he would not mind a comeback; he questioned the spy claim at the centre of the case—‘nobody’s proven that’—and said national security was not put at risk; and on raising the matter with President Xi he said, ‘I may,’ adding that one does not want to insult China just before arriving there. The Treasury Secretary’s position stands opposite: an outside successor and a dramatic shake-up, with answers on export compliance due within his 10 days. A prediction market puts the chance of a return at 32 percent, in the curtain account filed below. No statement from the board itself stands in the overnight file, and no finding has been made by the board, by any committee or by any court on any allegation in this file.

What the board will not say, the calendar partly answers today. The company publishes its third-quarter results at about 4:20 p.m. Eastern, with Ms. Kress’s written commentary, and holds its call at 5 p.m., at which only analysts and institutional investors may ask questions, in the accounts filed below. Consensus stands at about $108.9 billion of revenue in the backstop account filed below, roughly $109 billion in the curtain account, and $108 billion to $108.97 billion in the calls account filed below, against the company’s outlook of $108.0 billion, plus or minus 2 percent, with earnings of $2.47 to $2.50 a share and a gross margin of about 74 percent in the same readings. The quarter ended on October 25, before the resignation, so the numbers will show the company Mr. Huang ran; the guidance will show the one Ms. Kress runs. Beneath the numbers stands the guarantee file: $164.5 billion of total financial commitments disclosed in the last report, the largest block up to $108.5 billion of data-centre lease guarantees, almost all of it—up to $105 billion—behind a planned campus in Ohio, signed at $105 billion and not the $250 billion first reported, in the backstop account. The shares fell 4.12 percent on Monday to $156.14 in the closing accounts, stand nearly 35 percent below their peak, and have pushed the company’s market value under $4 trillion, in the market accounts filed below. Monday’s other Washington fact stands in the same file: the Commerce Department’s interim final rule closing the route by which Chinese firms rented American computing power abroad, with licences now required for the listed countries and a 90-day clock for centres already serving them, in the curtain account.

Shenzhen at 0400: ‘Groundbreaking’ in Beijing, Four Agendas in Four Capitals, and a Fourth Chair Still Empty

The Gulf at 0400: A Tanker Named Off Oman, Six Sunk in a Claim and None Confirmed, and No Word on the Radar for a Third Day

The Hormuz file gained a name in the night, and the name is the night’s hardest fact in the Gulf. A British maritime agency reported a tanker attacked about seven nautical miles north of Khasab, Oman, while leaving the strait, and a security monitor identified the ship as the Panama-flagged ON PHOENIX, hit three nautical miles off Oman, in the Hormuz account filed below, which compiles a Pakistani paper, a broadcaster, a Gulf paper, the maritime agency and a strait-tracking site. It is the first vessel named in a week of claims. Against it stands Tehran’s arithmetic, grown again: six tankers caught fire and sunk since Sunday in the Guards’ claim as carried in the same account, two of them said to have struck mines on an unauthorised route, and a third tanker left immobilised after striking a mine east of the strait on Monday in the regular navy’s claim. The claims could not be independently verified, and Iranian authorities did not identify the vessels involved, in the same account. Tracking data showed no commercial ship had crossed the strait at all by early Tuesday morning, in the same account.

Two files in the Gulf did not move. On the radar at Al-Riffa, the overnight file adds no assessment from any capital for a third day: the Guards’ six drones and a struck radar, Manama’s intercepts, the Ambassador’s all-intercepted sentence, Friday’s two drones down in the American denial, and no impacts to American forces in the spokesman’s sentence, which did not address the machine. One aggregator’s timeline, updated Tuesday morning, carries a further claim dated Tuesday, attributed to the Guards and naming radar equipment at Sheikh Isa, in the timeline account filed below; no capital has confirmed it, and the desk prints it as a claim in a timeline and nothing more. On the inspectors, Tehran’s deputy foreign minister said on Sunday that Iran will not let United Nations inspectors visit its damaged nuclear facilities until the war ends and the United States meets its conditions, in the Hormuz account. The rest of the file stands where the week left it: three American carriers in the region, the blockade in force, the President’s words that new strikes are highly unlikely while negotiations progress, Tehran’s words that it remains in a state of war, and the Khor Mor strike unclaimed.

France and Spain at 0400: A Strike Day Opens on the Eighth Anniversary, and a Fifth Tracking Moves the Seats Without Moving the Gap

France’s next fact is a weekday fact, and it opens today. The government’s wage answer stands at a 1.6 percent minimum-wage increase effective December 1, taking net monthly pay to about €1,502—a €24 increase the unions call too small—in the wage account filed below; earlier accounts in the file carried a 0.6 percent figure as the promise the unions rejected, and both figures are printed in the cocked hat. Open: its size, which Tuesday will supply.

political.org — France’s protests whole: the movement from the school blockades to the November 17 strike call, and the counts of the largest days

A protest movement that began in late September with students angry about underfunded schools has grown into a nationwide challenge to the government, producing some of the worst street violence in years. The movement began among high school and university students in Paris protesting poor school conditions, teacher shortages and infrastructure problems. The largest single day of protest, Thursday, November 5, drew 575,000 people by the Interior Ministry’s count and 1.6 million by the union’s. On Monday, November 9, the Prime Minister announced a 1.6 percent minimum-wage increase effective December 1; the unions rejected it. Public-sector unions have called a strike and demonstrations for Tuesday, November 17, and some groups have called for pan-European protests from November 20 to 22. The unrest first began in late September. The turning point came on Saturday, October 17. That day’s demonstrations were organized by the coalition of France’s main unions, with students and the Yellow Vest movement joining. France has a long record of street movements that start over a single grievance and widen; the 2018 protests began on November 17 in opposition to rising fuel taxes and swelled into a movement against the President and the biggest challenge of his presidency. The current wave combines union-led strikes, student mobilization and Yellow Vest participation, and is part of a broader wave of discontent across Europe, with young people in several countries staging similar protests in recent weeks.

Reuters — Spain’s right retains a firm poll lead despite Socialist gains: three polls on Monday, and the state pollster a major outlier

MADRID, November 16 (Reuters) — Spain’s conservative People’s Party and Vox would together win a parliamentary majority in the November 29 election, three opinion polls published on Monday showed, though the Prime Minister’s Socialists gained ground. The surveys are the first since last week’s campaign launch, which has focused on housing affordability, immigration, corruption and last month’s deadly train crash. A daily poll showed the Socialists rising to 30.3 percent from 29.9, exceeding the 30 percent mark for the first time in that poll since the 2023 election, with the Popular Party on 31.5 and Vox on 18, together winning more than 190 seats in the 350-seat lower house. A second poll projected 201 to 205 seats for the two right parties combined and 102 to 104 for the Socialists, whose support rose half a point to 26.1 percent. A third showed Socialist support up one point from a week earlier to 26.9 percent, with the right bloc winning 194 seats to the Socialists’ 109. The state pollster remained a major outlier: last week it projected 134 to 143 seats for the Socialists against 155 to 171 for the two right parties combined, suggesting the Prime Minister could retain power through a broad left-wing coalition.

Euronews, carried by Ekkow — five things to know before Anthropic goes public: pricing on Wednesday, trading on Thursday under ANT, at $70 to $78 a share

The company will launch an initial public offering on November 19. It is expected to set the final price of its shares on Wednesday, November 18, and begin trading on an exchange in New York the following day under the symbol ANT. The company has proposed a price of $70 to $78 a share, which would value it at about $1.2 trillion in the middle of that range. The listing will be the biggest test yet of whether investors believe artificial intelligence can make money over the long term. Not everyone is convinced: at a briefing organised by an investor-responsibility centre, governance and legal experts warned of ‘red flags’ in the company’s filings, and a New York City financial officer said the company is rushing toward a listing without answering fundamental questions about its readiness to operate as a publicly traded company. The filing shows a large technology company and a search company’s parent among its largest backers. In May, the company raised $65 billion from investors who valued it at $965 billion, the first time a funding round had valued it above its rival. It is offering 825 million shares to the public at $70 to $78 each; at the middle of the price range, that would raise roughly $61 billion, making it one of the largest listings ever. The company is a public benefit corporation, legally required to pursue a stated purpose as well as profit. It has committed to spend at least $518 billion with six suppliers over roughly a decade, to secure the chips, data centres and electricity its models need. Its direct competitor has pushed its own listing back to 2027 at the earliest and was recently valued at about $1.25 trillion in a private round, according to reports.
