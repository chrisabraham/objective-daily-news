The Airlines at Midnight: More Than 2,300 Cancelled at the Close, a Second Router at Amarillo, and No Root Cause

The day’s last count is larger than the day’s early count, and the desk prints both, at their hours. More than 2,300 flights were cancelled and some 7,700 delayed on Sunday, with more than 10,000 flights affected, in the final wire account above; the early wire account counted more than 1,600 cancelled and almost 3,600 delayed, and the tracker account counted nearly 1,700 cancelled by 1350, in the accounts filed below. The final shares are heavier than the early ones: 28 percent of JetBlue’s Sunday flights cancelled, against 27 percent earlier, and 17 percent of American’s, against 13 percent earlier, with United, Southwest and Delta at 3 to 5 percent in both counts, in the final account. Saturday stands at more than 6,400 cancelled and more than 8,000 delayed in the same account, and Friday’s stop, which halted nearly all departing flights for almost five hours, stands as the longest nationwide halt since September 11, 2001, in the same account. No second stop was ordered on Sunday. Monday’s cancellations stood at 33 on Sunday afternoon in the tracker account, and no later Monday figure stands in the midnight file; Monday will be read, as the evening edition put it, against the Secretary’s promise of a system probably fully normal on Monday, and against the Administrator’s earlier promise of full schedule integrity by Sunday’s close, which Sunday’s final count did not meet.

The cause file gained a second town, a disabled fleet of routers, and an admission. The replacement of a router at El Dorado, Arkansas, triggered problems with a recently replaced router in Amarillo, Texas, which started broadcasting and overloading routers at other centers, the Secretary said on a Sunday programme, in the final wire account above; the functionality of the originating routers has been disabled, and the Administration did the same for about 60 similar routers in the system, in the same account. The routers that experienced issues were purchased in 2001 and were obsolete by 2006, in the same account. On the man who installed the first router, the Secretary said he was a good American patriot and a veteran, that sabotage is not believed, and that there is no evidence of a cyberattack or other malicious intent—and then the admission that closes the file for the night: the Administration still does not know what ultimately caused a problem in maintenance work done thousands of times before without issue, and does not actually have the root cause, in the same account. The money file moved on Sunday in three voices: the President made his first public comments on the weekend’s disruption, calling for the $50 billion upgrade to be approved and done all at once, not in drips and drabs, in the same account; the Secretary’s request stands at $50 billion, up from $30 billion, after Congress approved $12.5 billion last year, in the same account; and two House members answered on Saturday that about $11 billion of that $12.5 billion remains unspent and asked why more is needed, in the same account, against the Secretary’s Sunday answer that last year’s money was earmarked for certain spending and did not include new software. Settled: Sunday’s cost, the two routers, and the absence of any sign of attack. Open: the root cause, in the Secretary’s own words.

Bahrain at Midnight: One Narrow American Sentence, No Word on the Radar, and Article 51 Claimed Both Ways

Washington’s silence, which the evening edition recorded as a fact of the file, ended before midnight—narrowly. A Central Command spokesman, Mr. Hawkins, told a newspaper that there were no impacts to American forces, in the radar account above; the statement did not say whether radar equipment or other property was damaged, and Manama had not answered the newspaper’s question about damage at the site, in the same account. That is the whole of the American word on Sunday’s claim at 0000: it answers for the men and not for the machine, and the desk prints it at exactly that width. The two claims it stands between are unchanged. The Guards say six drones of their Aerospace Force targeted the American air-defence radar at the Al-Riffa helicopter base, in a second phase of retaliation, in the Bahrain account filed below; the Defence Force says its air defences intercepted and destroyed several Iranian aerial attacks before they reached their targets, in the same account; and no damage and no casualties have been reported in any account in the file.

The written file around the claim is complete, and it is aligned on one side and answered on the other. Manama’s Foreign Ministry statement stands whole in the ministry account filed below: the strongest condemnation, Iran held fully responsible, the transformation of Bahraini territory into an arena for settling scores rejected, Security Council Resolution 2817 cited, and Bahrain’s right of self-defence under Article 51 affirmed, with a call on the Security Council, where Bahrain sits, to condemn the attacks and take decisive measures. Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Jordan and Egypt condemned the attacks and stood with Manama, in the condemnations account filed below, which carries their four sentences severally: a violation of international law and good neighbourliness from Riyadh, a flagrant violation of sovereignty from Doha, a threat to security and territorial integrity from Amman, and a dangerous escalation from Cairo. Tehran’s spokesman, Mr. Baghaei, defended the strikes as justified in self-defence and said Iran would keep responding to military aggression, in the evening file, and rejected a Cooperation Council demand that Iran pay compensation for its attacks, in the condemnations account above. Article 51 therefore stands claimed in the same file by Manama for its defence and by Tehran for its strikes. The President’s Saturday condition, that he might hold off on attacking Iran if there were no new provocations, stands unapplied to Sunday’s claim in any statement in the midnight file. Settled: the responses, all of them, in writing. Open: what, if anything, was hit at Al-Riffa.
