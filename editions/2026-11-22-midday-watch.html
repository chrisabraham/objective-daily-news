Bahrain at Noon: Two Injured Where the Night File Had None, a Call on the Security Council, and an American Word That Covers Personnel and Facilities and Not the Radars

The correction came from the ministry itself, on Sunday, in a statement posted on X. “As a result of the heinous Iranian attacks on Bahrain yesterday, a Bahraini citizen with disabilities sustained injuries and is in stable condition, while a Pakistani resident was also injured and is in critical condition. Both are receiving treatment in the hospital,” the Interior Ministry said, in the ministry account above. The overnight file, at 0400, carried the ministry’s Saturday standing: a civilian industrial facility struck, fire, material damage, and no injuries reported. That standing is now superseded by the ministry’s own Sunday statement, and the desk prints the correction here, in the open, as the house prints its corrections. The ministry did not disclose the victims’ identities or the locations of the attacks, in the identities account filed below. The Defence Force’s Saturday account stands beside the correction without contradicting it in terms: its air defences intercepted several Iranian aerial attacks, and shrapnel from the interceptions caused material damage but no casualties, in the accounts the overnight file carried—a statement about the interceptions, printed next to a ministry statement about the attacks as a whole.

Manama’s Sunday file adds a diplomatic step. Saudi Arabia and the Gulf Cooperation Council condemned the attack, in the day-268 account filed below. No date for any Council meeting stands in the midday file.

Tehran’s claim stands where the night left it, now with its code name in the file. The Guards’ Aerospace Force attacked two United States Patriot air defense radars at the Al-Riffa and Awali bases in a combined missile and drone operation under the code name “Ya Umm al-Banin,” in response to repeated violations of Iranian airspace by MQ-1 and MQ-9 drones, in the Guards account filed below and in the Iran Press account filed below. The American word at midday comes in two pieces, and the desk prints both pieces exactly. A United States official told CBS News that the attacks caused no damage to United States military personnel or facilities in the region, in the injured account above. And Central Command, which answered one Iranian claim in full on Saturday, has filed no located statement on the claimed Patriot radars, in the day-268 account. The claim it did answer was the F-16 claim: “Iran did not attack a US F-16 fighter aircraft yesterday—at all,” the command said, adding that all United States military fighter jets in the Middle East were safe and accounted for, in the denial account filed below. None of the Guards’ claims—the radars, the downed drones over the strait and Qeshm Island, or the F-16—was independently verified, in the day-268 account, whose cutoff is 0500 ET today. Settled: the two injured, their conditions, the condemnations, and the call on the Council. Open: the radars, the drones, and any American statement addressed to the Bahrain strike itself.

The day’s new strait fact is a ship removed from the record. MarineTraffic data appeared to show an Iranian tanker called Khat-Shekan—the name means line-breaker—sailing through the strait toward the blockade line. TankerTrackers said the transmission was a spoof and the vessel does not exist: all of the data supplementing the transmission is wrong, and there is nothing on satellite imagery to corroborate the movements, in the spoof account above, which also records the tracking firm’s judgment that Iran would not attempt to ship out a very large crude carrier now, given how many were fired upon in recent months. The imagery account carries the same finding in its own terms: the signal paired an Iranian identifier with another ship’s call sign, and no vessel stands on that track. Behind the spoof stands the convoy that is real in its staging and unconfirmed in its sailing. More than 14 tankers stand gathered at Kharg Island, in the spoof account and in the convoy account filed below; no independent confirmation has emerged that any convoy has departed Kharg or exited toward the Gulf of Oman, in the same account. The imagery cuts the other way on the loading claim: Sentinel imagery of November 21 shows the island’s T-jetty and berths with no tankers alongside at that scale, against claims that loading has resumed, in the imagery account. The blockade itself gained its day count on Saturday: 130 consecutive days, the United States military said, in the spoof account, with about 147 to 150 commercial vessels redirected or turned back since July 14, in the convoy account. Foreign Minister Araghchi’s word stands as Friday made it: the blockade must be broken, in the same account. Settled: Sunday’s two, Saturday’s 13, and the spoof. Open: the true count, the convoy’s sailing, and the loading at Kharg.

Brussels Reporter—Salvage teams assess grounded cargo ship off France

A cargo ship remains stranded off southwestern France as authorities work to prevent it from capsizing and causing pollution, with salvage experts assessing how to refloat it. The 169-metre Miro G ran aground near Cap de l’Homy in the Landes department, close to Lit-et-Mixe, shortly before 1 a.m. on Friday, November 20. Its ballast tanks have been filled with seawater to improve stability; the water will eventually have to be pumped out to lighten the vessel so it can be pulled free. Its two main generators have failed, disrupting power to the pumps, and engineers are working to restore an alternative electricity supply. The salvage tug Abeille Bretagne has arrived with a 1,000-metre towline. Officials said refloating could take several days or longer.

Euronews, carried on Ekkow—Salvage operation under way after cargo ship runs aground off French coast

A cargo ship remains stranded on the south-western coast of France as authorities work to prevent it from capsizing and causing potential environmental damage. Nineteen crew members had already been rescued, and a further 11 people—the captain, two other crew members, salvage experts and company representatives among them—were evacuated by helicopter on Saturday ahead of worsening weather. The ship was carrying no cargo but remained a potential pollution risk because of the fuel on board. Strong winds and rough seas are expected to continue, with officials warning that refloating could take several days or longer; the maritime authorities said they were working to take advantage of the first suitable weather window, but stressed that there was no guarantee the operation would succeed.
