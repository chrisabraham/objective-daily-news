Libya at Midnight: A Corvette Sunk at Her Berth, a Second Ship Hit by Evening, and No Claim From Kyiv

Sunday’s largest new file opened before dawn in a port that is not in the war. The Merkury, a Steregushchiy-class corvette of Russia’s Black Sea Fleet, was berthed at the Benghazi naval base in eastern Libya when two explosive-laden sea drones struck her in the early hours of Sunday, at least four more drones intercepted and destroyed, in the sinking account above and in the base account filed below. Satellite images showed most of the warship capsized and submerged, with only her bow remaining above water, in the sinking account; the imagery company Vantor identified the vessel as a Russian navy corvette, and imagery taken Sunday showed most of her capsized and sunk, smoke rising from part of her superstructure and debris scattered along the pier, in the imagery account filed below. Ten people, Russian and Libyan nationals, suffered minor injuries and were receiving treatment in Benghazi, in the medical sources carried in the sinking account and in the London account filed below; two medical sources in the first wire account said no deaths had been reported, in the wire account filed below, against local sources’ word, late on Sunday, of an unknown number of Libyan and Russian nationals killed, in the imagery account. The desk prints both and merges neither.

The official words are these. Libya’s eastern-based government said a preliminary investigation indicated the explosion occurred aboard a Russian ship docked at the port and that a drone attack was suspected; the vessel had stopped to refuel and resupply on her way back to Russia under maritime cooperation agreements, in the wire account. The same government said it has launched a joint investigation with Russia to uncover the circumstances of the attack, its source, and the party behind it, in the imagery account. The initial investigation’s finding, in the base account, is that several Ukrainian sea drones were launched at the ship from a base run by Ukrainian forces in western Libya. Ukraine’s security service is widely believed to be responsible, in the London account, and a military source said the strike was carried out by the SBU, in the New York account filed below. Kyiv itself has claimed nothing in any account in the file. Libya’s Tripoli-based government said it was following the reports with grave concern about the danger of exposing the country’s civilian ports to external conflict, in the imagery account. The ship’s own recent work stands in the New York account: she had been stationed in Benghazi for several days after escorting a convoy of cargo vessels used to transport military supplies.

The file widened before it closed. Hours after the corvette was struck, a second drone attack hit a Maltese-flagged commercial ship off the coast of Benghazi, in the wire account; the Health Ministry of the Benghazi-based administration said the injured crew members had been rescued and were receiving treatment, in the imagery account, and Libyan naval and air forces intercepted more than 20 drone boats off Benghazi on Sunday evening, in the military sources carried in the sinking account. The Baltic file closed in the same hours at the other end of the continent: working with Finnish and Estonian authorities, Russia managed to tow the Sparta II out of Finnish waters on Sunday night, in the two-ships account filed below, and Kaliningrad’s governor wrote that there is no threat to the region’s supplies, in the same account. The tow’s weather warning stands in the tow account filed below: rain, sleet and stronger winds expected on Sunday night and Monday, named by Finland’s sea safety officer as a concern before the tow began. Two Russian ships struck in one weekend, in the Baltic and in the Mediterranean, neither claim answered by Kyiv in the file. Settled: the Merkury’s loss and the Sparta II’s departure. Open: the second ship’s state, the joint investigation, and responsibility in Kyiv’s word.

Ukraine at Midnight: A Boy Alive at Fourteen Hours, a Toll Counted Three Ways, and a Search Still Running

Dymer’s day ended with the fact the whole file had waited for. A seven-year-old boy, named David, was pulled alive from beneath the collapsed concrete of the five-storey building on Sunday evening, almost 14 hours after the Shahed drone struck at about 5 a.m. while residents slept, in the rescue account above. Rescuers found him after discovering personal belongings recognised by his relatives; he spoke to them without panicking and repeatedly assured them he was all right—“Don’t rush, I’m fine”—in the Interior Ministry chief Ivan Vyhivskyi’s telling, in the same account, and he was taken for treatment in stable condition. President Zelensky confirmed the rescue on Sunday evening and warned that the search was far from over, in the same account: more than 130 emergency personnel are at the site with heavy machinery, canine teams and bomb-disposal experts, in the toll account, and the operation will continue until there is complete information about every single person, in the President’s words in the rescue account. The rescuers are also investigating witness reports of a telephone call from a woman believed to be under the rubble, in both accounts.

The toll does not stand in one figure at the stamp, and the desk prints the count as it stands. The confirmed death toll has risen to four, in the President’s word, in the toll account, which adds that roughly ten people remain unaccounted for and that the Emergency Service has identified two of the dead: a woman born in 1978 and a boy born in 2008. The regional head, Timur Tkachenko, said the toll in Dymer has risen to nine, announced on Telegram, with one more body found in the rubble, in the relayed account, which counts at least 12 dead across the Kyiv region in the night’s attacks: nine in Dymer, two in Vyshneve, where a strike hit a supermarket and wounded eight, and one in Bila Tserkva, where a private house caught fire. The missing are counted in the same spread: 14 unaccounted for in the President’s first word, revised to 15 as teams pushed deeper, in the missing account filed below; nine people might still be trapped, in Mr. Vyhivskyi’s later word, in the rescue account; 11 still being searched for, including two children who are not responding or are not reachable, in the relayed account. The day’s interruption stands in the file: the search was suspended in the middle of the afternoon under a new aerial threat, three explosions were heard as rescuers and civilians took shelter, and work resumed under continuing alerts, in the day account filed below.

The week behind the day stands in the President’s own summary, in the missing account: the preceding seven days were one of the war’s most intense aerial stretches, with Russian forces firing thousands of drones, guided bombs and missiles across 20 Ukrainian regions, and Ukraine pressing allied governments to accelerate delivery of anti-ballistic interceptors. The standing files did not move: Thursday night’s toll at at least nine killed, the three Dnipro crossings in their two states with the Darnytskyi assessment still unpublished, and Kryvyi Rih’s toll at two dead and 60 wounded. The North Atlantic Council meets in Brussels on Monday with Dymer now in the file before it. Settled: the boy, alive. Open: the count, when the rubble is cleared.

Antalya at Midnight: The Post-Mortem Names Access, a Letter of 130 Countries, and a Fixed Position

The climate file’s last entry of the day is an account of how the failure was made. As COP31 drifted into record overtime, a negotiator from a small island state complained that a promised meeting with the talks’ Turkish and Australian presidents had been cancelled yet again, while Saudi officials had regular access, allowing them disproportionate influence at Antalya, according to negotiators from Europe, small island states, Latin America, Asia and Africa, in the post-mortem account above. Riyadh was at the forefront of resisting language on reducing reliance on fossil fuels, and that was chiefly why a political agreement could not be reached for the first time since 2009, the negotiators said, in the same account. The opposing coalition’s instrument stands in the same file: a letter backed by around 130 countries, saying any deal at Antalya must include mention of transitioning away from fossil fuels, sent to the presidency—and the revised text that emerged early on Sunday largely ignored it, still naming no fossil fuels and keeping strong language promoting carbon capture, in the same account. “It seems like we are negotiating with the Saudis,” Tuvalu’s climate minister, Maina Talia, said at the time, in the same account.

The principals’ closing words stand as the evening filed them, with two additions. The Secretary-General of the United Nations, Antonio Guterres, said he shares the very profound frustration of all those who expected governments to rise to the moment, and that the take-home message from COP31 is clear: the world must do much more, in the Paris account filed below, which adds that the rejected text received support from Russia, China and the Arab countries. “This has been the worst ever COP and its collapse needs to be a wake up call for the world,” Mohamed Adow, director of Power Shift Africa, said, in the collapse account filed below. The presidency’s last word is in the business account filed below: there is nothing to do, we did all that we could, and there will be no new text from now on. The absence that strengthened the fixed position is named in the post-mortem: no active United States engagement at Antalya, and no close United States–China coordination of the kind that brokered compromises at earlier conferences; the United States did not take part for the second year in a row, in the analysis account filed below. Settled: the outcome, and now its mechanism in the negotiators’ telling. Open: where the process goes next, which no account in the file answers.

CNN—Global climate summit collapses as countries fail to agree on moving away from fossil fuels

The COP31 climate conference in Antalya, Turkey, collapsed Sunday as countries failed to agree to an overarching deal after more than two weeks of contentious negotiations. The breaking point was over the very cause of climate change itself: fossil fuels. The annual talks, which stretched deep into overtime over the weekend, hold the honor of being the longest COP in history and the first major breakdown since COP15 in Copenhagen in 2009. At the final meeting intended to approve the so-called Antalya Pact on Sunday, it became clear there would not be the necessary consensus between the more than 190 countries at the talks to adopt the deal; these did not include the United States, which has pulled out of international climate action. The European Union, Panama, the Marshall Islands and Norway were among those that rejected it. Darragh O’Brien, Ireland’s climate minister, said the bloc would not endorse a deal that does not even mention fossil fuels. COP31 president Murat Kurum acknowledged support was not there to gavel through the pact: “We have done our utmost,” he said, but “it is clear that there are parties in this hall who oppose this text. We recognize that there is no consensus on this document.” Experts criticized the conference as a stunning failure of leadership; “This has been the worst ever COP and its collapse needs to be a wake up call for the world,” said Mohamed Adow, director of the energy think tank Power Shift Africa.
