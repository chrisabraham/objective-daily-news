The Evening in Brief: A Market Restored With One Man, an Alliance Answered With an Aircraft, and a Ministry That Answered Nothing

The day whole is the largest one-day rise on Wall Street since April 2025, earned by a boardroom reversal at one company; an alliance meeting in Brussels that produced one aircraft and a sentence of solidarity; and a sunken corvette in Benghazi on which the state that owns her still has filed no word of its own. The S&P 500 closed up 4.73 percent at 7,237.17, the Nasdaq Composite up 6.51 percent at 24,089.56 and the Dow up 1,447.98 points, after Nvidia’s board reinstated Jensen Huang by unanimous vote and the stock gained 31.48 percent to $165.05, its best day since 2003, in the closing account filed below. The North Atlantic Council, meeting in Brussels at Poland’s request, authorized the forward deployment of an AWACS aircraft to Poland and held Russia fully responsible for the three Kh-101 missiles that came down on Polish territory on Friday, in the deployment account filed below. On Benghazi, the Russian defence ministry issued no statement by the stamp; the response in the file is the Kremlin’s referral, an ambassador’s word in Libya, and two Libyan governments speaking in two directions, in the response account filed below. In Dymer the toll stands at 11 and two people are still unknown. The desk takes the files in that order.

What the evening settled is the close, in full; the first considered responses to Mr. Huang’s return, from the President who claims the credit for it, from the congressional leaders who promise investigations of it, and from the two Republican members who broke with their president over it; the Council’s answer, which is defensive in its own stated terms; and Spain’s last published numbers, now frozen by law until the vote. What it left open is the rest: the defence ministry’s account of the Merkury, if it ever issues one; Dymer’s final count; the root cause of the November 13 ground stop, promised by Friday and still unreleased on the day the Senate’s answers fell due; and whether Monday’s rise is a recovery or a reprieve, in a week with three trading days left in it.

The Responses: The President Takes the Credit, the Leaders Promise the Investigations, and the Cheers in Santa Clara

The Evening Watch’s remit is the first considered responses, and on this story they arrived in full before the bell. The President’s is the fullest. On CNBC he said he spoke to the board directly and indirectly and convinced them—“I spoke to them, let me put it that way. And I convinced them. I made the deal”—that the board asked for assurance on the national security threat and received it, and that the government’s investigation showed Mr. Huang “had bad judgment, to do with somewhat unrelated incidents, but he did nothing wrong,” in the reactions account above and in the market talk file filed below. In his social media post he stated the assurance without qualification—the government has no reason to believe the return poses any risk to national security, and Attorney General Todd Blanche has confirmed the government has no evidence Mr. Huang engaged in unlawful conduct—in the wire account filed below. On CNBC the same assurance carried its limit in his own words: “We gave them assurance, at least with everything we know—and we know a lot. You can only give the assurance as to what you know,” in the risk account filed below, which also records his reason for pressing the return: that China might otherwise have asked Mr. Huang to head Huawei. “We have to protect our geniuses. We don’t have enough of them.”

The other side answered in the same hours, and the desk prints it at the same length. House Democratic Leader Hakeem Jeffries called the reinstatement “indefensible, irresponsible and incomprehensible,” said Congress will conduct a swift, thorough and wide-ranging investigation, and said the defense authorization must carry ironclad export control legislation, in the reactions account. Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer wrote that the President is putting his own financial interests and his billionaire friends before national security and pledged investigations of the administration, Mr. Huang and other Nvidia executives, in the same account. The break inside the President’s own party is two named members: Senator John Cornyn asked, “No national security risk? Based on what?”, and Representative Don Bacon said Mr. Huang was “compromised and exploited by a Chinese spy” and that Nvidia should have moved on without him, in the same account. From outside Congress, the National Women’s Law Center’s Fatima Goss Graves said the decision goes against every standard in addressing workplace harassment and broader principles of corporate governance, in the same account. The company’s own floor answered first and plainest: workers cheered Mr. Huang’s return to the Santa Clara headquarters, and he told them, “Thank you all for the second chance. I’m going to be a better CEO the second time around,” in the same account. OpenAI’s Sam Altman posted “Jensen!” within the hour, and Elon Musk and Tim Sweeney stand among the signatories of the public letter that said the board had not followed its own code of conduct in the resignation, in the same account. The analysts divide as the file divides: Oppenheimer restored its outperform rating—“Jensen’s return is a win for AI and a win for investors”—in the market talk file, while Morningstar’s Lindsey Stewart said the board’s reappointment of a man whose resignation it had just accepted leaves the governance questions unanswered, in the reactions account. Settled: every side has spoken, on the record, on the day. Open: the board’s continuing investigation, and the December 1 closed-door interview with three House committees that the midday file carried.

Brussels at Evening: One AWACS Forward, Russia Held Fully Responsible, and No Article Invoked

The North Atlantic Council’s extraordinary meeting, which the midday file carried as in session with no readout, produced its answer in the afternoon, and it is a measured one stated in the alliance’s own terms. NATO ambassadors met in the Council to discuss the incident, which the alliance’s spokesperson, Allison Hart, described as “dangerous,” holding Russia “fully responsible”; the Supreme Allied Commander Europe, General Alexus Grynkewich, has authorized the forward deployment of a NATO AWACS aircraft, and is examining the deployment of additional assets in an ongoing assessment of the alliance’s posture in Poland and elsewhere; “All Allies stand in full solidarity with Poland. NATO remains united, ready, and committed to defending against any threat,” in the deployment account above. Poland’s Deputy Defence Minister Pawel Zalewski, who represented Warsaw, announced the aircraft after the meeting and said all ambassadors expressed solidarity, agreed the incident was part of Russia’s broader escalation against the alliance and required an adequate response, and stressed that the answer is defensive—strengthening the capabilities that discourage further attacks, not escalation or offensive action—in the announcement account filed below. The record the room worked from stands as the prosecutors filed it: debris of three Russian Kh-101 cruise missiles, manufactured in Dubna in October or November 2026, recovered at Nowy Lipowiec, Rudnik nad Sanem and Molodycz, in the deployment account. Two particulars from the morning’s coverage belong in the evening file because they explain what the aircraft is for: the detection system worked, the antimissile systems and allied flights were activated, and Polish authorities say the weather was too bad for the precision a shoot-down required; and a Swedish Gripen pilot received authorization to fire on an incoming missile and did not fire, in the meeting account filed below and in the meeting file carried at midday. Poland has still not formally invoked Article 4, and no joint communique was expected or issued; Russia has not commented on the missile incursions in any account in the file. Settled: the deployment, and the solidarity, in words. Open: the further assets under examination, and Moscow’s answer to the incursions, which has not come.

Libya at Evening: The Ministry Silent at the Stamp, an Ambassador’s Warning, and Two Governments in One Country

The Benghazi file’s evening state is defined by what is not in it. The Russian defence ministry, to which the Kremlin referred every question in the morning, had issued no statement on the Merkury by the afternoon, and the Kremlin neither confirmed the strike, the sinking, nor any deaths; the Russian side still names neither the purpose of the voyage nor the fate of the crew, in the confirmation account filed below. The one Russian voice raised in answer came from outside Moscow: Ambassador Aydar Aganin, in an interview with Russian state television, called the attacks terrorist and said Moscow would not leave them unanswered, in the response account above. Kyiv’s claim stands complete in its own words—seven naval drones, launched from a mothership, sank the Merkury and destroyed the Ka-27 helicopter aboard her and hit the MV Angara—with one conflict the desk does not resolve: two Libyan officials described the corvette as severely damaged where Mr. Zelensky claims her sunk, and Libyan officials briefed on the attack said at least four drones were intercepted and two hit the ship, in the wire account filed below. The casualty file is likewise open in three tellings—ten wounded and no deaths in one report, no casualties on the ship herself in the eastern government’s statement, and a third report the file itself marks unconfirmed—with no official list, in the confirmation account.

Libya’s two governments spoke, and they did not say the same thing in the same direction. The eastern government, which controls Benghazi, opened its joint investigation with Russia, sent its letter to the Secretary-General and the Security Council calling for an international investigation, and its prime minister, Osama Hammad, raised the suspicion that the boats had been launched from western Libya and asked Kyiv for formal explanations, in the response account. The Tripoli government condemned the attack, called on the Ukrainian authorities to provide official clarification, and warned, with the United Nations mission, against the country becoming a battlefield for the war in Ukraine, in the same account and in the updated claim account filed below. The eastern government’s founding sentence stands in both tellings: Libya is not a party to the Russia-Ukraine war and will not allow its ports, territory or waters to be turned into an arena for settling scores. Settled: the investigations are opened, on both sides of the country. Open: the ministry’s word, the crew’s fate, and the ship’s state, sunk or damaged, on which the file still carries two governments’ ally and two accounts in disagreement.

Dymer at Evening: Eleven Dead, Two Unknown, and a Day of Mourning Kept Over an Unfinished Search

Dymer’s evening figures are the midday figures, held—and the holding is itself the news, because the search that was to resolve them is not finished. The building’s toll stands at 11, Governor Tymur Tkachenko’s figure, with the youngest of the dead 11 years old; the fate of two people is still unknown in President Zelenskiy’s Monday word; seven were injured, including two children, in the regional police’s count; and four other injured people are in hospital beside Davyd, seven, whose parents are with him, in the survivors account filed below. The wider Kyiv region count stands at at least 15 dead for Sunday’s attacks, of which the building accounts for 11, in the toll account filed below. The region kept Monday as a day of mourning while the work went on, and the work’s condition is in the file: search operations had to be halted several times because of the threat of further attacks and the danger from Shahed drones, in the survivors account—the same interruption, at the same building, that stopped the rescue at 1535 on Sunday in the Evening Watch’s file. The emergency service’s Sunday figure of eleven people sought, including two children, stands unretired in the toll account beside the president’s two; the desk prints both, as it printed them at midday, because no account in the evening file reconciles them. Mr. Zelenskiy’s Monday response to the strike was a call for tougher sanctions and export controls on Russia, in the survivors account. Settled: eleven, and the mourning. Open: the two unknown, and the count the finished search will file.

Washington at Evening: Sixty Aviation Groups Present a $30 Billion Bill, and the Root Cause Stands Unreleased Past Its Own Deadline Talk

The ground stop’s file gained its evening entry from the industry, not the agency. A coalition of more than 60 aviation groups—Airlines for America, Boeing, Airbus, GE Aerospace, the International Air Transport Association and the unions among the signatories—called on Congress on Monday to provide at least $30 billion quickly for air traffic control modernization, putting the November 13 stop’s cost at more than 18,000 canceled flights and more than 1 million passengers disrupted, and calling the system’s fragility a national spotlight Congress should answer sooner rather than later, in the coalition account filed below. The money’s own argument is in the same account: Secretary Duffy asked first for $30 billion and raised it to $50 billion after the incident; Senator Jerry Moran said the $50 billion figure appeared to come out of the blue and wants an accounting of why most of the $12.5 billion Congress approved last year has not been spent. On the cause itself, the evening file closes where the midday file left it, and the desk states the sequence plainly: Administrator Bryan Bedford said on Wednesday he expected to release the root-cause findings by Friday; the Transportation Department has not issued any findings, in the coalition account. Monday was also the date by which the Senate Commerce Democrats’ letter of November 16 asked for answers—on safety assessments, failover, independent validation and rollback for the Juniper routers—in the committee account filed below; no account of the answers stands posted in the file at the stamp, and the standing public record remains the agency’s own—the function disabled on the originating routers and on more than 60 others as a precaution, installations paused, the cause under investigation—in the ground-stop file filed below. Settled: the industry’s ask, and its number. Open: the findings, now past the administrator’s own Friday, on the senators’ own Monday.
