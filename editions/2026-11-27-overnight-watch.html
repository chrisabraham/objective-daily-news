The Night in Brief: A Cabinet Buys, a Price Index Argues, and a Strait Stays Empty

The night’s largest movement was made in Beijing, in an afternoon. China’s cabinet met on Friday under Premier Li Qiang and directed that state-backed investors—the national team, in the market’s name for them—play a bigger part in stabilising a stock market that had fallen to lows not seen in more than a year, in the Shanghai account filed below. The Shanghai Composite gained about 3 percent in afternoon trade and the technology board rose 12 percent. In Tokyo, the morning’s price figures put core inflation at 2.9 percent, the fastest rise in 16 months, three weeks before the Bank of Japan’s December meeting. Over Ukraine, the night was drones only in both capitals’ accounts: 155 launched in Kyiv’s count, 110 downed or suppressed, hits at 27 locations. In the strait, the visible counter counted no ship at all by dawn.

What the night settled is the cabinet’s direction and the market’s first answer to it; Tokyo’s November figure, printed against its forecast; the drone night’s launch and interception counts, in Kyiv’s figures; Brent’s early-Friday levels on both sides of $96; and Thursday’s crossing count in the strait, at two. What it left open is the size and duration of Beijing’s buying; the Bank of Japan’s December decision; the damage at the 27 locations, unnamed in the Air Force’s account; Ras Tanura’s state, still undescribed by its owner; the hulls Tehran counts on mines; the storm’s restoration total; and the Russian defence ministry’s word on its corvette.

Beijing at Dawn: The National Team Is Ordered In, and Shanghai Answers in an Afternoon

The cabinet’s words are the file, and they are printed in the form the official agency carried them. China will plan new policies for its capital markets, and state-backed national team investors will play a bigger role in stabilising markets, the Xinhua agency said on Friday, citing the cabinet, in the support account filed below. A companion account adds the machinery: Central Huijin will enhance its role as a quasi-stabilisation fund to ensure ample liquidity; the meeting called for policies to draw long-term funds into the market and for stronger defences against risks spilling in from overseas—the subject of the cabinet account filed below.

The market’s answer came the same afternoon. The Shanghai Composite gained 3 percent in early afternoon trading in one account and 3.3 percent in the markets view filed below; the blue-chip index soared with it, the two benchmarks on track for their best session in two years; the STAR 50 technology index surged 12 percent, and a semiconductor index within it 15 percent in the view’s figures; the yuan reversed early losses and rallied. The desk prints the afternoon as an afternoon: the indexes had not closed at the hour the accounts were filed, and a pledge’s first session is a reading, not a result.

Tokyo at Dawn: Core Prices at 2.9 Percent, and December’s Meeting Now the File

The Tokyo figures are the night’s cleanest central-bank file. Core consumer prices in the capital—fuel included, fresh food excluded—rose 2.9 percent in November from a year earlier, against a median forecast of 3.0 percent, up from 2.6 percent in October and the fastest rise since July 2025, in the inflation account filed below. Core prices have now stood above the Bank of Japan’s 2 percent target for three straight months.

The calendar the figures land on is fixed. The bank raised its rate to 1.25 percent in September after a June increase, held in October, and meets over two days ending December 18. A majority of analysts polled expect a December increase—to 1.50 percent in the market accounts filed below—and a further increase by the end of March, with the terminal rate put at 1.75 or 2.0 percent; one economist in the account names the condition plainly, that persistent market instability over the artificial-intelligence and chip outlook could still argue for waiting, in the Tokyo account filed below. Governor Ueda speaks on Monday, and the accounts treat the speech as the next reading. Settled: November’s figure, and its place above target. Open: December 18.

Ukraine at Dawn: 155 Drones in the Night, Hits at 27 Locations, and No Missiles in Either Account

Kyiv’s count of the night is complete as a count and empty as a map. Russia attacked overnight into November 27 with 155 strike and decoy drones, 83 of them jet-powered, launched from five places in Russia and from occupied Donetsk and Crimea, the Air Force said, in the drone account filed below. By 0800 air defences had shot down or suppressed 110, 51 of them jet-powered; hits were recorded at 27 locations and falling debris at four more; the attack was still under way at the hour of the report, with drones still in the air. The Air Force’s post does not say which regions were hit, what was damaged, or whether anyone was hurt, and the desk does not fill the blank from other nights.

The one locality file in the night is Izyum, in the Kharkiv region, where a combined strike—a rocket-system hit near the industrial zone at about 2330, then strike drones at about 0024—left six people injured, in the Izyum account filed below; whether Izyum stands among the Air Force’s 27 locations is not stated in either account. Moscow’s own account of the same night, in its defence trade press, lists a different war in the same hours: no missiles used; two 330-kilovolt substations damaged in Kryvyi Rih and Zhytomyr; four railway locomotives destroyed in three towns of the Dnipropetrovsk region; plants struck in Zaporizhzhia and Letychiv and a logistics centre in Bila Tserkva; and a bulk carrier carrying military cargo, in the ministry’s words, intercepted southwest of Odesa, in the ministry account filed below. The two accounts agree on the instrument and on nothing else; each is printed as its side’s statement. President Zelensky’s warning of a larger strike in preparation stands where Thursday put it, undated. Settled: the launch count and the interception count, in Kyiv’s figures. Open: the 27 locations, the carrier’s name and fate, and the strike said to be coming.
