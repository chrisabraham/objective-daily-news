The Midnight in Brief: What Thursday Settled, What It Left Open

Thursday settled the shape of its three files and left the substance of each one open: the talks have a European chair that no European has been named to fill and a prisoner exchange that three governments call the largest of the war and none will number; Nvidia has a former chief executive who has agreed to testify and a calendar of congressional dates that now runs into December; and the Iran file has a briefing on new strikes in Washington, a legal deadline on Friday, and air defences heard over Tehran in the night. At Casement Aerodrome in Baldonnel, west of Dublin, President Zelenskyy stood with Mr. Witkoff and Mr. Kushner on Thursday night and said the envoys had told him the exchange will be the biggest yet and that Europe will be in the negotiating team at the next meeting, in the Baldonnel account filed below; Mr. Kushner said the deal cannot be concluded without the Europeans at the table, and Mr. Ushakov, in Moscow’s evening statement, said the parameters of the exchange are still being agreed and named no agreement on Europe. In Washington, Mr. Huang agreed to testify before Thanksgiving, three House chairmen set transcribed interviews for December 1 and 2 and a hearing for December 3, and the Senate Banking chairman asked Mr. Huang and Secretary Lutnick to testify, in the wire account filed below. Attorney General Blanche said the Justice Department has not reached a conclusion on whether Ms. Alice Huang is a spy, and China denied that she is. Nvidia rose after hours. On Iran, President Trump was briefed on Thursday on plans for further strikes, in the Tehran account filed below, and Tehran answered with its spokesman’s defence of the closed strait and a Guard official’s promise of long and painful strikes. Spain’s campaign opened at midnight. Alaska counts again on Friday.

Dublin at Midnight: An Hour at Baldonnel, a Chair Asserted in Two Capitals, and No Name and No Number

The briefing the evening edition waited for took place, and it produced sentences from all three men and figures from none of them. The envoys spent about two hours with President Putin in Moscow on Thursday afternoon and about an hour with President Zelenskyy and his chief negotiator, Mr. Umerov, at the Irish military airfield, in the Baldonnel account above. President Zelenskyy said: “Our soldiers will come home,” and called the exchange “a real result,” in the same account and in the Dublin account filed below. Later in the night he wrote that the week had produced three things: prisoners will be released in the largest exchange of the war; European representatives are being involved in diplomacy more actively, at the negotiating table on equal terms; and the next multilateral meetings are being prepared, in the Baldonnel account above. Mr. Kushner said the envoys were able to finalize on the prisoner exchange, spoke of returning thousands of people to their families, and later posted that both sides had agreed to expand the next round to include the European partners, in the Dublin account above. Mr. Witkoff said another meeting is on the schedule. No date, no place and no prisoner figure was given by any of the three.

On Europe, the night holds two accounts of the same Kremlin conversation, and the desk prints them at full size, as it found them. Mr. Kushner, standing beside President Zelenskyy, said of President Putin that he is willing to give it a try with Europeans at the table, in the Baldonnel account above. Mr. Ushakov’s statement, published the same evening, called the conversation substantive, constructive, frank and useful, said President Putin backed the initiative for another, apparently the largest, exchange, with its parameters currently being agreed, and said of the Europeans that they must decide whether they want peace or the continuation of the war, their practical actions so far pointing to the latter, in the same account. Mr. Peskov added that Europe must first name a representative, in the same account. In Dublin, more than 40 European leaders ended their summit without naming one: Ms. Kallas said the treaties give the seat to Mr. Costa, who chairs the European Council, and to herself; Chancellor Merz named the E3 of Germany, France and the United Kingdom, and a group of five adding Italy and Poland; President Macron said the format will be determined later; and Mr. Costa said work would continue in several formats, in the Baldonnel account above. The Coalition of the Willing meets in Berlin next week, the next occasion named in the file for Europe to fill the chair it is said to hold. Mr. Ushakov’s statement carried a second road and a reminder: President Putin reminded the Americans of the draft treaties Russia handed to the United States and NATO in 2021—no expansion of the alliance, no weapons systems near Russia’s borders that threaten it, and a return of the alliance’s forces to their 1997 positions—and the two presidents will continue discussions at the APEC summit in China, in the Baldonnel account above and the Dublin account above. Settled: the briefing happened, the next meeting exists on a schedule, and the exchange is agreed in the word of every delegation. Open: its number, its date, Europe’s name, the land and the ceasefire.

Nvidia at Midnight: Testimony Agreed Before Thanksgiving, Interviews Set for December, and a Third Allegation Printed as an Allegation

The company file stopped being a company file on Thursday afternoon and became a congressional calendar. Mr. Huang confirmed that he would testify in Congress at some point before Thanksgiving, which falls on November 26; the testimony is expected behind closed doors, and Chairman Mast believes it will be recorded and transcribed, in the timeline account filed below. The three House chairmen—Mr. Mast, Mr. Moolenaar and Mr. Crawford—wrote to Mr. Huang and to the company’s general counsel, Mr. Teter, requiring closed-door transcribed interviews on December 1 and December 2 respectively, or subpoenas to compel them, and asked Under Secretary Kessler to testify at a December 3 hearing, in the wire account at the top of this report. Senator Scott, the Senate Banking chairman, sent letters on Thursday to Mr. Huang and to Secretary Lutnick asking both to testify, said he would consider subpoenaing Mr. Huang, and wrote that recent reports raise serious concerns about national security and the protection of sensitive American technology, in the same account; the account adds that senior White House officials were separately discussing whether Secretary Lutnick should be pushed out, over the administration’s refusal to add dozens of Chinese companies to a trade blacklist and over oversight of the company’s exports to China. On the central allegation the file gained its first official non-finding: Attorney General Blanche said on Thursday that the Justice Department has not yet reached a conclusion on whether Ms. Alice Huang is a spy, China denied that she is, and Ms. Huang could not be reached for comment, in the wire account above. No finding has been made by the board, by any committee or by any court on any allegation in this file.

Iran at Midnight: A Briefing on Further Strikes, a Friday Deadline in Congress, and Air Defences Over Tehran

The president’s Thursday sentences acquired a briefing behind them in the evening file. Senator Blumenthal told a television interviewer on Thursday that his impression from briefings and other sources is that an imminent military strike is very much on the table, in the same account. Friday carries a date in the American file that no other day this week has carried: it is the deadline for Congress to approve the war, failing which—or a 30-day extension the administration must also justify—the United States will have to scale back its offensive significantly under the War Powers Resolution of 1973, in the same account. A senior administration official said late on Thursday that, for the resolution, hostilities had ceased with the start of the April ceasefire, effectively resetting the clock, in the same account. The two sentences—a deadline on Friday, and a clock already reset—stand in the file unreconciled.

Tehran’s night was spent answering in advance. Air defence activity was heard in some areas of the capital late on Thursday, one agency reported, and another said air defences were engaging small drones and unmanned surveillance aircraft, in the same account; no strike on Tehran is confirmed in any account in the file. A senior Guard official said any new American attack, even if limited, would usher in long and painful strikes on its regional positions, and the aerospace force commander is quoted that the Americans have seen what happened to their regional bases and will see the same happen to their warships, in the same account. The Supreme Leader’s written message said the enemies’ abuses of the waterway would be eliminated under the strait’s new management, in the same account. The United Arab Emirates said on Thursday it had banned its citizens from travelling to Iran, Lebanon and Iraq and urged those there to leave, in the same account. Both blockades stand. Settled: the positions, again, from every authorized voice on both sides, now including a date in Congress. Open: whether Friday brings an approval, an extension, a strike, or none of the three.

Al Jazeera — Iran vows a long, painful response if the United States renews attacks

Iran says it will respond with long and painful strikes on American positions across the Gulf if Washington renews attacks, and has restated its claim to the Strait of Hormuz. Foreign Ministry spokesman Baghaei defended the closure of the strait on Thursday night as legitimate, legal and accepted under international law, and justified attacks on American assets in Gulf countries. Friday is the deadline for Congress to approve the war; without approval or a 30-day extension the United States will have to scale back its offensive under the War Powers Resolution, and a senior administration official said late on Thursday that, for the resolution, hostilities had ceased with the start of the April ceasefire, effectively resetting the clock. The account reports, from Axios, that President Trump received a briefing on Thursday on plans for a series of further military strikes, and Senator Blumenthal’s statement that his impression is that an imminent military strike is very much on the table. Air defence activity was heard in some areas of Tehran late on Thursday, one agency reported, and another said air defences were engaging small drones and surveillance aircraft. A senior Guard official said any new American attack, even if limited, would usher in long and painful strikes on its regional positions.

Particle — Spain’s two-week campaign opens after the housing decree is revalidated and the polls diverge

The official two-week campaign opened at midnight on Thursday, launching rallies, televised debates and canvassing before voting on November 29. The standing committee moved to validate the housing decree that triggered the snap election, returning the measures on evictions, two-year rental extensions and a 2 percent cap on rent rises to the centre of the campaign. Most private pollsters continue to project a PP–Vox bloc capable of an absolute majority, while the public CIS poll released at the campaign start forecasts a PSOE plurality and denies the right-wing bloc a majority. Demócrata — Ms. Colau kicks off the campaign in Vallecas with a message to Prime Minister Sánchez

Ms. Colau chose Vallecas to launch her coalition’s campaign for the November 29 elections, at a rally held hours before the official start, in one of Madrid’s neighbourhoods with a tradition of voting for the left. The standing body validated the housing decree by 35 votes to 34—protection against certain evictions, limits on speculative practices and an extraordinary two-year extension for contracts that meet the requirements—while the specific rule on automatic renewal of rental agreements fell after the PNV joined the rejection. The outcome left a partial legislative victory for the government. Ms. Colau demanded more courage against rentiers and speculators and called for the November 11 housing strike’s momentum to be carried into votes.

Reuters — Spain approves one of the housing decrees that triggered the snap election

The standing body of 69 caretaker deputies validated the broad housing decree by one vote—rent caps, a temporary ban on evictions and limits on corporate purchases of residential property, in force since its publication in early October—and rejected, also by one vote, the measure for the automatic renewal of rental contracts. The opposition’s arguments are carried beside the result: Mr. Feijóo’s pledge to repeal the decrees if he governs, and the argument that the measures reduce housing supply and push up prices. The official campaign for the November 29 election opens on Friday, and the housing deficit stands at about 750,000 homes on the central bank’s figures.
