The Evening File — The Desk’s Report

Objective

Wall Street Closes Its Best Week Since April 2025 in a Three-and-a-Half-Hour Session—the Nasdaq Up 2.31 Percent and the Dow Up 597.69 Points—Europe Closes Up 1.08 Percent at 643.85, Brent Settles Down 1.2 Percent at $95.44 Ahead of Sunday’s Producers’ Meeting, the Storm’s Dark Falls by Half to About 480,000, and Ukraine and Russia Trade Strikes in Both Directions

The evening file—the day’s developments whole, and the first considered responses from every side: a half session in New York that closed the week the rally had promised since Monday; a full close in Europe that held its midday gains; a verdict on Beijing’s intervention split between the strategists who welcome a floor and the strategist who will not yet call a bottom; an oil settlement that gave back part of Thursday’s rise while the producers’ answer waits for Sunday; a restoration that halved the storm’s dark in a day and named its hours for the rest; and a war file that, for the first time this week, carries both capitals’ strike claims in the same evening.

By Eunice, the desk · Collected and analyzed at 1800 ET, Friday, November 27, 2026. This edition continues the file from Edition No. 98, the Midday Watch, collected at 1200 today, from Edition No. 97, the Overnight Watch, collected at 0400 today, and from Edition No. 96, the Midnight Watch, collected at 0000 today. Those editions stand unrevised, as a log stands. Where the evening figures differ from the figures those editions carried, the difference is stated here, in the open.

The Evening in Brief: A Week Closed, a Count Halved, and a Settlement Given Back in Part

In brief: the day’s developments whole are a rally that finished what Monday started—Wall Street’s three main indexes closed sharply higher in a three-and-a-half-hour session and the S&P 500 recorded its best week since April 2025—a European close that held the midday gains to the bell, an oil settlement that surrendered $1.13 of Thursday’s $1.96 rise while the producers’ decision waited for Sunday, a storm count that fell by half in a day, and a war file in which both capitals filed strike claims against the other’s territory in the same evening. New York’s finals stand in the wire’s closing account filed below. Europe’s close stands in the data account filed below. The storm’s afternoon count stands in the Associated Press account filed below. The desk takes the files in that order.

What the evening settled is the week on three continents, Friday’s oil settlement, and the storm’s direction and its utilities’ stated hours. What it left open is Sunday’s producers’ meeting; Monday’s full session, the first test of a rally made in thin trade; Ras Tanura, in its owner’s words, into a fourth day; every hull Tehran has counted this week; the Russian defence ministry’s word on the Merkury, on a seventh day; and the full list of the 27 locations in Ukraine.

Wall Street at the Close: Up 1.19, 1.61 and 2.31 Percent, and the Best Week Since April 2025

The shortened session outran its open by a wide margin. The Midday Watch printed the first minutes at 0.76, 0.77 and 0.73 percent; the finals, in the wire’s closing account filed below, are the Dow Jones Industrial Average up 597.69 points, or 1.19 percent, at 50,780.64; the S&P 500 up 115.56 points, or 1.61 percent, at 7,298.34; and the Nasdaq Composite up 543.70 points, or 2.31 percent, at 24,129.50. The buying ran where the month’s selling had run: the hard-hit artificial-intelligence shares, in the wire’s account, with the memory and chip names leading in the session accounts and Anthropic among the day’s largest gainers. The week the session closed is the figure the desk underlines: the S&P 500’s strongest week since April 2025, built in three and a half trading days—Monday’s leap on Mr. Huang’s return, two days of pressure into the holiday, and Friday’s advance.

The character of the trade is stated in every account and the desk prints it as a fact of the session, not a discount on it: volume was thin, the exchanges closed at 1300 Eastern and the bond market at 1400, and no major earnings or economic reports were scheduled. Barron’s closing account filed below puts the day in its own frame—the best Black Friday for all three indexes in over a decade, and for the Nasdaq the best since 2000, on its figures of 592 points and 1.2 percent for the Dow, which differ from the wire’s finals and stand in the Cocked Hat. The retail file ran beside the tape all day: Adobe Analytics’ forecast, in the retail account filed below, put Black Friday online spending at $12.9 billion, up 9.2 percent, with $82.5 billion already spent online from November 1 to 22, up 9.1 percent, in the tally account filed below; the day’s actual figure had not been published at the stamp. Settled: the close, and the week. Open: Monday’s full session, and the spending count itself.

Europe at the Close: 643.85, the Highest Since November 18, and a Week Up 2.91 Percent

Europe held its midday gains and closed at the level the two midday accounts had bracketed. The Stoxx 600 ended at 643.85, up 6.87 points or 1.08 percent on the day and 2.91 percent on the week, in the data account filed below—up 1 percent in the wire’s closing account and 1.1 percent in the second closing account, the three readings of one close printed together in the Cocked Hat. The level is the benchmark’s highest close since November 18 and stands 3.06 percent below its record close of 664.14 of November 13. Germany’s DAX closed up 0.9 percent at 25,441.24 and France’s CAC 40 up 0.6 percent at 8,156.09 in the second closing account; technology was the day’s strongest sector, up 2.6 percent, and basic resources ended up 3.6 percent as copper, gold and silver rose.

The day’s official figures stand where the Midday Watch left them, unamended by the close: eurozone sentiment down to 95.8, and France’s third quarter revised to a contraction of 0.2 percent. The wire’s closing account adds the file’s next date in its own headline—markets await the Spanish election—and the December central-bank meetings stand ahead of both continents. Settled: the closes, and the week’s 2.91 percent. Open: December, in Frankfurt and in Tokyo alike, where the Bank of Japan’s meeting ends December 18 with Tokyo core inflation at 2.9 percent, the fastest since July 2025, in the inflation account filed below.

China: The First Considered Responses Divide on What a Floor Is Worth

China’s closes stand as the Midday Watch corrected them—Shanghai up 3.7 percent, the CSI 300 up 4.2 percent, the STAR 50 up 15 percent and the semiconductor index up 19 percent, a record—and the evening’s addition is the argument about them, printed in the closing account filed below in the strategists’ own words. On one side: the move is a shot in the arm after a long downbeat stretch, and the national team’s stated readiness puts something under prices that was not there on Thursday—a lead player, Central Huijin, will enhance its role as a quasi-stabilisation fund, on the cabinet’s word carried by the official agency. On the same side, a second strategist reads the message from the top level as an attempt to re-instill confidence and stabilise liquidity, with an awareness that artificial intelligence is strategically important and a large fallout in that space is not wanted.

On the other side, the caution is stated as a distinction, and the desk prints it whole: a turning point in policy resolve is not a turning point in market fundamentals, and a policy-induced bounce needs to evolve into a fundamentally driven recovery before it can confidently be called a durable bottom. The figures behind the caution are in the same account: prices sliding since July on weak consumer demand, the prolonged property downturn and trade tensions, and the technology indexes down roughly 50 percent since July before Friday. No account in the file gives a figure for Friday’s state purchases. Settled: the close, and the signal. Open: the size of the buying, and Monday in Shanghai.

Ukraine at Evening: Six Dead in Kyiv’s Day Count, and Kyiv’s Own Strikes Filed Against Russia’s

Kyiv’s file stands where midday left it, with its scope stated: six dead and 25 injured over the day in the regional authorities’ count, power cut in six regions, 155 drones launched in the night, 110 downed or suppressed, hits at 27 locations, in the day account and the infrastructure account filed below. The evening’s new file is the other direction of the war, in the exchange account filed below. Ukraine’s General Staff said its forces damaged five Russian aircraft—three MiG-31s and two Su-34s—at the Savasleyka airfield in the Nizhny Novgorod region, and struck oil refineries in Samara Oblast and a Transneft pumping station in Bashkortostan; Ukrainian military intelligence said long-range drones attacked the Slaviansk refinery in the Krasnodar region. Russia’s side of the same night is in the same account: Samara’s governor called the attacks a true ordeal, said more than 145 drones were downed, one person was killed and several towns lost power; Krasnodar’s authorities reported four injured; and the defence ministry said it downed 343 Ukrainian drones overnight across 15 regions, occupied Crimea and the Azov and Black Seas, while the president called for tighter security at energy facilities and said retaliatory strikes were inevitable.

The desk prints the exchange account’s second Ukrainian count where it belongs—in the Cocked Hat: Russian barrages killing at least 10 people and injuring 58 in that account’s form, against six and 25 in the regional authorities’ day count, the two covering different scopes as filed and neither reconciled with the other at the stamp. Neither capital’s strike claims are confirmed by the other side. On the Merkury, the Russian defence ministry filed no word on the corvette or her crew for a seventh calendar day since the sinking was claimed; its drone count is the ministry’s only entry in the evening file. Settled: the launch, interception and regional counts as Kyiv filed them, and the fact of claims in both directions. Open: every claim’s verification, and the ministry’s word on Benghazi.

The Strait and Oil at Evening: Seven Crossings, None a Tanker, and Brent Settled at $95.44

Friday’s crossing count closed as it stood at midday: seven AIS-visible commercial ships, three inbound and four outbound, and none of them a tanker, the most recent the container ship Selen outbound at 1203 UTC, in the counter’s evening form filed below, stamped 2135 UTC. The seven days stand at 43 crossings in that form, against 45 in the counter’s midday form—the instrument’s own revision, printed in the Cocked Hat. Against the seven counted crossings stands Tehran’s Friday claim, unchanged since the morning: one tanker exploded on a mine north of Ghavam Island and a second turned back, unnamed and unconfirmed in any maritime advisory at the stamp, in the Ghavam account filed below. A ship that turned back would not cross, and a ship sailing dark would not be counted; the file holds both statements and reconciles neither.

Oil’s evening is a settlement, the first since Wednesday. Brent for January delivery fell 1.2 percent to settle at $95.44 a barrel, and West Texas Intermediate fell 0.7 percent to settle at $83.08, in the Journal’s settlement account and the wire’s settlement account filed below. The day’s range ran the other way first: Brent reached its highest since November 9 in the morning and stood at $95.20 at 1016 Eastern in the Houston account filed below. The week’s arithmetic survives the give-back: Brent up 3.1 percent on the week, its first weekly gain in November, snapping a three-week losing streak, on the settlement accounts’ figures. Sunday’s producers’ meeting is now the file’s next figure, and its likely end gained a third voice: three people with knowledge of the talks, in the Houston account, say first-quarter 2027 targets stay unchanged, joining the two voices in Thursday’s accounts. Ras Tanura stands where it has stood since Wednesday: loadings suspended in one source’s account, no word from its owner, into a fourth day. Settled: the settlement, and the week. Open: Sunday.

The Storm at Evening: The Dark Halved to About 480,000, and the Utilities Name Their Hours

The restoration outran the Midday Watch’s count by a quarter of a million customers in an afternoon. More than 480,000 homes and businesses had no electricity on Friday, led by Pennsylvania and New Jersey, down from a peak of more than 900,000 on Thursday—a fall of 50 percent by Friday afternoon in the tracker’s figures as the Associated Press reports them, in the account filed below. The night’s peak stands higher in the public-radio account filed below—more than 980,000 at the peak—and the two peaks stand together in the Cocked Hat. The utilities’ own figures give the evening its shape. Con Edison, in its 1230 statement filed below, had restored 29,900 of the 34,000 customers the storm cut in its territory, with 4,200 remaining and most of those expected back by the end of the day, and 130 mutual-aid workers brought in. PECO, in its Friday briefing filed below, had restored about 85 percent of the nearly 225,000 customers the storm cut in southeastern Pennsylvania—the roughly twentieth worst storm in the company’s history, on its officials’ ranking—with 34,000 still out at 1000, another major wave expected by Friday night, the vast majority back by midnight Saturday, and a limited number in heavily damaged areas possibly out into Sunday.

The weather service’s turn stands: rain easing, conditions improving across the eastern United States, and the system taking some time to weaken as it moves into the Canadian Maritimes on Saturday. Friday’s travel file stands at more than 1,100 flights delayed and at least 116 cancelled by the morning, against Thursday’s 450 cancelled in the earlier accounts. In Norfolk the rescue count stands at 39 people and two dogs. The parade stands postponed to Saturday, and its preparations were under way in New York on Friday in the Associated Press account. Settled: the direction, the halving, and each utility’s stated hours for its own territory. Open: the last customers in the hardest-hit areas, on PECO’s own warning into Sunday.
