The Drone Night: 1,246 in Moscow’s Count, 162 in Kyiv’s, and No Third Count in the File

Moscow’s figure is the largest single-night interception claim in the file’s record. Air defence forces shot down 1,246 Ukrainian drones overnight, the defence ministry said on Saturday; more than 400 drones were launched toward the capital, of which 123 were downed in close proximity to the city, the mayor said on his channel. The regional accounts that accompany the ministry’s figure are specific in two places. In the Bryansk region, a Ukrainian drone attacked a bus in the village of Klimovo and wounded nine people, the governor said. In the Samara region, Ukraine made another attempt to attack industrial facilities overnight, with no deaths or injuries, that governor said. The ministry’s figure is a claim of interceptions, not of launches; how many drones Ukraine launched in total, no account in the night states, and the desk does not derive a launch count from an interception count.

Kyiv’s count of the inbound night is complete in its own form. Russia attacked from 1800 on November 27 with 162 strike drones—Shahed types, 74 of them jet-powered, with Gerbera and Parodiya decoys among them—launched from Oryol, Millerovo and Kursk in Russia and from occupied Donetsk and Hvardiiske in Crimea, the Air Force said. By 0800, air defences had shot down or suppressed 141, 54 of them jet-powered; hits were recorded at 13 locations and debris at seven more; the attack was still under way at the hour of the report, with drones still in the air. Moscow’s own account of its outbound night, in its defence press, names a different set of objects: four railway locomotives struck in the Dnipropetrovsk, Zhytomyr and Kharkiv regions while carrying weapons and equipment; a dry-cargo vessel struck in the Black Sea south of Odesa, which the ministry said was delivering military cargo; a fuel tank at Kilia in the Odesa region; and a substation in the Kyiv region powering defence-industry enterprises, in the ministry account filed below. The two outbound accounts agree that drones were the instrument and on no target list. Settled: both counts, as each capital’s statement. Open: the damage at the 13 locations, and any figure for the night that does not come from a belligerent.

Kyiv’s Ask: The President Names the Shortage a Matter of Lives

The interview the president gave in Kyiv stands in the file beside the counts, and the desk prints it as his statement of his position. Ukraine faces a very large deficit of interceptors against ballistic missiles, and Washington is delaying deliveries of Patriot systems, President Zelensky told the BBC, in the interview account filed below. “It’s a pity they made a focus on Middle East and they help them more,” he said. “The deficit of these missiles—that is people’s lives.” His forces now destroy about 70 percent of Russia’s fastest drones, he said, a figure he said was improving. Restrictions on the Starshield satellite service still apply over occupied territory and still hobble the interception of jet-powered drones there, he said; asked who is responsible, he answered that SpaceX is the technical party and that he does not know who made the political decision, in the satellite account filed below. The Abu Dhabi round of talks earlier in November produced no clear progress, he said; a further round is expected in December, and whether European representatives will sit at it remains uncertain. Settled: the president’s position, in his words. Open: the December round, and the deliveries.

Libya at Dawn: Two Convoys Turn Back, and the Ministry Answers Nothing

The Benghazi file’s six days of silence from Moscow were not broken by Moscow in the night; they were broken around it. A convoy of Russian ships carrying military vehicles bound for Libya has turned around in the Mediterranean after Ukrainian maritime drones sank the corvette in Benghazi port on November 22, three people from military and security services told the wire on Friday, in the convoy account filed below. A second convoy, escorted by the frigate Golovko, turned around near the Strait of Sicily on the same day as the Benghazi attack, a European military official who had been tracking it said; a Ukrainian military intelligence official and a separate Middle East security official confirmed that the second flotilla had turned from heading to Libya and was now paused off the coast of Algeria. The diversion had not been previously reported. Russia’s defence ministry did not immediately respond to a request for comment sent outside office hours, the account records—which is the ministry’s whole contribution to the file at the stamp, on the seventh calendar day since the sinking was claimed. Settled: the convoys’ turn, on the tracking officials’ word. Open: the ministry’s account of the Merkury and her crew, the paused flotilla’s destination, and whether the Libya run is abandoned or postponed.

The Strait at Dawn: Three Claims, Three Denials, and Eight Crossings on Friday

The strait’s night was an exchange of statements over a quiet counting line. Iran’s navy commander said American forces had seized two Iranian oil tankers and that the Guards’ navy had retaken them while the Americans aboard slept; that Iranian forces struck the carrier Abraham Lincoln on the first day of the American operation, forcing it to seek evacuation help; and that the United States charges shipowners $2.5 million for passage through the strait. The American command answered all three in one statement on Friday and denied all three, in the denial account filed below: “Iran no longer has a navy. The entire situation the Iranian military commander described is another fabricated lie.” The carrier strike group was well defended and never struck, the command said, and completed its deployment and returned to the United States last month; there is no toll, and thousands of ships and billions of barrels have transited freely and safely under American protection, in the command account filed below. The desk prints both sides’ words as their words; no third account in the night speaks to the tankers or the carrier.

The counting line’s own figures are Friday’s, and they are the week’s best day and still a fraction of the old traffic. Friday counted eight crossings, four inbound and four outbound, one of them a tanker, the last the products tanker Hartati inbound at 2146 UTC; the seven days stand at 38 crossings, 20 inbound and 18 outbound, at 5.4 a day, about 6 percent of the pre-crisis rate, in the counter’s brief filed below. Settled: Friday’s eight, and the two commands’ positions. Open: the Khasab photograph’s ship, and every hull Tehran counts.
