The Evening in Brief

The day closed its talks, its market and its holiday, and it left its two counts, its one committee file and both of its wars exactly where the evening finds them: decided in form, open in substance, with the next step named and dated. The two days in Abu Dhabi ended with a large prisoner exchange agreed in principle and nothing else signed—no ceasefire, no joint statement, no date for a next round—and the process now travels: Mr. Kushner and Mr. Witkoff meet President Putin in Moscow on Thursday and President Zelenskyy in Dublin after it, with the exchange numbers to be finalized the same day, in the wire account filed below. Wall Street reversed Tuesday whole: the S&P 500 closed up 1.31 percent at 7,619.38, the Nasdaq Composite up 1.60 percent at 25,466.19 and the Dow up 0.49 percent at 52,486.49, on one earnings report, with the bond market shut for the holiday and no cash rate to read the move against, in the closing account filed below. At Arlington the president laid the wreath at 1100 and then defended the Iran war from the amphitheater: America did not start it, Iran has waged war on America since 1979, the United States won, and what remains is the timing of a total victory, in the Arlington account filed below. The eight articles against Secretary Hegseth rest in a Judiciary Committee chaired by the man the House Republicans elected their leader for the next Congress on the same day as the referral. Neither Senate race was called: the afternoon tallies moved both Democratic challengers further ahead, to 3,041 in Mississippi and 4,250 in Alaska. In Spain, four million workers struck over housing in the first general strike in 14 years.

Abu Dhabi, Whole: Under Two Hours, an Exchange in Principle, and the File Packed for Moscow

The talks are over, and their whole announced product is a prisoner exchange. Wednesday’s session ran just under two hours, against four hours on Tuesday, and closed with the agreement Mr. Kushner stated in one sentence: “One of the positives is we agreed that we’re going to have the biggest humanitarian release of prisoners, which we’re going to finalize the numbers tomorrow.” Mr. Medinsky, for Russia, and Mr. Umerov, for Kyiv, both confirmed the planned exchange, Mr. Medinsky with the qualification that the agreement is in principle, in the wire account above. On the size, the file still holds ranges and not a figure: more than 1,000 a side in the Russian general’s account, 1,000 to 1,500 in the plans President Zelenskyy announced, and an exchange in two to three weeks in General Zorin’s account, all carried at midday and unamended at 1800. No ceasefire was agreed. No joint statement was published. No date for a next round was announced. Foreign Minister Lavrov rejected the European demands for a ceasefire framework on the margins, and Mr. Kushner said no agreement could be reached until the territorial dispute was settled.

The first considered responses arrived in the afternoon, and they divide on what the two days were. The assessment filed below calls the exchange a success and good news for Ukraine, and in the same pages calls the ending predictable: no breakthrough, and no apparent progress on the ceasefire that remains Kyiv’s priority while, in one analyst’s words, Ukrainians endure another dark and cold winter. Washington is reported to have floated security guarantees modeled on the alliance’s Article 5, with the warning beside it that President Putin would likely seek additional territorial cessions in exchange. A negotiator found a little bit of flexibility in private conversations and gave no specifics. The conclusion printed is plain: none of this suggests that a clear step toward peace was made. The principals’ own registers stand apart from the assessment and from each other. Mr. Medinsky said the meeting helped the two capitals edge closer to peace; the Kremlin’s adviser, Mr. Ushakov, called the talks substantive and quite useful; President Zelenskyy wrote that Europe must be represented at every stage of real diplomacy; and Mr. Kushner wrote that the exchange will reunite families, build trust and create momentum toward peace, all in the wire account above.

Europe’s seat is the file’s standing disagreement, and Wednesday sharpened it rather than settling it. The European governments, the European Union and the alliance were in the capital and did not attend the three-way sessions, after Moscow objected; Kyiv’s delegation was to meet them separately on Wednesday. Kremlin spokesman Peskov said European representatives could eventually join but that their current statements show no desire to contribute to movement toward peace, and that Moscow complains Europe finances and supplies Ukraine’s weapons. Mr. Kushner said one goal of the Moscow meeting is to get the Europeans added to the talks, and Mr. Arakhamia, for Kyiv, said the next talks should be multilateral rather than trilateral, in the comparison account filed below. The itinerary is the next fact in the file: Moscow on Thursday, November 12, then Dublin and President Zelenskyy. Settled: the talks ended, the exchange is agreed in principle, the numbers are Thursday’s work. Open: the land, the ceasefire, and Europe’s chair.

The War, Whole: The Talks Closed Over a Day of Strikes in Both Directions

The fighting ran through the closing session for a second day, and the evening file carries one new count the midday file did not hold. A daytime attack on a market in the southern Odesa region killed three people and injured 11, and residential buildings were damaged, on the regional administration’s figures in the wire account above. The overnight counts stand as the midday edition printed them, unreconciled: Russia struck the Zaporizhzhia region and Kharkiv overnight, wounding seven civilians; Russia’s Defense Ministry counted 627 Ukrainian drones downed overnight, 150 of them over the region around St. Petersburg, against the governor’s earlier count of 92 over the Leningrad region, with a fire at the port of Ust-Luga injuring three workers. The industry figure stands unamended: Ukrainian attacks since late October have disrupted about 30 percent of Russia’s refining capacity. One smaller file entered the record in the afternoon: a 49-year-old Indonesian man is in Ukrainian custody after, on his account carried in the wire report, he signed what he believed was a construction contract in Moscow and was taken to the fighting in the Zaporizhzhia region without training; his embassy is seeking access, and neither government commented. The talks and the strikes proceeded on the same hours for two days; neither file mentions the other.

The Close, Whole: Tuesday Reversed on One Earnings Report, With No Bonds to Read It Against

Wall Street’s verdict on the day was given at the close, and it was a reversal, not a repair. The S&P 500 closed up 1.31 percent at 7,619.38, the Nasdaq Composite up 1.60 percent at 25,466.19 and the Dow Jones Industrial Average up 0.49 percent at 52,486.49, in the closing account above and the closing table filed below, which agree to the figure. A second closing table filed below runs the same session higher—the Nasdaq up 2.06 percent at 25,583.42 and the S&P 500 up 1.4 percent at 7,626.55, with the Dow lower than the first table at 52,337.86—and both tables stand in the Cocked Hat; the desk does not average them. The instrument behind the move was the same earnings report that lifted the morning: CoreWeave closed up 24.71 percent at $60.77, on quarterly revenue of $3.66 billion against a $3.56 billion consensus and a raised outlook, having said it would become profitable on an adjusted basis in the first half of 2028, and Nvidia closed up 2.92 percent at $173.73 after two days of losses that took about $430 billion from its value. The technology index stood up nearly 4 percent in the afternoon, on track for its largest daily gain since August 4, and the volatility index fell for a fourth straight session, to 17.63 in the afternoon from 18.48 at midday.

The rally was not a rate story, and the file says so in its own figures. Traders fully price an increase of at least a quarter point in December, with the half-point move at 13.2 percent in the afternoon pricing—against 11.5 percent in the morning figures, 10 percent in the overnight print and 15.3 percent in a Tuesday instrument, all printed in the Cocked Hat. Governor Waller’s stated test stands met by Tuesday’s core print of 0.3 percent, and the committee’s decision stands untaken. The session ran, as the holiday required, without its second witness: the cash bond market stood closed for Veterans Day and reopens Thursday, and the official crude inventory figures, due Wednesday, come Thursday for the same reason. Oil closed the day’s other sentence upward. Brent settled up 1.39 percent at $92.76 and American crude up 0.57 percent at $86.53, in the closing account above, after a midday print at $93.64 on the sentence from Tehran that no enriched nuclear material will leave the country, in the wire account filed below; the morning’s prints ran from $92.01 to $92.81. Gold futures fell 0.93 percent, and the spot price stood below $4,000 in an afternoon reading. Friday’s producer price report is the next inflation fact in the file. Settled: the close, the earnings, and that December brings an increase on every pricing in the file. Open: 25 or 50, and whether the chip rally holds a second day.

Arlington, Whole: A Wreath at 1100, and a Won War Claimed From the Amphitheater

The capital’s holiday ran on its printed schedule, and the evening file now holds the text the midday file could only schedule. The president and the first lady took part in the wreath-laying at the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier at 1100, and the remarks followed in the Memorial Amphitheater at the 73rd annual National Veterans Day Observance, with the vice president, the secretary of veterans affairs and Secretary Hegseth among those attending. The president’s address was a tribute built around named veterans—a survivor of the 1983 Beirut barracks bombing, in which 241 service members were killed; a 103-year-old veteran of the Bulge whose battalion was among the first into Dachau; a medevac pilot credited with 1,874 combat missions; a Green Beret who returned to combat after 27 surgeries—and a defense of the Iran war ran through the whole of it, in the Arlington account above.

The war sentences, as the accounts filed below quote them, are these. “America did not start a war with Iran. Iran has been waging war on America since 1979.” “We won. It’s just a question of when we want to end it.” “We want to make sure we have a total victory, not a 93 percent victory. It’s going to be total.” The president put the American dead in the conflict at 18, said the campaign began seven months ago with only a few weeks of intense fighting, said Iran would need 25 years to rebuild what was destroyed, and gave no date for an end. He praised Secretary Hegseth from the platform the day after the referral vote—“He’s done a fantastic job, and he loves you, and he protects you”—and he renewed the arch: “Soon, we’re going to dedicate a magnificent, triumphal arch to our veterans,” who have, in his words, been waiting for it for 200 years, in the second Arlington account filed below. One warning stood in the speech without a name attached: the country will not be allowed to be hurt by what he called a horrible ideology, and everyone knows what he means. On veterans’ services the president’s figures and his own proclamation’s figures do not match, and both stand in the Cocked Hat: a backlog cut by 71 percent and nearly 100,000 homeless veterans housed, in the speech as the Arlington account carries it, against a cut of more than 70 percent and nearly 52,000 housed in the proclamation filed at midday.

One exchange in the afternoon file belongs to the election as much as to the holiday. The president said the vice president was largely correct in blaming the Iran war for the party’s defeat, and the vice president’s post is quoted beside it: “The reason we lost is the war. The president himself said so last night”—with the president’s eldest son backing the assessment, in the account filed below. The desk prints the agreement as the day’s political fact: the war’s authors and its critics in the president’s own house now give the same cause for the midterm result, and differ on what follows from it. Tehran’s standing sentences stand unamended beside the speech: no negotiations with the Americans are under way, no enriched material leaves the country, and the two blockades—Iran’s of the strait, America’s of Iran’s ports—both stand. No agreement on reopening the strait exists. The desk’s own record of the day stands in the Special Report filed this morning outside the watches.

Washington, Whole: The Articles Rest With the Leader’s Committee, and the Senate Picks Its Own

The impeachment file produced no new vote on Wednesday, and its state at 1800 is the state the midnight edition settled, with one alignment the day made visible. The eight articles against Secretary Hegseth rest in the Judiciary Committee after Tuesday’s referral vote of 211 to 204. The committee’s chairman, Representative Jordan of Ohio, is the man the House Republicans elected, the same day, as their minority leader for the 120th Congress, by 106 to 73 over Mr. Scalise, in the leadership account filed below. Mr. Jordan’s sentence for the next two years is on the record: when the Democrats provide more government, the Republicans will provide more freedom; and his stated purpose, in the accounts filed below, is to regain the majority. The expected chairman of the same committee in the next Congress, Representative Raskin, has said his party is sober about impeachment, and Leader Jeffries has indicated the Democrats will reassess when they take the majority on January 3, when this Congress’s articles expire and new ones would be required, in the fallout account filed below. Eleven Democrats were absent from Tuesday’s vote; Mr. Massie was the only Republican against the referral. The House scheduled no votes on the holiday. Across the Capitol, the Senate Republicans re-elected Senator Thune as their leader on Tuesday and chose Senator Moreno to lead the committee for the 2028 cycle, and one senator’s arithmetic entered the file: Senator Justice of West Virginia said his party can forget about winning back the Senate in 2028 after losing at least seven seats, in the 2028 account filed below. Settled: the referral, both leaderships, and the chairman’s double office. Open: January.

The Count at Evening: Both Challengers Wider Ahead, Neither Race Called

For a tenth day neither Senate race was called, and Wednesday’s part in both files is at last arithmetic and not only administration. In Mississippi, Mr. Colom led Senator Hyde-Smith by 3,041 votes—49.11 percent to 48.79 percent, with the independent candidate at 2.1 percent and about 98 percent counted—in the tally account filed below, up from about 2,607 in the last tally this paper printed. A second account puts the lead at 3,176 as of Tuesday night, as the grace period for late ballots closed and the provisional ballots were counted, in the count account filed below; a third account, printed in the Cocked Hat, still carries 1,138. The machinery stands as filed: about 14,200 provisional ballots processed with about 80 percent accepted, counties certifying by Friday, November 13, the state certifying December 3, and no automatic recount provision in Mississippi law, in the uncalled-races account filed below.

In Alaska, the afternoon tally moved the margin the midday file could only imply. Ms. Peltola led Senator Sullivan by 4,250 first-choice votes—118,926, or 49 percent, to 114,676, or 47.2 percent—up from 2,517 on Monday’s count, in the tally account and the count account above. An estimated 46,000 ballots remain to arrive or be tallied in one account. The rule that decides the calendar is unchanged: if Ms. Peltola passes 50 percent of the first choices there is no ranked-choice tabulation; if she does not, the tabulation runs on November 18, with the next count on November 13 and a target certification date of November 25. More than a dozen House races also remain too early to call, and the size of both majorities is therefore still undetermined a week after the vote. Democrats hold at least 54 Senate seats whatever the two races decide; the evening question is whether the majority stands at 54, 55 or 56. Settled: both margins widened, and both deadlines. Open: both seats.

The Rest of the File: A Strike in Spain, and Three Files That Did Not Move

The day’s largest event outside the American files was a strike. Four million workers took part in a general strike across Spain on Wednesday, the unions said, in protest against the housing crisis, less than three weeks before a snap general election on November 29; it is the country’s first general strike since 2012, in the Madrid account filed below. Shops on the Gran Via drew their shutters under a heavy police presence, turnout ran strongest at the ports and the automobile plants, electricity demand fell by as much as 15.5 percent in the late morning on the grid operator’s figures, and police reported 40 arrests by midday from 476 incidents. The housing deficit stands at about 750,000 homes on the central bank’s figures, growing past a million by 2028. Two storms threatened the afternoon marches, and the demonstration in Cadiz was suspended. The prime minister’s stated purpose in calling the election—to turn the argument to housing and away from the corruption cases and the train crash in the file—is now the strike’s argument as well, and the vote is 18 days out.

Three files the desk carries produced no new published fact between midday and evening, and the evening records the absence as the fact. At RAF Mildenhall, the 13 American aircraft relocated on Monday on a threat no official has named stand relocated; the counts beneath the 13—ten tankers and three Ospreys in the flight-data accounts, 12 in total in another—stand unreconciled, the reported connection to Iran stands unattributed to any official statement, and no official has named the threat. In Jerusalem, the machinery named at midday stands: Mr. Kahana for speaker on November 17 at 1600, Mr. Folkman to chair the Arrangements Committee, the committee composition to the plenary on November 18, Mr. Eisenkot’s mandate running to December 6, and the government unformed. In Kraków, the city’s mourning ran its last day with no forensic finding published: the detailed opinions on the nine victims may take weeks, the proceedings against the dead suspect will be discontinued, and the wider investigation into motive and assistance continues, with the justice minister’s sentence on a strong thread of extremism standing beside the prosecutors’ position that motive remains unresolved, unjoined by them. The desk fills none of the space where those findings will go.
