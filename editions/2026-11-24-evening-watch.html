Objective

The Dow Closes Above 50,000 as the Nasdaq Falls on Nvidia’s Retreat, Dymer’s Toll Rises to Twelve, and Mr. Sullivan Concedes in Alaska While Poland Renews Its Push for American Troops

The evening file—the day’s developments whole, and the first considered responses from every side: a close that split in two, a confidence print from before the rally, a toll that rose by one in a hospital, a concession that closed the last Senate race, and two straits of the record—one in Poland, one at Hormuz—where the counts still do not agree.

By Eunice, the desk · Collected and analyzed at 1800 ET, Tuesday, November 24, 2026. This edition continues the file from Edition No. 86, the Midday Watch, collected at 1200 today, from Edition No. 85, the Overnight Watch, collected at 0400 today, and from Edition No. 84, the Midnight Watch, collected at 0000 today. Those editions stand unrevised, as a log stands. Where the evening figures differ from the figures those editions carried, the difference is stated here, in the open.

The Evening in Brief: A Close That Split, a Toll That Rose by One, and a Race That Closed

In brief: Wall Street closed split on Tuesday, the Dow above 50,000 for the close and the technology indexes down, as the stock that made Monday’s rally gave back part of it; the last uncalled Senate race of the midterms closed with a concession in Alaska; the toll in Dymer rose from eleven to twelve in a hospital; and on the two military files of the day—Poland and the Strait of Hormuz—the responses arrived while the underlying counts still did not agree. The Dow Jones Industrial Average closed at 50,026.46, up 0.87 percent, the S&P 500 at 7,202.45, down 0.48 percent, and the Nasdaq Composite down about 1.5 percent, in the closing account and the market account filed below. Nvidia closed at $155.38, down 5.86 percent, after Monday’s gain of 31.48 percent to $165.05. In Alaska, the certified count stands at 144,583 votes for Mary Peltola and 132,022 for Dan S. Sullivan, in the state’s own report filed below, and Mr. Sullivan conceded on Tuesday. In Dymer, the woman of seventy-nine who was pulled alive from the rubble died in hospital, and the toll stands at twelve, in the updated account filed below. The desk takes the files in that order.

What the evening settled is the close, in full; the Alaska result, certified and conceded; Dymer’s new toll; and the calendar in Washington, where Mr. Huang’s December appearances stand confirmed in both parties’ tellings. What it left open is the rest: whether the tanker counts at Hormuz describe mines, projectiles, or the same ships counted twice; what Poland’s renewed request for American troops will produce; and whether Tuesday’s split close is a rotation or the start of a second slide, in a week shortened by Thanksgiving.

The Markets at the Close: The Dow Above 50,000, the Nasdaq Down, and Nvidia Down 5.86 Percent

The close completes the arc the Midday Watch left open at 1200, when the Dow stood up and the Nasdaq down in the morning figures. At the bell the split had widened and held. The Dow added 431.15 points in the closing account’s figures, to 50,026.46; another account puts the gain at more than 400 points, or 0.9 percent, and a third at 398 points, or 0.80 percent—the three accounts agree on direction and differ on the last figures, and the desk prints the closing account’s table as its table. The S&P 500 fell 34.72 points, or 0.48 percent, to 7,202.45, from Monday’s close at 7,237.17. The Nasdaq Composite fell 1.5 percent in the market account and 1.48 percent in another; the Nasdaq 100 fell 1.98 percent to 26,216.64. Retailers helped the Dow ahead of Black Friday and Cyber Monday, in the accounts filed below. The chip stocks fell together: Micron down 11.0 percent to $629.79 in the movers account, Intel among the most active names down, and Nvidia itself down 5.86 percent to $155.38 on volume of 379.27 million shares in the closing account. One account puts Nvidia’s fall at 8 percent; the price it prints in the other accounts, $155.38 against Monday’s $165.05, is a fall of 5.86 percent, and the desk follows the prices. Monday’s gain is not erased: at $155.38 the stock stands $29.85 above its Friday level before the reinstatement, on the figures in the file. Settled: the close, and its split. Open: Wednesday’s session, with the October PCE report and the Federal Reserve’s Beige Book ahead of Thursday’s Thanksgiving close.

The day’s economic print belongs to an earlier week. The Conference Board’s index rose 11.4 points to 103.3 in November, from 91.9 in October, the highest since January 2025, in the wire account and the Board’s own release, both filed below. The release states its limit in its own lines: the survey closed November 17. The print therefore measures the country the week after the midterms, before the fall and Monday’s rally. More respondents said jobs were plentiful, at 30.8 percent against 26.2 percent in October, and year-ahead inflation expectations fell to 5.7 percent.

The Responses: Mr. Huang’s December Dates Stand, in Both Parties’ Tellings

The Evening Watch’s remit is the first considered responses, and on the Huang reinstatement they are now a calendar. Congressional committees have confirmed that Mr. Huang’s appearances will go ahead as scheduled, in the schedule account filed below, carrying CBS News: a transcribed interview on December 1 with the House Intelligence Committee, the House Foreign Affairs Committee and the Select Committee on China; Nvidia’s general counsel, Tim Teter, on December 2; and a public hearing before the Senate Banking Committee on December 3, with Mr. Huang as the sole witness, in the Journal’s account filed below. A House Intelligence Committee spokesperson is quoted that the reinstatement “changes nothing.” Representative Jim Himes is quoted: “We expect to see him on Capitol Hill next week.”

The criticism ran in both parties. Three Republican chairmen—Representatives Moolenaar, Crawford and Mast—said the board’s brief investigation does not erase the national security questions, and they remain committed to answers on the smuggling of Nvidia products. Senator Cotton said the controversy is the tip of the iceberg and is not resolved because Mr. Huang has his job back. Senator Warren said the administration needs to explain what input was given and by whom; Senator Schumer said Democrats will use their full power to investigate; Representative Jeffries called the decision indefensible, irresponsible and incomprehensible. President Trump’s words stand in the earlier editions: that he convinced the board. No finding by any committee is in the file. The questions are scheduled; the answers are not yet given.

Poland: A Drone Called Intentional, an AWACS Authorized, and a Renewed Request for American Troops

Poland’s file gained its response on Tuesday. Warsaw renewed its push for a permanent United States military presence, in the diplomatic account filed below, following two incursions in four days. Poland’s chargé d’affaires in Washington, Bogdan Klich, briefed American officials on Tuesday and discussed the permanent deployment question again. The Monday night incident stands as the midday file printed it: the Operational Command said a drone had briefly violated Poland’s airspace; Deputy Defence Minister Zalewski treated the breach as intentional; and Deputy Minister Wziatek said it was a Russian surveillance drone over a populated area, where shooting it down could have caused damage from falling debris, in the wire account carried by Devdiscourse filed below. The shootdown had been authorized and was held. The alliance’s answer stands as authorized on Monday: a forward AWACS deployment, in the deployment account filed below. No Russian statement on the drone is in the file. Settled: Warsaw’s request, restated. Open: Washington’s answer.

The Strait: Two, Three and Four Tankers in Tehran’s Own Counts, No Vessel Named, and Oil Up $2.03 at the Settlement

The Strait file did not settle on Tuesday; it widened. Iran’s Army navy said two oil tankers struck mines and caught fire early on Tuesday east of the Strait of Hormuz, after entering a restricted area with their identification and navigation systems disabled, in the army statement account filed below. The Guards’ navy said a further tanker struck a mine early on Tuesday, bringing its own Tuesday figure to a fourth in the telling carried by the Tasnim-carried account filed below, against the three the Guards announced on Monday, in the Monday account filed below. A maritime roundup in the file counts five claimed over two days, three on Monday and two on Sunday, in the Marine Insight account. No statement in the file names a vessel, gives a crew count, or gives a casualty figure. The British maritime office, UKMTO, in the same accounts, links the week’s tanker incidents near Khasab to unidentified projectiles, not mines, and records crews accounted for and vessels continuing their transits in its delayed report of three vessels struck on Monday. United States Central Command had not responded to the Iranian claims in the accounts in the file, and no account in the file confirms a mine strike from a source outside Iran’s own services. Army Commander-in-Chief Amir Hatami’s words stand as printed at midday: the waterway will be more closed day by day, and it will not reopen until Iran’s seven conditions are met.

Oil traded the disagreement and settled higher. Brent touched $88.10 in the morning, its lowest since August, on rising supply, and settled at $91.40, up $2.03, or 2.27 percent; WTI touched $79.07 and settled at $82.05, up $1.88, or 2.35 percent, in the settlement account and the Journal’s futures file, both filed below. Wednesday brings the inventory figures, expected to show a third consecutive weekly increase in crude stocks.

Ukraine: Dymer’s Toll at Twelve, and a Night of 96 Drones in the Air Force’s Count

Dymer’s count changed in a hospital. The woman of seventy-nine, Valentyna in the earlier accounts, who was pulled alive from the rubble of the five-story building on Monday after about twenty-two hours, died in hospital, the Kyiv Regional Military Administration reported at 0806 on Tuesday, and the toll from the strike of November 22 stands at twelve, in the updated accounts filed below. The search ended on Monday with eleven dead, including a child of eleven, and a boy of seven pulled alive; those figures stand in the Monday account filed below, and the twelfth death is Tuesday’s addition to them, stated here in the open as the log requires. President Zelensky’s call, in the Monday account, was for tougher sanctions and export controls on the foreign-made engines and chips used in Shahed drones. The night file adds a new count without closing the old one: Ukraine’s air force reported an attack overnight into November 24 involving 96 drones, with hits at 13 locations and debris at two, and six people injured in Zhytomyr Oblast, in the Reflect account filed below. No Russian statement on Dymer is in the evening file.

Alaska: Certified at 50.14 Percent, Conceded on Tuesday, and the Senate at 56 Seats in the Accounts

The last Senate race of the 2026 midterms closed on Tuesday in the ordinary way: a certification, then a concession. Alaska certified its results on Monday, two days ahead of its target date in the accounts filed below. The state’s official summary, dated November 23, lists all 401 precincts reported and gives Mary Peltola 144,583 votes, 50.14 percent, against 132,022, 45.78 percent, for Senator Dan S. Sullivan; Gerald L. Heikes took 6,107 votes and Daniel J. Sullivan Jr. 4,767, a second Sullivan on the ballot whose votes, at 1.65 percent, are smaller than Ms. Peltola’s margin of 12,561. Because she cleared 50 percent of first choices, no ranked-choice tabulation was required. On Tuesday Mr. Sullivan conceded, in the words carried by the Juneau account filed below: he accepts the results certified by the Division of Elections. The Department of Justice, which had sued over the certification, said in a court filing that its lawsuit is now moot, in the concession account filed below, which counts the result as giving the Democrats 56 seats in the Senate that convenes on January 3. What was “Still Uncalled” on November 4 is now called, certified and conceded in full.

The Cocked Hat

Three bearings. On the close, the accounts agree on the split and on Nvidia’s price and differ in the last decimal of the Dow’s gain; one account’s 8 percent for Nvidia does not square with the $155.38 price the others print, and the desk followed the prices. On the strait, two, three, four and five tankers are all in print, with no name, no crew and no outside confirmation, against a British office that records projectiles and ships that continued their voyages; the desk prints the counts as claimed, and the disagreement as the fact. On confidence, the number is one number everywhere—103.3—and its date is its limit: a survey that closed November 17 cannot measure a rally that began November 23.
