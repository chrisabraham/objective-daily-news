Congress is pushing back against President Trump’s intervention with Nvidia’s board, demanding the newly reinstated chief executive Jensen Huang answer questions before lawmakers. Mr. Huang is due before three House committees for closed-door questioning on December 1, and before Senator Tim Scott’s Banking Committee on December 3. Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer pledged to investigate, writing that President Trump ‘is putting his own financial interests and the needs of his billionaire buddies before our national security,’ and signalled that next week’s hearings will not be the end of it. Incoming House Speaker Hakeem Jeffries called Mr. Huang’s reinstatement ‘indefensible, irresponsible and incomprehensible,’ and promised Congress ‘will not be deterred’ from a ‘swift, thorough and wide-ranging investigation.’ California Attorney General Rob Bonta said Mr. Huang’s comeback changes nothing about his intent to probe whether Nvidia misled investors about its ties to China. Asked on CNBC on Monday whether he had promised Nvidia’s board that Mr. Huang would not be prosecuted, President Trump answered: ‘I gave them enough assurance.’ A House Foreign Affairs Committee spokesperson said Mr. Huang’s appearance remains on the calendar.

CNN—Inside the Trump pressure campaign that restored Jensen Huang as Nvidia’s CEO

President Trump had been stewing for days over the stock market’s deepening sell-off when he decided there was only one way to stop the slide: put Jensen Huang back atop Nvidia. His conclusion late last week sparked a West Wing pressure campaign to convince the company to reinstate its chief executive just 11 days after he resigned under pressure, several people familiar with the matter told CNN. In a flurry of calls over the weekend, top officials led by Vice President JD Vance privately leaned on Nvidia’s board to bring back Mr. Huang, and assured the company he posed no national security risk, multiple sources said. President Trump called the firm’s board directly to personally vouch for Mr. Huang. ‘I’ve been speaking to the board directly and indirectly, and I felt very strongly about it,’ President Trump said Monday on CNBC. ‘I think we have one of our great stars back in play.’ The bid was so secretive that even some senior Nvidia staffers were not aware until shortly before the company announced it on Monday morning, a source said. President Trump initially floated former Apple chief executive Tim Cook taking over Nvidia as a way to restore the market’s confidence, to no avail, in the same account.

NBC Palm Springs, carrying the agency file—FAA Pauses New Juniper Router Installations After Nationwide Ground Stop

The Federal Aviation Administration is pausing the installation of new Juniper telecommunications routers as it investigates the nationwide airline ground stop. An FAA spokesperson confirmed the pause as investigators work to determine whether the communications failure was caused by defective equipment or an installation or configuration error. According to Democratic senators on the committee overseeing aviation, FAA contractor L3Harris plans to install 3,648 Juniper routers; about 400 have been installed so far, but only 69 are operational. The senators called for an immediate halt to new installations, questioning whether the equipment was adequately tested and why backup systems failed to prevent the widespread disruption. The FAA has not identified a definitive cause of the outage, which affected communications and radar feeds at 17 of the nation’s 22 en route air traffic control centers. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy previously said upgrade work on an aging telecommunications system could have contributed to the problem. HPE, the parent company of router manufacturer Juniper Networks, told the Wall Street Journal its equipment was operating as configured. L3Harris says it is working with the FAA on the investigation. The pause will remain in place as investigators work to determine what caused the failure.

Terminair—Senators ask FAA to pause Juniper router installs after nationwide ground stop

The senators’ request would hold the router conversion at 400 installed devices until the FAA shows the new equipment is ready, and the cause is still unidentified. The FAA ordered a nationwide ground stop on Friday, November 13, that lasted more than four hours; about 3,500 flights were grounded, and the stop was the longest nationwide ground stop in 25 years in one account. The FAA said its legacy equipment became unstable when data flooded the FAA Telecommunications Infrastructure network; it named sites in Amarillo, Texas, and El Dorado, Arkansas, as the origin of the excess data, and said the instability affected 17 of its 22 Air Route Traffic Control Centers. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said a contractor was replacing copper telecommunications lines with fiber at a remote location when the router was switched on. The FAA said L3Harris blocked the two locations and the network stabilised, and that the FAA disabled the broadcasting function on all new routers as a precaution. Senate Commerce Committee Democrats, led by Ranking Member Maria Cantwell, wrote to Secretary Duffy and Administrator Bedford demanding a clear account of the root cause; on their understanding from L3Harris, the conversion involves 3,648 routers to Juniper devices, 400 installed and 69 operational. They asked for an immediate pause on deploying more Juniper devices, resuming only after the FAA shows improved operational testing, independent validation, verified failover capability and rollback procedures, and said Congress expects a briefing before installations resume.
