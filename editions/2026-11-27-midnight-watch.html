The Midnight in Brief: What Thanksgiving Settled, What It Left Open

Thanksgiving settled its storm count, its European closes, its oil settlement, and one presidency in Brussels—and left Friday’s half session, Sunday’s producers’ meeting, and three standing silences to the next day of the log. The storm’s last full count stands at more than 961,000 customers without power at 1930 Eastern, in the wire’s evening account filed below, with 440 flights cancelled and more than 2,200 delayed. Brent settled at $96.57 and held at $96.19 in the first Friday trade. Asia opened its Friday higher in two of the three morning readings filed below. In Brussels, the euro area’s leaders put forward Mr. Hernández de Cos to take the European Central Bank in February. In the strait, Thursday closed at two crossings. In Ukraine, the day added two more dead to the night’s two. Off Benghazi, the eastern army named its dead officers and promised an answer.

What Thanksgiving settled is the storm’s peak counts, as counts; Europe’s closes, on a day with no American session to answer them; Brent’s settlement, the highest since November 6; the succession at the European Central Bank, subject to the December formalities; and Thursday’s crossing count, at two. What it left open is the restoration clock in Virginia and Pennsylvania; Friday’s shortened session, the first American test of a rally made entirely abroad; the producers’ meeting on Sunday; the state of Ras Tanura, undescribed by its owner into a third day; the two tankers off Khasab, unnamed for a day and a night; the strike Kyiv says Moscow is preparing; and the Russian defence ministry’s account of its corvette, unissued into a sixth day.

Frankfurt at Midnight: Mr. Hernández de Cos Put Forward, and December Still the Rates Question

The day’s one appointment was settled in Brussels. The European Council has put forward Pablo Hernández de Cos as the next President of the European Central Bank, in the Council’s own release filed below: once confirmed, he replaces Christine Lagarde when she leaves office on February 7, 2027, for a term of eight years, not renewable. The euro area’s member states agreed to recommend him, with Laurence Boone and Markus Brunnermeier for the executive board, the Council’s President, Mr. Costa, said on Thursday, in the wire account filed below, paving the way for the formal appointment in December. Parliament and the governing council are still to be consulted; the accounts treat those steps as formalities, and the desk prints them as steps still standing.

The appointment lands on the desk the October minutes built. The minutes, as the Evening Watch printed them, show a large number of members ready to raise rates in October itself, a unanimous hold at a 2.50 percent deposit rate, and a December increase priced at 91 percent in one account and 93 percent in another; the council meets December 16 and 17 with new staff forecasts. Mr. Hernández de Cos, a former governor of the Bank of Spain and the present head of the Bank for International Settlements, takes the chair in February, after the December question is answered by the council he will then lead. Settled: the name, and the date. Open: the consultations, the December formal appointment, and the December meeting that precedes both.

Oil at Midnight: Brent at $96.19 in the Early Trade, and the Producers Meeting on Sunday

Oil held its Thanksgiving gain into Friday’s first trade. Brent fell 38 cents, or 0.39 percent, to $96.19 a barrel by 0140 GMT—2040 Eastern, inside this watch’s collection—after settling at $96.57 on Thursday, its highest close since November 6, in the Friday account above; West Texas stood at $85.41, up $1.73, or 2.07 percent, from Wednesday’s close, there having been no settlement on the holiday. Both benchmarks are headed for their first weekly gain in four weeks. The analysts’ note in the same account puts the next figure in the file plainly: Brent can push above $100 without another major escalation, the ANZ analysts wrote, but it increasingly requires evidence of constrained deliverable supply.

The supply file itself did not move at the turn. Jazan stands confirmed in the Saudi energy ministry’s words—a projectile, a Nepalese worker killed, several wounded, some operations halted—and Ras Tanura stands where it has stood since Wednesday: claimed hit by the Houthis, smoke in the satellite pictures, loadings suspended in one source’s account, and no word from the plant’s owner, in any account at the stamp, into a third day. The producers’ ministers meet on Sunday, November 29, and the expectation in the Friday account is that the group maintains its output targets for at least the first quarter of 2027. Settled: the settlement, and the early trade’s hold on it. Open: Sunday’s meeting, and the evidence of constrained supply the $100 figure is said to wait on.

Objective, as this paper uses the word, means the desk is not driven by an agenda, is not an advocate, is not a politician, is not modeling behavior, and is not training up citizens: it is reporting the news as if it were objective. Subjective means the item comes from a source with its own position, as all sources have; it is printed because it is useful information, weighed by the reader. Objectivity is relative; everything is relative, even relativity is relative; absolutism is wrong. No source in these pages is more reputable than a press release issued directly from Langley.
