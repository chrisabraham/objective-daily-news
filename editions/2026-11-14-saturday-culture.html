Paris Counts Photography at 200, Tokyo Opens 6,500 Booths, and Thailand Lights Its Rivers

A curated fair in Paris and an open fair in Tokyo stand at the two ends of the same Saturday: one counts its exhibitors in hundreds and its medium in centuries, the other counts its booths in thousands and admits its artists without a jury. Shanghai closes, Tallinn continues, Jeddah closes, Mexico City opens, two fashion weeks peak on Sunday, Abu Dhabi and Tokyo prepare firsts for next week, and Thailand begins a fortnight of floating lights. The desk files the week in that order.

By Eunice, the desk · Collected and analyzed at 0410 ET, Saturday, November 14, 2026. The second Saturday Culture Feature of this paper, filed ahead of the day's watches under the Weekend Features ruling of November 4, 2026. International by house rule: no country's framework is applied to another country's culture. The clippings the report was drawn from are filed beneath it, each stamped subjective.

The Week in Brief

The week's cultural centre, measured by what is open this Saturday, is divided between two fairs that could not be built more differently: Paris Photo, in its 29th edition at the Grand Palais, where 242 exhibitors sell and show a medium that turns 200 this year, and Tokyo's Design Festa, in its 64th volume at Tokyo Big Sight, where more than 6,500 booths of original work are open to anyone, professional or amateur, with no jury at the door. Around those two poles the week is full and dated: Shanghai's West Bund Art and Design closes on Sunday, November 15; Tallinn's Black Nights festival is in its middle days, running to November 22 with 489 titles; Jeddah's Wahid exhibition, with more than 60 Saudi and Pakistani artists, closes today; Mexico City's Museo Tamayo opens the Sao Paulo Bienal's travelling exhibition today, to the public on Sunday; two fashion weeks, in The Gambia and in Dubai, hold their main events on Sunday; Abu Dhabi counts five days to the first Frieze fair in the emirate, on November 19; Tokyo counts six days to a new museum's first exhibition, on November 20; and Thailand's Loi Krathong calendar opens on Sunday at Sukhothai, inside a national calendar running November 15 to 29 with the full moon on November 24.

Paris: A Medium Turns 200 Under the Glass Roof

Paris Photo opened on November 12 and runs to Sunday, November 15, and its subject this year is its own age. The fair is in its 29th edition at the Grand Palais, with 242 exhibitors, 195 galleries and 47 publishers from 38 countries, in the fair account filed below. Its programme is built around the Bicentenary of Photography. The Centre Pompidou brings more than 100 works from its collection by nearly 30 artists, arranged in three parts, Making the Image, At Infinity, and Identities. Thomas Ruff is presented in a retrospective hang made with five galleries. The Prix Pictet, founded in 2008, is shown for the first time in the nave. The Main sector holds 150 galleries, and the geography of the stands runs from Bogota and Mexico City to Makati City and Casablanca, in the same account.

The fair's sections divide the medium by its forms, and the oldest forms stand beside the newest. Voices, curated under the title Disappearance, rests on the paradox its curators state in the account: a tool made to preserve what might disappear, celebrated at a time when its traditional form is unstable. Digital, curated by Marco de Mutiis, holds the medium's origins as a technology beside the forms expected to shape seeing next. Emergence carries twenty projects from galleries backing new artists. In the nave, the earliest processes are on the stands themselves: camera clara drawings, photogenic drawings, calotype negatives, daguerreotypes and photograms by the medium's first practitioners, among them Daguerre, Talbot, Girault de Prangey, Anna Atkins and Hippolyte Fizeau, in the same account. A second account filed below gives the fair's scale in rounder numbers, more than 220 exhibitors from over thirty countries, names the Elles x Paris Photo trail for women artists among its sections, and records Ruff's large-scale installation at the entrance. The two counts stand apart in the Cocked Hat.

Tokyo: 6,500 Booths, No Jury, and a Museum That Opens Next Week

Design Festa vol. 64 opens today at Tokyo Big Sight and runs through Sunday, and its rule is admission. The event, born in 1994, is open to anyone with original works, professional or amateur, in the event account filed below. More than 10,000 artists gather at every event. This edition fills the West and South Halls with over 6,500 booths, from 10:00 a.m. to 6:00 p.m. both days, with an expected attendance of approximately 140,000 across the two days. Its floor is divided by kind: bright booths and dark booths, live painting completed across the two days in view of the visitors, workshops, a show stage, a food area, and the One Coin Runway, where a visitor takes the stage for 500 yen. A one-day ticket is 800 yen in advance and 1,000 yen on the day. Where Paris selects, Tokyo admits; the two fairs are the week's clearest pair, and the desk prints them as a pair.

Tokyo's second item is an opening that has not happened, announced on November 11 with its dates fixed. MoN Takanawa, the Museum of Narratives in Minato, will present Feel the Sound from November 20, 2026, to March 22, 2027, as part of its opening commemorative programme, produced in collaboration with the Barbican Centre in London, in the museum's announcement filed below. The exhibition is built of installations combining sound, vibration, music and technology, presented as feeling sound with the whole body. This presentation is its first run in Japan, after its original run at the Barbican. A media preview stands on November 19, with tours led by its curator, Luke Kemp. The museum is operated by the East Japan Railway Foundation for Cultural Innovations.

Shanghai Closes, Abu Dhabi Prepares: The Fair Calendar Moves West

West Bund Art and Design closes on Sunday in Shanghai, in the last days of Shanghai Art Week. The fair runs from November 10 to 15 at the West Bund Art Center in Xuhui District. One gallery's account filed below lists its own stand, 1C05, and the artists it carries: Tunji Adeniyi-Jones, Georg Baselitz, Tracey Emin, Mona Hatoum, Damien Hirst, Marguerite Humeau, Yoko Matsumoto, Shigeo Otake, Marina Rheingantz and Tesfaye Urgessa among them, with Antony Gormley, Ibrahim Mahama, Park Seo-Bo, Isamu Noguchi, Liu Wei, Michael Armitage and Etel Adnan also on its list. A single gallery's list is the bearing the desk has for Shanghai this week, and it is printed at that size: it establishes dates, place and one stand's contents, and nothing about the fair's whole.

Abu Dhabi's first Frieze fair opens on November 19, four days after Shanghai closes, and its line-up was published in full on October 22. The inaugural Frieze Abu Dhabi runs from November 19 to 22 at Manarat Al Saadiyat, with more than 85 galleries from 67 cities and more than 35 countries and regions, in the fair's account filed below. It joins a network that already runs in London, Los Angeles, New York, Chicago and Seoul. It builds on Abu Dhabi Art's seventeen years in the region, is led by Director Dyala Nusseibeh, and stands among the Louvre Abu Dhabi, the Zayed National Museum and the forthcoming Guggenheim Abu Dhabi. Its main Galleries section holds more than 60 galleries from 56 cities and 32 countries. Frieze Masters Abu Dhabi, selected by Marika Sardar, takes the Indian Ocean as its theme, from antiquity to the early 20th century. Global Futures, selected by Tarini Malik, carries solo presentations. The regional anchoring is named plainly in the account: galleries from the Emirates, Lebanon, Saudi Arabia, Tehran and Istanbul, beside 20 European galleries.

Tallinn: 489 Titles in the Middle Days

The Tallinn Black Nights Film Festival is the week's longest event still running, and its arithmetic checks. The festival runs 17 days, from November 6 to 22, in its 30th anniversary edition, with 489 titles from 87 countries and regions: 230 features and 259 shorts, which sum to the stated total, in the review file filed below, published on November 13. Its opening film, Ulya, by Latvian director Viesturs Kairiss, is a production of all three Baltic countries, drawn from the life of basketball player Uljana Semjonova. Its two competitions carry 24 films and 21 premieres. The review names the programme's prominent themes as war, displacement, identity, and intergenerational conflict and trauma. The same review prints its own judgment on the festival's standing, largest in scale among the smaller A-list festivals and lowest among them in prestige, and the desk files that judgment as the review's, in the clipping, where a judgment belongs.

Fashion: Two Main Events on One Sunday

Gambia International Fashion Week has been running since November 10, and its main event is Sunday, November 15. The week, in its 4th edition, was founded in 2023 as a non-profit platform for Gambian and African designers, models, creatives and young talent, in the week's account filed below. Its 2026 theme is Roots to Runway, Luxury Born in Gambia, with a stated focus on sustainability, recycling and Made in Gambia. The main event stands at the Sir Dawda Kairaba Jawara International Conference Centre at Bijilo, bringing Gambian and international designers together. Around it, the week carries showcases, cultural experiences, industry conversations and sustainable fashion initiatives to November 17.

Dubai's fashion event is one day long, and it stands on the same Sunday. Dubai Sustainable Fashion Week takes place on November 15 at Atlantis, The Palm, as an initiative of Grupo Zostenible, in the event's own account filed below. Its day is set out in order: morning welcome and networking, midday talks on sustainable fashion and innovation, afternoon designer presentations, evening runway shows, an evening industry panel, and a night media and VIP experience. Its five stated dimensions are runway shows, innovation, global networking, culture and art, and responsible luxury. The same account marks its programme provisional and its full programme as coming soon, with the running order stated in general terms; the desk prints both facts, the date and the provisional programme, at their sizes.

Jeddah Closes Today, Mexico City Opens Today

Wahid closes today in Jeddah, after three days in a consulate. The joint Saudi-Pakistani exhibition opened on November 12 at the Consulate General of Pakistan in Jeddah, with more than 60 artists from both countries, under the theme One Spirit, Shared Heritage, Diverse Expressions, in the exhibition account filed below, published today. Wahid means One. Its programme is visual artworks and traditional Arabic calligraphy; its contributors include Pakistan's Ambassador to Saudi Arabia, Ahmad Farooq, and calligrapher Shafiq-uz-Zaman. Officials of both countries spoke at its opening, and attendance across its three days was by prior registration.

Mexico City's opening today is a travelling exhibition at its last stop. The travelling exhibition of the 36th Bienal de Sao Paulo, Not All Travellers Walk Roads, Of Humanity as Practice, opens at the Museo Tamayo today, November 14, and runs to the public from November 15 to February 7, 2027, with works by 26 artists, curated by Anna Roberta Goetz, in the Bienal's own notice filed below. It is the first time the Bienal's travelling programme has come to Mexico, and the last stop of this edition's tour. The notice stands carried over from last week's file, where the desk filed it ahead of the opening; today the date it named has arrived.

Thailand: A Fortnight of Lights Opens on Sunday

Loi Krathong's national calendar opens on November 15 and runs to November 29, and its centre is a date still ten days off. The festival is observed on the full moon of the twelfth Thai lunar month, which falls on Tuesday, November 24 this year, in the festival account filed below, checked on November 7 against the Tourism Authority of Thailand's calendar and the Bangkok Metropolitan Administration's notices. Decorated baskets are floated as a gesture of gratitude for water; in the north, Yi Peng sky lanterns are released, a separate ritual on the same nights. The fortnight is a map of dated programmes: Sukhothai Historical Park, from November 15 to 24, with candlelight and fireworks in the ancient capital; Bangkok's clusters at Rama VIII Bridge, Phra Sumen Fort, Maha Kan Fort and the Prem Prachakon Canal, with a historical route running from November 17 to 29; Phuket's Saphan Hin, from November 16 to 25; Chiang Saen's Yi Peng on the Mekong, from November 20 to 24; Tak's Loi Krathong Sai, 1,000 floating krathongs, from November 21 to 24; and Chiang Mai's CAD KhomLoi on November 24 and 25. Peak evenings in most cities are November 23 to 25. Saturday therefore stands at the edge of the festival: nothing floats tonight in the national programme, and everything floats within fifteen days.

The Cocked Hat

Three bearings never give a point; they give a small triangle, and this paper prints the triangle. First, Paris Photo's size: 242 exhibitors, 195 galleries and 47 publishers from 38 countries in the fair account, against more than 220 exhibitors from over thirty countries in the second account. The two statements are compatible, since the larger count satisfies the smaller one's floors, and the desk prints the exact count as the fair account's and the floor as the second account's, without merging them. Second, Tokyo's attendance: the 140,000 figure for Design Festa is the event account's expected attendance, published before the doors opened today; it is a published scale, not a counted crowd, and no counted figure can stand in the file until the event has run. Third, Dubai's programme: a full day's running order stands published for November 15, and the same account calls that programme provisional and its full programme still to come; the date is fixed and the contents are general, and both facts stand. Against the triangle, the load-bearing facts are single-valued: Paris Photo runs November 12 to 15 in its 29th edition; Design Festa vol. 64 runs November 14 and 15 with over 6,500 booths; West Bund runs November 10 to 15; Tallinn runs November 6 to 22 with 230 features and 259 shorts summing to its stated 489 titles; Wahid ran November 12 to 14 with more than 60 artists; the Bienal's travelling exhibition opens at the Tamayo today and to the public on Sunday; the Gambia and Dubai fashion main events stand on Sunday, November 15; Frieze Abu Dhabi opens on November 19; Feel the Sound opens in Tokyo on November 20; and Loi Krathong's full moon is November 24, inside a national calendar running November 15 to 29.
