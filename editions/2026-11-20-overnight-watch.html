Kyiv at 0400: The Answer Promised in Shenzhen Arrives on the Bridges

The response President Putin promised in Shenzhen arrived before dawn in Kyiv, and it arrived on the bridges. Explosions began shortly after 01:25 local time on Friday, minutes after Ukraine's air force warned of ballistic missiles heading for the capital, and waves of missiles and drones followed until after 06:30, in the night account filed below. Russia's Defence Ministry said it carried out a “massive strike” with land-, sea- and air-launched precision weapons and drones, in the same account, and in a post to Telegram said it targeted Ukrainian military and energy facilities, port infrastructure in the Odesa region, and transport infrastructure in Kyiv, in the strike account above. The strikes lasted more than six hours, in the strike account.

Three bridges over the Dnipro were damaged, according to Mayor Vitali Klitschko, in the strike account: the Podilsko-Voskresenskyi bridge, where traffic from the right bank to the left bank is closed; the Darnytskyi bridge, damaged and under assessment; and the Southern bridge, hit again and limited to vehicles up to 10 tonnes and public transport up to 18 tonnes, in the barrage account filed below. A critical infrastructure facility was damaged, in the same account, and parts of the capital lost power, with emergency outages reported across Kyiv, in the barrage account; the state electricity operator called the situation in the power grid “extremely critical,” in the strike account, and emergency outages were put in place in the Lviv, Zakarpattia and Ivano-Frankivsk regions. Fires broke out in the Obolonskyi, Podilskyi and Holosiivskyi districts, in the night account; falling debris set a three-storey building alight in Obolonskyi, and an office building on the grounds of an infrastructure facility caught fire in Holosiivskyi.

The dead stand at five in the strike account's count and at three in Kyiv in the barrage account's, with the difference printed in the Cocked Hat: three killed in Kyiv and two in the western city of Lviv, in the strike account's breakdown, against three killed and about 15 injured in Kyiv in the barrage account; three or more workers from the city's water company were among the dead, in the payback account filed below. Kyiv residents were advised to avoid driving and businesses told to ask employees to work from home on Friday, in the bridges account filed below, as the attack continued into the morning. The strike follows Thursday's daytime drone attack on Kyiv, which killed one man and damaged the Dutch and Austrian embassies and St. Volodymyr's Cathedral, as the midnight file carried it, and it follows the third Ukrainian strike on the Kerch Bridge in four days, after which Mr. Putin said there would be no agreement on a ceasefire or on not hitting energy infrastructure, and that Russia's response would be “much more painful” than what Kyiv had done. A Russian account of the night claims a fourth crossing, the Northern bridge, and the Paton bridge, were also hit, in the Russian account filed below; the desk prints the disagreement in the Cocked Hat. Settled: the strike, the three bridges named by the mayor, and the dead. Open: the full count, the bridges' repair, and what the promised response strikes next.

Reuters—Japan's core inflation accelerates in October, cementing rate hike expectations

Japan's core consumer prices rose 2.2 percent in October from a year earlier, data showed on Friday, accelerating slightly from September in a sign of broadening price pressure that cements the case for a near-term interest rate hike by the central bank. The data will be among factors the Bank of Japan will scrutinise at its next two-day policy meeting ending on December 18, when many market players expect the central bank to hike its policy rate to 1.5 percent from the current 1.25 percent. The increase in the core consumer price index, which strips away the effect of volatile fresh food, compared with a median market forecast for a 2.3 percent rise and followed a 2.1 percent gain in September. An index that strips away the effects of both volatile fresh food and fuel, which is closely watched by the Bank of Japan as a better gauge of demand-driven price pressures, also rose 2.2 percent in October from a year earlier after increasing 2.4 percent in September.

USA Today—Trump gives a posthumous pardon to baseball hit leader Pete Rose

President Donald Trump gave a posthumous pardon to baseball icon Pete Rose, the sport's hit leader who was convicted of cheating on his taxes and kept out of the National Baseball Hall of Fame. “I have just given a Complete and Total Pardon to the Late, Great Pete Rose, a true Baseball SUPERSTAR & LEGEND, whose record of 4,256 Hits in a Season will never be broken,” Trump said in a social media post. Rose, baseball's career hits leader at 4,256, entered guilty pleas on April 20, 1990, to two counts of filing false tax returns, admitting he failed to report $354,968 during a four-year period. MLB commissioner Rob Manfred posthumously removed Rose from the ineligible list in May 2025 and later acknowledged that Trump urging him to do so in an April meeting at the White House influenced his decision. Rose is now eligible for Hall of Fame consideration. As a player whose contributions largely came before 1980, he will be under consideration by the 12-person Classic Baseball Committee. The group will next vote in December 2027 for induction in July 2028.
