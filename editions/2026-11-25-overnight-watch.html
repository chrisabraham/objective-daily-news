The Night in Brief: A Rally Returned, a Claim Widened, a Count Raised

The night’s largest movement ran backward. Asia, which leapt on Tuesday on Mr. Huang’s return to Nvidia—Seoul up about seven percent, Taipei up nine in the Overnight Watch’s file of that morning—gave the move back on Wednesday, in the wire account filed below and in the Dow Jones account filed below, and the two accounts do not agree on the size of the fall. Against the reversal stand two settled items: Dell Technologies rose 4.1 percent in after-hours trade in the wire account, on the record quarter it filed after Tuesday’s bell, and Wednesday’s American calendar is the fullest of the shortened week—the core PCE price index, durable goods, revised third-quarter product and jobless claims at 0830 Eastern, new-home sales at 1000 and the Beige Book at 1400, ahead of Thursday’s Thanksgiving close. In the Gulf, the Houthis claimed on Wednesday what Saudi attribution had assigned them on Tuesday, and added a refinery to the claim. In Ukraine, the air force counted 112 drones in the night. In the strait, Tehran counted two more tankers. In Moscow, the defence ministry counted nothing at all, into a fifth day.

What the night settled is Dell’s direction after hours, in the wire’s reading; the hearing dates for the ground stop, December 1 and December 2; Ukraine’s count of the night’s drones, as the air force files it; and the strait’s Tuesday, corrected by its own counter to four crossings. What it left open is the tariff file’s contents and timing; Wednesday’s inflation print and the session that trades on it; the fires the Houthis claim at Ras Tanura; the tankers Tehran names by number and never by name; and the ministry’s word on the Merkury.

Two causes are named in the accounts, and the desk prints both as the accounts give them. The second is a file reopened in Washington: the Trade Representative’s office is preparing to release the findings of a months-long investigation into alleged excess industrial capacity and industrial subsidies among 16 major trading partners, including China and the European Union, three people with knowledge of the matter told the wire, and the findings could pave the way for new tariff measures. No findings have been released at the stamp, and no rate is named in any account in the file. Settled: the selling, and the file’s existence as reported. Open: the findings, their date, and their numbers.

Riyadh at Dawn: The Claim Arrives, and It Is Larger Than the Strike

At midnight the Riyadh file stood on Saudi attribution: the authorities assigned Tuesday’s second strike on the Public Investment Fund tower to Yemen’s Houthi fighters, the coalition promised a decisive response, and no claim had been issued by 1600 GMT. In the night the claim arrived, and it is larger than the strike it answers for. The Houthis said on Wednesday they had attacked the Ras Tanura oil refinery and Riyadh’s financial district, in the claim account filed below; their spokesman, Yahya Saree, said the refinery attack caused fires and that the financial district was attacked in three phases. The coalition’s Wednesday word is narrower and different in kind: air defences intercepted and destroyed four drones over Ras Tanura, in the Eastern Province. No Saudi account in the file confirms a fire at the refinery, and no account in the file describes damage at Ras Tanura beyond the interceptions the coalition reports.

Tuesday’s strike itself stands as the midnight file printed it: 11 wounded in the second hit in less than a week on the capital’s tallest tower, two Saudis, two Indians, three Nepalese and four Egyptians, in the casualty account filed below, with the wire noting it could not determine when the Civil Defence photograph was taken or the full extent of the damage. What the night changes is the shape of the exchange: a tower struck twice now stands beside a refinery claimed struck and defended in the same Wednesday statements, and the response the coalition promised on Tuesday has not been described in any account in the file at the stamp. Settled: the claim, as a claim, and the four interceptions, as the coalition files them. Open: the refinery’s state, and the response.

The Strait at Dawn: Two More Tankers by Tehran’s Count, None by the Counter’s

Tehran’s count rose again in the night, on the same terms as every rise before it. Two oil tankers exploded and caught fire after striking naval mines while attempting an unauthorized route, off the southern and eastern coasts of the strait, in the Guards’ Navy statement carried in the Tehran account filed below and in the second form of the statement filed below; the tankers had their navigation and positioning systems switched off, the statement says, and the warning to ships sailing dark was repeated. The same statement’s frame reaches backward: four other tankers exploded and caught fire in the preceding hours, which is Tuesday’s four as Tehran announced them. No vessel is named, no crew is accounted for, and Central Command has filed no answer at the stamp.

The visible counter’s night moved in the opposite direction: it corrected its own Tuesday. Four commercial ships crossed on November 24, one inbound and three outbound, one of them a tanker, the last the general cargo ship Palau inbound at 2127 UTC, in the strait count filed below—one more crossing than the three the Midnight Watch printed from the same counter, the correction stated here in the open. The seven days stand at 49 crossings, about 8 percent of the normal rate, and on Wednesday no ship had crossed by 0637 UTC, which is 0137 Eastern, inside this watch’s collection. The second instrument’s wider count, 27 ships in the 24 hours to 0001 UTC, stands as the midnight file printed it. Settled: Tuesday’s four, in the one instrument, and Wednesday’s two, in Tehran’s statements as statements. Open, as it has been open all week: the tankers themselves.

The Benghazi file’s night adds a legal sentence and nothing from the ministry the Kremlin named. Ukraine’s Foreign Ministry spokesman, Heorhii Tykhyi, said on November 24 that the strikes on the Merkury and the second ship were an exercise of Ukraine’s right of self-defence under Article 51 of the United Nations Charter, and that Russian warships, cargo ships carrying military equipment and shadow-fleet tankers are legitimate targets, in the Benghazi account filed below. The same account records the eastern command’s word that two Libyan naval personnel died on November 24 of wounds from the November 22 attack, and the eastern government’s head naming the attack in his own terms. The imagery account, in the basing account filed below, stands as it stood: satellite imagery shows the corvette capsized with only her forward bow above water, where she had stopped to refuel and resupply under the cooperation agreement while escorting Russian cargo ships. Russia’s defence ministry had issued no statement on the Merkury by the time of the account’s November 25 update—not a confirmation, not a denial, not a list of her crew—and the silence the Midnight Watch counted into a fourth day now runs into its fifth. Settled: Kyiv’s legal position, stated in its spokesman’s words. Open: the ministry’s word, the crew’s full fate, and the joint inquiry’s answer.

Objective, as this paper uses the word, means the desk is not driven by an agenda, is not an advocate, is not a politician, is not modeling behavior, and is not training up citizens: it is reporting the news as if it were objective. Subjective means the item comes from a source with its own position, as all sources have; it is printed because it is useful information, weighed by the reader. Objectivity is relative; everything is relative, even relativity is relative; absolutism is wrong. No source in these pages is more reputable than a press release issued directly from Langley.
