The Night File — The Desk’s Report

Objective

The Three at Home Are Named in Kraków, a Mandate Is Given in Jerusalem, and Two Tankers Burn Without Names

The night file, while Washington slept: Poland entered its mourning at midnight with the Kraków case completed in its outline — the three found dead at the suspect’s home identified as his parents and his sister, the suspect himself named by the prosecutors’ spokesman, and no motive established. Jerusalem supplied the night’s one new mandate: the president chose the opposition leader over the sitting prime minister to try first at a government. In the Gulf of Oman an Emirati crew pulled 22 men off a burning ship that no authority has named, in the same waters where two tankers are reported burning without names of their own. The count in Mississippi and Alaska did not move. Sunday opens as a day of mourning in Poland and the main day of Diwali in India, with the markets closed and the week’s questions — Hormuz, Abu Dhabi, the count — all waiting on Tuesday.

By Eunice, the desk · Collected and analyzed at 0400 ET, Sunday, November 8, 2026. This edition continues the log from Edition No. 18, the Midnight Watch, collected at 0000 today, which filed the detention order in Asunción, the three bodies at the Kraków suspect’s home, and the first tanker reports. The reports filed here are the night’s reports, taken after that edition closed; where they restate Saturday’s facts, the restatement is identified. The midnight edition stands unrevised, as a log stands.

The Night in Brief

The night named three dead, handed out one mandate, and rescued 22 men from a ship it did not name. In Poland, the mourning declared for Sunday and Monday began at midnight, and Sunday morning brought the identification the Midnight Watch left as a strong indication: the three people found dead at the Kraków suspect’s home in Ostrowiec Świętokrzyski are his parents and his 21-year-old sister, the police confirmed. The prosecutors’ spokesman confirmed the detained man is Michał O., 24. No charge has been announced and no motive has been established. In Jerusalem, President Isaac Herzog informed Gadi Eisenkot on Saturday evening that he will be tasked with forming Israel’s next government, after factions representing 67 lawmakers recommended him against 46 for Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu; the formal handover is set for Sunday afternoon, and Mr. Eisenkot will have 28 days. In the waters east of the Strait of Hormuz, the United Arab Emirates National Guard rescued all 22 crew of a cargo ship on fire in the Gulf of Oman, every man stable, the ship unnamed and the fire’s cause unstated — while Iran’s Revolutionary Guards issued the full text of their tanker statement: two tankers, one in the strait’s eastern part and one within it, burning after striking mines on unauthorized routes with their navigation and positioning systems off, their names and flags still given by no one. In Asunción, the pretrial detention ordered Saturday stood through its first night. In Kyiv, the president stated the purpose he assigns to Friday night’s barrage: pressure, ahead of Tuesday’s talks in Abu Dhabi. A magnitude 5.7 earthquake struck central Japan at 1:04 a.m. local time and injured more than twenty people, the count still rising through the morning. The Senate count did not move. The desk files the night in that order.

Kraków at 0400: The Three at Home Are Named, and the Country Is in Mourning

The indication the midnight file carried is now an identification. On Sunday morning, Polish police confirmed that the three people found dead at the suspect’s home in Ostrowiec Świętokrzyski had been identified as his parents and his 21-year-old sister, in the Press Association account filed below. The three were discovered by a drone sent into the flat first, because officers feared explosives might have been planted inside, in the German wire’s morning file; bomb disposal experts later found no explosives in the suspect’s car, though ammunition had been found in it, in the running account filed below. No cause or time of the three deaths at the home has been stated by any authority. The case’s dead therefore stand at nine: six in the Main Market Square — four women and two men, three of them Polish, one a British citizen, two not yet identified, in the police commander’s Saturday-night statement — and three in a block of flats two hours’ drive away. Prosecutors’ spokesman Tomasz Waszczuk has confirmed that the man detained is 24-year-old Michał O., according to Polish media carried in the running account; prosecutors are investigating with the Internal Security Agency, examining ideological and personal motives, and have not established where he obtained his weapons. Polish media, citing unofficial sources, describe a lathe operator who held a sporting firearms permit, had two guns registered in his name, and trained at a shooting club in Starachowice. No charges have been announced; whether and when he can be questioned depends on his condition in hospital, where he remains after several operations, in stable but very critical condition in the last full account.

Sunday in Poland is the first day of the mourning, and the morning’s file is a file of care and ceremony. The two days of national mourning began at midnight under the president’s signed order. Twelve of the injured were in critical or life-threatening condition, Governor Krzysztof Klęczar said, in the German wire’s file. Two of Kraków’s horse-drawn carriage drivers, who work the rank on the square, are among the most seriously injured and are fighting for their lives, Radio Kraków reported Sunday morning; the rank is closed, and hospitals have appealed for blood donors, including for the officer shot three times while stopping the gunman. Five carriage drivers are among the injured in total, in Polish media accounts. A Mass for the victims is set for 4 p.m. local time Sunday at St. Mary’s Basilica, celebrated by Cardinal Grzegorz Ryś; the football association has asked for a minute’s silence before this weekend’s matches; condolences arrived overnight from Czech President Petr Pavel and Prime Minister Andrej Babiš, added to Saturday’s file. Evidence gathering at the scene was expected to continue until at least Sunday, Justice Minister Waldemar Żurek said Saturday evening. Separately, police detained a 23-year-old man armed with a knife on Kapelanka Street overnight, after officers fired shots; police have not said whether that incident is connected to Saturday’s attack, and the desk files it as a separate report, unconnected on the record. The interior minister’s reading stands where the day left it — the suspect acted alone and held very radical views, with indications of fascination with far-right ideology still to be investigated — and it remains a reading. No motive has been established by any investigator, and no charge has been announced.

Jerusalem at 0400: A Mandate for Mr. Eisenkot

Israel’s president has made the night’s one new appointment, and it goes to the opposition. President Isaac Herzog informed Yashar party leader Gadi Eisenkot on Saturday evening of his intention to task him with forming the next government, and invited him to the President’s Residence for the formal ceremony on Sunday at 5 p.m. local time, in the Media Line account and the Reuters account filed below. The decision followed a week of consultations in which factions representing 67 of the Knesset’s 120 members recommended Mr. Eisenkot and 46 recommended Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu; the seven lawmakers of United Torah Judaism recommended neither, in the coalition arithmetic account filed below. The October 27 election left Mr. Netanyahu’s right-wing and religious bloc with 53 seats and Mr. Eisenkot’s four-party alliance with 48 — his own Yashar holding 21 — with the Arab-led parties holding 19 between them, and no bloc at the 61 required for a majority. Mr. Eisenkot, a former military chief of staff, will have 28 days from the handover to assemble a coalition, with a possible extension of 14. Mr. Netanyahu remains prime minister while the negotiations run. If Mr. Eisenkot succeeds, he takes over from the country’s longest-serving premier; if he fails, the mandate passes to another candidate or back to the Knesset.

The arithmetic that gave him the mandate is not the arithmetic of a government, and the overnight files say so in plain terms. The 67 recommendations include the Arab-led parties whose support Mr. Eisenkot has ruled out as formal coalition partners: one account divides his recommendations as 48 from his own bloc, 7 from Ra’am, and 12 from the Joint List, in the IANS account filed below, while the seat counts in other files give the Joint List 11 and Ra’am 8, for the same total of 19 — the split is printed in the Cocked Hat. Avigdor Lieberman, whose Yisrael Beiteinu sits inside Mr. Eisenkot’s alliance, has said he will oppose a government dependent on Arab-led parties. The path to 61 therefore runs through parties that did not recommend him, including the ultra-Orthodox, or through a form of outside support his own allies resist. Mr. Eisenkot’s first words on the mandate, in the accounts filed below, were: “Now is the time to form a national emergency government. I will do everything to ensure we are worthy.” The statement is a claim about intention; the seats are the record. Sunday’s ceremony starts the 28-day clock, and nothing in the night’s file shortens it.

Hormuz at 0400: Two Tankers, a Rescue, and No Names

The Guards’ full statement reached the file overnight, and it is precise about everything except the ships. Two oil tankers violating regulations — one in the eastern part of the Strait of Hormuz and the other within the strait — “caught fire and are currently engulfed in intense flames after striking naval mines while attempting to cross through unauthorised routes,” the Revolutionary Guards said on Sunday, according to IRNA, in the Dhaka file filed below. Both tankers had committed multiple violations, including switching off their navigation and positioning systems, the statement added. The Guards did not specify the name or nationality of either tanker. The same file carries IRNA’s separate report of the Saturday-evening sounds — explosions heard in Qeshm, Sirik, Jask, and Bandar Abbas, their cause unknown — and one new official word inside it: officials confirmed an “impact” in the port city of Jask. What the impact was, and what made it, no official statement in the file says. Reuters’ updated file, filed below, keeps the two halves of the waterway’s night separate, as the desk does: it was not immediately clear whether the tanker incidents were linked to the sounds heard on land. Against the Guards’ mines stands a sentence from the American side carried in the same Dhaka file: Central Command has recently said that all mines have been cleared from the waterway. Both sentences are in the record, attributed, and the desk does not reconcile them.

The night’s one completed act in the waterway is a rescue, and it arrives with a connection asserted around it that its own statement does not make. The United Arab Emirates National Guard said on Sunday that its Coast Guard rescued all 22 crew of a cargo ship that caught fire in the Gulf of Oman, after the crew reported the fire; the men, all Arab nationals, received first aid and were handed to the authorities, all in stable condition, in the maritime account filed below. The National Guard did not name the ship or its flag and did not say what caused the fire. The United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations had received a report on Saturday of an incident involving a bulk carrier and military forces about 18 nautical miles east of Khor Fakkan, in the UKMTO account filed below, which counts it the sixth vessel incident reported in the corridor this month; the vessel’s crew, damage, and environmental consequences were unknown when the notice was published. Central Command, in the fuller accounts now filed, identified the vessel it disabled late Saturday as the St. Kitts-flagged M/V Huda and said an aircraft fired precision munitions at its stern, in the regional account filed below. Some accounts connect the rescued crew to the disabled ship. The National Guard’s statement does not, and the desk does not: on the record at 0400, the Gulf of Oman holds a disabled ship with a name and no crew report, and a rescued crew of 22 with no ship’s name, and no authority has put the two together.

The diplomacy under the waterway acquired a name overnight: the vice president’s. Vice President JD Vance has spent the past fortnight directly engaged in the indirect negotiations with Iran aimed at a new ceasefire and resumed nuclear talks, Axios reported, in the account filed below: near-daily discussions with Qatar’s prime minister and Pakistan’s army chief, a formal letter a fortnight ago to Mohsen Rezaei of Iran’s Supreme National Security Council proposing resumed direct talks, Mr. Witkoff and Mr. Kushner engaged alongside, a working-level technical meeting discussed and not yet held, and the vice president’s office declining comment. The president remains amenable to letting the effort proceed, the account’s officials say, with American forces ready to strike if he orders it. The report is a report of a backchannel, sourced to two people with knowledge of it; no government has confirmed its terms. It stands beside Saturday’s public record — progress claimed in Washington and Doha, no agreement, no text — as the night’s one new bearing on the talks, and the larger decision on resuming the war in full was again not announced.

Asunción at 0400: The Detention’s First Night

Nothing in the Asunción file moved overnight, and the desk files the stillness with the sequence now complete. The detention ordered Saturday by Judge Humberto Otazú stood through its first night: former President Santiago Peña held before trial at a military facility — the headquarters of the Presidential Escort Regiment, in the local accounts — and former central bank chief Carlos Carvallo held in a detention center, on the charges the evening filed, breach of trust and administration for personal gain against both men and producing false public documents against Mr. Carvallo besides. The fuller wire account filed below completes the custody sequence the day’s editions carried in pieces: house arrest on Friday, a move to the military facility Friday night for security reasons as protests gathered outside his home, and Saturday’s pretrial order as a separate court decision following the formal charges. Mr. Peña’s plea stands at not guilty, through his attorney, Ricardo Preda; each of his two charges could carry up to ten years, the prosecutor said. President Pedro Alliana governs into his second full day, and the deposit-guarantee ledger stands at $160 million paid to nearly 1.2 million savers. Protests over the Ueno collapse continue in the accounts. Sunday in Asunción is a day without a scheduled court item in any file the desk took in; the case’s next facts will be procedural — the asset-freeze requests and the six months prosecutors asked to formulate the final case — and none arrived in the night.

Kyiv at 0400: Pressure, Stated as Pressure

Ukraine’s president has put his reading of Friday night’s barrage on the record, and the overnight files carry it whole. Russia is ramping up its attacks to put pressure on Ukraine and its partners at the trilateral talks, President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Saturday, in the Arab News account filed below: “By intensifying its attacks on Ukraine and our infrastructure at this very moment, Russia is trying to additionally lay the groundwork for putting pressure on Ukraine’s position and that of its partners.” The barrage itself stands as the evening and midnight editions filed it — 36 ballistic missiles and 172 drones in the president’s count, four missiles intercepted, nine people wounded including a child in the Associated Press account filed below — with the damage ledger filled in overnight: the energy ministry’s statement that a massive attack on energy facilities plunged five regions into darkness, consumers in Dnipropetrovsk, Donetsk, Kyiv, Zhytomyr, and Kharkiv left without power and transmission in the capital significantly hampered, as temperatures drop toward winter. The president’s account adds that missiles among the 36 were KN-23s manufactured and supplied by North Korea, and that the interception figure reflects a shortage of Patriot interceptors. The reading — pressure — is the president’s; the ministry’s ledger is the file’s. Russia’s defence ministry’s own overnight figures run the other way: 411 Ukrainian drones shot down over ten Russian regions, and one person killed in Russia’s Belgorod region when a Ukrainian drone hit a private home in Grayvoron, in the Guardian’s war briefing filed below. The talks open in Abu Dhabi on Tuesday and Wednesday, November 10 and 11 — the fourth American-brokered round, the last in Geneva in February — with both delegations named, as the midnight edition filed them. No agenda, draft, or text has been published by any side.

The Count at 0400: A Sunday Without Figures

Neither Senate race was called in the night, and no new figure was published for either. The overnight accounts restate the standing figures without changing them: in Mississippi, the Democrat, Scott Colom, leads the incumbent Republican, Cindy Hyde-Smith, by 2,574 votes in the wire’s Friday tally — 467,930 to 465,356, about 49.1 percent to 48.8 with the independent just over 2 percent — in the Saturday update and the two-races account filed below; in Alaska, the Democrat, Mary Peltola, leads the incumbent Republican, Dan Sullivan, by 637 first-choice votes at 48.2 to 47.9, with roughly 74 percent of the expected vote counted. Sunday is a counting day in neither state’s published procedure. The count speaks next on Tuesday, November 10, three times over: Alaska’s next update is scheduled that day and is expected to carry almost all the early votes and a large batch of absentee ballots; Mississippi’s mail ballots postmarked by Election Day must arrive by that day, and its boards of commissioners have until that day to complete the counting of provisional ballots. Counties certify by November 13; Alaska’s absentee ballots arrive through November 18, when the ranked-choice tabulation is set if no candidate reaches 50 percent. Democrats hold at least 54 Senate seats whatever the two races decide, and Colorado’s 4th District stands uncalled with them, the Democrat, Eileen Laubacher, ahead by 3,172 votes in Friday’s figures. The House tallies in the Sunday accounts do not agree with each other — one Sunday explainer carries 248 seats called for the Democrats and 37 races uncalled, against the wire-based tallies of Saturday — and the disagreement is printed in the Cocked Hat.

Shizuoka at 0400: A 5.7 in the Night, the Count of the Hurt Still Rising

Central Japan supplied the night’s one act of nature, at 1:04 a.m. local time. An earthquake with a preliminary magnitude of 5.7 struck western Shizuoka Prefecture at a depth of about 40 kilometres, registering upper 5 on the Japanese seismic intensity scale in Chita, Aichi Prefecture — the strongest shaking observed in Aichi since March 1997, and upper 5 in western Shizuoka for the first time in 107 years, in the Kyodo account and the morning account filed below. The American survey measured the same quake at 5.5; the two magnitudes are printed in the Cocked Hat. No tsunami warning was issued. The early accounts counted three injured — a woman in her 50s taken to hospital with a fractured ankle in Tokoname, and a man in his 40s and a woman in her 80s who fell and struck their heads in Toyota, both conscious, in the first full account filed below — and the morning’s counts rose past twenty injured across Aichi and Shizuoka, with the tallies still differing by source at 0400. No abnormalities were detected at the Hamaoka nuclear power plant, its operator said, and the meteorological agency assessed the possibility that the quake was linked to a Nankai Trough megaquake as low, while advising residents in the strongly shaken areas to stay alert for about a week for further tremors up to upper 5, particularly in the next two to three days. Tokaido Shinkansen trains ran at reduced speed between Shizuoka and Gifu-Hashima and had resumed normal speed before noon local time; the central government set up a liaison office. Sunday in the region is an assessment day; the injury count is the figure still moving.

Sunday Opens: Mourning, Lights, a Ceremony, and a Monday

The day the night hands over is an observance day on three calendars at once. In Poland it is the first of two days of national mourning, with Kraków’s own mourning running through November 11 and the victims’ Mass at St. Mary’s at 4 p.m. In India it is the main day of Diwali, the festival of lights, whose five days run from Dhanteras on November 6 to Bhai Dooj on November 11, with Lakshmi Puja at dusk. In Jerusalem at 5 p.m. local time, the presidency formally hands Mr. Eisenkot his mandate and starts his 28 days. The markets are closed; Monday’s open will be the first to price a weekend that put two tankers on fire in the world’s oil strait, and Monday is also the day Stephen Miller takes office as chief of staff in Washington, the appointment the evening edition filed, and the first working day of the new Congress’s majority in the chamber the voters have already decided. Everything the week left open with a date on it carries Tuesday’s date: the Abu Dhabi talks open, the American inflation report is due, Mississippi’s mail deadline and Alaska’s next count fall, and the president’s larger Iran decision, promised Thursday and not announced in four editions since, has no date at all. The log proceeds to Sunday.

The Cocked Hat

Three bearings never give a point; they give a small triangle, and this paper prints the triangle. On Kraków’s suspect, the police accounts give his age as 24 and the updated international files give 25; the prosecutors’ spokesman’s confirmation, as carried in Polish media, gives 24 and a name, Michał O. On the wounded, the counts run 15 injured, 16 in hospital including the two officers and the suspect himself, and 12 in critical or life-threatening condition within them — three true sentences counting three different sets. On the distance from Kraków to the house in Ostrowiec Świętokrzyski, the files give 93 miles, about 120 miles, and just under 200 kilometres. On the three at the house, the overnight police caution — most likely his parents and sister, formal confirmation when the scene work finishes — stands in the running account beside the Sunday-morning police confirmation, in the Press Association account, that the three have been identified as his parents and his 21-year-old sister; the desk prints the confirmation as the later and fuller bearing, and the caution as the record of how it arrived. On Japan’s quake, the meteorological agency gives magnitude 5.7 and the American survey 5.5, and the injured run 3 in the first full account, 15 in a morning tally, and over 20, 22, and 24 in the later ones, by prefecture and by hour. On Israel’s Arab-led parties, the files agree on 19 seats and divide them 11 and 8 and also 12 and 7, between the Joint List and Ra’am. On the House, one Sunday tally counts 248 Democratic seats called and 37 races open, where Saturday’s wire-based accounts counted more called and fewer open; the Senate figures, by contrast, are single-valued in every account: 2,574 and 637, uncalled. On Hormuz’s tankers, the statements now agree on two, and agree on nothing else that would let a reader find them: no name, no flag, no crew, no confirmation independent of the Guards — beside an American statement, in the same files, that all mines in the waterway have been cleared. Against the triangle, the night’s single-valued facts are these: the three at the Kraków house are identified as the suspect’s parents and sister, and Poland’s mourning has begun; Mr. Eisenkot has been chosen to try first at a government, with the ceremony Sunday and 28 days to run; 22 crew are rescued and stable in the Gulf of Oman, from a ship no authority has named; the Guards say two tankers are burning on mines, and no side has named them; Mr. Peña spent the night in pretrial detention; the Abu Dhabi delegations stand named for Tuesday; no Senate race was called; and central Japan was shaken at upper 5 for the first time in 107 years, with more than twenty hurt and the count still rising. The log proceeds to Sunday.
