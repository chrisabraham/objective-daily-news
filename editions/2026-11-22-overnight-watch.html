The Night in Brief: One Attack in the Gulf in Two Tellings, a Tow That No Account Confirms, and a Strait at Zero

The night’s file is an attack on Bahrain that its two principals describe as two different attacks, a burning ship in the Baltic whose promised tow no account in the file confirms, and a strait that opens Sunday with no crossing counted in one reading of its counter and one crossing in another. Bahrain’s interior ministry reports an Iranian attack on a civilian industrial facility—fire, material damage, no injuries, the site unnamed—in the agency account filed below and in the Europe account filed below. The Revolutionary Guards, in a statement carried by IRNA, claim a combined missile and drone attack on United States positions at the Al-Riffa and Awali bases and the destruction of two Patriot radars, in the strikes account filed below. No United States confirmation of damage to any American equipment stands in the file at the stamp, in the dossier account filed below. The Sparta II’s tow, promised for Saturday night in the defence minister’s words, is not reported begun or done in any account the desk read; Finland’s prime minister said the war is spilling over, in the Baltic account filed below. The strait’s news brief records no crossing yet on Sunday as of 06:27 UTC, in the strait count filed below, while the same counter’s traffic table lists one, in the traffic account filed below—the desk prints the disagreement in the Cocked Hat. The desk takes the files in that order.

What the night settled is narrow: that Bahrain was attacked and a fire burned at a site Manama calls civilian; that alerts were issued, lifted and issued again; that interceptions were made, in Bahrain’s account; and that the week’s gold loss is a closed figure. What it left open is the rest: what was actually hit in Bahrain, whether the Sparta II moved in the night, the strait’s true Sunday count, the Darnytskyi’s damage, and every dated step of the week ahead, beginning with the North Atlantic Council on Monday.

The Gulf at 0400: Radars in Tehran’s Account, a Civilian Plant in Manama’s, and No American Word in the File

The Gulf’s overnight file opens where the week’s Bahrain file has stood since November 13—with the island attacked and the accounts apart—and adds missiles to a series that had run on drones. Bahrain’s interior ministry said an Iranian attack struck a civilian industrial facility on Saturday, starting a fire that Civil Defence teams worked to contain, with material damage and no injuries, and it did not name the facility or its location, in the agency account above and in the Munich account filed below. Residents saw smoke rising over Sitra Island, home to industrial sites including the state oil company’s refinery, in the agency account. The ministry’s night ran in alerts: a first alert urging residents to move to the nearest safe location, an all-clear, and then a second alert, in the interceptions account filed below. The Defence Force said its air defences intercepted a number of Iranian aerial attacks and that shrapnel from the interceptions caused material damage but no casualties, in the same accounts.

Tehran’s account names different targets and a different result. The Guards’ Aerospace Force carried out a combined missile-and-drone attack on United States positions at the Al-Riffa and Awali bases and struck the radars of two Patriot air defence systems, in the strikes account and in the claims account filed below. The Guards presented the strike as retaliation: for violations of Iranian airspace by United States MQ-1 and MQ-9 drones earlier that day, in the verification account filed below, and for an attack on a radar installation on Iran’s Hengam Island, in the dossier account. The Guards provided no evidence of damage to the radars, in the verification account, and neither the United States military nor Bahrain had confirmed damage to any American equipment by the early hours of Sunday, in the dossier account. Inside Iran’s own file the targets divide: the Fars agency named the Shaikh Isa and Al Juffair bases rather than Al-Riffa and Awali, in the strikes account, and IRNA carried a third version, that an interceptor missile launched from the Fifth Fleet base came down on Sitra Island and set off a large fire, in the fire account filed below. The available accounts do not establish whether the industrial site and the reported radar targets were separate locations, in the verification account. The desk prints the three tellings in the Cocked Hat and resolves none of them. The operation is the first in the series since November 13 in which Iran has claimed missiles as well as drones, in the dossier account.

The blockade’s file stands where the week left it, with its convoy unmoved in the night’s reading. More than 14 tankers stand gathered at Kharg Island to test the blockade, in the morning account filed below and in the Kharg account filed below; no Iranian official has confirmed a departure date, in the same account, and no departure stands in the overnight file. The one independent count in the file cuts the other way: nine large ships at anchor in the latest cloud-free frame of November 17, most parked since October, in the imagery digest filed below. A tracking firm’s second word on its own first report stands in the file: “Wishful thinking. That’s definitely not what’s going on,” in the convoy account filed below, which also records 31 laden sanctioned tankers already past the strait, holding in the Arabian Sea. Foreign Minister Araghchi’s condition stands as the midnight file printed it: as long as Iran’s demands are not met, discussion of the strait’s opening is pointless, in the morning account. Settled: the attack, the fire, the alerts and the claim. Open: the targets, the damage, and any American answer.

The Baltic at 0400: A Tow Promised for the Night and Not Confirmed at the Stamp, and a Prime Minister’s Word for the Spread

The Baltic file ends the night one announcement past the midnight stamp and no fact past it. The Sparta II, struck by two drones west of Gogland on Saturday morning, anchored off Loviisa and still burning at the turn of the log, was to be towed out of Finland’s territorial waters on Saturday night, in Defence Minister Häkkänen’s statement to Helsingin Sanomat, as the midnight file printed it. No account in the overnight file reports the tow begun, completed or postponed, and the desk files the ship exactly where the evening left her: at anchor, burning, her 16 crew in Russia. What the night added is the frame around her. Prime Minister Orpo said on X that “the war is spilling over into our neighbouring areas,” that Finnish authorities are handling the situation in the Gulf of Finland, and that Ukraine has the right to fight against the aggressor, in the Baltic account. President Stubb called the events serious and said he was in close contact with the country’s foreign and security leadership, in the wire account the search file carried. Swedish combat aircraft, operating under Finnish command, took part in safeguarding Finland’s and the alliance’s territorial integrity, in Sweden’s defence minister’s word, in the Baltic account and in the spillover account filed below. Finland twice imposed temporary air and sea traffic restrictions as a precaution against stray drones, and the Estonian vessel Kindral Kurvits deployed at Finland’s request, in the summary account filed below, which also names the crew’s ship: the Kapitan Sakharov, which took the 16 from their liferaft.

On the crew, the night’s accounts divide, and the division is filed as it stands. The Border Guard’s account, carried in every Western file, has the crew evacuated in lifeboats to a Russian vessel and on their way home. Kaliningrad’s governor, Mr. Besprozvannykh, wrote that a Russian rescue vessel approached the ship and was told by the crew that they did not need to be evacuated, that the crew was carrying out the necessary work and planned to continue on its way, in the governor account filed below; the same account records the governor’s preliminary finding of two drones and no threat to the territory’s supplies. On the ship herself, one account adds the sanctions history whole: the European Union sanctioned her owner and never the ship, which kept the St. Petersburg–Kaliningrad run past four allied coasts, in the sanctions account filed below; coastguard commanders said the vessel was known to have carried weapons and that nobody yet knows what is in her containers this time, in the same account. The strike’s position is given three ways in the file—international waters west of Gogland in Finland’s placing, about 11 nautical miles north of Vaindloo in Estonia’s, and three nautical miles from the Russian border in the Leningrad governor’s—in the summary account. The suspected helicopter violation stands under investigation at 4.1 kilometres near Loviisa at about 12:34, in the same account. Kyiv has claimed nothing in any account in the file. Settled: the strike, the drift, the anchorage, the evacuation in the Finnish account, and the allied patrols. Open: the tow, the cargo, the crew’s two tellings, and responsibility in Kyiv’s word.

The same counter’s traffic table lists one crossing today: the bulk carrier Lolo Gate, outbound at 06:13 UTC bound for Kandla, with the seven-day figure at 55 and eight crossings in the last 24 hours, in the traffic account above. The struck ships end the night where the midnight file left them. France at 0400: A Bulker Ballasted Down With Her Generators Dead, and a Law Still Waiting on Its Referral

The Landes file gained its machinery overnight and no movement. The Miro G, aground on sand near Lit-et-Mixe since 00:55 on Friday, has had her ballast tanks filled with seawater to improve her stability and reduce the risk of capsizing or moving farther toward the beach; the water must eventually be pumped out to lighten her before she can be pulled free, in the salvage account filed below. Her two main generators have failed, disrupting power to the pumps, and engineers are working to restore an alternative supply, in the same account. The salvage tug Abeille Bretagne has arrived with a 1,000-metre towline and cannot approach closer than about 900 metres in the shallow water, in the same account. The ship’s company thinned further on Saturday: after the 19 crew airlifted off on Friday, another 11 people—the captain, two other crew members, salvage experts and company representatives among them—were lifted off by helicopter ahead of deteriorating weather, in the digest account filed below, where the overnight file’s earlier standing of three remaining aboard no longer holds. Strong winds and rough seas are expected to continue, officials said refloating could take several days or longer, and no account promises success at the first weather window, in the salvage account. No pollution has been detected; she carries a little over a hundred tonnes of diesel, in the grounding account filed below. Settled: the ballast, the generators, the tug’s distance, and the empty ship. Open: the refloating.

Paris’s file did not move, and the desk records the standing with one consequence the night’s reading adds. The police firearms law, adopted Friday by 205 votes to 110, creates the rebuttable presumption and amends Article L.435-1; it has not been promulgated and has not entered into force, in the text account filed below. The consequence the overnight account states plainly: officers and gendarmes who have fired a lethal shot will no longer automatically be placed in custody, in the custody account filed below. The Senate left announced it would refer the matter to the Constitutional Council to block promulgation, in the same account; no filing of the referral stands in the overnight file, as at the midnight stamp. Promulgation should follow within 15 days unless the Council is seized, in the text account. Settled: the vote, the text and the custody consequence. Open: the referral, and the Council’s word.
