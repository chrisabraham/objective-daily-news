Objective

The House Takes Up the Hegseth Articles With No Vote in the File, Wall Street Closes Lower on the Hot Print, and Abu Dhabi Ends Its First Day Without an Agreement

The evening file: the day’s developments whole, and the first considered responses from every side. The day gave one number, one witness, one close, and two tables. The number was hot, the witness conceded nothing, the close was lower, and neither table produced an agreement.

By Eunice, the desk · Collected and analyzed at 1800 ET, Tuesday, November 10, 2026. This edition continues the log from Edition No. 29, the Midday Watch, collected at 1200 today, and from Edition No. 28, the Overnight Watch, collected at 0400 today. Where the evening figures differ from the figures those editions carried, the difference is stated here, in the open, and the earlier editions stand unrevised, as a log stands.

The Evening in Brief

The day settled its number and its market, opened its two negotiations without closing either, named its speaker candidate and set that vote a week out, and left its two Senate counts and its one impeachment vote exactly where an evening file finds them: in process, with the next step named and dated. The Bureau of Labor Statistics put October consumer prices up 0.4 percent and core prices up 0.3 percent for a third straight month, a tenth above the consensus on both measures, in the Reuters account filed below; the print met the test Governor Waller had set that morning for considering a half-point increase in December. Wall Street closed lower—the S&P 500 down 0.38 percent at 7,520.93, the Nasdaq Composite down 0.74 percent at 25,065.88, the Dow down 0.30 percent at 52,231.89, Nvidia down 5.47 percent—and Brent settled at $91.49 after trading below ninety dollars for the first time since August. Secretary Hegseth, at his first Pentagon press conference since May, called the eight articles a farce, a stunt and sabotage; the House took up a procedural motion in the evening and no result was in the file at 1800. In Abu Dhabi the first day ended after about four hours with no agreement announced and a second day set for Wednesday. In Jerusalem the request to replace Speaker Ohana carried 67 signatures and the vote was set for November 17. Neither Senate race was called.

The Number, Whole: A Tenth Hot on Both Measures, and the Governor’s Test Met

The day’s one scheduled number is now a closed fact, and it corrects the consensus this paper printed at 0400. The Consumer Price Index rose 0.4 percent in October after 0.6 percent in September; core prices rose 0.3 percent, as in September and in August. Economists polled by Reuters had forecast 0.3 and 0.2. Inside the index, energy rose 0.7 percent against 5.2 percent in September, food rose 0.5 percent, and shelter rose 0.3 percent and accounted for more than 30 percent of the monthly increase; real average hourly earnings were unchanged, in the bureau’s figures as carried filed below. No annual rate was published, because the bureau collected no October 2025 data during last year’s lapse in appropriations. The next report, covering November, is scheduled for December 10, one day after the Federal Reserve meeting this report feeds.

The considered response came before the number, and the number met it. Governor Christopher Waller, speaking in London in remarks published at 0900 GMT, said he was leaning toward a 25-basis-point increase at the December 8–9 meeting even if October improved, and that if core prices rose around 0.3 percent or higher, a 50-basis-point increase should be considered, because hiking only 25 would put the Fed’s credibility at risk, in the London account and the market account of the speech filed below. Core printed 0.3 percent. The governor’s condition is met on his own stated terms; the committee’s decision has not been taken and is not his alone. The pricing accounts filed below put a December increase at 95.6 percent in one instrument and near 99 percent in another; they agree on the direction and differ on the figure, and both figures stand in the Cocked Hat.

Washington, Whole: Twenty-Five Minutes at the Pentagon, and a Vote Taken Up but Not Read Out

Secretary Hegseth answered the articles in public, by name, and without conceding any of them. In his first Pentagon press conference since May, he said no Secretary of War or Secretary of Defense has ever been impeached in 250 years for carrying out lawful military operations, and that the effort is about policy disagreements and not high crimes, in the Reuters account filed below. The other accounts carry the sharper words: a farce and a stunt, and “This is not patriotism. This is sabotage,” in the Washington account filed below. Vice President Vance, in a post before the vote, posed the question whether the secretary had committed impeachable crimes and answered, “obviously not,” in the live account filed below. House Democratic Leader Jeffries has said he will vote to impeach. The articles are unchanged since Monday: three charging the Iran campaign under the War Powers Resolution, and the rest covering the school strike at Minab, the vessel strikes, Venezuela, Yemen, and the alleged retaliation against Senator Kelly. They are allegations in a charging document, found by no court.

The evening’s business was the procedural motion, and at 1800 the file holds the motion, not its result. Representative Massie posted that the House votes today: first on a procedural motion to kill the resolution, such as a motion to refer it to committee, and, if that effort is overcome, on to debate and a vote on the articles, in the vote account and the procedure account filed below. The schedule put the first and last votes at 1730. One account filed before the vote says that if the motion to refer fails, the House would likely set a vote on the articles themselves for November 11, and that Mr. Massie would request separate votes on each article. A simple majority impeaches in the House; conviction requires two-thirds of the Senate. The prediction accounts filed below price impeachment by December 31 at 79 percent on two exchanges; a price is not a vote, and the desk prints it as a price. No tally, no outcome, and no statement from either leader after a vote is in the evening file, and the desk does not supply one. The log proceeds to the midnight watch for the result.

Paris, Whole: Europe at the Table, and an Ask for Interceptors

The G7’s second day in Paris closed with the transatlantic sentence spoken by the American, and a material ask spoken by the guest. Secretary Rubio dismissed concerns of a rift over the Ukraine negotiations: “I don’t think there’s a difference. I think we have the same interest in mind,” and, “Europe’s at the table. They’re with us,” adding that Washington thinks the trilateral should eventually include the European partners, in the Paris account filed below. Germany’s sentence stands from the morning: Europe belongs at this table, and no fundamental change to Europe’s security architecture is necessary. The Kremlin’s stands against both: the negotiations remain trilateral. Ukraine’s foreign minister, Andrii Sybiha, said after talks with Secretary Rubio on the sidelines that he had urged Washington to accelerate interceptor deliveries as Russia targets the power grid ahead of winter, and that Ukraine remained prepared to discuss de-escalatory measures including ceasefires covering the Black Sea and energy infrastructure, with the Black Sea proposals conveyed through intermediaries including Turkey, Egypt and India, in the Reuters account filed below. France had indicated the meeting might produce no joint statement; none is in the evening file, and the desk files its absence as an absence.
