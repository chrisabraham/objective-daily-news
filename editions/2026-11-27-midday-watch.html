In New York the correction runs the other way: the premarket figures near 1.4, 1.7 and 2.4 percent became, at the open, 0.76, 0.77 and 0.73 percent on the three main indexes, in the opening account filed below. What midday settled is China’s closes and their place since October 2024; New York’s opening levels against Wednesday’s closes; Europe’s midday levels in two accounts an hour apart; Ukraine’s day count—six dead and 25 injured in the regional authorities’ figures, with power cut in six regions; Friday’s first claimed hull in the strait, north of Ghavam Island; Friday’s crossing count in the visible counter, at seven by midday; and the storm’s count at 0330 Eastern, 736,000 customers still out. China at Midday: The Close Corrects the Afternoon, and the Record Is in the Technology Lines

The cabinet’s direction stands as the Overnight Watch printed it, and the market’s answer is now a close instead of an afternoon. China will plan new policies for its capital markets, and state-backed national team investors will play a bigger role in stabilising markets, the official agency said, citing the cabinet, in the support account filed below; Central Huijin will enhance its role as a quasi-stabilisation fund. The closing account adds two pieces of machinery the afternoon accounts did not carry: relaxations to margin lending rules, aimed at reducing selling pressure, and a separate report that regulators would tighten rules on share sales by controlling shareholders and guard against listings by firms that inflate earnings.

The figures at the bell are the correction. The Shanghai Composite ended 3.7 percent up and the CSI 300 4.2 percent higher, both notching their biggest daily gains since October 2024, when China rolled out stimulus to revive the market; the indexes had stood at 17-month lows before the session. The STAR 50 surged 15 percent for its biggest one-day jump in two years, against 12 percent in the afternoon accounts; the semiconductor sub-index soared 19 percent, its biggest single-day gain on record, against 15 percent in the afternoon’s markets view. Hong Kong followed: the Hang Seng gained 2.1 percent and its technology index more than 6 percent. The context in the closing account is plain and is printed as it stands: prices had been sliding since July on weak consumer demand, the prolonged property downturn and trade tensions threatening exports, and the technology indexes had slumped roughly 50 percent since July. One investor’s line in the account states the reading the desk can verify no further: at least now there is something to count on—the national team will come to the rescue if things really turn south. Settled: the close, and its rank since 2024. Open: whether the buying continues on Monday, and at what size; no account in the file gives a figure for Friday’s purchases.

The session’s shape is fixed by the calendar. Inside the open, the smaller companies and the chips led in the session account filed below: the small-cap fund up 0.86 percent, the Nasdaq-100 fund up 0.76 percent, Nvidia up 1.8 percent in early trading against 3 percent in the premarket, Micron up 4.8 percent, SanDisk up 8.5 percent, and Anthropic up 2.6 percent. Settled: the open, and the hour of the close. Open: the close itself, an hour after this edition’s stamp.

Ukraine at Midday: The 27 Locations Begin to Get Their Names, Six Dead Over the Day, and Power Cut in Six Regions

The Overnight Watch printed Kyiv’s count complete as a count and empty as a map: 155 drones launched, 110 downed or suppressed by 0800, hits at 27 locations, and no regions named in the Air Force’s post. The midday file fills part of that map from the regions’ own accounts, in the attack account filed below. In Izyum, the combined rocket and drone strike injured six people and damaged blocks of flats and private houses, administrative buildings, a municipal utility facility and vehicles, with fires in parts of the city. In Kharkiv, a morning drone attack on two districts injured three residents. In the Dnipropetrovsk region, more than 20 attacks across five districts killed one person and injured two. In the Vinnytsia region, energy and railway infrastructure were damaged, with no casualties.

The day’s wider count is now stated, and its scope is stated with it. Russian attacks killed six people and injured 25 across Ukraine over the day to November 27, according to the regional authorities, in the day account filed below: an 86-year-old woman killed in Kharkiv, where seven were injured; one killed and six injured in Kherson; one killed and three injured in Donetsk; and the remaining figures spread across the regions in the accounts filed below, including Zaporizhzhia, where one man was killed and at least 16 injured over the day in the infrastructure account filed below. The desk prints the scope plainly, as one account in the file states it: the casualty figures cover attacks over the preceding day, not deaths and injuries from the overnight drone launches alone, and they remain provisional. The energy count is six regions without power—Dnipropetrovsk, Zaporizhzhia, Kirovohrad, Sumy, Kharkiv and Kherson—after strikes on substations and distribution networks, the first deputy energy minister said; the same account names the disruption heaviest in Dnipropetrovsk and Kherson. Estonia scrambled NATO fighter jets after a temporary air threat warning over possible drone activity, in the infrastructure account; no account in the file reports a crossing into Estonian airspace. Moscow’s account of the night stands where the Overnight Watch printed it—substations, locomotives, industrial plants, no missiles—and no Russian response to the day’s casualty figures stands in the midday file. Settled: the launch and interception counts, and the regions now named. Open: the full list of 27 locations, and the strike Kyiv says is being prepared.

The Strait at Midday: A Tanker Claimed North of Ghavam Island, a Second Turned Back, and Seven Crossings in the Counter

Tehran’s Friday claim arrived in the morning and follows the week’s form exactly. Two oil tankers attempted to pass secretly through an unauthorized route north of Ghavam Island on Friday morning; one exploded after colliding with a naval mine, and the other was forced to turn back, the Guard Navy said in a statement carried by Fars and reported by Anadolu, in the Ghavam account filed below and in the second form filed below, which names the island Ghanem in its rendering. The statement named no vessel, gave no flag, cargo, crew status or casualty count, and at the stamp no maritime advisory, ship-owner statement or American response had confirmed the explosion in any account in the file. The claim extends a week of near-daily announcements: two tankers north of Khasab on Thursday, a gas carrier on Wednesday in one form, three tankers under escort on Monday in the Ghavam account’s timeline, and two supertankers the day before that—each unnamed, each unconfirmed by the maritime office, whose confirmed file stands at Wednesday’s two projectile incidents.

The visible counter’s Friday, empty at dawn, is no longer empty. Seven commercial ships had crossed since 0000 UTC by the midday reading—three inbound and four outbound—against two crossings in all of Thursday in the counter’s current form, in the counter’s traffic page filed below. The morning’s names are the container ship TAVA4 inbound and the general cargo ship Pacific Harmony outbound, both at 0702 UTC in the counter’s brief form, and the bulk carrier New Pioneer inbound at 0613; around midday UTC the counter lists the container ships Selen and SSL Krishna outbound. The seven days stand at 45 crossings, 6.4 a day, about 7 percent of the pre-crisis 84.8 a day, and the status stands restricted, as since October 19. Whether either of the Guards’ two tankers stands among the counter’s seven, no account in the file says; a ship that turned back would not cross, and a ship sailing dark would not be counted. Settled: Friday’s seven crossings in the one instrument, and Thursday’s two in its current form. Open, as all week: every hull Tehran counts, and every crew.

Ras Tanura stands exactly where Thursday left it: loadings suspended after Wednesday’s claimed strike, in a shipping source’s statement to the refinery account filed below; whether they have resumed unclear; and no word from the plant’s owner in any account at the stamp, into a third day. Settled: Thursday’s settlement, and the range of Friday’s readings. Open: Sunday’s decision, the deliverable-supply evidence, and Ras Tanura.

The Storm at Midday: 736,000 at 0330, One Outage in Four Restored Overnight, and the Weather Service’s Turn

The storm’s midday count is a correction to the night’s, and it runs downward by a quarter. Nearly 736,000 customers were still without electricity at 0330 Eastern on Friday, down from close to one million on Thursday night, in the recovery account filed below, citing the outage tracker. A second count, on Friday morning without an hour attached, puts more than 690,000 outages in the storm account filed below. Pennsylvania held about 146,000 customers out on Friday morning, concentrated in the east of the state, and Pennsylvania and New Jersey together held about 278,000. Maryland stood at 81,790 in the outages account filed below, down from roughly 140,000 on Thursday night. In New York, the utility figures run by territory in the New York account filed below: 27,512 customers out at 0950 in one territory, Sullivan County hardest hit at 8,316; 3,134 out at 0935 in a second; 5,734 out at 0800 on Long Island, where the utility said it had restored more than 54,800 of about 60,000 affected—90 percent—and expected the vast majority of the rest restored by Friday night.

The weather itself has begun its turn, in the weather service’s own words: widespread inclement weather on Friday morning for a large portion of the eastern United States, conditions improving throughout the day, and the huge system taking some time to weaken as it moves into the Canadian Maritimes on Saturday. The travel file adds Friday’s morning figures to Thursday’s: more than 300 flights cancelled and 9,800 delayed in the storm account’s Friday count, against more than 450 cancelled and 2,500 delayed on Thanksgiving Day in the recovery account. The flood file stands: waist-high water in Norfolk with at least 39 rescue operations, Washington’s coastal flood warning extended to noon on Friday, and Central Park’s 1.82 inches the wettest Thanksgiving on record in the city. The parade stands postponed to Saturday. No utility in the file has announced a single hour for full restoration; the Virginia account in the overnight file put the hardest-hit repairs at several days, and the Long Island account’s Friday-night expectation is the nearest deadline any utility has given. Settled: the 0330 count, and the direction. Open: the restoration clock, state by state.

Libya at Midday: The Silence Stands on a Sixth Day

The claims have not been independently verified, and the Guards did not provide the vessels’ identities or details of casualties. The maritime agency recorded an incident approximately 50 nautical miles west of Dubai on November 25, in which a tanker’s master reported an explosion from an unknown projectile close to the vessel, and an outbound tanker struck by an unknown projectile 18 nautical miles east of Khasab on the same date. The accounts do not confirm the claim that two tankers struck sea mines.

The Wall Street Journal—Oil prices mixed ahead of OPEC+ meeting as Middle East supply risks persist

Oil prices were mixed on Friday as traders weighed renewed threats to Middle Eastern supplies ahead of a meeting of OPEC and its allies during the weekend. Brent crude futures were broadly flat at $96.56 a barrel, while West Texas Intermediate rose 1.8 percent to $85.15 a barrel. Sunday’s meeting is expected to discuss collective output policy for 2027, with existing agreements set to expire at the end of this year, and the group’s assessment of members’ sustainable production capacities will help determine individual quotas. The meeting could end without a final decision, leaving current quotas in place, an analyst at Commerzbank said; with the exception of Iraq, most members are producing well below existing quotas, while some countries, including Russia, are unable to increase output further.

Reuters—Brent trades near month’s high as Mideast conflict threatens supply

Brent crude futures hovered near this month’s high on Friday as ongoing attacks on tankers in the Strait of Hormuz and Saudi energy infrastructure heightened concerns over oil exports. Brent fell 38 cents, or 0.39 percent, to $96.19 a barrel by 0140 GMT, after gaining nearly $2 on Thursday to settle at their highest since November 6. The energy ministry said a projectile struck the Jazan refinery on Thursday, temporarily halting some operations and killing a Nepalese worker. Given the disruption, the producers’ group is likely to maintain its output targets for at least the first quarter of 2027 at a meeting on Sunday, two people with knowledge of the talks said. Ukraine has intensified drone attacks on Russian oil refineries, with the latest incident causing a fire at the Slavyansk ECO refinery. An incident at the Bacalhau oilfield offshore Brazil has halted production for an estimated five to eight weeks, the operator and its partner said on Wednesday.

CNN—Houthis strike Saudi oil infrastructure as Riyadh intensifies attacks on Yemen

A projectile struck an oil refinery in Saudi Arabia’s southwestern Jazan region on Thursday, killing a Nepalese worker and injuring several others, according to the Saudi Ministry of Energy; the strike sparked a fire and temporarily suspended some operations. The attack came a day after the Houthis claimed to have struck Saudi Arabia’s biggest oil refinery at Ras Tanura. The Houthi armed forces said the attack caused a fire at Ras Tanura, part of one of the world’s largest crude oil export terminals, with a refining capacity of 550,000 barrels per day. A shipping source told CNN on Thursday that oil loadings at the terminal were suspended after the attack, though it was unclear whether they have resumed. The state oil company did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

ANI, carried by Industries News—Thanksgiving storm: recovery underway after nearly 1 million power outages

Power restoration efforts are underway across the northeastern United States after a Thanksgiving storm knocked out electricity for nearly one million utility customers, flooded coastal communities and disrupted air and road travel, with Pennsylvania and New Jersey continuing to report significant outages. Nearly 736,000 customers were still without electricity as of 3:30 a.m. ET on Friday, down from close to one million on Thursday night, citing the outage-tracking site. Pennsylvania held approximately 146,000 customers without electricity on Friday morning, and Pennsylvania and New Jersey together held around 278,000. In Norfolk, waist-high floodwaters inundated streets, prompting at least 39 rescue operations. A coastal flood warning in Washington was extended until noon on Friday. New York City received 1.82 inches of rain in Central Park, its wettest Thanksgiving on record. More than 2,500 flights were delayed and over 450 cancelled across the United States on Thanksgiving Day, with disruption continuing into Friday morning.
