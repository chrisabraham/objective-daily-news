Dublin at 0400: A Team Named by Mr. Costa, an Evasive Answer From the Kremlin, and a War That Ran Under the Diplomacy

Europe’s chair, empty through two editions, has a shape this morning and still no names in it. On Friday the President of the European Council, Mr. Costa, said the European team would be represented by the European Union’s institutions and the E5 nations of France, Germany, Italy, Poland and the United Kingdom, and that the team will act in full unity and in close coordination with all member states, in the CNN account at the top of this report. The shape answers Thursday’s three formats in part: Ms. Kallas’s treaty reading named Mr. Costa and herself; Chancellor Merz named the E3 and a group of five; President Macron said the format would be determined later. Mr. Costa’s Friday sentence keeps the institutions and adopts a five that adds Italy and Poland to the E3’s three and includes the United Kingdom from outside the Union. It does not name the persons who will sit, and Chancellor Merz’s pointed sentence to Washington stands in the same account: “Please, no surprises. Keep playing as part of Team Europe and as part of the team with Ukraine.”

Moscow’s Friday answer is the file’s second new sentence, and it commits the Kremlin to nothing. Spokesman Peskov told journalists that President Putin has never refused to engage with the Europeans, and that if the Europeans truly have someone authorized to hold discussions, Moscow would be prepared to talk with them; once Moscow understands their actual intentions and their willingness to contribute to the peace process, it can decide regarding a dialogue in an expanded format, in the CNN account above. President Putin’s own words at Thursday’s meeting stand beside it: Moscow has never been opposed to engaging in dialogue with Europe and has never broken it off, and territorial issues will always be a bone of contention. Against those sentences stand the week’s harder ones, unwithdrawn: aide Ushakov’s reading that the Europeans must decide whether they want peace or the continuation of the war, Foreign Minister Lavrov’s judgment that European involvement is not a promising approach, and delegation head Medinsky’s reported refusal, all standing in the earlier editions. Mr. Dmitriev’s sentence is the mildest in the Russian file: working on European security is a great, important step for Europe. Settled: a team shape is named in Europe, and Moscow has not refused it in so many words. Open: whether Moscow accepts it, who fills it, and when and where it sits.

The exchange and the ground war moved on their own clocks while the format was argued. The prisoner file’s numbers still do not meet: the exchange will exceed 1,000 on each side and may take place within two to three weeks, General Zorin said, according to Interfax, in the envoys account above, which also records the sides considering up to 1,500 each; President Zelenskyy’s 1,000 to 1,200 a side stands from Wednesday; and the caution in the envoy account filed below stands over all of it—until Moscow and Kyiv confirm lists, categories, verification and transfer arrangements, the exchange is a negotiating goal, not a completed operation. On the ground, at least 10 people were killed and 48 injured in Ukraine from Thursday into Friday in regional officials’ figures, Russian air defenses intercepted nearly 1,198 Ukrainian drones overnight into Friday in the defense ministry’s account with at least eight killed in Russia in CNN’s tally of governors’ figures, Ukraine’s forces hit drone production and repair sites at Rostov-on-Don on Friday, and Ukrainian troops still faced Starshield speed limits of 161 kilometres per hour on Friday, President Zelenskyy said, with Kyiv asking Washington and SpaceX for clarification and SpaceX’s standing answer that there had been no change in its service—all in the CNN account above. Nvidia at 0400: A Financing File Opens in the Journal’s Account, the Deadline Stays Passed, and Tuesday Is the Next Date

The night’s new Nvidia reporting is not about Congress or the board; it is about the company’s own money. Nvidia used its balance sheet to finance artificial-intelligence startups and data-centre operators so they would keep buying its chips instead of letting prices fall, according to a Wall Street Journal report carried overnight in the financing account at the top of this report. The financing answered a surplus of silicon and memory ordered from Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company; instead of cutting prices to clear it, the company funded the customers that would absorb it. The scale in the filing, as analysed by SemiAnalysis, is commitments and guarantees of about $530 billion, up from $184 billion a quarter earlier, including $279 billion of supply and capacity obligations and $108.5 billion of maximum gross guarantee exposure, in the same account. Inside that figure sits the August argument the report reconstructs: Ms. Kress argued for reining in exposure to smaller cloud providers and to OpenAI and Anthropic and cut a proposed OpenAI guarantee from about $250 billion to $125 billion, while Mr. Huang pushed the other way and privately acknowledged the largest cloud companies were moving toward their own chips. The desk prints the report as the Journal’s account, carried in one overnight source; no company answer to it stands in the file.

The Apex Wire — Nvidia funded startups to buy its own chips, a Journal report says

Nvidia used its own balance sheet to finance artificial-intelligence startups and data-centre operators so they would keep buying its chips instead of letting prices fall, according to a Wall Street Journal report, as the chipmaker closed its worst week since April 2025, down 13 percent and $573 billion in market value. The report says the financing answered a surplus of silicon and memory ordered from Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company: instead of cutting prices, the company funded the customers that would absorb it. Ms. Kress, now interim chief executive, pushed back in a tense August meeting, cutting a proposed OpenAI guarantee from about $250 billion to $125 billion. Nvidia’s filing shows commitments and guarantees of about $530 billion, up from $184 billion a quarter earlier, including $279 billion of supply and capacity obligations and $108.5 billion of maximum gross guarantee exposure. The board is searching for a permanent chief executive and has approached Arm chief executive Rene Haas as one of several candidates, the account says. Nvidia reports third-quarter results on Tuesday, November 17, with Ms. Kress presenting.

Reuters, via WIXX — producer prices rise moderately in October, but inflation broadens out

American producer prices rose moderately in October, but inflation at the factory gate posted its largest annual gain in four years, supporting the view that the Federal Reserve could continue raising interest rates after next month’s widely expected increase. The Producer Price Index for final demand rose 0.3 percent last month after an upwardly revised 1.4 percent surge in September, the Bureau of Labor Statistics said, and shot up 6.5 percent year over year in October, the largest annual gain since November 2022, after advancing 6.3 percent in September. With the producer and consumer data in hand, economists estimated core personal consumption expenditures inflation rose 0.3 percent in October, and forecast it advancing 3.2 percent over the year. Financial markets anticipated another increase in borrowing costs in January, the CME FedWatch tool showed, wavering between a quarter-point hike in December and room for a half-point increase. Governor Waller’s comments and the hot consumer readings stand behind the pricing; the target range stands at 3.75 to 4.00 percent.

CoinGabbar — the October producer data puts the December Federal Reserve hike in focus

The October producer price data landed on November 13, 2026, and sent a mixed message: monthly producer inflation slowed to 0.3 percent, below the 0.4 percent economists expected, yet the annual rate climbed to 6.5 percent, the highest since November 2022, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics. Particle News — Spain’s two-week campaign kicks off as parties race to mobilize voters

The official campaign opened on Friday, November 13, with Prime Minister Sánchez, Mr. Feijóo and Mr. Abascal launching nationwide tour events in key provinces. Mr. Sánchez set a 75 percent participation target for the November 29 vote, and his party is elevating Ms. Begoña Gómez, planning an event with Brazil’s President Lula da Silva and promising further surprises to mobilize the progressive bloc. The Popular Party is pushing a vote-conversion message, with regional leader Alfonso Rueda urging voters to channel discontent with the government into support for Mr. Feijóo and to prevent Vox from becoming decisive. A GAD3 survey for ABC gives the Popular Party a clear advantage while the state survey projects a Socialist lead, so turnout and tight regional contests such as La Rioja could decide which bloc reaches the 176 seats needed for a progressive majority. Recent events shaping the campaign include the Urda train accident and ministerial fallout and judicial probes touching Socialist circles.

RussPain — the Wild interviews lay bare divisions over housing and migration in Spain

Broadcaster Jordi Wild’s campaign interviews put competing positions on housing and migration in focus, alongside a dispute over Ceuta. On housing, Mr. Sánchez wants to change the land law to enable more construction, Ms. Colau opposes speculation, and Mr. Feijóo backed building more homes, linking a million new properties to 500,000 jobs, in a campaign unfolding amid an acute housing-affordability crisis; the housing measures followed a general strike and large demonstrations over housing costs, the Associated Press reported. On the economy, Mr. Sánchez said Spain was experiencing one of the strongest periods in its democratic history and pointed to increases in the minimum wage and pensions; Mr. Feijóo disputed the positive assessment, saying he did not believe the data. On migration, Mr. Feijóo claimed half a million immigrants were receiving payments despite not having worked a single hour in the previous year. Ms. Colau called for tax reform so people with greater wealth pay more, and described Mr. Feijóo as a sincere man of the right.
