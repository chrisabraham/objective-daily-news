The Columns

Editorials

This paper has opinions, the way any paper kept by people in motion does. It files them here, in the open, under the largest stamp it owns.

The desk’s reports carry the objective stamp, which is a claim about method: no agenda, no advocacy, no behavior modeled, no citizens trained. An editorial is the opposite instrument—an argument, made on purpose—and the paper will not let the two be confused. Every editorial and column in this section carries the giant subjective stamp at its head, and the word editorial in its furniture. A reader who wants only the record can skip this section entire and lose nothing but our opinions, which is the correct price for them.

The fine print, in brief. Objective, as this paper uses the word, means the desk is not driven by an agenda, is not an advocate, is not a politician, is not modeling behavior, and is not training up citizens: it is reporting the news as if it were objective. Subjective means the item comes from a source with its own position, as all sources have; it is printed because it is useful information, weighed by the reader. Objectivity is relative; everything is relative, even relativity is relative; absolutism is wrong. No source in these pages is more reputable than a press release issued directly from Langley.
